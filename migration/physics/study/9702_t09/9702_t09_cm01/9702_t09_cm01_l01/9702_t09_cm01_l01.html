A distance equation that cannot work

A design card proposes a distance equation. Read its symbols first:

  • x means distance, measured in metres (m).
  • v means speed: distance travelled per time taken, measured in metres per second (m/s).
  • t means elapsed time, measured in seconds (s).
  • a means acceleration: the rate of change of velocity. Velocity includes speed and direction. Its change is measured in m/s, then divided by elapsed time in s.

That gives acceleration the unit (m/s)/s = m/(s × s) = m/s². The raised 2 counts two second factors. We can also write m/s as m s−1, and m/s² as m s−2. Negative unit powers mean division.

The card claims:

x = vt + at

Here v is speed, a is acceleration and t is time. Square brackets mean “the unit of”. For example, [x] means the unit of x.

  1. [x] = m. The symbol m here means metre, the SI base unit of length.

  2. [vt] = (m s−1)(s) = m. The symbol s means second. The power −1 means “per second”.

  3. [at] = (m s−2)(s) = m s−1.

Conclusion: the equation is not homogeneous. It cannot be correct as written.

What homogeneity means

Homogeneity rule

An equation is homogeneous only when both sides, and every term joined by addition or subtraction, reduce to the same SI base-unit form.

A term is one complete part separated by + or −. Multiplication and division stay inside the term while you simplify its units.

A pure number, such as 2, has no unit. It is dimensionless. A stated constant may or may not be dimensionless, so never assume.

  1. Separate the terms joined by + or −.
  2. Replace each quantity by its SI base-unit form.
  3. Simplify one term at a time.
  4. Compare the power of every base unit.
  5. State exactly what the comparison shows.

Recall kg for kilogram, m for metre, s for second, A for ampere and K for kelvin. An exponent is a raised power. The ledger below has one column for each unit power.

Example 1: check every term

Worked example

Does E = Cmv2 + Fd pass the unit check?

E is energy, m is mass, v is speed, F is force and d is distance. In this example, C has no unit, so [C] = 1.

  1. Left side:

    [E] = kg m2 s−2

  2. First right-hand term. Squaring m s−1 squares both factors:

    [Cmv2] = (1)(kg)(m s−1)2
    = (kg)(m2 s−2)
    = kg m2 s−2

  3. Second right-hand term:

    [Fd] = (kg m s−2)(m)
    = kg m2 s−2

  4. All three terms match.

    Answer: the equation is homogeneous.

Check-in 1

For a supplied model, y is a length in metres (m), v is speed in m/s, t is time in seconds (s), and c is a pure number. Which equation passes the unit check?

Example 2: find a missing power

In this supplied model, R is a force, M a mass, L a length and t elapsed time. In R = MLn/t2, the superscript n is an unknown power. We find it by matching exponents.

[R] = kg m s−2, [M] = kg, [L] = m and [t] = s.

  1. Substitute complete units:

    kg m s−2 = (kg)(mn) / s2

  2. Writing division by s2 as s−2 gives:

    kg m1 s−2 = kg mn s−2

  3. The kilogram and second powers already match. Compare metre powers:

    1 = n

  4. Check the whole equation with n = 1:

    kg × m1 × s−2 = kg m s−2

    Answer: n = 1.

Check-in 2

A model claims E = Bv², where E is energy with unit kg m²/s² and v is speed with unit m/s. Must the multiplying constant B have a unit?

Example 3: does the coefficient need a unit?

  • Consider P = CρAu3.
  • Here P means power, energy transferred per time taken; C is a multiplying constant, also called a coefficient.
  • The Greek letter ρ, read “rho”, represents density here.
  • A represents area and u represents speed.
  • This italic A is a quantity symbol; the upright unit A in the ledger means ampere.

[P] = kg m2 s−3, [ρ] = kg m−3, [A] = m2 and [u] = m s−1.

  1. First leave C aside and simplify the other factors:

    [ρAu3] = (kg m−3)(m2)(m s−1)3

  2. Cube the whole speed unit:

    (m s−1)3 = m3 s−3

  3. Add powers when multiplying like base units:

    kg m−3 × m2 × m3 s−3
    = kg m−3+2+3 s−3
    = kg m2 s−3

  4. The product already has the unit of power.

    Answer: [C] = 1. The coefficient is dimensionless in this equation.

A coefficient is not automatically dimensionless. Work out what unit is needed.

What the check can prove

For example, both mv2 and Fd have energy units. That does not tell us whether either expression models a particular situation, or whether a numerical factor such as ½ is needed.

Recall

  1. Separate terms joined by + or −.
  2. Substitute SI base units for each quantity and any stated constant.
  3. Simplify each complete term.
  4. Compare every base-unit power.
  5. For an unknown power, equate exponents. For an unknown constant unit, find the factor needed to complete the match.

Precise conclusion: a mismatch means the equation cannot be correct as written. A match means it passes the homogeneity check; it does not prove the physics.

More worked examples

Worked example 4

Check a quotient using fractions

A supplied model claims t² = d/a. Here t is elapsed time in seconds (s), d is distance in metres (m), and a is acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²). The acceleration is non-zero so division is defined.

  1. The left side squares time: [t²] = s × s = s².
  2. Substitute the complete right-side units: [d/a] = m/(m/s²).
  3. Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by that fraction turned over: m × (s²/m).
  4. Cancel the metre factor above and below: m s²/m = s².
  5. Both sides have unit s², so this model passes the unit check.

The different proposal t² = da would give m × (m/s²) = m²/s² on the right. That does not match s².

Worked example 5

Inspect every added term

  • Suppose H is energy with unit kg m²/s².
  • Test H = mv² + Ft.
  • Here m means mass in kilograms (kg), v means speed in metres per second (m/s), F means force in newtons (N = kg m/s²), and t means time in seconds (s).
  • In unit expressions, upright m means metre.
  1. Square the entire speed unit: (m/s)² = m²/s².
  2. The first term gives [mv²] = kg × (m²/s²) = kg m²/s².
  3. The second term gives [Ft] = (kg m/s²) × s = kg m/s.
  4. The first term matches energy; the second does not.
  5. The equation is not homogeneous.

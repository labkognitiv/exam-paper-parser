Stem-and-leaf diagrams

Keep every value.
See the shape.

A list can hide its pattern. A stem-and-leaf diagram orders the values while keeping every observation visible, so you can read, compare and calculate from the same display.

Start the lesson

Order first

  • Ascending order means smallest to largest.
  • Keep repeated values. Repetition tells us how often a value occurs.
  • The median is the middle value after ordering.
  • If there are two middle values, add them and divide by 2 to find their average.
  • For an odd number of values, remove the median before finding the lower and upper quartiles.
  • The lower quartile, , is the middle of the lower half.
  • The upper quartile, , is the middle of the upper half.
Worked example

Five decimal values

3.7, 2.9, 4.1, 3.2, 2.6

  1. Order them: 2.6, 2.9, 3.2, 3.7, 4.1.
  2. The middle, third value is 3.2.
  3. Lower half: 2.6, 2.9. Upper half: 3.7, 4.1.

Median = 3.2; Q1 = 2.75; Q3 = 3.9.

Order, keep repeats, then locate the middle.

Split each value into a stem and a leaf

  • The stem holds the leading digit or digits.
  • The leaf is the final digit.
  • For whole numbers from 12 to 35, the stem is the tens digit and the leaf is the units digit.
  • A key joins one stem and leaf to decode one real value.
  • The key must include the context or unit.
  • Leaves are written in increasing order from left to right.
Worked example

Waiting times

12, 14, 17, 21, 23, 23, 28, 31, 35 minutes

Key: 2 | 3 means 23 minutes.

  • Nine original values give nine leaves.
  • The repeated 23 gives two leaves.
  • The most frequent value is the mode, so the mode is 23 minutes.

Compare two groups on one shared stem

  • A back-to-back stem-and-leaf diagram puts one group on each side of a shared stem.
  • The shared stem gives both groups the same place-value scale.
  • Right leaves increase as you move away from the stem.
  • Left leaves also increase as you move away from the stem, so the smallest left leaf sits nearest the stem.
  • A double key must name both groups and decode both sides.

Key ideas

  1. Order every value and keep repeats.
  2. Split each value into a stem and final-digit leaf.
  3. Draw every stem on a regular scale, including empty stems.
  4. Order leaves away from the stem on both sides.
  5. Key the diagram with a decoded value, context and unit.
  6. Count leaves against the original number of values.
  7. Interpret medians, quartiles and modes from the ordered display.

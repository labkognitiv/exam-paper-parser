Combinations and unordered choice

Choose a group.
Ignore its order.

A committee has members, but it has no first member or second member. That single observation tells us when to count with combinations.

Start the lesson · Review the key ideas

Order or selection?

  • A permutation is an arrangement in which order matters.
  • AB and BA are different permutations because the positions have changed.
  • A combination is a selection in which order does not matter.
  • Choosing A and B gives the same group as choosing B and A.
  • Before choosing a formula, ask: would changing the order create a genuinely new outcome?

Definition · Combination

A combination is a choice of objects where the order of the chosen objects does not matter.

Arrange positions: permutation. Choose a team or group: combination.

Combination notation

nCr means “choose r objects from n distinct objects”. Distinct means the objects can be told apart.

  • n is the number available.
  • r is the number chosen.
  • The symbol ! means factorial: n! = n × (n - 1) × ... × 2 × 1.
  • For example, 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24.
  • The expression n - r is the number not chosen.
  • The factor r! removes the different orders of the same chosen group.
  • nCr = nCn-r . Choosing who is in is equivalent to choosing who is out.
nCr = n! / [r!(n - r)!]

For 8 objects with 3 chosen, substitute n = 8 and r = 3:

8C3 = 8! / (3!5!)
= (8 × 7 × 6 × 5!) / [(3 × 2 × 1) × 5!]
= (8 × 7 × 6) / (3 × 2 × 1)
= 336 / 6 = 56
Worked example

Choose a committee

A committee of 4 is chosen from 10 different students. How many committees are possible?

Solution
  1. Order does not matter, so use a combination.
  2. Number = 10C4 = 10! / (4!6!)
  3. = (10 × 9 × 8 × 7) / (4 × 3 × 2 × 1)
  4. = 210 committees.

Also, 10C4 = 10C6 : choosing the 4 included students fixes the 6 left out.

Check-in A

A museum chooses 2 of 7 posters to use, ignoring their display positions. Which count is correct?

Choose from separate categories

  • Some teams must contain a stated number from each category.
  • Make one combination for each category.
  • If both category choices are required, multiply their counts.
Worked example

Singers and musicians

Choose 2 singers from 6 and 3 musicians from 5.

Solution
  1. Choose the singers: 6C2 = 15.
  2. Choose the musicians: 5C3 = 10.
  3. Both choices are required, so multiply.
  4. Total = 15 × 10 = 150 groups.

AND means multiply the valid category choices.

Divide people into groups

  • For groups of different sizes, choose each group in turn.
  • After all but one group have been chosen, the final group is forced.
  • Do not multiply by another combination when everyone remaining must form that final group.
Worked example

Different-sized groups

Divide 9 people into groups of 4, 3 and 2.

Solution
  1. Choose 4 of the 9 people: 9C4 = 126.
  2. Choose 3 of the remaining 5: 5C3 = 10.
  3. The final 2 people form the last group automatically.
  4. Total = 126 × 10 = 1260 divisions.
Worked example

Unequal groups

Eight different people are divided into groups of 5 and 3.

Solution
  1. Choose the group of 3: 8C3 = 56.
  2. The remaining 5 people form the other group automatically.
  3. There are 56 divisions.

8C3 = 8C5 , so choosing either unequal group gives the same count.

Equal unlabelled groups

  • Labelled groups have different names or roles, such as Dormitory A and Dormitory B.
  • Unlabelled groups have no names that distinguish them.
  • Equal-sized unlabelled groups are overcounted when the groups swap places in the calculation.
  • Within each pair, its 2 people can be written in 2! orders. Divide by 2! for each of the three pairs.
  • (2!)³ means 2! × 2! × 2!: one factor for each pair.
  • For k equal-sized unlabelled groups, divide by k! to remove the k! orders of those groups.
6 people into three unlabelled pairs: 6! / [(2!)³ × 3!] = 15.

Restrictions change the pool or the cases

Worked example

Team selection

A club has 6 painters and 5 designers. A team contains 3 painters and 2 designers.

Solution
  1. Choose painters: 6C3 = 20.
  2. Choose designers: 5C2 = 10.
  3. Total = 20 × 10 = 200 teams.
Worked example

Fixed members

Seven people are chosen from 12 club members. Ava and Bilal must be included.

Solution
  1. Ava and Bilal already fill 2 places.
  2. That leaves 5 places and 10 other people.
  3. Number = 10C5 = 252.
Worked example

Favourite items

Nina has 9 different books. She chooses 4 and must include her favourite novel and favourite biography.

Solution
  1. The 2 favourite books are fixed.
  2. Choose the remaining 2 books from the other 7.
  3. Number = 7C2 = 21 choices.

Fix compulsory items first. Reduce both the pool and the places.

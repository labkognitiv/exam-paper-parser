At least and at most selections

Turn the words into every valid case.

A team condition such as “at least three” usually describes several possible category counts. List them all, count each case, and add without overlap.

Start the lesson · Review the key ideas

Recall combinations, then translate the restriction

  • A selection chooses a group without positions, so order does not matter.
  • An arrangement puts objects into positions, so order matters.
  • Use a combination for a selection.
  • Distinct objects can be told apart.
  • The symbol ! means factorial: 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24.
  • By definition, 0! = 1.
  • nCr means choose r distinct objects from n available distinct objects without order.
  • In nCr, n is the number available and r is the number chosen.
  • The symbol ≤ means less than or equal to.
  • The counts n and r are whole numbers with 0 ≤ r ≤ n.
  • The expression n - r is the number not chosen.
  • The denominator is the part below the fraction bar.

Combination formula

nCr = n! / [r!(n - r)!]
  • When choices come from separate categories and all are required, use a separate combination for each category, then multiply.
  • Example: choose 2 from 5 and 3 from 6.
  • 5C2 × 6C3 = 10 × 20 = 200.

At least and at most

  • At least 4 means 4 or more.
  • At most 2 means 2 or fewer, so the allowed values are 0, 1 and 2.
  • All category counts must add to the required team size.
  • Every count must stay within the number available in that category.

Example case list

A team of 6 contains at least 4 members from category A. If everyone else is from category B, the complete cases are:

CaseABCheck
1424 + 2 = 6

3606 + 0 = 6
  1. List every valid category-count case.
  2. Within each case, multiply the category combinations.
  3. Add the counts from the separate cases.
  4. Check that the cases are exhaustive: every valid outcome is included.
  5. Check that they are disjoint: no outcome belongs to two cases.

Work through “at least” and “at most”

Worked example

At least 3 pianists

A group of 5 is chosen from 6 pianists and 5 singers. How many groups contain at least 3 pianists?

Solution
  1. At least 3 pianists means 3, 4 or 5 pianists.
  2. Case 1: 3 pianists and 2 singers.
    6C3 × 5C2 = 20 × 10 = 200.
  3. Case 2: 4 pianists and 1 singer.
    6C4 × 5C1 = 15 × 5 = 75.
  4. Case 3: 5 pianists and 0 singers. Choosing none from a category happens in one way, so 5C0 = 1.
    6C5 × 5C0 = 6 × 1 = 6.
  5. The three cases cannot overlap because each has a different number of pianists. They include every allowed count from 3 to 5.
  6. Total = 200 + 75 + 6 = 281 groups.
Worked example

At most 1 junior

A team of 4 is chosen from 5 juniors and 7 seniors. How many teams contain at most 1 junior?

Solution
  1. At most 1 junior means 0 juniors or 1 junior.
  2. Case 1: 0 juniors and 4 seniors.
    5C0 × 7C4 = 1 × 35 = 35.
  3. Case 2: 1 junior and 3 seniors.
    5C1 × 7C3 = 5 × 35 = 175.
  4. Total = 35 + 175 = 210 teams.
Check-in A

A team of 5 is chosen from 7 artists and 4 coders. It needs at least 4 artists. Which complete case list is correct?

Use a complement when the excluded cases are shorter

Complement rule

If the desired cases are numerous but the cases that fail are few, use:

required count = total count - failing count.
Worked example

At least 2 red balls

Five balls are selected from 8 distinct red balls and 5 distinct blue balls.

Solution
  1. Total selections: choose any 5 from 13.
    13C5 = 1287.
  2. The failing selections contain fewer than 2 red balls.
  3. Failing case 1: 0 red and 5 blue. 5C5 = 1.
  4. Failing case 2: 1 red and 4 blue.
    8C1 × 5C4 = 8 × 5 = 40.
  5. Failing total = 1 + 40 = 41.
  6. Required = 1287 - 41 = 1246 selections.
Worked example

At least two car colours

Four cars are selected from 6 distinct silver cars, 4 distinct black cars and 3 distinct red cars.

Solution
  1. “At least two colours” fails only when all four cars have one colour.
  2. Total selections: 13C4 = 715.
  3. All silver: 6C4 = 15.
  4. All black: 4C4 = 1.
  5. All red is impossible because only 3 red cars are available, so it contributes 0.
  6. Required = 715 - (15 + 1 + 0) = 699 selections.
Check-in B

Four distinct tokens are selected from 6 white and 4 black tokens. How many selections contain at least 1 black token?

Three categories: build every case

Worked example

At least one from each activity

A team of 6 is chosen from 5 archers, 6 bowlers and 4 climbers. It must contain at least one from each activity and more bowlers than climbers.

Solution
  1. Let A, B and C be the numbers of archers, bowlers and climbers.
  2. The symbol ≥ means greater than or equal to. The symbol > means strictly greater than. Recall that ≤ means less than or equal to.
  3. Require A + B + C = 6, A ≥ 1, B ≥ 1, C ≥ 1 and B > C.
ABCCount
3215C3 × 6C2 × 4C1 = 600
2315C2 × 6C3 × 4C1 = 800
1415C1 × 6C4 × 4C1 = 300
1325C1 × 6C3 × 4C2 = 600
  • These cases are exhaustive: solving the sum and inequalities gives no other non-negative integer triple.
  • They are disjoint because one team cannot have two different category-count triples.

Total = 600 + 800 + 300 + 600 = 2300 teams.

Worked example

Mixed lower and upper bounds

A team of 7 is chosen from 6 swimmers, 8 cyclists and 5 runners. It must contain exactly 1 swimmer, at least 4 cyclists and at most 2 runners.

Solution
  1. Let S, C and R be the category counts. Fix S = 1.
  2. The other counts satisfy C + R = 6, with C ≥ 4 and R ≤ 2.
  3. The complete valid cases are (S,C,R) = (1,4,2), (1,5,1), (1,6,0).
  4. Case (1,4,2): 6C1 × 8C4 × 5C2 = 4200.
  5. Case (1,5,1): 6C1 × 8C5 × 5C1 = 1680.
  6. Case (1,6,0): 6C1 × 8C6 × 5C0 = 168.
  7. Different triples are disjoint, and C = 4, 5, 6 exhausts every possible count because C cannot exceed the 6 remaining team places.
  8. Total = 4200 + 1680 + 168 = 6048 teams.

Key ideas

WordsAction
At least kList k and every larger possible count.
At most kList 0 through k.
Several required categoriesMultiply category combinations within each case.
Several valid casesAdd their counts after checking they are disjoint.
Complete case listCheck the counts add to the team size and stay within each pool.
Many desired cases, few failing casesUse total - failing.
Repeated letters selected, not arrangedChoose how many copies occur; identical copies do not create extra choices.

More worked examples

Worked example

More men than women

A committee of 5 is chosen from 7 men and 6 women.

Solution
  1. More men than women gives the complete cases (3,2), (4,1), (5,0).
  2. 3 men, 2 women: 7C3 × 6C2 = 35 × 15 = 525.
  3. 4 men, 1 woman: 7C4 × 6C1 = 35 × 6 = 210.
  4. 5 men, 0 women: 7C5 × 6C0 = 21 × 1 = 21.
  5. Total = 525 + 210 + 21 = 756 committees.
Worked example

At least 5 marbles of one colour

Six marbles are selected from 9 distinct red marbles and 5 distinct blue marbles.

Solution
  1. The complete valid cases are 5 red and 1 blue; 6 red and 0 blue; 1 red and 5 blue.
  2. Zero red and 6 blue is impossible because only 5 blue marbles exist.
  3. 5R,1B: 9C5 × 5C1 = 126 × 5 = 630.
  4. 6R,0B: 9C6 = 84.
  5. 1R,5B: 9C1 × 5C5 = 9 × 1 = 9.
  6. Total = 630 + 84 + 9 = 723 selections.
Worked example

Orchestra with three bounds

A group of 6 musicians is chosen from 9 violinists, 5 cellists and 3 bass players. It must contain at least 4 violinists, at least 1 cellist and at most 1 bass player.

Solution
  1. The complete cases (violinists, cellists, bass) are (4,1,1), (4,2,0), (5,1,0).
  2. (4,1,1): 9C4 × 5C1 × 3C1 = 1890.
  3. (4,2,0): 9C4 × 5C2 = 1260.
  4. (5,1,0): 9C5 × 5C1 = 630.
  5. Six violinists would leave no place for the required cellist, so there are no further cases.
  6. Total = 1890 + 1260 + 630 = 3780 groups.
Worked example

Selections from ELEMENTAL

Five letters are selected from ELEMENTAL. E appears 3 times, L appears twice, and M, N, T and A occur once each. The selection must contain at least one E and at most one L.

Solution
  • E can occur 1, 2 or 3 times.
  • L can occur 0 or 1 time.
  • The remaining places are filled from the four distinct single-occurrence letters M, N, T and A.
  • Repeated copies of E or L are identical, so choosing which copy adds no factor.

The six rows are exhaustive because they use every allowed E/L pair that leaves 0 to 4 single-letter places. They are disjoint because each row has different E and L counts.

Total = 1 + 4 + 4 + 6 + 6 + 4 = 25 selections.

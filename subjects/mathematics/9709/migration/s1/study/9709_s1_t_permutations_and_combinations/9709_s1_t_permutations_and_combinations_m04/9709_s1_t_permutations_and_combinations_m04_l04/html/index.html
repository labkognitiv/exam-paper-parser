Named restrictions

Translate the condition before you count.

Small phrases such as “not both” and “exactly one” describe different allowed groups. State the condition precisely, then count cases or subtract the forbidden set.

Start the lesson · See the key ideas

Start with combinations and complements

  • A combination selects a group of distinct objects without ordering them.
  • Distinct means that the objects can be told apart.
  • Let n be the number available and r the number selected.
  • The notation ⁿCᵣ counts those unordered selections.

Factorial

Read n! as “n factorial”. It means n × (n − 1) × ⋯ × 2 × 1. By definition, 0! = 1.

  • For combinations, n and r are whole numbers.
  • The selected number cannot exceed the available number: 0 ≤ r ≤ n.
  • The symbol ≤ means “less than or equal to”.

Combination formula

ⁿCᵣ = n! / [r!(n − r)!]

  • A restriction is a condition that removes some otherwise possible selections.
  • The total includes every selection before the restriction.
  • The forbidden set contains exactly the selections that break the condition.
  • The allowed count is total minus forbidden.
  • A complement is everything outside the set being described. It often supplies the forbidden count.
  • If named people must be included, fix them first. Reduce both the remaining pool and the remaining places.
  • A-only means A is selected and B is not.
  • B-only means B is selected and A is not.
  • At least one allows one or both.
  • At most two allows zero, one or two, but forbids all three.

  • For exactly one, multiply one case by 2 only when A and B are interchangeable under every other restriction. Otherwise count A-only and B-only separately.
  • Cases are disjoint when no selection belongs to more than one case. Add disjoint cases.

“Not both” removes one forbidden case

Worked example

Priya and Tomas cannot both be chosen

A group of 5 is chosen from 11 people. Priya and Tomas cannot both be chosen. How many groups are possible?

Solution

  1. Count every group: ¹¹C₅ = 462.

  2. For the forbidden groups, fix Priya and Tomas. Choose the remaining 3 members from the other 9.

    ⁹C₃ = 84

  3. 462 − 84 = 378

    There are 378 allowed groups.

Worked example

Check the same idea by cases

A team of 4 is chosen from 9 people. Asha and Ben cannot both be chosen.

Solution

  1. Neither is chosen: choose all 4 from the other 7.

    ⁷C₄ = 35

  2. Exactly one is chosen. Asha and Ben are interchangeable here, so choose which one in 2 ways, then choose 3 of the other 7.

    2 × ⁷C₃ = 2 × 35 = 70

  3. The cases are disjoint, so add them.

    35 + 70 = 105

    There are 105 teams.

Check: ⁹C₄ − ⁷C₂ = 126 − 21 = 105.

Match the words to the forbidden set

Worked example

Exactly one of two twins

A committee of 6 is chosen from 14 people. Exactly one of two twins must be selected.

Solution

  1. Choose which twin is included: 2 ways.

  2. The other twin is excluded. Choose the remaining 5 members from the other 12.

    ¹²C₅ = 792

  3. 2 × ¹²C₅ = 2 × 792 = 1584

    There are 1584 committees.

Worked example

At least one captain

A team of 5 is chosen from 13 people. At least one of two captains must be selected.

Solution

  1. At least one means subtract the complement: teams containing neither captain.

  2. Total teams: ¹³C₅ = 1287.

  3. With neither captain, choose all 5 from the other 11: ¹¹C₅ = 462.

  4. 1287 − 462 = 825

    There are 825 teams.

Worked example

A forbidden pair across categories

A project team has 3 engineers, 2 designers and 1 analyst, chosen from 6 engineers, 5 designers and 4 analysts. One named engineer and one named designer cannot both be chosen.

Solution

  1. Count all teams.

    ⁶C₃ × ⁵C₂ × ⁴C₁ = 20 × 10 × 4 = 800

  2. For the forbidden set, fix the named engineer and designer. Choose 2 more engineers from 5, 1 more designer from 4, and 1 analyst from 4.

    ⁵C₂ × ⁴C₁ × ⁴C₁ = 10 × 4 × 4 = 160

  3. 800 − 160 = 640

    There are 640 teams.

  • Fix named people in a counted case, then reduce both places and pool.
  • Only double one exactly-one case when the named people are interchangeable under every other condition.
  • Add disjoint cases. Subtract one precisely described forbidden set.

More worked examples

Worked example

At most two of three brothers

A committee of 6 is chosen from 15 people, including three brothers. How many committees contain at most two brothers?

Solution

  1. Total committees: ¹⁵C₆ = 5005.

  2. The only forbidden case contains all three brothers. Fix them, then choose 3 of the other 12.

    ¹²C₃ = 220

  3. 5005 − 220 = 4785

    There are 4785 committees.

Worked example

Exactly one of Maya and Leo

A group of 5 is chosen from 13 people. Exactly one of Maya and Leo must be included.

Solution

  1. Maya and Leo are interchangeable under the remaining conditions, so choose which one is included: 2 ways.

  2. Exclude the other. Choose the remaining 4 members from the other 11.

    ¹¹C₄ = 330

  3. 2 × ¹¹C₄ = 2 × 330 = 660

    There are 660 groups.

Worked example

Category quotas with a forbidden pair

A sports team has 4 forwards, 3 defenders and 1 goalkeeper, chosen from 6 forwards, 5 defenders and 3 goalkeepers. One named forward refuses to play with one named defender.

Solution

  1. Count all teams.

    ⁶C₄ × ⁵C₃ × ³C₁ = 15 × 10 × 3 = 450

  2. For the forbidden teams, fix the named forward and defender. Choose 3 more forwards from 5, 2 more defenders from 4, and 1 goalkeeper from 3.

    ⁵C₃ × ⁴C₂ × ³C₁ = 10 × 6 × 3 = 180

  3. 450 − 180 = 270

    There are 270 teams.

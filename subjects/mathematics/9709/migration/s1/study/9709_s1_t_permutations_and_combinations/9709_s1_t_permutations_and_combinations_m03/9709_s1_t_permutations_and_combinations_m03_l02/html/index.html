Objects that stay together

Tie the group into one block.

If several objects must remain next to one another, first arrange the whole group as one unit. Then count the possible orders inside it.

Start the lesson · See the key ideas

Count the outside, then the inside

  • An arrangement is an ordering of objects.
  • Objects are distinct when each can be told apart.
  • Objects stay together when they occupy consecutive positions, with no other object between them.
  • Treat a group that must stay together as one block.
  • Arrange that block with the objects outside it.
  • Then multiply by the possible orders inside the block.

Factorial

For a positive integer n, n! means n × (n − 1) × ⋯ × 2 × 1. It counts the arrangements of n distinct objects.

ABCDE

Look at the outside first: the ABC block, D and E make three units.

Worked example

Three buses stay together

Three distinct buses and six distinct cars stand in a line. How many arrangements keep all three buses together?

Solution

  1. Tie the buses into one block. The bus block and six cars make 7 units.

  2. Arrange those units in 7! ways.

  3. The three distinct buses can change order inside their block in 3! ways.

  4. 7! × 3! = 5040 × 6 = 30240

    There are 30 240 arrangements.

Check-in 1

Seven distinct photographs are arranged in a row. Three named photographs must stay together. How many arrangements are possible?

Two groups become two blocks

  • If two separate groups must each stay together, make two blocks.
  • Each whole block is one outside unit.
  • The two blocks may swap positions.
  • Multiply by the arrangements inside each block.

Worked example

Five boys and six girls

Five named boys and six named girls stand in a row, with all boys together and all girls together.

Solution

  1. The two blocks can appear as BG or GB, so the outside has 2! = 2 orders.

  2. The five distinct boys have 5! orders inside their block.

  3. The six distinct girls have 6! orders inside their block.

  4. 2 × 5! × 6! = 172800

    There are 172 800 arrangements.

Worked example

Two family groups and four others

Five distinct Keene children must stay together, two distinct Uzuma children must stay together, and four other distinct people join the row.

K1K2K3K4K5U1U2ABCD

Solution

  1. The two blocks and four other people make 6 outside units, arranged in 6! ways.

  2. Inside the blocks there are 5! × 2! orders.

  3. 6! × 5! × 2! = 172800

    There are 172 800 arrangements.

Check-in 2

Two distinct chefs must stay together and four distinct waiters must stay together. How many row arrangements are possible?

Key ideas

  1. Make one block for each group that must stay together.

  2. Count the outside units. A block counts as one unit.

  3. Arrange the outside units. Use a factorial when they are distinct.

  4. Count inside each block. Distinct objects can change order; identical objects cannot create new orders.

  5. Multiply the outside and inside counts.

More worked examples

Worked example

Repeated letters inside blocks

How many arrangements of REQUIREMENT have the two Rs together and the three Es together?

Solution

  1. Treat RR and EEE as single blocks.

  2. The outside units are RR, EEE, Q, U, I, M, N and T: 8 distinct units.

  3. There is no extra internal multiplier. Swapping identical Rs or identical Es does not create a new arrangement.

  4. 8! = 40320

    There are 40 320 arrangements.

Worked example

Adults together and children together

Four named adults and three named children stand in a row. The adults must stay together and the children must stay together.

Solution

  1. Order the two blocks in 2! = 2 ways.

  2. Arrange the adults in 4! ways and the children in 3! ways.

  3. 2 × 4! × 3! = 288

    There are 288 arrangements.

Worked example

Together in different rows

A front row has 5 seats and each of 6 other rows has 5 seats. Mina and Ali sit together in the front row; three other named friends sit together in one other row. Find the number of seatings.

12345

Solution

  1. In a row of five seats, a pair can occupy seats 1-2, 2-3, 3-4 or 4-5. That is 4 adjacent positions.

    Mina and Ali can swap order, giving 4 × 2 = 8 front-row seatings.

  2. Choose one of the 6 other rows for the three friends: 6 ways.

  3. Three consecutive seats can start at seat 1, 2 or 3: 3 positions.

    The three distinct friends have 3! = 6 orders, giving 3 × 3! = 18 seatings in that row.

  4. 8 × 6 × 18 = 864

    There are 864 seatings.

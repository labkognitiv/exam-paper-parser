Mixed repeated-object restrictions

Handle one restriction at a time.

Repeated letters already create overcounting. Fixed places, together conditions, separation and gaps add another decision. Translate each condition before multiplying or subtracting.

Start the lesson · Review the key ideas

Start with the repeated-object rule

  • A permutation is an arrangement where order matters.
  • Distinct objects can be told apart.
  • The symbol ! means factorial: 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24.
  • The letter n represents the total number of objects.
  • If all n objects are distinct, there are n! arrangements.
  • Identical objects cannot be told apart.
  • If one kind repeats a times, divide by a! because its internal swaps do not make new arrangements.
  • If another kind repeats b times, divide by b! as well.
  • The denominator is the part below the fraction bar. Here it contains a! × b! × ... .

Repeated-object rule

arrangements = n! / (a! × b! × ...)

Use one denominator factor for each kind of repeated object. Count each repetition once.

  • A fixed place has a named object that is already assigned to it.
  • A block ties objects together and treats them as one unit.
  • A complement count subtracts unwanted arrangements from all possible arrangements.
  • A gap is a space before, between or after already arranged objects.
  • A specified separation states exactly or at least how many objects must lie between two chosen objects.

Fix repeated letters or join them into a block

Worked example

SUMMERTIME with E at each end

SUMMERTIME has 10 letters. M repeats 3 times and E repeats twice.

Solution
  1. Fix one E at each end. Both Es are now placed.
  2. The 8 remaining letters are S, U, M, M, M, R, T and I.
  3. Only M repeats, three times.
  4. 8! / 3! = 40 320 / 6
  5. There are 6720 arrangements.
Worked example

SHOPKEEPER with all three Es together

Replace EEE by one block. The block must stay intact, so it behaves as one unit.

Solution
  1. All three Es are already inside the single EEE block, so do not list another E.
  2. The eight units are EEE, S, H, O, P, P, K and R.
  3. P is the only repeated unit, and it appears twice.
  4. 8! / 2! = 40 320 / 2
  5. There are 20 160 arrangements.
Check-in A

ARRANGER has A fixed at each end. The middle letters are R, R, R, N, G and E. Which count is correct?

Use a complement for one specified pair

  • Directly listing every separated arrangement is often awkward.
  • Instead count all arrangements.
  • For one specified pair, count the unwanted arrangements where that pair forms one block.
  • Then subtract: pair apart = all arrangements - pair together.
  • For three or more objects, not all together only rules out one whole block. It may still allow two objects next to each other.
  • No two adjacent is stricter: no pair may occupy neighbouring positions. The gap method enforces this.
Worked example

The Ls are separated in JEWELLERY

JEWELLERY has 9 letters. E repeats 3 times and L repeats twice.

Solution
  1. Count every arrangement, dividing for both repeated kinds:
    9! / (3! × 2!) = 30 240.
  2. For the unwanted case, treat LL as one block.
  3. There are now 8 units. The three Es still repeat:
    8! / 3! = 6720.
  4. Subtract the together case from the total.
  5. 30 240 - 6720 = 23 520 arrangements.
Check-in B

How many arrangements of RABBIT have the two Bs separated?

Place identical objects into gaps

  • Use the gap method when identical objects must be separated.
  • Arrange all other objects first.
  • Each space before, between or after them is a gap.
  • Putting at most one chosen object in each gap guarantees separation.
Worked example

The Es are not together in SUMMERTIME

Solution
  1. First arrange the 8 non-E letters S, U, M, M, M, R, T and I.
  2. The three Ms repeat, so this gives 8!/3! arrangements.
  3. Eight arranged letters create 9 gaps, including both end gaps.
  1. Choose 2 different gaps for the two identical Es.
  2. The notation C(9,2), also written 9C2, means choose 2 gaps from 9 without order.
  3. There are 9 × 8 ordered choices of two different gaps. Each pair appears in two orders, so divide by 2:
    C(9,2) = (9 × 8) / 2 = 36.
  4. (8! / 3!) × 36 = 6720 × 36
  5. There are 241 920 arrangements.

Count exact and minimum separation

  • If exactly k objects lie between two positions, their position numbers differ by k + 1.
  • List every possible position pair before arranging the remaining objects.
  • Identical chosen objects do not swap into a second order.
  • For “at least”, include every allowed difference from the smallest one upward.
Worked example

Exactly three letters between identical Rs

Eleven positions contain two identical Rs, three identical Es, and six other distinct letters. Exactly 3 letters must lie between the Rs.

Solution
  1. Exactly 3 letters between the Rs means their position numbers differ by 4.
  2. The pairs are (1,5), (2,6), (3,7), (4,8), (5,9), (6,10) and (7,11): 7 pairs.
  3. The Rs are identical, so there is no extra factor of 2.
  4. The other 9 letters contain three identical Es and six distinct letters. They can be arranged in 9!/3! ways.
  5. 7 × (9! / 3!) = 7 × 60 480
  6. There are 423 360 arrangements.
Worked example

At least five letters between identical As

Nine positions contain two identical As, another identical pair, and five other distinct letters. At least 5 letters must lie between the As.

Solution

  1. At least 5 letters between the As means their position numbers differ by 6, 7 or 8.
  2. The As are identical, so the 6 position pairs need no swap factor.
  3. Arrange the other 7 letters. One pair among them is identical, so there are 7!/2! arrangements.
  4. 6 × (7! / 2!) = 6 × 2520
  5. There are 15 120 arrangements.

Key ideas

RestrictionCounting move
Repeated identical objectsDivide by one factorial for each repeated kind.
Fixed placesFill them first; count only what remains.
Objects togetherTreat the whole group as one block.
One specified pair apartUse all - that pair together.
Three or more objects not all togetherUse all - one whole block; some adjacency is still allowed.
No two of several objects adjacentPlace them in different gaps.
Exactly k betweenList position pairs whose numbers differ by k + 1.
At least k betweenAdd the pair counts for every allowed larger difference.
Identical pair in chosen positionsDo not multiply by 2 for swapping them.

Count identical objects once. Never divide for the same repetition twice.

More worked examples

Worked example

CATERPILLAR with fixed Rs and separated As

Arrange CATERPILLAR with R at each end and the two As not together.

Solution
  1. Fix the two Rs at the ends.
  2. The remaining letters are C, A, T, E, P, I, L, L and A.
  3. Count all middle arrangements. A repeats twice and L repeats twice:
    9! / (2! × 2!) = 90 720.
  4. For the unwanted case, treat AA as one block. There are 8 units, with L repeated twice:
    8! / 2! = 20 160.
  5. Required = 90 720 - 20 160 = 70 560 arrangements.
Worked example

DELIVERED with EEE together and Ds separated

Solution
  1. Treat EEE as one block.
  2. The seven units are EEE, D, D, L, I, V and R. D repeats twice.
  3. Count all arrangements of these units:
    7! / 2! = 2520.
  4. For the unwanted case, DD is also one block. The six units EEE, DD, L, I, V and R are distinct:
    6! = 720.
  5. Required = 2520 - 720 = 1800 arrangements.
Worked example

TOMORROW with fixed Rs and Os not all together

TOMORROW has T, O, M, O, R, R, O and W.

Solution
  1. Fix both Rs at the ends.
  2. The remaining letters are T, O, M, O, O and W.
  3. Count all middle arrangements, dividing for the three Os:
    6! / 3! = 120.
  4. For the unwanted case, treat OOO as one block. The four units OOO, T, M and W are distinct:
    4! = 24.
  5. The condition is “not all together”. Two Os may still be adjacent; only the single OOO block is forbidden.
  6. Required = 120 - 24 = 96 arrangements.

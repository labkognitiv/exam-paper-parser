Fixed positions and specified gaps

Fix the places.
Then arrange what remains.

A word may have letters fixed at its ends. Two people may need a precise gap between them. Draw the positions first, remove the unavailable choices, and only then count.

Start the lesson · Review the key ideas

Recall the basic arrangement rules

  • A permutation is an arrangement where order matters.
  • Distinct objects can be told apart.
  • The symbol ! means factorial.
  • For example, 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24.
  • The letter n represents the number of objects.
  • There are n! ways to arrange n distinct objects in n positions.
  • If one kind of identical object repeats r times, divide by r! because swapping those identical objects creates no new arrangement.
  • If several kinds repeat, divide by the factorial for each repeated kind.
Worked example

Repeated letters in CAMERAMAN

CAMERAMAN has 9 letters. A appears 3 times and M appears twice.

Solution
  1. Start with 9! arrangements as if all letters were distinct.
  2. Divide by 3! for the three identical As.
  3. Divide by 2! for the two identical Ms.
  4. 9! / (3! × 2!) = 362 880 / 12
  5. There are 30 240 arrangements.

Fix required positions first

  • A position is one numbered place in the line.
  • A fixed object has only one allowed position.
  • Put every fixed object into its place before counting.
  • Then list only the objects that remain and arrange them in the open positions.
Worked example

Fix the As in CAMERAMAN

Place the three As in positions 1, 5 and 9.

Solution
  1. The remaining letters are C, M, E, R, M and N.
  2. There are 6 letters to arrange, with M repeated twice.
  3. 6! / 2! = 720 / 2
  4. There are 360 arrangements.
Worked example

COCOONED begins with O and ends with N

  • COCOONED has exactly 8 letters: C, O, C, O, O, N, E, D.
  • Fix O in the first position and N in the eighth position.
Solution
  1. The remaining letters are C, C, O, O, E and D.
  2. Arrange 6 letters. Divide by 2! for the two Cs and by 2! for the two Os.
  3. 6! / (2! × 2!) = 720 / 4
  4. There are 180 arrangements.
Check-in A

TATTOO begins with T and ends with O. How should the four middle letters T, T, A and O be counted?

Turn a specified gap into position pairs

  • A slot is one position in the line.
  • “Exactly one person between A and B” means their position numbers differ by 2.
  • First list every possible pair of positions.
  • Then count the two orders A–B and B–A.
  • Finally arrange everyone else in the remaining slots.
Worked example

One person between A and B

Nine distinct people stand in a line. Exactly one person must stand between A and B.

Solution
  1. The possible position pairs are (1,3), (2,4), (3,5), (4,6), (5,7), (6,8) and (7,9).
  2. That gives 7 choices of position pair.
  3. A and B can swap places within the chosen pair: 2 ways.
  4. The other 7 distinct people fill the other slots in 7! ways.
  5. 7 × 2 × 7! = 7 × 2 × 5040
  6. There are 70 560 arrangements.

Combine fixed places with exclusions

Worked example

ANDROMEDA with fixed As and separated Ds

Arrange ANDROMEDA with A at each end and the two Ds not together.

Solution
  1. Fix A in positions 1 and 9.
  2. The remaining letters are N, D, R, O, M, E and D.
  3. Count all arrangements with the ends fixed. The two Ds repeat:
    7! / 2! = 2520.
  4. A block means objects tied together and counted as one unit.
  5. For the forbidden arrangements, treat DD as one block. The units DD, N, R, O, M and E are 6 distinct units:
    6! = 720.
  6. Subtract forbidden arrangements from all fixed-end arrangements.
  7. Required = 2520 - 720 = 1800 arrangements.
Worked example

Two letters between the Ls in BALLOON

BALLOON has 7 letters, including two Ls and two Os.

Solution
  1. Exactly two letters between the Ls means their position numbers differ by 3.
  2. The possible L-position pairs are (1,4), (2,5), (3,6) and (4,7): 4 choices.
  3. The Ls are identical, so swapping them does not create another arrangement.
  4. Arrange B, A, O, O and N in the remaining 5 positions. Divide by 2! for the two identical Os.
  5. 5! / 2! = 120 / 2 = 60.
  6. Total = 4 × 60 = 240 arrangements.
Check-in B

Seven distinct people stand in a line with exactly two people between Quinn and Rosa. Which setup is correct?

Key ideas

ConditionFirst move
Objects fixed in named positionsPlace them first; arrange only what remains.
Identical objects repeatDivide by the factorial of each repeated count.
Exactly g objects between two distinct objectsTheir position numbers differ by g + 1.
Two distinct objects use a position pairInclude 2 orders unless another condition fixes their order.
A pair uses a forbidden fixed positionRemove that pair before multiplying.
Objects must not be togetherCount all valid fixed-position arrangements, then subtract the together case.

More worked examples

Worked example

Two middle positions and two sides

Ten people stand in a line. Ana and Ben occupy positions 5 and 6. Four named adults stand on one side, and four named children stand on the other side. Named means each person is distinct.

Solution
  1. Ana and Ben can swap middle positions: 2 ways.
  2. The left side is positions 1 to 4; the right side is positions 7 to 10.
  3. Choose which side contains the adults: 2 ways.
  4. Arrange the four named adults: 4! ways.
  5. Arrange the four named children: 4! ways.
  6. 2 × 2 × 4! × 4! = 2 × 2 × 24 × 24
  7. There are 2304 arrangements.
Worked example

A fixed fourth position and a three-person gap

Eight distinct people stand in a line. C is fixed in position 4, and exactly three people stand between D and E.

Solution
  1. Exactly three people between D and E means their positions differ by 4.
  2. The candidate pairs are (1,5), (2,6), (3,7) and (4,8).
  3. C occupies position 4, so pair (4,8) is unavailable. Three pairs remain.
  4. D and E can swap within the chosen pair: 2 ways.
  5. The other 5 distinct people fill the remaining positions in 5! ways.
  6. 3 × 2 × 5! = 3 × 2 × 120
  7. There are 720 arrangements.

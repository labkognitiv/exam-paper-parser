Restricted arrangements

Keep apart. Fill gaps. Alternate.

The words “not together” and “no two together” describe different restrictions. Choose the counting method that matches the restriction.

Start the lesson · Key ideas

Read the restriction first

  • Distinct objects can be told apart.
  • Identical objects are indistinguishable, so swapping them does not make a new visible order.
  • The symbol ! is read “factorial”. It means multiply descending whole numbers.
  • For example, 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24.
  • Two objects are adjacent, or together, when they occupy neighbouring positions.
  • Not together for one specified pair can be counted as total arrangements minus arrangements where that pair is together.
  • No two together for a whole group is usually handled by arranging the other group, then filling different gaps.
  • Alternating means the two groups take turns, one object from each group in succession.
  • When alternating group sizes differ by 1, the larger group must occupy both ends.

Three reading cues

One pair not together: total − together.

No two from one group together: arrange the other objects, then use gaps.

Alternating: identify the possible group patterns before arranging individuals.

One pair not together: use a complement

  • A complement is everything in the total set that does not meet the unwanted condition.
  • To count A and B not together, first count every arrangement.
  • Then count arrangements where A and B are together and subtract.
  • When together, treat AB as one block.
  • The block can be AB or BA, giving 2 internal orders.

Worked example

Five distinct objects include A and B. Count arrangements where A and B are not together.

  1. Total arrangements: 5! = 120.
  2. Treat A and B as one block. The block plus the other three objects makes 4 objects.
  3. Arrange the four objects: 4! ways.
  4. Arrange A and B inside the block: 2 ways, AB or BA.
  5. Together: 4! × 2 = 48.
  6. Not together: 120 − 48 = 72.

Your turn

Arrange AABBCD so that the two Bs are not together.

  1. Total arrangements: divide by 2! for the As and 2! for the Bs.
  2. Total = 6!2! × 2! = 180.
  3. For the unwanted case, treat BB as one block.
  4. The block, two identical As, C and D make 5 objects, with A repeated twice.
  5. BB together = 5!2! = 60.
  6. Required = 180 − 60 = 120.

No two together: arrange, then use gaps

  • A gap is a position before, between or after arranged objects.
  • Five arranged objects create six gaps.
  • To separate three objects, place them in three different gaps.
  • If the objects being placed are distinct, choosing and ordering their gaps is a permutation.
  • The notation nPr counts ordered placements of r distinct objects into n positions.

Worked example: five girls and three boys

Five distinct girls and three distinct boys stand in a line. How many arrangements have no two boys adjacent?

  1. Arrange the five distinct girls first: 5! ways.
  2. They create six gaps: before the first girl, four between girls, and after the last girl.
  3. Place three distinct boys in three different gaps.
  4. 6P3 = 6 × 5 × 4 = 120.
  5. Total = 5! × 6P3 = 120 × 120 = 14 400.

Your turn: AAAABCD

How many arrangements of AAAABCD have no two As adjacent?

  1. Arrange the distinct letters B, C and D: 3! ways.
  2. They create exactly four gaps.
  3. There are four identical As, so one A must fill every gap.
  4. Because the As are identical, filling all four gaps has only 1 visible result for each BCD order.
  5. Total = 3! × 1 = 6.

Alternating groups: count the patterns

  • Write the group pattern before arranging individuals.
  • Six distinct girls and five distinct boys can alternate only as G B G B G B G B G B G.
  • The larger group has one extra member, so a girl must occupy both ends.
  • Arrange the girls among their six G positions: 6! ways.
  • Arrange the boys among their five B positions: 5! ways.
  • There is only one group pattern, so do not multiply by 2.
  • Total = 6! × 5! = 86 400.

Key ideas

One pair apart

Count total minus together. Treat the pair as a block for the unwanted case.

No two together

Arrange the other objects, expose gaps, then place at most one restricted object in each gap.

Alternating

List the possible group patterns first, then arrange each group in its assigned positions.

Check

Ask whether objects are distinct, whether end gaps are allowed, and whether there is one pattern or two.

More worked examples

Equal groups can start either way

Four named men and four named women stand alternately in a row.

  1. There are two valid patterns: M W M W M W M W, or W M W M W M W M.
  2. Arrange the four men in their four positions: 4! ways.
  3. Arrange the four women in their four positions: 4! ways.
  4. Multiply by 2 for the two different patterns.
  5. Total = 2 × 4! × 4! = 1152.

Cars at both ends and buses apart

Seven distinct cars and three distinct buses stand in a line. A car must be at each end, and no two buses may be adjacent.

  1. Arrange the seven cars: 7! ways.
  2. The cars create eight gaps in total.
  3. The two outside gaps would put a bus at an end, so they are not allowed.
  4. Exactly six internal gaps remain, one between each neighbouring pair of cars.
  5. Choose and order three internal gaps for the three distinct buses.
  6. 6P3 = 6 × 5 × 4 = 120.
  7. Total = 7! × 120 = 604 800.

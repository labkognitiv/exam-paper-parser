Independence of events

Test the evidence before you multiply.

Independence means knowing one event happened does not change the probability of the other. Mutually exclusive events cannot happen together. These ideas are different.

Start the lesson · Return to key ideas

Notation and meaning

An event is a result or collection of results. A sample space contains all possible results. For equally likely outcomes, count the favourable results and divide by the total.

  • P(A) means the probability that event A occurs.
  • A ∩ B means both events occur. The symbol ∩ is read “intersection”.
  • P(A given B) means the probability of A after restricting attention to outcomes where B occurred.
Die example

Roll one fair six-sided die numbered 1 to 6. Let A = even and B = greater than 3. Then A ∩ B = {4, 6}, so P(A ∩ B)=2/6=1/3.

Because B = {4,5,6} contains three outcomes, P(A given B)=2/3.

Independence: two tests

Product test

A and B are independent exactly when:

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)
  1. Find P(A) and P(B).
  2. Find P(A ∩ B) directly from outcomes, a table or a tree.
  3. Calculate P(A) × P(B).
  4. Compare the two values and state the conclusion.
Conditional test

When P(B) > 0 (the condition has positive probability), independence also means:

P(A given B) = P(A)

Knowing B occurred does not change the chance of A.

Independence is not exclusivity

Mutually exclusive

A and B cannot happen together, so P(A ∩ B)=0.

Independent

Knowing one event happened does not change the probability of the other, so P(A ∩ B)=P(A)×P(B).

If P(A) and P(B) are both positive, their product is positive. Therefore two positive-probability mutually exclusive events are not independent.

Quick example

On one fair die, A = roll 1 and B = roll 2. Their intersection has probability 0, but P(A)P(B)=(1/6)(1/6)=1/36. They are not independent.

Worked example: conditional check

Conditional probability divides the probability of both events by the probability of the condition: P(A given B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B). The numerator is above the fraction line; the denominator is below it.

For a fair die, A = even and B = greater than 3.

  1. B={4,5,6}, P(B)=3/6.
  2. A ∩ B={4,6}, P(A ∩ B)=2/6.
  3. P(A given B)=(2/6)/(3/6)=2/3.
  4. P(A)=3/6=1/2, so the conditional and original probabilities differ. A and B are not independent.

Product check: P(A)P(B)=1/4, while P(A ∩ B)=1/3.

Worked example: two distinguishable dice

A fair red die and a fair blue die are rolled independently. An ordered pair records (red score, blue score). Let A = total is 7 and B = red score is even. There are 36 equally likely ordered outcomes.

  1. A has (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1), so P(A)=6/36=1/6.
  2. Half of red scores are even, so P(B)=18/36=1/2.
  3. A ∩ B has (2,5), (4,3), (6,1), so P(A ∩ B)=3/36=1/12.
  4. P(A)P(B)=(1/6)(1/2)=1/12. Therefore A and B are independent.

Worked example: arrangements

The factorial 4! means 4 × 3 × 2 × 1: four choices for the first position, then three, two and one for the remaining positions.

Letters A, B, C and D are arranged randomly. E = A is in position 2 or 3; F = B is left of C.

  1. There are 4!=24 equally likely arrangements. A occupies 2 of 4 positions, so P(E)=1/2.
  2. Swap B and C in any arrangement. This pairs each B-before-C arrangement with exactly one C-before-B arrangement, so P(F)=1/2.
  3. With A in position 2, the valid arrangements are BACD, BADC and DABC. With A in position 3, they are BCAD, BDAC and DBAC. These six satisfy both conditions, so P(E ∩ F)=6/24=1/4.
  4. P(E)P(F)=1/4. Therefore E and F are independent.

Method map

  1. Define events and identify the correct sample space or table totals.
  2. Calculate P(A), P(B), and P(A ∩ B) from outcomes, counts, a table or tree.
  3. Calculate P(A) × P(B) and compare.
  4. When P(B)>0, also compare P(A given B) with P(A). Use exact fractions where possible and state a conclusion.

Quick checks

Check A. A = a student is Year 12 and B = prefers mathematics. From a table, P(A)=0.4, P(B)=0.55 and P(A ∩ B)=0.20. Are A and B independent?

Check B. Events C and D have P(C)=0.3, P(D)=0.2 and P(C ∩ D)=0.06. Which statement is correct?

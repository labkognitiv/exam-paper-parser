Notation before the examples

  • P(E) means “the probability of event E”. An event is a named outcome or group of outcomes.
  • AND along one path means every stage happens, so multiply. OR between disjoint paths means either path happens, so add. Disjoint means the paths cannot both describe the same trial.
  • “At least one” is often easiest as 1 − P(none), using the complement: everything outside the event.
  • Independent means one result does not change the next probability. Dependent means the next probability depends on what just happened.
  • W means win and L means lose in a path such as WLL. The symbols < and ≤ mean “less than” and “less than or equal to”. Every probability must satisfy 0 ≤ x ≤ 1.
  • A power records repeated factors: (3/4)³ means (3/4) × (3/4) × (3/4), for three independent repeats.

Independent events: all must happen

Worked example

Seeing three animals

A visitor independently has probabilities 0.8, 0.7 and 0.5 of seeing three animals. Find the probability of seeing all three.

Solution
  1. All three independent events must happen on one path, so multiply.
  2. P(all three) = 0.8 × 0.7 × 0.5
  3. = 0.28

At least one: use the complement

Worked example

Success within two attempts

A player succeeds on a first attempt with probability 0.65. If the first attempt fails, success on the second attempt has probability 0.30. Find the probability of success within two attempts.

Solution
  1. The complement of success within two attempts is failure on both attempts.
  2. P(fail first) = 1 − 0.65 = 0.35
  3. P(fail both) = 0.35 × (1 − 0.30) = 0.35 × 0.70 = 0.245
  4. P(success within two) = 1 − 0.245 = 0.755
  5. The second-attempt probability is used only after the first failure.

Total probability finds an unknown

Worked example

Cyclist route lateness

A cyclist chooses route A with probability 0.3 and route B with probability 0.7. The probability of being late is x on A and 2x on B. Overall P(late) = 0.34. Find x.

Solution
  1. Build the complete event from the two disjoint late routes.
  2. P(late) = 0.3x + 0.7(2x) = 0.34
  3. 0.3x + 1.4x = 0.34
  4. Collect the x terms: 0.3x + 1.4x = 1.7x. Divide both sides by 1.7 to isolate x.
  5. 1.7x = 0.34
  6. x = 0.20
  7. Both route probabilities weight their conditional late probabilities.

Enumerate ordered dice outcomes

Worked example

Total score 5 from three dice

Let S be the event “the three dice have total score 5”. Three independent fair six-sided dice are thrown. Find P(S).

Solution
  1. Each die is distinguishable, so different orders are different outcomes.
  2. Each score is at least 1. Start from (1,1,1), total 3. The remaining 2 points either both go to one die, giving (1,1,3), or go to two dice, giving (1,2,2). A permutation is a rearrangement of the scores.
  3. The six orders are (1,1,3), (1,3,1), (3,1,1), (1,2,2), (2,1,2) and (2,2,1). Each pattern contributes 3.
  4. Total ordered outcomes = 6³ = 216.
  5. P(total 5) = 6/216 = 1/36

Repeated categories in any order

Worked example

Rice, pasta and soup

On each of 5 independent days, Ava chooses rice with probability 0.5, pasta with probability 0.3 or soup with probability 0.2. Find the probability of rice on 2 days, pasta on 2 days and soup on 1 day.

Solution
  1. One fixed order has probability (0.5)²(0.3)²(0.2).
  2. The factorial symbol means a descending product: 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1. Start with 5! orders, then divide by 2! for the repeated rice positions and 2! for the repeated pasta positions, so valid orders = 5!/(2!2!) = 30.
  3. P = 30(0.5)²(0.3)²(0.2)
  4. = 30(0.0045) = 0.135

Dependent days: first success

Worked example

First rainy day is Tuesday

An event begins on Sunday. P(rain on Sunday) = 0.3. On a later day, P(rain) = 0.6 after rain and 0.2 after a dry day. Find the probability that the first rainy day is Tuesday.

Solution
  1. First rain on Tuesday means Sunday dry, Monday dry, Tuesday rain.
  2. P(Sun dry) = 0.7
  3. The vertical bar “|” means “given that”, so P(Mon dry | Sun dry) is the probability of a dry Monday after a dry Sunday.
  4. P(Mon dry | Sun dry) = 0.8
  5. P(Tue rain | Mon dry) = 0.2
  6. P = 0.7 × 0.8 × 0.2 = 0.112
  7. Use the previous day’s result to choose the next probability.

Exactly one success

R means rainy and D means dry. A path such as RDDD records the weather in order from day 1 to day 4.

Worked example

Exactly one rainy day in four

For a 4-day event, P(rain on day 1) = 0.3. On each later day, P(rain) = 0.6 after rain and 0.2 after a dry day. Find the probability of rain on exactly one day.

Solution
  1. List the four disjoint paths with exactly one rainy day: RDDD, DRDD, DDRD, DDDR.
  2. RDDD = 0.3(0.4)(0.8)(0.8) = 48/625
  3. DRDD = 0.7(0.2)(0.4)(0.8) = 28/625
  4. DDRD = 0.7(0.8)(0.2)(0.4) = 28/625
  5. DDDR = 0.7(0.8)(0.8)(0.2) = 56/625
  6. P = (48 + 28 + 28 + 56)/625 = 160/625 = 32/125 = 0.256

Transfer then select

Worked example

Two boxes of balls

Box A contains 3 red and 2 blue balls. Box B contains x red and 6 blue balls. One ball is chosen at random from A and moved to B, then one ball is chosen at random from B. The count x is a nonnegative whole number. Find the probability that both balls involved are blue.

Solution
  1. P(blue transferred) = 2/5
  2. After a blue transfer, Box B has x + 7 balls, including 7 blue balls.
  3. P(blue selected | blue transferred) = 7/(x + 7)
  4. P(both blue) = (2/5)(7/(x + 7)) = 14/[5(x + 7)]
  5. Update Box B before using its conditional probability.

Combine valid orders

Worked example

Three vehicle directions

Write S for straight, L for left and R for right. At a junction, a vehicle turns left with probability 0.5, right with probability 0.2 or goes straight with probability 0.3. For three independent vehicles, find the probability that one goes straight and the other two both turn the same way.

Solution
  1. There are two valid patterns: SLL and SRR.
  2. SLL: 0.3(0.5)² = 0.075, in 3 orders gives 0.225
  3. SRR: 0.3(0.2)² = 0.012, in 3 orders gives 0.036
  4. The two patterns are disjoint, so add them.
  5. P = 0.225 + 0.036 = 0.261

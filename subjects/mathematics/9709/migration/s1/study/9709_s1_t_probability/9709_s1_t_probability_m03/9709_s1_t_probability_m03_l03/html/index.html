Conditional probability

Change the pool,
then condition.

Multi-stage probability needs careful bookkeeping. Update the contents after each change, describe the condition, and use only the complete paths that fit.

Start the lesson

Three rules to begin

  • A route is one complete sequence of outcomes.
  • Multiply branch probabilities along one route.
  • Add every mutually exclusive route that reaches the event. These are separate possible sequences, so no trial is counted twice.
  • “Given B” means we restrict attention to outcomes where B happened.
  • The conditional symbol P(A | B) means “probability of A given B”.

Conditional probability

P(A | B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B)

The denominator, below the fraction line, is the complete probability of condition B. It must be positive. The numerator, above the line, is the probability that both requested events happen. P means probability and “and” requires both events.

  • After removal, transfer or addition, change the total and colour counts before the next probability.

Worked example: which bag?

A spinner chooses Bag A with probability 1/3 and Bag B with probability 2/3. Bag A has 6 white and 3 red marbles. Bag B has 4 white and 5 red marbles. Two marbles are drawn without replacement. Given that one is white and one is red, find P(Bag B).

  1. One of each means white then red or red then white. In A, these two routes have equal probability, so multiply either one by 2: 2 × (6/9)(3/8) = 1/2.
  2. One of each in B: 2 × (4/9)(5/8) = 5/9.
  3. P(B and one of each) = (2/3)(5/9) = 10/27.
  4. P(one of each) = (1/3)(1/2) + (2/3)(5/9) = 29/54.
  5. Condition on one of each: P(B | one of each) = (10/27)/(29/54) = 20/29 ≈ 0.690.

Changing pools

Start with 4 red and 3 blue counters. Remove a red, then add a green.

  1. After removing red: 3 red, 3 blue, total 6.
  2. After adding green: 3 red, 3 blue, 1 green, total 7.
  3. Only now write the next probability: P(next blue) = 3/7.

Update first; multiply second; condition last.

Worked example: an added counter

A box contains 4 red and 3 blue counters. One counter is chosen at random and not replaced. One green counter is then added. A second counter is removed. Given that the second counter is blue, find the probability that the first counter was red.

  1. If red is first, the updated box has 3 red, 3 blue and 1 green. If blue is first, it has 4 red, 2 blue and 1 green. Both totals are 7.
  2. P(first red and second blue) = (4/7)(3/7) = 12/49.
  3. P(second blue) = (4/7)(3/7) + (3/7)(2/7) = 18/49.
  4. Use the conditional formula: P(first red | second blue) = (12/49)/(18/49) = 2/3.

The added green keeps the second total at 7.

Worked example: transfer between boxes

Box A contains 5 red and 2 blue balls. Box B contains 4 red and 3 blue balls. Each choice is at random. One ball moves from A to B, then one ball is chosen from B. Given that the chosen ball is blue, find P(the transferred ball was blue).

  1. Moving a blue makes B contain 4 red and 4 blue; moving a red makes B contain 5 red and 3 blue. The receiving total is always 8.
  2. P(A blue and B blue) = (2/7)(4/8) = 1/7.
  3. P(B blue) = (5/7)(3/8) + (2/7)(4/8) = 23/56.
  4. P(A blue | B blue) = (1/7)/(23/56) = 8/23.

The contents of B depend on the colour transferred.

Longer paths

For three stages, describe the event as valid route codes. In the diagram, F means fine and R means rainy.

Numerator and denominator

Keep only routes satisfying the requested start and final condition in the numerator. Add every route satisfying the final condition in the denominator.

Worked example: three days

The probability day 1 is fine is 0.6. After a fine day, the next is fine with probability 0.7. After a rainy day, the next is fine with probability 0.4. Given day 3 is rainy, find P(day 1 was fine).

  1. Write F for fine and R for rainy. After F, the rain probability is 1 − 0.7 = 0.3. After R, it is 1 − 0.4 = 0.6.
  2. Both FFR and FRR satisfy fine on day 1 and rainy on day 3.
  3. P(day 1 fine and day 3 rainy) = 0.6 × 0.7 × 0.3 + 0.6 × 0.3 × 0.6 = 0.126 + 0.108 = 0.234.
  4. For the denominator, add RFR and RRR too: P(day 3 rainy) = 0.234 + 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.3 + 0.4 × 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.234 + 0.048 + 0.144 = 0.426.
  5. Divide the joint probability by the condition: P(day 1 fine | day 3 rainy) = 0.234/0.426 = 39/71 ≈ 0.549, to three significant figures.

Worked example: at least one success

A player makes three attempts. The first succeeds with probability 0.3. Each later attempt succeeds with probability 0.5 after a success and 0.2 after a failure. Given at least one success, find the probability of exactly one success.

  1. S means success and F means failure. Exactly one success has routes SFF, FSF and FFS. “At least one” means one, two or three successes.
  2. P(exactly one) = 0.3×0.5×0.8 + 0.7×0.2×0.5 + 0.7×0.8×0.2 = 0.120 + 0.070 + 0.112 = 0.302.
  3. P(no success) = 0.7×0.8×0.8 = 0.448, so P(at least one) = 1 − 0.448 = 0.552.
  4. P(exactly one | at least one) = 0.302/0.552 = 151/276 ≈ 0.547.

Worked example: second-turn win

A bag contains 4 red and 4 white discs. Sam and Tom alternately remove one disc without replacement; Sam starts. The first player to remove two red discs wins. Given Tom wins on his second turn (the fourth draw overall), find P(Sam’s first disc was red).

  1. R means red and W means white. Tom draws second and fourth. He must draw red on both turns; Sam must not already have two reds after draw 3.
  2. If Sam’s first disc is red and Tom wins on his second turn, the only sequence is RRWR.
  3. P(RRWR) = (4/8) × (3/7) × (4/6) × (2/5) = 2/35.
  4. Before excluding an earlier win, P(draws 2 and 4 are red) = (4/8) × (3/7) = 3/14. Any two specified draw positions have the same red probability.
  5. Remove RRRR, because Sam wins on draw 3: P(RRRR) = (4/8) × (3/7) × (2/6) × (1/5) = 1/70.
  6. P(Tom wins on his second turn) = 3/14 − 1/70 = 1/5.
  7. P(Sam’s first disc red | Tom wins on his second turn) = (2/35)/(1/5) = 2/7.

Worked example: different colours

A bag is chosen at random. Bag X has 5 red and 3 blue marbles; Bag Y has 3 red and 2 blue marbles. One marble is removed and a new blue marble is added to the bag. A second marble is removed. Given different colours were removed, find P(Bag Y).

  1. After red is removed, adding blue increases the available blue count. After blue is removed and a blue is added, the original counts return. X always has 8 marbles for the second draw; Y always has 5.
  2. P(different | X) = (5/8)(4/8) + (3/8)(5/8) = 20/64 + 15/64 = 35/64.
  3. P(different | Y) = (3/5)(3/5) + (2/5)(3/5) = 9/25 + 6/25 = 3/5.
  4. P(Y and different) = 1/2 × 3/5 = 3/10.
  5. P(different) = 1/2 × 35/64 + 1/2 × 3/5 = 367/640.
  6. P(Y | different) = (3/10)/(367/640) = 192/367 ≈ 0.523.

Include both colour orders inside each chosen bag.

Key ideas

  • Write the condition and event before calculating.
  • Update every pool after a change.
  • Multiply a complete route.
  • Add all mutually exclusive valid routes.
  • Use P(A | B) = P(A and B)/P(B), with P(B) as the denominator.

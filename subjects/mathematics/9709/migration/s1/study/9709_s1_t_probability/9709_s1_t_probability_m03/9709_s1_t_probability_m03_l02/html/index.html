Reverse probability trees

Start with the outcome you know.

A forward tree follows causes to outcomes. A reverse probability asks which starting branch is likely after a final outcome has been observed.

Start the lesson · Return to key ideas

Three moves for a reverse question

  • An event is a result or collection of results. P(A) means the chance that event A occurs.
  • A branch describes the next possible result. A route combines branches through the stages. Different complete routes are separate alternatives.
  • A fraction divides its numerator (top) by its denominator (bottom). Here the denominator is the probability of the condition, which must be greater than zero.
  • Along one route, multiply branch probabilities.
  • If several routes finish at the same outcome, add their route probabilities.
  • For “A given B”, divide the route ending in A and B by every route ending in B.
Conditional probability

The phrase “A given B” means B is already known. The possible outcomes are restricted to routes ending in B.

P(A given B) = P(A and B) / P(B)
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B given A)

For example, if P(A) = 0.4 and P(B given A) = 0.6, then P(A and B) = 0.4 × 0.6 = 0.24.

Numerator: the route or routes that satisfy both A and B. Denominator: every route that finishes at B.

Reverse the tree

Let A be the first-stage event and B the final event. A′ means “not A”. The apostrophe is a complement mark.

P(B) = P(A and B) + P(A′ and B)
P(A given B) = P(A and B) / P(B)

Worked example: large jar

The symbol ≈ means approximately equal. Three significant figures keeps three digits from the first nonzero digit, using the next digit to round.

A jar is Deluxe with probability 0.6 and Everyday with probability 0.4. A Deluxe jar is large with probability 0.3; an Everyday jar is large with probability 0.2. Find the probability that it is Everyday, given that it is large.

  1. Numerator route: P(Everyday and large) = 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.08.
  2. Denominator includes both large routes: P(large) = 0.6 × 0.3 + 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.18 + 0.08 = 0.26.
  3. Divide: P(Everyday given large) = 0.08/0.26 = 4/13 ≈ 0.308 to 3 s.f.

Work forward along the tree before dividing backwards.

Worked example: successful retake

First-time pass probability is 0.8. A first failure is followed by exactly one retake, passed with probability 0.5. Given eventual success, find the probability the first attempt failed.

  1. First fail then retake pass: 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.1.
  2. Eventual pass has two routes: 0.8 + 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.9.
  3. Therefore P(failed first given eventually passes) = 0.1/0.9 = 1/9 ≈ 0.111 to 3 s.f.

Complements on branches

Worked example · replies

P(X)=0.7, P(Y)=0.3. Reply after X: 0.4; reply after Y: 0.8.

  1. Complement each reply branch: no reply after X is 0.6, after Y is 0.2.
  2. P(no reply)=0.7×0.6+0.3×0.2=0.48.
  3. P(Y given no reply)=(0.3×0.2)/0.48=1/8.

Worked example: three contact methods

Text, email and app have probabilities 0.4, 0.25 and 0.35. Immediate reply probabilities are 0.5, 0.2 and 0.6. Given no immediate reply, find the probability of email.

  1. No immediate reply is the complement of immediate reply: after text it is 0.5, after email 0.8, and after app 0.4.
  2. Email and no reply: 0.25×0.8=0.20.
  3. All no-reply routes: 0.4×0.5+0.25×0.8+0.35×0.4=0.54.
  4. P(email given no reply)=0.20/0.54=10/27≈0.370 to 3 s.f.

All three no-reply routes belong in the denominator.

Worked example: no fruit

Pasta, soup and rice have probabilities 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2. Fruit probabilities are 0.2, 0.7 and 0. Given no fruit, find the probability the choice was not soup.

  1. No-fruit probabilities are 0.8 after pasta, 0.3 after soup, and 1 after rice. “Not soup” allows both pasta and rice.
  2. Not soup and no fruit: 0.5×0.8+0.2×1=0.60.
  3. No fruit: 0.5×0.8+0.3×0.3+0.2×1=0.69.
  4. P(not soup given no fruit)=0.60/0.69=20/23≈0.870 to 3 s.f.

Final check: advanced group

Choose one club member at random. A percentage is a count per hundred, so 40% = 40/100 = 0.4. 40% of club members are male. Of males, 30% are advanced; of females, 20% are advanced. Given advanced, find the probability the member is male.

  1. P(male and advanced)=0.4×0.3=0.12.
  2. P(advanced)=0.4×0.3+0.6×0.2=0.24.
  3. P(male given advanced)=0.12/0.24=1/2.

Key ideas

  • Translate “given” into a restricted final outcome.
  • Multiply along each route.
  • Add every route that finishes at the known outcome for the denominator.
  • Put only the requested route or routes in the numerator.
  • Use complements before multiplying when a branch is “not”.

Quick checks

Check A. A route has probability 0.3 and then 0.4. What is its joint probability?

Check B. An observed outcome B can arise through routes A and B with probability 0.18 and A′ and B with probability 0.12. What is P(A given B)?

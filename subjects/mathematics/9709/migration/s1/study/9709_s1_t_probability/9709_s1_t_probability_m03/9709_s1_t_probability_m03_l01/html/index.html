Conditional probability

Restrict first.
Then count.

The words “given that” shrink the outcomes you are allowed to use. Count inside that restricted set, then identify the outcomes that also satisfy the event you want.

Start the lesson

Read the event language

A sample space lists the possible results. An event is a chosen result or group of results. New information can remove outcomes from that space.

Notation before the formula

  • P(A) means the probability that event A happens.
  • A and B means both events happen together. The symbol ∩ means “and”.
  • A′ means A does not happen. The word “given” introduces the condition.
  • P(A given B), written P(A | B), asks: among the B outcomes, what fraction are also A?

The denominator is the restricted set B. The numerator is the part of B where A also happens.

Conditional probability

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B), provided P(B) > 0
  • The denominator, at the bottom of the fraction, is the probability of B. P(B) > 0 means that condition has a positive probability, so we can divide by it.
  • The numerator, at the top, is the probability that both A and B happen. When using counts, all elementary outcomes must be equally likely.

For P(A | B), which set supplies the denominator?

Recall routes and restricted counts

  1. If P(A) = 0.4 and the chance of B after A is 0.7, the route where both occur has probability 0.4 × 0.7 = 0.28.
  2. If 18 of 30 selected students are girls, choosing one of those students at random gives P(girl) = 18/30 = 3/5.
  3. The condition determines which students or outcomes form the total.

Worked example

Swims given under 18

Choose a club member at random. Find P(swims given under 18).

Solution
  1. The condition “under 18” selects the first row.
  2. That row contains 30 people. Of them, 18 swim.
  3. P(swims | under 18) = 18/30 = 3/5 = 0.6

Count equally likely outcomes

This grid lists two independent spins of a fair spinner numbered 1, 2, 3. In each ordered pair, the first value records the first spin and the second records the second spin. All nine pairs are equally likely.

When outcomes are equally likely, the conditional fraction becomes a count divided by a count.

Restrict two dice

Worked example

Sum 6 given at least one 2

Two independent fair four-sided dice numbered 1, 2, 3, 4 are rolled. Find P(sum is 6 given at least one die shows 2).

Solution
  1. Restrict first: the outcomes with at least one 2 are (2,1), (2,2), (2,3), (2,4), (1,2), (3,2), (4,2). There are 7.
  2. Among these, sum 6 occurs for (2,4) and (4,2). There are 2.
  3. P(sum 6 | at least one 2) = 2/7

Do not double-count

  • List the restricted outcomes in an order that names each ordered pair once.
  • (2,2) belongs to the condition but must appear once, not twice.
  • Count the required outcomes only after the restricted set is complete.

Complements inside a condition

  • “Given not B” means the condition is B′.
  • Use P(B′) = 1 − P(B) for the new denominator.
  • Then use the same structure: P(A | B′) = P(A ∩ B′) / P(B′).
Worked example

A given not B

If P(B) = 0.35 and P(A ∩ B′) = 0.26, find P(A | B′).

Solution
  1. P(B′) = 1 − 0.35 = 0.65
  2. P(A | B′) = 0.26/0.65
  3. = 0.4

Combine routes

The symbol ≈ means “approximately equal”. Three significant figures keeps three digits starting at the first nonzero digit; the next digit decides rounding.

Worked example

Bus and not late

A student travels by bus with probability 0.3 and bicycle with probability 0.7. Being late has probability 0.2 by bus and 0.05 by bicycle. Find P(bus given not late).

Solution
  1. The not-late probabilities are 1 − 0.2 = 0.8 by bus and 1 − 0.05 = 0.95 by bicycle.
  2. P(bus and not late) = 0.3 × 0.8 = 0.24
  3. P(not late) = 0.3 × 0.8 + 0.7 × 0.95 = 0.24 + 0.665 = 0.905
  4. P(bus | not late) = 0.24/0.905 = 48/181
  5. ≈ 0.265 (three significant figures)

Add separate routes

Worked example

Coffee given ice

Mia chooses tea, coffee or juice with probabilities 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2. Ice is added with probabilities 0.1, 0.4 and 0.8 respectively. Find P(coffee given ice).

Solution
  1. P(coffee and ice) = 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12
  2. P(ice) = 0.5 × 0.1 + 0.3 × 0.4 + 0.2 × 0.8 = 0.33
  3. P(coffee | ice) = 0.12/0.33 = 4/11
  4. ≈ 0.364 (three significant figures)

Worked example

A product after an odd first score

A fair spinner numbered 1, 2, 3 is spun twice independently. Given that the first score is odd, find the probability that the product of the scores is at most 3.

  • A product is the result of multiplication. “At most 3” includes 3 and every smaller value.
  • The odd possible first scores are 1 and 3. Keep only those two rows of the outcome grid.
First scorePossible second scoresSecond scores giving product at most 3
11, 2, 31, 2, 3
31, 2, 31
Solution
  1. The condition leaves 2 × 3 = 6 equally likely pairs.
  2. The required pairs are (1,1), (1,2), (1,3) and (3,1). Their products are 1, 2, 3 and 3.
  3. Four of the six restricted outcomes satisfy the question: P(product at most 3 | first score odd) = 4/6 = 2/3.
  4. Check the excluded pairs: (3,2) gives 6 and (3,3) gives 9, both greater than 3.

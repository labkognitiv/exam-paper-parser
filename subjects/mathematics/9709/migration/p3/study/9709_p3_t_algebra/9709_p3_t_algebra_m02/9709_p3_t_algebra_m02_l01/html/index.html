Quotients and remainders

If a polynomial does not divide exactly, what is left? Polynomial long division separates the original expression into a multiple of the divisor and a smaller remainder.

What division is saying

A polynomial is an expression made from constants and whole-number powers of a variable, such as x² + 2x − 1. A coefficient is the number multiplying a power; the coefficient of x² here is 1.

  • The dividend is the polynomial being divided.
  • The divisor is the non-zero polynomial dividing it.
  • The quotient records how many polynomial multiples fit.
  • The remainder is what is left after the final subtraction.
Division identityP(x) = D(x)Q(x) + R(x)

Here P(x) is the dividend, D(x) the divisor, Q(x) the quotient and R(x) the remainder.

A polynomial's degree is its highest power of x with a non-zero coefficient. Its leading term is the term with that highest power. Stop only when the remainder has lower degree than the divisor.

degree of R < degree of D
Divisor degree 1constant remainder
Divisor degree 2linear or constant remainder
Divisor degree 3quadratic, linear or constant remainder

Build the quotient one term at a time

Write every power in descending order. If a power is missing, insert a zero coefficient. For example, x⁴ = x⁴ + 0x³ + 0x² + 0x + 0. These zero terms change no value; they keep like powers aligned.

Divide
Divide the current leading term by the divisor's leading term.
Multiply
Multiply that new quotient term by the whole divisor.
Subtract
Put the product in brackets, then subtract every term.
Repeat
Use the new leading term until the remainder degree is small enough.

Signs change during subtraction, not while copying. Brackets make this visible.

Worked example

Divide x⁴ by x² + 2x − 1

First restore the missing powers: x⁴ + 0x³ + 0x² + 0x + 0.

  1. x⁴ ÷ x² = x². Multiply by the whole divisor and subtract: x⁴ − (x⁴ + 2x³ − x²) = −2x³ + x².
  2. −2x³ ÷ x² = −2x. Subtract −2x³ − 4x² + 2x to get 5x² − 2x.
  3. 5x² ÷ x² = 5. Subtract 5x² + 10x − 5 to get 5 − 12x.

The remainder is linear, so its degree is 1. This is lower than the divisor's degree 2.

Q(x) = x² − 2x + 5, R(x) = 5 − 12x

Key ideas

  1. Write powers in descending order and insert zero placeholders.
  2. Divide leading terms, multiply by the full divisor, subtract in brackets, then repeat.
  3. Stop when the remainder degree is lower than the divisor degree.
  4. State quotient and remainder, then rebuild the dividend to check.

Bigger coefficients

Divide 6x⁴ + x³ − x² + 5x − 6 by 2x² − x + 1.

  1. 6x⁴ ÷ 2x² = 3x². Subtraction leaves 4x³ − 4x² + 5x − 6.
  2. 4x³ ÷ 2x² = 2x. Subtraction leaves −2x² + 3x − 6.
  3. −2x² ÷ 2x² = −1. Subtraction leaves 2x − 5.

Q(x) = 3x² + 2x − 1, R(x) = 2x − 5

Your turn, with the solution shown

Divide 2x⁴ + 1 by x² − x + 2.

  1. Use placeholders: 2x⁴ + 0x³ + 0x² + 0x + 1.
  2. 2x⁴ ÷ x² = 2x². Subtract 2x⁴ − 2x³ + 4x² to get 2x³ − 4x² + 0x + 1.
  3. 2x³ ÷ x² = 2x. Subtract 2x³ − 2x² + 4x to get −2x² − 4x + 1.
  4. −2x² ÷ x² = −2. Subtract −2x² + 2x − 4 to get −6x + 5.

Q(x) = 2x² + 2x − 2, R(x) = 5 − 6x

Sign checks

Missing powers

Do not replace a missing term by x³. That adds a new term.

Write 0x³. It preserves the value and the column.

Subtracting a line

Write the whole product in brackets.

(x³ + 2x² − x + 1) − (x³ + 3x²) = −x² − x + 1.

Stopping

A quadratic divisor cannot leave a quadratic remainder. Continue until the remainder is linear, constant or zero.

Stating the answer

Give both parts clearly: quotient = … and remainder = ….

Rebuild to check

  • Multiply the divisor by your quotient.
  • Add your remainder.
  • The result must equal the original dividend term for term.
(x² − x + 2)(2x² + 2x − 2) + (5 − 6x) = 2x⁴ + 1

In the expansion, the linear terms are 2x + 4x − 6x = 0. The cubic and quadratic terms also cancel, leaving 2x⁴ + 1.

If the reconstruction fails, first check the signs in each subtraction.

Before you leave

Say the four actions once more: divide, multiply, subtract, repeat. Then check that the remainder degree is small enough.

Resolving forces

One force.
Two useful directions.

A sloping force can push sideways and upwards at the same time. Components tell us exactly how much acts in each chosen direction.

Start the lesson

Start by choosing directions

  • A force is a push or pull. Its magnitude tells us its size.
  • A force also has a direction, so we draw it as an arrow.
  • A component is the part of a force acting in one chosen direction.
  • Horizontal means level from left to right. Vertical means straight up and down.
  • Choose two perpendicular directions. They meet at a right angle.
  • A right angle is a quarter-turn, or 90°.
  • For horizontal and vertical axes, right and up are usually positive; left and down are negative.
  • The symbol θ\thetaθ, read “theta”, names an angle.
  • The symbol FFF names the force magnitude.
  • The force and its two components make a right-angled triangle.
  • The hypotenuse is the side opposite the 90° angle. Here it is the original force arrow.
  • Relative to θ, the shorter side touching θ is adjacent; the shorter side across from θ is opposite.

Components of one force

X=Fcos⁡θX=F\cos\thetaX=Fcosθ
Y=Fsin⁡θY=F\sin\thetaY=Fsinθ
  • FFF is the force magnitude, measured in newtons, written N.
  • XXX is the horizontal component; YYY is the vertical component.
  • The adjacent component uses cosine.
  • The opposite component uses sine.
  • If the angle is measured from the vertical, the vertical component is adjacent and uses cosine.
  • These formulae first give component magnitudes. Apply a plus or minus sign after checking the chosen positive direction.

Worked example

A force near a river

A 60 N force acts at 25° to the direction of a river. Find its components along and perpendicular to the river.

  1. The river direction is adjacent to the angle, so use cosine.
  2. 60cos⁡25∘=54.4 N60\cos25^\circ=54.4\text{ N}60cos25∘=54.4 N along the river.
  3. The perpendicular direction is opposite the angle, so use sine.
  4. 60sin⁡25∘=25.4 N60\sin25^\circ=25.4\text{ N}60sin25∘=25.4 N perpendicular to the river.

Answer: 54.4 N along the river and 25.4 N perpendicular to it.

The component method

  1. Draw axes or name two perpendicular directions.
  2. Mark each force angle from a known direction.
  3. Write a signed component for every force.
  4. Add only components in the same direction.
  5. Use the total to answer the question asked.

Look at the arrow first. Decide the sign before touching the calculator.

  • A component pointing in the positive direction has a plus sign.
  • A component pointing in the negative direction has a minus sign.
  • Coplanar forces lie in the same flat plane.
  • The resultant is the single force with the same overall effect as all the forces together.

Add components in one direction

Two forces along a river

Forces of 60 N and 50 N act at 25° and 15° to the same river direction.

  1. First component: 60cos⁡25∘=54.460\cos25^\circ=54.460cos25∘=54.4 N.
  2. Second component: 50cos⁡15∘=48.350\cos15^\circ=48.350cos15∘=48.3 N.
  3. Both point along the river, so add them.
  4. 54.4+48.3=102.754.4+48.3=102.754.4+48.3=102.7 N.

Answer: 102.7 N in the river direction.

Find a direction from components

  • The x- and y-axes divide the plane into four regions called quadrants.
  • The first quadrant is the up-and-right region.
  • Inverse tangent, written tan⁻¹, finds an angle from a known tangent ratio.
  • Anticlockwise means turning opposite to the hands of a clock.
  • If the horizontal and vertical components are known, they form the two shorter sides of a right triangle.
  • The tangent ratio compares the opposite side with the adjacent side.
  • Use inverse tangent to recover the angle.
  • Then check the signs of both components to identify the correct quadrant.
tan⁡θ=YX,θ=tan⁡−1 ⁣(YX)\tan\theta=\frac{Y}{X},\qquad \theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{Y}{X}\right)tanθ=XY​,θ=tan−1(XY​)

X=16.7 NX=16.7\text{ N}X=16.7 N and Y=8.98 NY=8.98\text{ N}Y=8.98 N.

  3. Both components are positive, so the force is in the first quadrant.

Key ideas

  • Choose two perpendicular directions.
  • Beside the marked angle: Fcos⁡θF\cos\thetaFcosθ.
  • Opposite the marked angle: Fsin⁡θF\sin\thetaFsinθ.
  • Give each component its correct sign.
  • Add components acting along the same axis.
  • Use tan⁡θ=Y/X\tan\theta=Y/Xtanθ=Y/X to find direction, then check the quadrant.

Exam-style resultant

Three forces act at a point: 20 N along +x, 30 N at 40° above +x, and 16 N at 70° below -x.

  1. The 20 N force contributes +20 N horizontally and 0 N vertically.
  2. The 30 N force points right and up, so both components are positive.
  3. The 16 N force points left and down, so both components are negative.
  4. X=20+30cos⁡40∘−16cos⁡70∘X=20+30\cos40^\circ-16\cos70^\circX=20+30cos40∘−16cos70∘.
  5. X=20+23.0−5.47=37.5 NX=20+23.0-5.47=37.5\text{ N}X=20+23.0−5.47=37.5 N.
  6. Y=30sin⁡40∘−16sin⁡70∘Y=30\sin40^\circ-16\sin70^\circY=30sin40∘−16sin70∘.
  7. Y=19.3−15.0=4.25 NY=19.3-15.0=4.25\text{ N}Y=19.3−15.0=4.25 N.
  8. Both totals are positive, so the resultant lies in the first quadrant.
  9. Use the unrounded components in the final calculation: θ=tan⁡−1(4.248546/37.509011)=6.46∘\theta=\tan^{-1}(4.248546/37.509011)=6.46^\circθ=tan−1(4.248546/37.509011)=6.46∘.

Answer: components 37.5 N right and 4.25 N up; direction 6.46° anticlockwise from +x.

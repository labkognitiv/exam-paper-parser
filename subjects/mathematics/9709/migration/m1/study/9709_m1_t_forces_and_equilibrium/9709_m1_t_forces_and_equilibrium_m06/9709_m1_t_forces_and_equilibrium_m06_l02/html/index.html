Forces and equilibrium · Module 6 · Lesson 2

Two connected rough systems

Two particles joined over a pulley must move together. Each contact has its own reaction and friction, while one light taut string carries one common tension.

Key ideasWorked examplesRecap

Treat both particles as one connected system

  • A particle is modelled as a single point. Its mass mmm is measured in kilograms.
  • A rough contact can exert friction along its surface. The coefficient μ\muμ describes the roughness and has no unit.
  • A normal reaction is the surface push on a particle. It acts perpendicular to the surface.
  • Equilibrium means the total force on each particle is zero.
  • An axis is a chosen reference line. Parallel means in the same direction. Perpendicular means at 90∘90^\circ90∘. For each particle, use axes parallel and perpendicular to its plane.
  • A taut string stays straight and does not go slack. An inextensible string has constant length, so one particle moving down makes the other move up.
  • A light string has negligible mass. A smooth pulley has no friction.
  • One light taut string over a smooth pulley has the same tension TTT on both sides.
  • The particles usually have different normal reactions, RAR_ARA​ and RBR_BRB​.
  • The same coefficient μ\muμ may apply at both contacts, but their friction magnitudes can differ.
  • Weight is mgmgmg vertically down. ggg is gravitational acceleration in m s−2\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}ms−2, read as metres per second squared. N\mathrm NN means newton.

Resolve weight on a slope

  • If a plane is at α\alphaα to the horizontal, its inward normal is at the same α\alphaα to the vertical. Both reference directions rotate through the same angle.
  • So the angle between vertical weight and its into-plane component is α\alphaα.
  • The weight and its components form a right triangle. Weight mgmgmg is the hypotenuse, the longest side opposite the right angle.
  • The adjacent side touches α\alphaα and is not the hypotenuse. The opposite side faces α\alphaα.
  • cos⁡α=adjacenthypotenuse\cos\alpha=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}cosα=hypotenuseadjacent​. The into-plane component is mgcos⁡αmg\cos\alphamgcosα.
  • sin⁡α=oppositehypotenuse\sin\alpha=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinα=hypotenuseopposite​. The down-plane component is mgsin⁡αmg\sin\alphamgsinα.

Limiting friction

  • Limiting equilibrium means the particle is at rest and about to slip.
  • At that instant friction has its maximum magnitude, F=μRF=\mu RF=μR.
  • For ordinary rest, required friction can be smaller: F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR.

Choose compatible friction directions

  • If B is about to move down its plane, the taut string makes A move up its plane.
  • Friction on B then acts up B's plane. Friction on A acts down A's plane.
  • The opposite pair applies if A is about to move down.
  • The weight component is always down the particle's plane. Its sign follows your chosen positive direction.
  • Use FA=μRAF_A=\mu R_AFA​=μRA​ and FB=μRBF_B=\mu R_BFB​=μRB​ only when both contacts are limiting.
  1. State the connected impending motion.
  2. Find RAR_ARA​ and RBR_BRB​ separately.
  3. Write each friction magnitude and direction.
  4. Write one parallel equilibrium equation for A and one for B.
  5. Eliminate the common tension TTT, then solve.

Angles before inverse sine

  • In a right triangle, sin⁡θ=oppositehypotenuse\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinθ=hypotenuseopposite​.
  • If the sine ratio is known, the inverse-sine key returns the angle: θ=sin⁡−1(x)\theta=\sin^{-1}(x)θ=sin−1(x).

Worked example

Same coefficient, two reactions

A is 0.8 kg on a rough 45° plane. B is 1.2 kg on a rough 30° plane. The same coefficient of friction applies at both contacts. Both contacts are at limiting friction, and the system is in equilibrium. Take g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2. Find μ\muμ.

  1. B's weight component is 12sin⁡30∘=6N12\sin30^\circ=6\mathrm N12sin30∘=6N. A's is 8sin⁡45∘=42N8\sin45^\circ=4\sqrt2\mathrm N8sin45∘=42​N. Since 6>426>4\sqrt26>42​, B tends down and A tends up.
  2. The reactions are:
    RA=8cos⁡45∘=42,RB=12cos⁡30∘=63R_A=8\cos45^\circ=4\sqrt2,\qquad R_B=12\cos30^\circ=6\sqrt3RA​=8cos45∘=42​,RB​=12cos30∘=63​
  3. Both contacts are limiting:
    FA=42μ,FB=63μF_A=4\sqrt2\mu,\qquad F_B=6\sqrt3\muFA​=42​μ,FB​=63​μ
  4. For A, take up its plane as positive. Friction is down:
    T=8sin⁡45∘+FAT=8\sin45^\circ+F_AT=8sin45∘+FA​
  5. For B, take down its plane as positive. Friction and tension are up:
    12sin⁡30∘=T+FB12\sin30^\circ=T+F_B12sin30∘=T+FB​
  6. Substitute for T,FA,FBT,F_A,F_BT,FA​,FB​:
    6−42=(42+63)μ6-4\sqrt2=(4\sqrt2+6\sqrt3)\mu6−42​=(42​+63​)μ
    μ=6−4242+63=0.0213809…\mu=\frac{6-4\sqrt2}{4\sqrt2+6\sqrt3}=0.0213809\ldotsμ=42​+63​6−42​​=0.0213809…

Answer: μ=0.0214\mathbf{\mu=0.0214}μ=0.0214.

Worked example

Known coefficient, unknown angle

B is 0.4 kg on a rough 25° plane with μ=0.8\mu=0.8μ=0.8. A is 0.9 kg on another plane inclined at θ\thetaθ. A 2.5 N force acts up B's plane, towards the pulley.

Those facts alone do not determine θ\thetaθ. Continue under two explicit conditions: A's plane is smooth, and B is at limiting equilibrium about to move up its plane. Find θ\thetaθ, taking g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2.

  1. B is about to move up, so friction on B acts down. A moves down its smooth plane.
  2. For B, perpendicular to the plane:
    RB=4cos⁡25∘=3.625231…NR_B=4\cos25^\circ=3.625231\ldots\mathrm NRB​=4cos25∘=3.625231…N
  3. B is limiting:
    FB=μRB=0.8(4cos⁡25∘)=2.900184…NF_B=\mu R_B=0.8(4\cos25^\circ)=2.900184\ldots\mathrm NFB​=μRB​=0.8(4cos25∘)=2.900184…N

    2.5+T=4sin⁡25∘+FB2.5+T=4\sin25^\circ+F_B2.5+T=4sin25∘+FB​
    T=4sin⁡25∘+0.8(4cos⁡25∘)−2.5=2.090657965…NT=4\sin25^\circ+0.8(4\cos25^\circ)-2.5=2.090657965\ldots\mathrm NT=4sin25∘+0.8(4cos25∘)−2.5=2.090657965…N

Key ideas recap

  • One light taut string over a smooth pulley has one common tension.
  • Find the two reactions separately.
  • The same coefficient does not mean equal friction.
  • Connected impending motions must be compatible with the fixed string length.
  • Friction opposes each particle's impending motion.
  • Use F=μRF=\mu RF=μR at a contact only when that contact is limiting.
  • Write both parallel equations before eliminating TTT.

Constant-speed vehicle and power

  • Speed vvv is distance travelled per second, measured in m s−1\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}ms−1.
  • Power PPP is energy transferred per second. Its unit is the watt, written W\mathrm WW.
  • Along the force direction, work transferred equals force × distance. Dividing by time gives force × distance per second, so power equals force × speed.
  • For a driving force in the direction of motion, P=FvP=FvP=Fv. Therefore F=P/vF=P/vF=P/v.
  • At constant speed in a straight line, acceleration and resultant force are zero.
  • Resistance acts opposite the vehicle's motion.

A 1400 kg vehicle travels up a slope at a constant 32 m s⁻¹. The engine power is 64 000 W and resistance is 1250 N. Find the slope angle θ\thetaθ, taking g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2.

  1. Find the driving force from power:
    F=Pv=6400032=2000NF=\frac Pv=\frac{64000}{32}=2000\mathrm NF=vP​=3264000​=2000N
  2. At constant speed, forces along the slope balance:
    2000=1250+14000sin⁡θ2000=1250+14000\sin\theta2000=1250+14000sinθ
  3. Rearrange:
    sin⁡θ=75014000=0.0535714…\sin\theta=\frac{750}{14000}=0.0535714\ldotssinθ=14000750​=0.0535714…
  4. Use inverse sine:
    θ=sin⁡−1 ⁣(75014000)=3.07089…∘\theta=\sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{750}{14000}\right)=3.07089\ldots^\circθ=sin−1(14000750​)=3.07089…∘

Answer: θ=3.07∘\mathbf{\theta=3.07^\circ}θ=3.07∘.

Two rough planes: 30° and 20°

A is 1 kg on a rough 30° plane. B is 2 kg on a rough 20° plane. The same coefficient applies at both contacts. Both contacts are at limiting friction, with B about to move down its plane. Take g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2. Find an equation for μ\muμ and hence its value.

  1. B moves down and A moves up. Friction on B acts up; friction on A acts down.
  2. Find the reactions and limiting frictions:
    RA=10cos⁡30∘,RB=20cos⁡20∘R_A=10\cos30^\circ,\quad R_B=20\cos20^\circRA​=10cos30∘,RB​=20cos20∘
    FA=10μcos⁡30∘,FB=20μcos⁡20∘F_A=10\mu\cos30^\circ,\quad F_B=20\mu\cos20^\circFA​=10μcos30∘,FB​=20μcos20∘
  3. For A, up its plane is positive:
    T=10sin⁡30∘+FAT=10\sin30^\circ+F_AT=10sin30∘+FA​
  4. For B, down its plane is positive:
    20sin⁡20∘=T+FB20\sin20^\circ=T+F_B20sin20∘=T+FB​
  5. Substitute the equation for TTT into B's equation:
    20sin⁡20∘=10sin⁡30∘+10μcos⁡30∘+20μcos⁡20∘20\sin20^\circ=10\sin30^\circ+10\mu\cos30^\circ+20\mu\cos20^\circ20sin20∘=10sin30∘+10μcos30∘+20μcos20∘
  6. Collect the μ\muμ terms and divide by 10:
    2sin⁡20∘−sin⁡30∘=μ(cos⁡30∘+2cos⁡20∘)2\sin20^\circ-\sin30^\circ=\mu(\cos30^\circ+2\cos20^\circ)2sin20∘−sin30∘=μ(cos30∘+2cos20∘)
    μ=2sin⁡20∘−sin⁡30∘cos⁡30∘+2cos⁡20∘=0.0670356…\mu=\frac{2\sin20^\circ-\sin30^\circ}{\cos30^\circ+2\cos20^\circ}=0.0670356\ldotsμ=cos30∘+2cos20∘2sin20∘−sin30∘​=0.0670356…

Answer: μ=0.0670\mathbf{\mu=0.0670}μ=0.0670.

Exam-style connected system

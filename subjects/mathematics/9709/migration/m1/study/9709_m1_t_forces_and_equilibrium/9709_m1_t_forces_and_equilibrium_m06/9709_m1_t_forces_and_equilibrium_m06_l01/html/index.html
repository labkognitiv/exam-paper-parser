Build the model

  • Mass is measured in kilograms, kg. Its symbol is m.
  • A force is a push or pull. It is measured in newtons, N.
  • Gravitational acceleration has symbol g. Its unit m s−2\text{m s}^{-2}m s−2 is read “metres per second squared”.
  • Weight is the force of gravity: W=mgW=mgW=mg. Unless told otherwise, use g=10 m s−2g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}g=10 m s−2.
  • A plane is an inclined flat surface. The angle θ\thetaθ is measured in degrees from the horizontal.
  • Perpendicular directions meet at a right angle of 90°.
  • In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is the longest side. The adjacent side is the other side touching θ\thetaθ; the opposite side does not touch θ\thetaθ.
  • sin⁡θ=oppositehypotenuse\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinθ=hypotenuseopposite​ and cos⁡θ=adjacenthypotenuse\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}cosθ=hypotenuseadjacent​.
  • A component is the part of a force in one direction. To resolve means to split a force into perpendicular components.
  • The normal reaction acts perpendicular to contact. Its magnitude is R.
  • Equilibrium means the resultant force is zero.
  • The coefficient of friction is μ\muμ. Static friction satisfies F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR.
  • At limiting equilibrium, motion is about to start and F=μRF=\mu RF=μR.

String and pulley words

  • A light string has negligible mass.
  • A taut string has no slack.
  • An inextensible string keeps a fixed length.
  • A smooth pulley has no friction.
  • These conditions give one common tension T throughout the string.
  • Tension pulls each particle along the string.

Weight points vertically down. The inward normal is perpendicular to the plane. Since vertical and horizontal are also perpendicular, the angle between weight and the inward normal is θ\thetaθ.

In the weight triangle, W is the hypotenuse. The down-plane component is opposite θ\thetaθ; the into-plane component is adjacent.

For the particle on the plane

R=mgcos⁡θR=mg\cos\thetaR=mgcosθ
weight component down the plane=mgsin⁡θ\text{weight component down the plane}=mg\sin\thetaweight component down the plane=mgsinθ

A rough plane can exert friction. A smooth plane cannot.

For the hanging particle

When the hanging particle has zero acceleration, its vertical forces balance:

T=mgT=mgT=mg

This includes rest or constant velocity. Do not use this balance for an accelerating hanging particle.

Friction

Impending motion is motion about to happen. Friction opposes that direction.

0≤F≤μR0\leq F\leq\mu R0≤F≤μR

At limiting equilibrium, F=μRF=\mu RF=μR.

Find the unknown mass

Unknown mass on a 30° plane

A block of mass m kg lies on a rough 30° plane with μ=0.2\mu=0.2μ=0.2. It is connected by a light, taut, inextensible string over a smooth pulley to a hanging 4 kg mass. The system is at rest and the block is about to move up the plane. Find m.

  1. The hanging mass is at rest:
    T=4g=40 NT=4g=40\text{ N}T=4g=40 N
  2. For the block, perpendicular balance gives
    R=mgcos⁡30∘R=mg\cos30^\circR=mgcos30∘

    F=μR=0.2mgcos⁡30∘F=\mu R=0.2mg\cos30^\circF=μR=0.2mgcos30∘
  4. Resolve up the plane:
    T=mgsin⁡30∘+FT=mg\sin30^\circ+FT=mgsin30∘+F
  5. Substitute T=40T=40T=40, g=10g=10g=10:
    40=10msin⁡30∘+0.2(10mcos⁡30∘)40=10m\sin30^\circ+0.2(10m\cos30^\circ)40=10msin30∘+0.2(10mcos30∘)
    40=5m+1.7320508…m40=5m+1.7320508\ldots m40=5m+1.7320508…m

    m=406.7320508…=5.941725… kgm=\frac{40}{6.7320508\ldots}=5.941725\ldots\text{ kg}m=6.7320508…40​=5.941725… kg

m = 5.94 kg, to 3 significant figures.

Eliminate the tension

One smooth plane, one rough plane

Particle A of mass 2 kg rests on a smooth 10° plane. Particle B of mass 3 kg rests on a rough 20° plane. They are connected by a light, taut, inextensible string over a smooth pulley. Determine what can be concluded about B’s coefficient of friction.

  1. A’s plane is smooth. Resolve A up its plane:
    T=2gsin⁡10∘=20sin⁡10∘=3.4729636… NT=2g\sin10^\circ=20\sin10^\circ=3.4729636\ldots\text{ N}T=2gsin10∘=20sin10∘=3.4729636… N
  2. For B, perpendicular balance gives
    R=3gcos⁡20∘=30cos⁡20∘=28.1907786… NR=3g\cos20^\circ=30\cos20^\circ=28.1907786\ldots\text{ N}R=3gcos20∘=30cos20∘=28.1907786… N
  3. B’s weight component down its plane is larger than T, so the required friction acts up the plane:
    F=3gsin⁡20∘−TF=3g\sin20^\circ-TF=3gsin20∘−T
    F=30sin⁡20∘−20sin⁡10∘=6.7876407… NF=30\sin20^\circ-20\sin10^\circ=6.7876407\ldots\text{ N}F=30sin20∘−20sin10∘=6.7876407… N
  4. At rest, static friction only requires F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR:
    μ≥30sin⁡20∘−20sin⁡10∘30cos⁡20∘\mu\geq\frac{30\sin20^\circ-20\sin10^\circ}{30\cos20^\circ}μ≥30cos20∘30sin20∘−20sin10∘​
    30sin⁡20∘−20sin⁡10∘30cos⁡20∘=0.240775…\frac{30\sin20^\circ-20\sin10^\circ}{30\cos20^\circ}=0.240775\ldots30cos20∘30sin20∘−20sin10∘​=0.240775…

μ≥30sin⁡20∘−20sin⁡10∘30cos⁡20∘\mu\geq\frac{30\sin20^\circ-20\sin10^\circ}{30\cos20^\circ}μ≥30cos20∘30sin20∘−20sin10∘​. The boundary is approximately 0.241.

If B were additionally stated to be in limiting equilibrium, equality would give the exact boundary, approximately 0.241.

Watch the friction arrow

Impending up

Friction acts down the plane.

T=mgsin⁡θ+FT=mg\sin\theta+FT=mgsinθ+F

Impending down

Friction acts up the plane.

mgsin⁡θ=T+Fmg\sin\theta=T+Fmgsinθ=T+F

Friction is not automatically toward the pulley or down the plane. It opposes the impending motion.

Key ideas

  • Mark the possible motion before drawing friction.
  • Resolve perpendicular to the plane to find R.
  • Resolve along the plane and string to use T.
  • A light string with a smooth pulley has the same tension on both sides.
  • Use T=mgT=mgT=mg for a hanging particle with zero acceleration.
  • Rest gives F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR; limiting equilibrium gives equality.

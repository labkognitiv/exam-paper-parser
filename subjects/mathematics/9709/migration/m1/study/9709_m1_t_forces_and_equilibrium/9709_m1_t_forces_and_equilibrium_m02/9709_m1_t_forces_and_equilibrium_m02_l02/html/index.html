Multi-force equilibrium

Many forces.
Two balance equations.

When a particle stays in equilibrium, every horizontal push and every vertical push must balance. More forces do not create a new rule.

Start the lesson

Balance every component

  • A particle models an object as one point.
  • Coplanar forces act in the same flat plane.
  • Equilibrium means the resultant force is zero.
  • A component is the part of a force acting in one chosen direction.
  • A newton, written N, is the unit used to measure force.
  • A sloping force and its perpendicular components form a right-angled triangle.
  • Relative to a marked angle, the shorter side touching the angle is adjacent and uses cosine; the shorter side across from it is opposite and uses sine.
  • Horizontal means left-right; vertical means up-down. These directions are perpendicular, so they meet at 90°.
  • Choose right and up as positive. Left and down are then negative.
  • Resolve every sloping force before writing equations.

Two balance equations

∑Fx=0\sum F_x=0∑Fx​=0
∑Fy=0\sum F_y=0∑Fy​=0
  • The Greek capital letter Σ, read “sigma”, means add all terms.
  • FxF_xFx​ means a horizontal force component.
  • FyF_yFy​ means a vertical force component.
  • Equivalently: total right = total left, and total up = total down.
  1. Mark positive x- and y-directions.
  2. Resolve every sloping force.
  3. List all right and left components.
  4. List all up and down components.
  5. Write the two balance equations.
  6. Solve them together for the unknowns.

Tick off each force once in each direction. A force perpendicular to an axis contributes zero on that axis.

Worked example

Rebuild an unknown force

Four forces keep a particle in equilibrium. The balance equations give Pcos⁡θ=16.1P\cos\theta=16.1Pcosθ=16.1 and Psin⁡θ=38.8P\sin\theta=38.8Psinθ=38.8. Find PPP and θ\thetaθ.

  1. PPP is the hypotenuse, the side opposite the 90° angle.
  2. Pythagoras’ theorem says the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the two shorter sides.
  3. So P=16.12+38.82P=\sqrt{16.1^2+38.8^2}P=16.12+38.82​. Squaring means multiplying each component by itself; the square root reverses that operation.
  4. P=42.0 NP=42.0\text{ N}P=42.0 N to 3 significant figures.
  5. The tangent ratio is opposite ÷ adjacent. Here the angle is measured from +x, so tan⁡θ=38.8/16.1\tan\theta=38.8/16.1tanθ=38.8/16.1.
  6. Inverse tangent reverses tangent to find the angle: θ=67.5∘\theta=67.5^\circθ=67.5∘ to 3 significant figures.

Answer: P=42.0 N, θ=67.5∘P=42.0\text{ N},\ \theta=67.5^\circP=42.0 N, θ=67.5∘

Two unknown magnitudes

Solve simultaneous equations

Forces 10 N, FFF N, 2F2F2F N and GGG N hold a particle in equilibrium. GGG is 60° from the upward vertical; 2F2F2F is 40° above +x. Thus F acts left, G acts up-left, 2F acts up-right, and 10 N acts down.

  1. Vertical balance: Gcos⁡60∘+2Fsin⁡40∘=10G\cos60^\circ+2F\sin40^\circ=10Gcos60∘+2Fsin40∘=10.
  2. Horizontal balance: F+Gsin⁡60∘=2Fcos⁡40∘F+G\sin60^\circ=2F\cos40^\circF+Gsin60∘=2Fcos40∘.
  3. These are simultaneous equations: the same FFF and GGG must satisfy both.
  4. Keep full calculator values while solving.
  5. Rearrange the horizontal equation: Gsin⁡60∘=F(2cos⁡40∘−1)G\sin60^\circ=F(2\cos40^\circ-1)Gsin60∘=F(2cos40∘−1).

Angles measured from the vertical

  • Read where the angle starts before choosing sine or cosine.
  • From the vertical, the vertical component is adjacent: Tcos⁡θT\cos\thetaTcosθ.
  • The horizontal component is opposite: Tsin⁡θT\sin\thetaTsinθ.

Key ideas

  • Equilibrium gives exactly two independent balance equations in the plane.
  • Include every force or record its zero component.
  • Use exact trigonometric expressions until the final calculation.
  • Two unknown magnitudes usually require simultaneous equations.
  • Rebuild a force with Pythagoras; find its angle with an inverse trigonometric ratio.
  • Read whether an angle is measured from horizontal or vertical.

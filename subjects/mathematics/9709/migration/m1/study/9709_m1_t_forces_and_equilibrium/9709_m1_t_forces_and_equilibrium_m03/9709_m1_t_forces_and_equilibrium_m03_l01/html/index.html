Two balance equations.

A knot stays still while two sloping strings pull upward and a weight pulls down. Resolve every force at the knot, then use horizontal and vertical equilibrium together.

Start the lesson

Read the forces at the knot

  • A particle is a small object whose size is ignored in the model.
  • A string under tension pulls objects attached to its ends.
  • Tension acts along a string and pulls away from the particle.
  • Force is measured in newtons, written N.
  • Weight acts vertically downward.
  • Horizontal and vertical directions meet at a right angle of 90°.
  • A component is the part of a force along one chosen direction.
  • Relative to an angle in a right triangle, the adjacent side touches the angle and the opposite side does not.
  • Equilibrium means the resultant force is zero, so there is no acceleration.
  • At the knot, left and right components cancel. Upward components support the weight.

Angles measured above the horizontal

T1cos⁡α=T2cos⁡βT_1\cos\alpha=T_2\cos\betaT1​cosα=T2​cosβ
T1sin⁡α+T2sin⁡β=WT_1\sin\alpha+T_2\sin\beta=WT1​sinα+T2​sinβ=W
  • T₁ and T₂ are the two tension magnitudes.
  • α (alpha) and β (beta) are the angles above the horizontal.
  • Cosine gives the horizontal component because it lies next to the stated angle.
  • Sine gives the vertical component because it lies opposite the stated angle.

The two-equation method

Simultaneous equations are equations that must both be true for the same unknown values. We solve them together.

  1. Isolate the particle or knot and draw only the forces acting on it.
  2. Choose horizontal and vertical positive directions.
  3. Resolve each sloping tension into components.
  4. Write horizontal balance: left equals right.
  5. Write vertical balance: upward equals downward.
  6. Solve the two simultaneous equations, keeping extra calculator digits until the end.

Worked example

Angles measured from the horizontal

A particle of weight 24 N is held by two strings. The left string is at 50° to the horizontal and the right string is at 35°. Find both tensions.

Solution
  1. T1cos⁡50∘=T2cos⁡35∘T_1\cos50^\circ=T_2\cos35^\circT1​cos50∘=T2​cos35∘
  2. T1sin⁡50∘+T2sin⁡35∘=24T_1\sin50^\circ+T_2\sin35^\circ=24T1​sin50∘+T2​sin35∘=24
  3. T1=T2cos⁡35∘cos⁡50∘T_1=\frac{T_2\cos35^\circ}{\cos50^\circ}T1​=cos50∘T2​cos35∘​
  4. T2(cos⁡35∘sin⁡50∘cos⁡50∘+sin⁡35∘)=24T_2\left(\frac{\cos35^\circ\sin50^\circ}{\cos50^\circ}+\sin35^\circ\right)=24T2​(cos50∘cos35∘sin50∘​+sin35∘)=24
  5. T2=15.4858… NT_2=15.4858\ldots\text{ N}T2​=15.4858… N

Angles measured from the vertical

A particle of weight 30 N is held by two strings. The left string makes 25° with the vertical and the right string makes 40° with the vertical. Call the tensions A and B.

  • When an angle is measured from the vertical, the vertical component is adjacent and uses cosine.
  • The horizontal component is opposite and uses sine.
Solution
  1. Asin⁡25∘=Bsin⁡40∘A\sin25^\circ=B\sin40^\circAsin25∘=Bsin40∘

A = 21.3 N and B = 14.0 N, to 3 significant figures.

Exact direction ratios

  • A right-angled triangle has one 90° angle.
  • The hypotenuse is opposite the 90° angle and is the longest side.
  • A 3-4-5 triangle has horizontal, vertical and hypotenuse lengths 3, 4 and 5.
  • A 5-12-13 triangle has horizontal, vertical and hypotenuse lengths 5, 12 and 13.
  • Use side divided by hypotenuse. No angle calculation is needed.

Set up a 42 N case

The left string has 3-4-5 direction and tension A. The right string has 5-12-13 direction and tension B. The weight is 42 N.

Solution

An extra horizontal force

A particle of weight 18 N is held by two strings and a horizontal force of 10 N to the right. The left string is at 60° and the right string at 30° to the horizontal. Call the tensions A and B.

Solution

  1. The 10 N force has no vertical component. It appears only in horizontal balance.

    Acos⁡60∘=Bcos⁡30∘+10A\cos60^\circ=B\cos30^\circ+10Acos60∘=Bcos30∘+10
  2. Asin⁡60∘+Bsin⁡30∘=18A\sin60^\circ+B\sin30^\circ=18Asin60∘+Bsin30∘=18
  3. A=3B+20A=\sqrt3B+20A=3​B+20
  4. 32(3B+20)+12B=18\frac{\sqrt3}{2}(\sqrt3B+20)+\frac12B=1823​​(3​B+20)+21​B=18
  5. 2B+103=182B+10\sqrt3=182B+103​=18
  6. B=9−53=0.339746… NB=9-5\sqrt3=0.339746\ldots\text{ N}B=9−53​=0.339746… N
  7. A=5+93=20.5885… NA=5+9\sqrt3=20.5885\ldots\text{ N}A=5+93​=20.5885… N

Worked example

Tension from hanging weights

  • Light means the string's own mass is ignored.
  • Inextensible means the string does not stretch.
  • Smooth means there is no friction at the support.
  • For each light string over its smooth support, tension keeps the same size along that string.
  • The two strings are separate, so their tensions are 12 N and 16 N respectively, not equal to each other.

A left light string passes over a smooth support to a static 12 N hanging weight. A separate right light string passes over another smooth support to a static 16 N hanging weight.

Solution

  1. The left angle is 30° from the vertical. The right angle is α from the vertical.

    12sin⁡30∘=16sin⁡α12\sin30^\circ=16\sin\alpha12sin30∘=16sinα
  2. sin⁡α=616=0.375\sin\alpha=\frac{6}{16}=0.375sinα=166​=0.375
  3. α=22.0∘\alpha=22.0^\circα=22.0∘
  4. W=12cos⁡30∘+16cos⁡22.0∘W=12\cos30^\circ+16\cos22.0^\circW=12cos30∘+16cos22.0∘
  5. W=25.2 NW=25.2\text{ N}W=25.2 N

α = 22.0° and W = 25.2 N.

Worked example

Struts and symmetry

  • A strut is a rigid support that can push.
  • Symmetry means the left and right arrangements mirror each other.
  • In this setup, every other force is vertical and centred on the symmetry line. Therefore the two strut forces have equal magnitude.
  • The horizontal components are equal and opposite, so they cancel.

A 900 N load is supported by a vertical rope of tension 500 N and two identical struts at 45° to the vertical. Find the force S in each strut.

Solution
  1. 500+2Scos⁡45∘=900500+2S\cos45^\circ=900500+2Scos45∘=900
  2. 2Scos⁡45∘=4002S\cos45^\circ=4002Scos45∘=400
  3. S=4002cos⁡45∘=282.843… NS=\frac{400}{2\cos45^\circ}=282.843\ldots\text{ N}S=2cos45∘400​=282.843… N

Force in each strut = 283 N, to 3 significant figures.

Check the force directions first

Incorrect

Drawing tension toward the particle.

T1cos⁡α+T2cos⁡β=WT_1\cos\alpha+T_2\cos\beta=WT1​cosα+T2​cosβ=W

Correct

Tension pulls away from the particle along each string.

T1cos⁡α=T2cos⁡βT_1\cos\alpha=T_2\cos\betaT1​cosα=T2​cosβ

T1sin⁡α+T2sin⁡β=WT_1\sin\alpha+T_2\sin\beta=WT1​sinα+T2​sinβ=W

A force diagram decides the signs before any algebra starts.

Key ideas

  1. Draw only the forces acting at the particle or knot.
  2. Make every tension arrow pull away along its string.
  3. Choose components from the line the angle is measured from.
  4. Write one horizontal and one vertical balance equation.
  5. Use exact direction ratios when a right triangle is given.
  6. Solve simultaneously and round only the final tensions.
  7. Substitute the answers back into both balances.

Convert mass to weight first

  • Gravitational acceleration is written g. Here g = 9.8 m s⁻², read as 9.8 metres per second squared.
  • Weight is the force W = mg, so mass must be converted to weight before equilibrium equations are written.

A particle of mass 2.5 kg is held in equilibrium by two strings. The left string makes 35° with the horizontal and the right string 50°. Use g = 9.8 m s⁻² and find tensions A and B.

  • Mass, measured in kilograms (kg), describes how much matter the particle has.
  • g is gravitational acceleration. Here its stated value is 9.8 metres per second squared, written 9.8 m s⁻².
  • Weight is the force W = mg, so it is measured in newtons.
Solution
  1. W=mg=2.5(9.8)=24.5 NW=mg=2.5(9.8)=24.5\text{ N}W=mg=2.5(9.8)=24.5 N

  3. Asin⁡35∘+Bsin⁡50∘=24.5A\sin35^\circ+B\sin50^\circ=24.5Asin35∘+Bsin50∘=24.5
  4. A=Bcos⁡50∘cos⁡35∘A=\frac{B\cos50^\circ}{\cos35^\circ}A=cos35∘Bcos50∘​
  5. (Bcos⁡50∘cos⁡35∘)sin⁡35∘+Bsin⁡50∘=24.5\left(\frac{B\cos50^\circ}{\cos35^\circ}\right)\sin35^\circ+B\sin50^\circ=24.5(cos35∘Bcos50∘​)sin35∘+Bsin50∘=24.5

Forces and equilibrium · Module 3 · Lesson 2

Smooth rings, beads and pulleys

A small ring can be pulled by several string sections at once. Draw the forces on the ring, use the equal-tension rule only when its conditions hold, then balance horizontal and vertical components.

Key ideasWorked examplesRecap

Start with the object and its forces

  • A particle is modelled as a single point. A small ring or bead is often treated as a particle.
  • A string can pull but cannot push. Its pull is called tension, written TTT.
  • A light string has negligible mass.
  • A smooth contact has no friction.
  • The tension is the same through one continuous light string when the string passes smoothly through a ring or over a smooth pulley.
  • Two separate strings do not automatically have the same tension.
  • Tension pulls the chosen ring outward, along each string section.
  • A pulley changes the direction of a string. For one light string over a smooth pulley, both sections have the same tension.
  • Weight is W=mgW=mgW=mg, vertically downward. Here mmm is mass in kilograms and ggg is gravitational acceleration in m s−2\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}ms−2, read as metres per second squared.
  • N\mathrm NN means newton, the unit of force.

Contact with a smooth wire

  • A wire can push a bead or ring with a normal reaction, written RRR.
  • The tangent is the straight direction of the wire at the contact point.
  • The reaction is perpendicular to that tangent. Perpendicular directions meet at exactly 90∘90^\circ90∘.
  • The reaction is a different force from tension.

Resolve the forces

  • Equilibrium means the total force is zero.
  • Choose horizontal and vertical axes. Right and up can be positive.
  • A component is the part of a force in one chosen direction.
  • The hypotenuse is the longest side. It is opposite the right angle.
  • The adjacent side touches the chosen angle and is not the hypotenuse.
  • The opposite side faces the chosen angle.
  • For a right-angled triangle, cos⁡θ=adjacenthypotenuse\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}cosθ=hypotenuseadjacent​ and sin⁡θ=oppositehypotenuse\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinθ=hypotenuseopposite​.
  • The force is the hypotenuse. If its angle is measured from the horizontal, the horizontal component uses cosine and the vertical component uses sine.
  • If its angle is measured from the vertical, those choices swap.
  1. Choose the ring or bead.
  2. Draw only the forces acting on it.
  3. Mark the same TTT only on sections of the same light string through a smooth contact.
  4. Balance horizontal components.
  5. Balance vertical components and solve the equations together.

Worked example

A ring held by one light string

The same light string leaves a smooth ring on both sides. Its sections make 30∘30^\circ30∘ and 40∘40^\circ40∘ with the vertical. A horizontal force of 2N2\mathrm N2N acts left. The ring supports a mass mmm and is in equilibrium. Find the tension, then find the mass when g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2.

  1. Both string sections have tension TTT.
  2. Balance horizontal components. Since the angles are measured from the vertical, the horizontal parts use sine:
    Tsin⁡40∘=Tsin⁡30∘+2T\sin40^\circ=T\sin30^\circ+2Tsin40∘=Tsin30∘+2
  3. Collect the two tension terms:
    T(sin⁡40∘−sin⁡30∘)=2T(\sin40^\circ-\sin30^\circ)=2T(sin40∘−sin30∘)=2
    T=14.0068176…NT=14.0068176\ldots\mathrm NT=14.0068176…N
  4. Balance vertical components. Both tension components act upward:
    mg=Tcos⁡30∘+Tcos⁡40∘mg=T\cos30^\circ+T\cos40^\circmg=Tcos30∘+Tcos40∘
  5. Keep the full value of TTT until the end:
    m=14.0068176…(cos⁡30∘+cos⁡40∘)10=2.28601046…m=\frac{14.0068176\ldots(\cos30^\circ+\cos40^\circ)}{10}=2.28601046\ldotsm=1014.0068176…(cos30∘+cos40∘)​=2.28601046…

Answers: T=14.0N\mathbf{T=14.0\mathrm N}T=14.0N and m=2.29 kg\mathbf{m=2.29\mathrm{\,kg}}m=2.29kg.

Keep T unrounded until the final step to obtain 2.29 kg. The 2 N force remains in the full force diagram even though it has no vertical component.

Find the applied horizontal force

A smooth ring is threaded on one light string. The string sections rise at 70∘70^\circ70∘ to the left and 45∘45^\circ45∘ to the right, measured from the horizontal. The ring has weight 2N2\mathrm N2N. Find the tension and the horizontal force needed for equilibrium.

  1. Balance vertical components:
    Tsin⁡70∘+Tsin⁡45∘=2T\sin70^\circ+T\sin45^\circ=2Tsin70∘+Tsin45∘=2
    T=2sin⁡70∘+sin⁡45∘=1.214477…NT=\frac2{\sin70^\circ+\sin45^\circ}=1.214477\ldots\mathrm NT=sin70∘+sin45∘2​=1.214477…N
  2. The rightward tension component is larger than the leftward one. The applied force must therefore act left.
  3. Balance horizontally:
    Tcos⁡45∘=Tcos⁡70∘+PT\cos45^\circ=T\cos70^\circ+PTcos45∘=Tcos70∘+P
    P=T(cos⁡45∘−cos⁡70∘)=0.443389…NP=T(\cos45^\circ-\cos70^\circ)=0.443389\ldots\mathrm NP=T(cos45∘−cos70∘)=0.443389…N

Answers: T=1.21N\mathbf{T=1.21\mathrm N}T=1.21N; P=0.443N left\mathbf{P=0.443\mathrm N\text{ left}}P=0.443N left.

Could P act right? Both it and the larger rightward tension component would reinforce the imbalance. Equilibrium requires P left. Keep T unrounded until the final step to obtain 0.443 N.

Tension and normal reaction

A ring rests in equilibrium against a smooth curved wire. Tension acts up-left at 25∘25^\circ25∘ above the horizontal. The normal reaction acts up-right at 50∘50^\circ50∘ above the horizontal. The weight is 40N40\mathrm N40N. Find TTT and RRR.

  1. Balance horizontal components:
    Tcos⁡25∘=Rcos⁡50∘T\cos25^\circ=R\cos50^\circTcos25∘=Rcos50∘
  2. Rearrange:
    T=Rcos⁡50∘cos⁡25∘T=\frac{R\cos50^\circ}{\cos25^\circ}T=cos25∘Rcos50∘​
  3. Balance vertical components:
    Tsin⁡25∘+Rsin⁡50∘=40T\sin25^\circ+R\sin50^\circ=40Tsin25∘+Rsin50∘=40
  4. Substitute for TTT:
    R(cos⁡50∘sin⁡25∘cos⁡25∘+sin⁡50∘)=40R\left(\frac{\cos50^\circ\sin25^\circ}{\cos25^\circ}+\sin50^\circ\right)=40R(cos25∘cos50∘sin25∘​+sin50∘)=40
    R=37.5312…N,T=26.6185…NR=37.5312\ldots\mathrm N,\qquad T=26.6185\ldots\mathrm NR=37.5312…N,T=26.6185…N

Answers: T=26.6N\mathbf{T=26.6\mathrm N}T=26.6N and R=37.5N\mathbf{R=37.5\mathrm N}R=37.5N.

Find the tension and applied force

A smooth ring of weight 15N15\mathrm N15N is threaded on one light string. The sections rise at 20∘20^\circ20∘ to the left and 50∘50^\circ50∘ to the right, measured from the horizontal. A horizontal force PPP acts right. Find TTT and PPP.

  1. Balance vertically:
    Tsin⁡20∘+Tsin⁡50∘=15T\sin20^\circ+T\sin50^\circ=15Tsin20∘+Tsin50∘=15
    T=15sin⁡20∘+sin⁡50∘=13.537117…NT=\frac{15}{\sin20^\circ+\sin50^\circ}=13.537117\ldots\mathrm NT=sin20∘+sin50∘15​=13.537117…N
  2. Balance horizontally:
    P+Tcos⁡50∘=Tcos⁡20∘P+T\cos50^\circ=T\cos20^\circP+Tcos50∘=Tcos20∘
    P=T(cos⁡20∘−cos⁡50∘)=4.019238…NP=T(\cos20^\circ-\cos50^\circ)=4.019238\ldots\mathrm NP=T(cos20∘−cos50∘)=4.019238…N

Answers: T=13.5N\mathbf{T=13.5\mathrm N}T=13.5N and P=4.02N right\mathbf{P=4.02\mathrm N\text{ right}}P=4.02N right.

Keep the tension unrounded until the final step to obtain 4.02 N.

Key ideas recap

  • Tension pulls away from the ring along the string.
  • One continuous light string through a smooth ring or over a smooth pulley has equal tension throughout.
  • A smooth wire's reaction is perpendicular to its local tangent.
  • Choose sine or cosine from the stated angle reference.
  • For equilibrium, horizontal components balance and vertical components balance.
  • Keep full calculator values until the final answer.

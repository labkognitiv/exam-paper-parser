Angled friction

Resolve first.
Then use friction.

An angled force changes the horizontal balance and the normal reaction at the same time. Find both components before using the friction law.

Start the lesson

What changes?

  • Mass is measured in kilograms, written kg. The letter m represents mass.
  • A force is a push or pull. It is measured in newtons, written N.
  • The letter g represents gravitational acceleration. The unit m s−2\text{m s}^{-2}m s−2 is read “metres per second squared”.
  • Weight is the force of gravity: W=mgW=mgW=mg.
  • A particle models an object as a point mass.
  • A rough contact can exert friction.
  • Two perpendicular directions meet at a right angle of 90°.
  • An angle measures a turn. The Greek letter θ\thetaθ is read “theta”. Here it measures the angle from the horizontal.
  • The letter P represents the magnitude of the applied force.
  • In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is the longest side and is opposite the 90° angle.
  • The adjacent side touches θ\thetaθ. The opposite side does not.
  • cos⁡θ=adjacenthypotenuse\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}cosθ=hypotenuseadjacent​ and sin⁡θ=oppositehypotenuse\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinθ=hypotenuseopposite​.
  • A component is the part of a force in one chosen direction.
  • To resolve a force means to split it into perpendicular components.
  • The normal reaction has magnitude R and acts perpendicular to the contact surface.
  • Friction acts along the contact surface and opposes motion or impending motion. Its magnitude is F.
  • The coefficient of friction is the constant μ\muμ in the friction model.
  • Equilibrium means zero resultant force. Horizontal forces balance and vertical forces balance.
  • Constant speed in a straight line means zero acceleration, so the forces also balance.
  • At the point of sliding, motion is about to begin and friction has reached its limiting value.

Choose the correct friction rule

0≤F≤μR0\leq F\leq \mu R0≤F≤μR

For static contact, friction adjusts up to μR\mu RμR. Equality holds at limiting equilibrium. For a moving object, use F=μRF=\mu RF=μR only when the stated sliding-friction model specifies it.

0.8 kg particle, 5 N upward pull

A 0.8 kg particle rests on a rough horizontal table. A 5 N force acts at angle θ\thetaθ above the horizontal, where tan⁡θ=3/4\tan\theta=3/4tanθ=3/4. The particle is on the point of sliding. Take g=10 m s−2g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}g=10 m s−2. Find μ\muμ.

  1. Since tan⁡θ=3/4\tan\theta=3/4tanθ=3/4, choose opposite side 3 and adjacent side 4.
  2. Pythagoras says the hypotenuse squared equals the sum of the other two sides squared. A square multiplies a number by itself; the positive square root reverses this. Use it to find the hypotenuse:
    32+42=25=5\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=\sqrt{25}=532+42​=25​=5
  3. Now form the exact ratios:
    cos⁡θ=45,sin⁡θ=35\cos\theta=\frac45,\qquad \sin\theta=\frac35cosθ=54​,sinθ=53​
  4. Horizontal balance gives the friction magnitude:
    F=5cos⁡θ=5(45)=4 NF=5\cos\theta=5\left(\frac45\right)=4\text{ N}F=5cosθ=5(54​)=4 N
  5. The weight is mg=0.8(10)=8 Nmg=0.8(10)=8\text{ N}mg=0.8(10)=8 N. Vertical balance gives
    R+5sin⁡θ=8R+5\sin\theta=8R+5sinθ=8
    R+5(35)=8⇒R=5 NR+5\left(\frac35\right)=8\quad\Rightarrow\quad R=5\text{ N}R+5(53​)=8⇒R=5 N
  6. At the point of sliding, friction is limiting:
    F=μR⇒4=5μF=\mu R\quad\Rightarrow\quad 4=5\muF=μR⇒4=5μ

μ=0.8\mu=0.8μ=0.8

A downward push

Find the mass

A block is pushed to the right along a rough horizontal floor at constant speed. The force is 50 N at 20° below the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.3. Use the specified sliding-friction model F=μRF=\mu RF=μR and take g=10 m s−2g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}g=10 m s−2. Find the mass m.

  1. Horizontal balance:
    F=50cos⁡20∘F=50\cos20^\circF=50cos20∘
  2. The downward component adds to the weight:
    R=mg+50sin⁡20∘R=mg+50\sin20^\circR=mg+50sin20∘
  3. Apply the stated sliding model:
    50cos⁡20∘=0.3(mg+50sin⁡20∘)50\cos20^\circ=0.3\left(mg+50\sin20^\circ\right)50cos20∘=0.3(mg+50sin20∘)
  4. Divide both sides by 0.3:
    50cos⁡20∘0.3=mg+50sin⁡20∘\frac{50\cos20^\circ}{0.3}=mg+50\sin20^\circ0.350cos20∘​=mg+50sin20∘
  5. Subtract 50sin⁡20∘50\sin20^\circ50sin20∘:
    mg=50cos⁡20∘0.3−50sin⁡20∘mg=\frac{50\cos20^\circ}{0.3}-50\sin20^\circmg=0.350cos20∘​−50sin20∘
  6. Divide by g=10g=10g=10:
    m=50cos⁡20∘/0.3−50sin⁡20∘10m=\frac{50\cos20^\circ/0.3-50\sin20^\circ}{10}m=1050cos20∘/0.3−50sin20∘​
    m=13.9514429… kgm=13.9514429\ldots\text{ kg}m=13.9514429… kg

m = 14.0 kg, to 3 significant figures.

Rings on rough rods

  • Tension is a pulling force along a taut string.
  • A ring is threaded around a rod.
  • The rod supplies contact forces.
  • Friction acts along the rod.
  • The normal reaction acts perpendicular to the rod.
  • R denotes the non-negative magnitude of the normal reaction.
  • If an assumed direction gives a negative signed result, reverse the reaction arrow and use its positive magnitude.
  • For the upward reaction shown, R+Tsin⁡θ=mgR+T\sin\theta=mgR+Tsinθ=mg.
  • Along the rod, F=Tcos⁡θF=T\cos\thetaF=Tcosθ when friction balances a rightward pull.

A greatest or least allowable force often places the contact at limiting equilibrium. Decide the impending direction first, then draw friction opposite it.

Symbolic greatest pull

A crate of mass m is pulled by a force X at angle α\alphaα above the horizontal, where 0≤α<90∘0\leq\alpha<90^\circ0≤α<90∘. It is about to move to the right. Find X in terms of m,μ,g,αm,\mu,g,\alpham,μ,g,α.

  1. Impending motion means motion is about to begin. Friction is limiting and acts left:
    F=μRF=\mu RF=μR
  2. Vertical balance:
    R=mg−Xsin⁡αR=mg-X\sin\alphaR=mg−Xsinα
  3. Horizontal balance:
    Xcos⁡α=FX\cos\alpha=FXcosα=F
  4. Substitute R:
    Xcos⁡α=μ(mg−Xsin⁡α)X\cos\alpha=\mu\left(mg-X\sin\alpha\right)Xcosα=μ(mg−Xsinα)
  5. Collect the X terms:
    X(cos⁡α+μsin⁡α)=μmgX\left(\cos\alpha+\mu\sin\alpha\right)=\mu mgX(cosα+μsinα)=μmg
    X=μmgcos⁡α+μsin⁡αX=\frac{\mu mg}{\cos\alpha+\mu\sin\alpha}X=cosα+μsinαμmg​

The contact condition must also hold: R=mg−Xsin⁡α≥0R=mg-X\sin\alpha\geq0R=mg−Xsinα≥0.

Key ideas

  • Resolve the angled force before writing balance equations.
  • An upward component usually reduces R; a downward component increases R.
  • On a horizontal rod, friction is horizontal and the normal reaction is vertical.
  • Choose friction direction from the actual or impending motion.
  • Use F=μRF=\mu RF=μR only at a stated limiting condition or under a stated sliding-friction model.

6 kg block at constant speed

A 6 kg block is pulled to the right at constant speed by a 20 N force at 30° above the horizontal. Take g=10 m s−2g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}g=10 m s−2. Under the specified sliding-friction model, find μ\muμ.

  1. Vertical balance:
    R+20sin⁡30∘=60R+20\sin30^\circ=60R+20sin30∘=60
    R=60−10=50 NR=60-10=50\text{ N}R=60−10=50 N
  2. Horizontal balance:
    F=20cos⁡30∘=103=17.320508… NF=20\cos30^\circ=10\sqrt3=17.320508\ldots\text{ N}F=20cos30∘=103​=17.320508… N
  3. Use the stated sliding model:
    μ=FR=10350=0.346410…\mu=\frac FR=\frac{10\sqrt3}{50}=0.346410\ldotsμ=RF​=50103​​=0.346410…

μ=0.346\mu=0.346μ=0.346 to 3 significant figures.

0.4 kg ring, two parts

A ring of mass 0.4 kg is threaded on a rough horizontal rod. A string pulls it with tension T at 30° above the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.5, and the ring is on the point of moving right. Take g=10 m s−2g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}g=10 m s−2.

  1. The weight is 0.4g=4 N0.4g=4\text{ N}0.4g=4 N. Perpendicular balance gives
    R+Tsin⁡30∘=4R+T\sin30^\circ=4R+Tsin30∘=4
    R=4−12TR=4-\frac12TR=4−21​T
  2. Motion is impending to the right, so limiting friction acts left:
    F=μR=12RF=\mu R=\frac12RF=μR=21​R
  3. Balance along the rod:
    Tcos⁡30∘=12(4−12T)T\cos30^\circ=\frac12\left(4-\frac12T\right)Tcos30∘=21​(4−21​T)
  4. Collect T terms:
    T(32+14)=2T\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}+\frac14\right)=2T(23​​+41​)=2
    T=23/2+1/4=823+1T=\frac{2}{\sqrt3/2+1/4}=\frac{8}{2\sqrt3+1}T=3​/2+1/42​=23​+18​
    T=1.7920739… NT=1.7920739\ldots\text{ N}T=1.7920739… N

R = 4 - 0.5T and T = 1.79 N, to 3 significant figures.

The force picture

  • A particle models an object as one point.
  • A smooth plane exerts no friction force.
  • A component is the part of a force acting in one chosen direction.
  • An axis is a chosen direction used to measure components.
  • Equilibrium means the resultant force is zero, so forces balance along each axis.
  • Weight WWW acts vertically downward. In W=mgW=mgW=mg, mmm is mass in kilograms, kg, and ggg is gravitational acceleration.
  • Here g=10 m s−2g=10\ \mathrm{{m\,s^{{-2}}}}g=10 ms−2, read “10 metres per second squared”. Force is measured in newtons, N.
  • The normal reaction RRR acts at 90° to the plane. A right angle is 90°.
  • The line of greatest slope points straight up or down the steepest path on the plane.

Resolve the weight

The weight and its two perpendicular components form a right-angled triangle. The weight is the hypotenuse, opposite the 90° angle.

Relative to angle α\alphaα, the side across from it is opposite, so sin⁡α=opposite/hypotenuse\sin\alpha=\text{{opposite}}/\text{{hypotenuse}}sinα=opposite/hypotenuse. The shorter side touching it is adjacent, so cos⁡α=adjacent/hypotenuse\cos\alpha=\text{{adjacent}}/\text{{hypotenuse}}cosα=adjacent/hypotenuse.

The vertical and horizontal reference lines are perpendicular. The slope and its normal are also perpendicular. Rotating both reference lines through the incline angle keeps the angle equal to α\alphaα; therefore the angle between downward weight and the into-plane normal is α\alphaα.

Wsin⁡αacts down the planeW\sin\alpha\quad\text{{acts down the plane}}Wsinαacts down the plane
Wcos⁡αacts into the planeW\cos\alpha\quad\text{{acts into the plane}}Wcosαacts into the plane

The dashed component arrows describe parts of the weight. They are not extra real forces.

An applied force angled to the slope

Component arrows are calculation aids, not additional forces.

Let a force PPP act at angle θ\thetaθ to the slope. Its adjacent component is Pcos⁡θP\cos\thetaPcosθ along the slope. Its opposite component is Psin⁡θP\sin\thetaPsinθ perpendicular to the slope.

  • If PPP pulls above the slope, Psin⁡θP\sin\thetaPsinθ acts away from the plane.
  • If PPP pushes below the slope, Psin⁡θP\sin\thetaPsinθ acts into the plane.
  1. Draw only the real forces: weight, reaction and applied force.
  2. Choose axes along and perpendicular to the plane.
  3. Resolve the weight and applied force.
  4. Use the parallel equation first if θ\thetaθ is unknown.
  5. Use the normal equation, keeping the applied force's actual direction.

Worked example: test whether contact is possible

An 8 kg particle and a 100 N pull

An 8 kg particle rests on a smooth plane inclined at 30°. A 100 N force acts at angle θ\thetaθ above the line of greatest slope. Take g=10g=10g=10.

  1. Along the plane, equilibrium would require 100cos⁡θ=80sin⁡30∘100\cos\theta=80\sin30^\circ100cosθ=80sin30∘.
  2. 100cos⁡θ=40100\cos\theta=40100cosθ=40, so cos⁡θ=0.4\cos\theta=0.4cosθ=0.4.
  3. Inverse cosine is the calculator operation that returns an angle from a known cosine ratio.
  4. So θ=cos⁡−1(0.4)=66.4218…∘\theta=\cos^{{-1}}(0.4)=66.4218\ldots^\circθ=cos−1(0.4)=66.4218…∘.
  5. Because the force is above the slope, its normal component pulls away from the plane.
  6. Normal balance would require R+100sin⁡θ=80cos⁡30∘R+100\sin\theta=80\cos30^\circR+100sinθ=80cos30∘.
  7. R=80cos⁡30∘−100sin⁡(66.4218…∘)=−22.3695… NR=80\cos30^\circ-100\sin(66.4218\ldots^\circ)=-22.3695\ldots\ \mathrm{{N}}R=80cos30∘−100sin(66.4218…∘)=−22.3695… N.
  • A contact force cannot pull the particle into the plane, so RRR cannot be negative.
  • The calculated angle balances the along-slope components.
  • The stated resting contact is physically impossible. The particle would lose contact with the plane.

Conclusion: θ=66.4∘\theta=66.4^\circθ=66.4∘ would require R=−22.4 NR=-22.4\ \mathrm{{N}}R=−22.4 N; no contact equilibrium exists.

Direction changes the normal equation

Keep the same mass, slope, 100 N magnitude and θ=66.4218…∘\theta=66.4218\ldots^\circθ=66.4218…∘. If the 100 N force instead pushed at that angle below the slope, its normal component would act into the plane.

  1. The conditional normal equation is R=80cos⁡30∘+100sin⁡θR=80\cos30^\circ+100\sin\thetaR=80cos30∘+100sinθ.
  2. R=69.2820…+91.6515…R=69.2820\ldots+91.6515\ldotsR=69.2820…+91.6515….
  3. R=160.9335… N=161 NR=160.9335\ldots\ \mathrm{{N}}=161\ \mathrm{{N}}R=160.9335… N=161 N to 3 significant figures.

This is a different force direction. It does not alter the original above-slope setup.

Conditional below-slope push: R=161 NR=161\ \mathrm{{N}}R=161 N.

Key ideas

  • Use axes along and perpendicular to the plane.
  • Weight gives Wsin⁡αW\sin\alphaWsinα down the slope and Wcos⁡αW\cos\alphaWcosα into it.
  • An above-slope pull reduces the required reaction; a below-slope push increases it.
  • A negative calculated reaction means contact cannot be maintained.
  • Keep full precision until the final rounding.

Forces and equilibrium · Module 5 · Lesson 2

One limiting case on a rough inclined plane

A rough slope can stop a block from sliding. First decide which way the block is about to move. Friction acts the other way, and reaches its maximum only at the limiting point.

Key ideasWorked examplesRecap

Build the force picture

  • An inclined plane is a sloping surface. Its angle to the horizontal is α\alphaα.
  • A rough plane can exert friction along its surface.
  • The normal reaction RRR acts perpendicular to the plane. Perpendicular directions meet at 90∘90^\circ90∘.
  • Weight is W=mgW=mgW=mg, vertically down. Mass mmm is in kilograms.
  • ggg is gravitational acceleration in m s−2\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}ms−2, read as metres per second squared.
  • N\mathrm NN means newton, the unit of force.
  • Equilibrium means the total force on the object is zero.
  • An axis is a chosen reference line used to measure components and directions.

Choose axes along the slope

  • The line of greatest slope is the straight direction directly up or down the plane.
  • To resolve a force means to replace it with components in chosen directions.
  • One axis runs parallel to the plane. The other is perpendicular to it.
  • The plane is tilted by α\alphaα from the horizontal. Its inward normal is tilted by the same α\alphaα from the vertical, because both reference directions rotate through the same angle.
  • Therefore, the angle between the vertical weight and its into-plane component is also α\alphaα.
  • The weight and its components form a right-angled triangle.
  • The weight is the hypotenuse, the longest side opposite the right angle.
  • The side beside α\alphaα is the adjacent side. It is one of the two shorter, non-hypotenuse sides. The side facing α\alphaα is the opposite side.
  • tan⁡α=oppositeadjacent\tan\alpha=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}tanα=adjacentopposite​.
  • Pythagoras gives c=a2+b2c=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}c=a2+b2​ for a right triangle. A square such as a2a^2a2 means a×aa\times aa×a. The square root reverses squaring.
  • The inverse-tangent key finds an angle from a ratio: α=tan⁡−1 ⁣(oppositeadjacent)\alpha=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\right)α=tan−1(adjacentopposite​).
  • cos⁡α=adjacenthypotenuse\cos\alpha=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}cosα=hypotenuseadjacent​ and sin⁡α=oppositehypotenuse\sin\alpha=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinα=hypotenuseopposite​.
  • So the weight component down the plane is Wsin⁡αW\sin\alphaWsinα. The component into the plane is Wcos⁡αW\cos\alphaWcosα.

Friction: equality or inequality?

  • Impending motion is the direction the block would move next if friction could not hold it.
  • Friction acts opposite impending motion, or opposite actual motion when sliding.
  • The coefficient has no unit.
  • The coefficient of friction μ\muμ is a number that describes the roughness of the contact.
  • Limiting equilibrium means the block is still at rest but is on the point of moving. Then friction has its maximum magnitude: F=μRF=\mu RF=μR.
  • If the block is simply at rest, friction adjusts to the value required, up to that maximum: F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR.
  • An applied pull above the plane reduces RRR. This reduces the maximum possible friction μR\mu RμR; actual friction still equals only the amount required for equilibrium.
  1. Decide the impending motion.
  2. Draw friction in the opposite direction.
  3. Resolve perpendicular to the plane to find RRR.
  4. Resolve along the plane to find the required friction.
  5. Use F=μRF=\mu RF=μR only at the limiting point. Otherwise use F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR.

Worked examples

Worked example

Just prevent sliding down

A 3 kg block rests on a rough plane inclined at 60∘60^\circ60∘. A 15 N force acts up the slope. The block is just prevented from sliding down. Take g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2. Find RRR, the friction and μ\muμ.

  1. Without friction, the block would move down. Limiting friction acts up the plane.
  2. Perpendicular to the plane:
    R=3gcos⁡60∘=3(10)(12)=15NR=3g\cos60^\circ=3(10)\left(\frac12\right)=15\mathrm NR=3gcos60∘=3(10)(21​)=15N
  3. Along the plane, the two upward forces balance the weight component:
    15+F=3gsin⁡60∘15+F=3g\sin60^\circ15+F=3gsin60∘
    F=30(32)−15=10.980762…NF=30\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)-15=10.980762\ldots\mathrm NF=30(23​​)−15=10.980762…N
  4. The block is at the limiting point, so F=μRF=\mu RF=μR:
    μ=FR=153−1515=3−1=0.7320508…\mu=\frac{F}{R}=\frac{15\sqrt3-15}{15}=\sqrt3-1=0.7320508\ldotsμ=RF​=15153​−15​=3​−1=0.7320508…

Answers: R=15N\mathbf{R=15\mathrm N}R=15N, F=11.0N\mathbf{F=11.0\mathrm N}F=11.0N, μ=0.732\mathbf{\mu=0.732}μ=0.732.

Just about to move up

A 1.3 kg particle rests on a rough plane where tan⁡θ=125\tan\theta=\frac{12}{5}tanθ=512​. A 20 N force acts up the line of greatest slope. The particle is on the point of moving up. Find μ\muμ, taking g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2.

  1. The relation tan⁡θ=125\tan\theta=\frac{12}{5}tanθ=512​ says opposite ÷ adjacent is 12 ÷ 5. Choose an opposite side of 12 and an adjacent side of 5.
  2. Use Pythagoras to find the hypotenuse:
    52+122=25+144=169=13\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=\sqrt{25+144}=\sqrt{169}=1352+122​=25+144​=169​=13
  3. Now sin⁡θ=1213\sin\theta=\frac{12}{13}sinθ=1312​ and cos⁡θ=513\cos\theta=\frac5{13}cosθ=135​.
  4. Inverse tangent gives the plane angle:
    θ=tan⁡−1 ⁣(125)=67.3801…∘\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{12}{5}\right)=67.3801\ldots^\circθ=tan−1(512​)=67.3801…∘

  6. Perpendicular to the plane:
    R=1.3gcos⁡θ=13(513)=5NR=1.3g\cos\theta=13\left(\frac5{13}\right)=5\mathrm NR=1.3gcosθ=13(135​)=5N
  7. Along the plane:
    20=13sin⁡θ+F=12+F20=13\sin\theta+F=12+F20=13sinθ+F=12+F
    F=8NF=8\mathrm NF=8N
  8. At the limiting point:
    μ=FR=85=1.6\mu=\frac FR=\frac85=1.6μ=RF​=58​=1.6

An angled pull changes the reaction

A 12 kg block rests on a rough plane inclined at 24∘24^\circ24∘. A string pulls with 65 N at 36∘36^\circ36∘ above the plane. The block is on the point of sliding up. Find RRR, the friction and μ\muμ, taking g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2.

  1. Impending motion is up, so limiting friction acts down.
  2. The pull has component 65sin⁡36∘65\sin36^\circ65sin36∘ away from the plane. Perpendicular balance gives:
    R+65sin⁡36∘=120cos⁡24∘R+65\sin36^\circ=120\cos24^\circR+65sin36∘=120cos24∘
    R=120cos⁡24∘−65sin⁡36∘=71.4194135…NR=120\cos24^\circ-65\sin36^\circ=71.4194135\ldots\mathrm NR=120cos24∘−65sin36∘=71.4194135…N

    65cos⁡36∘=120sin⁡24∘+F65\cos36^\circ=120\sin24^\circ+F65cos36∘=120sin24∘+F
    F=65cos⁡36∘−120sin⁡24∘=3.7777075…NF=65\cos36^\circ-120\sin24^\circ=3.7777075\ldots\mathrm NF=65cos36∘−120sin24∘=3.7777075…N

    μ=FR=3.7777075…71.4194135…=0.0528947…\mu=\frac FR=\frac{3.7777075\ldots}{71.4194135\ldots}=0.0528947\ldotsμ=RF​=71.4194135…3.7777075…​=0.0528947…

Worked example

Enough friction for rest

A particle needs 3 N of friction to remain at rest. The normal reaction is 4 N. Find the condition on μ\muμ.

  1. This is a rest condition, so compare the required friction with the maximum:
    F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR
  2. Substitute the required friction and reaction:
    3≤4μ3\leq4\mu3≤4μ
  3. Divide by 4:
    μ≥34=0.75\mu\geq\frac34=0.75μ≥43​=0.75

Condition: μ≥0.75\mathbf{\mu\geq0.75}μ≥0.75.

  • If μ=0.75\mu=0.75μ=0.75, the particle is at limiting equilibrium.
  • If μ>0.75\mu>0.75μ>0.75, it can rest with friction below its maximum.
  • If μ<0.75\mu<0.75μ<0.75, there is not enough friction.

Worked example

Least coefficient with no applied force

A 4 kg block rests on a rough plane inclined at 30∘30^\circ30∘. There is no applied force. Find the least value of μ\muμ for equilibrium, taking g=10 m s−2g=10\mathrm{\,m\,s^{-2}}g=10ms−2.

  1. The block tends to slide down, so friction acts up.
  2. Perpendicular to the plane:
    R=40cos⁡30∘=203NR=40\cos30^\circ=20\sqrt3\mathrm NR=40cos30∘=203​N
  3. The required friction is the weight component down the plane:
    F=40sin⁡30∘=20NF=40\sin30^\circ=20\mathrm NF=40sin30∘=20N
  4. For equilibrium, required friction must not exceed the maximum:
    20≤μ(203)20\leq\mu(20\sqrt3)20≤μ(203​)
    μ≥13=0.577350…\mu\geq\frac1{\sqrt3}=0.577350\ldotsμ≥3​1​=0.577350…

Least value: μ=0.577\mathbf{\mu=0.577}μ=0.577.

Key ideas recap

  • Friction opposes impending or actual motion.
  • Resolve perpendicular to the plane first to find RRR.
  • Resolve along the plane to find the friction required.
  • Use F=μRF=\mu RF=μR only when motion is impending.
  • Use F≤μRF\leq\mu RF≤μR for a block that is simply at rest.
  • A pull above the plane lowers the normal reaction and the maximum possible friction.
  • Keep full calculator values until the final rounding.

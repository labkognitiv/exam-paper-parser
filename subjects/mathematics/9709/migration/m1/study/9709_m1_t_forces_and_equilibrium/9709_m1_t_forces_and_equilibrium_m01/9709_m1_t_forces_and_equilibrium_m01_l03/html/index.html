Constrained resultants

A direction condition tells you which component must cancel. Learn to translate the words, choose useful axes and solve only what the question needs.

Key ideasWorked examplesRecap

A direction changes the equation

  • A resultant is the single force with the same overall effect as all the forces together.
  • A component is the part of a force measured in one chosen direction.
  • An axis is a chosen reference line. The horizontal axis is x and the vertical axis is y.
  • Parallel lines point in the same direction. Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle of 90°.
  • XXX is the total horizontal component. YYY is the total vertical component.
  • If the resultant lies along the horizontal axis, its vertical component is zero.
  • If the resultant is perpendicular to a force or line, its component along that force or line is zero.
  • Equilibrium means the resultant is zero, so both component totals are zero.
  • An equilibrant has the same magnitude as the old resultant and the opposite direction.
  • The magnitude of a force is its size, without direction.
  • The letter RRR often labels the resultant.
  • N\text{N}N means newton, the unit used to measure force.

Read the direction condition before choosing the zero component.

Translate the constraint

  • Resultant along the x-axis: Y=0Y=0Y=0.
  • Resultant has no x-component: X=0X=0X=0.
  • Resultant perpendicular to a line lll: component along lll is zero.
  • Resultant in the direction of a given force: component perpendicular to that direction is zero.
  • Equilibrium: X=0X=0X=0 and Y=0Y=0Y=0.

Resolve an angled force

  • θ\thetaθ names an angle.
  • An angled force and its horizontal and vertical components form a right-angled triangle.
  • The force magnitude is the hypotenuse: the longest side, opposite the right angle.
  • When θ\thetaθ is measured from the horizontal, the horizontal side is adjacent to the angle and the vertical side is opposite it.
  • cos⁡θ=adjacenthypotenuse\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}cosθ=hypotenuseadjacent​, so the horizontal component is Fcos⁡θF\cos\thetaFcosθ.
  • sin⁡θ=oppositehypotenuse\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}sinθ=hypotenuseopposite​, so the vertical component is Fsin⁡θF\sin\thetaFsinθ.
  • The arrow direction decides the sign: right and up are positive; left and down are negative.
  • Pythagoras gives R2=X2+Y2R^2=X^2+Y^2R2=X2+Y2. Taking the positive square root gives R=X2+Y2R=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}R=X2+Y2​. A square such as X2X^2X2 means X×XX\times XX×X; the square root reverses squaring.
  • tan⁡θ=oppositeadjacent\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}tanθ=adjacentopposite​. To recover the angle from a ratio, use inverse tangent: θ=tan⁡−1(ratio)\theta=\tan^{-1}(\text{ratio})θ=tan−1(ratio).

The six-step method

  1. Mark the required direction of the resultant.
  2. Choose axes parallel and perpendicular to that direction.
  3. Resolve every force along those axes.
  4. Set the unwanted component equal to zero.
  5. Solve for the unknown force or angle.
  6. Use the wanted component to find the resultant if asked.

Positive direction is a choice. State it, then give components acting that way a plus sign and components acting the other way a minus sign.

Worked example

Resultant along the horizontal

Forces of 4 N, F NF\text{ N}F N and 6 N act at a point. The 4 N force is 30° above the horizontal, the force FFF is 60° above it, and 6 N acts downward. Find FFF if the resultant is horizontal.

Solution
  1. Horizontal resultant means the vertical total is zero.
  2. 4sin⁡30∘+Fsin⁡60∘−6=04\sin30^\circ+F\sin60^\circ-6=04sin30∘+Fsin60∘−6=0
  3. 2+Fsin⁡60∘−6=02+F\sin60^\circ-6=02+Fsin60∘−6=0
  4. Fsin⁡60∘=4F\sin60^\circ=4Fsin60∘=4
  5. Divide both sides by sin⁡60∘\sin60^\circsin60∘ to leave FFF alone.
  6. F=4sin⁡60∘=4.62 NF=\frac{4}{\sin60^\circ}=4.62\text{ N}F=sin60∘4​=4.62 N

Answer: F=4.62 NF=4.62\text{ N}F=4.62 N.

Resultant perpendicular to a force

The force F NF\text{ N}F N points left. The 30 N force points up and right, and the 20 N force points down and right. Each makes 60° with the rightward horizontal. The resultant points vertically, perpendicular to FFF. Find FFF.

Solution
  1. Choose rightwards as positive, along the line of FFF.
  2. Perpendicular resultant means its component along FFF is zero.
  3. 20cos⁡60∘+30cos⁡60∘−F=020\cos60^\circ+30\cos60^\circ-F=020cos60∘+30cos60∘−F=0
  4. 10+15−F=010+15-F=010+15−F=0

Answer: F=25 NF=25\text{ N}F=25 N.

Replacement force

A 50 N force is replaced by P NP\text{ N}P N in the same direction, at angle θ\thetaθ above the horizontal. Two other forces contribute downward vertical components of 11.2 N and 28.8 N. These are component values, not the full force magnitudes. The new resultant is horizontal. Find PPP.

Solution
  1. The replacement force supplies upward component Psin⁡θP\sin\thetaPsinθ.
  2. The horizontal-resultant condition makes the vertical total zero.
  3. Psin⁡θ−11.2−28.8=0P\sin\theta-11.2-28.8=0Psinθ−11.2−28.8=0
  4. P=40sin⁡θ NP=\frac{40}{\sin\theta}\text{ N}P=sinθ40​ N

Answer: P=40/sin⁡θ NP=40/\sin\theta\text{ N}P=40/sinθ N.

Equilibrant force

The existing resultant has components 20 N right and 15 N up. Find the single additional force that produces equilibrium.

Solution
  1. R=202+152=25 NR=\sqrt{20^2+15^2}=25\text{ N}R=202+152​=25 N
  2. tan⁡θ=1520,θ=36.9∘\tan\theta=\frac{15}{20},\qquad \theta=36.9^\circtanθ=2015​,θ=36.9∘
  3. The equilibrant reverses both components: 20 N left and 15 N down.

Answer: 25 N at 36.9° below the negative x-axis.

Key ideas

  • Translate the direction statement before resolving.
  • Resolve along and perpendicular to the required direction.
  • Set the unwanted component to zero.
  • For equilibrium, both component totals are zero.
  • An equilibrant matches the old resultant in magnitude and reverses its direction.
  • Check signs against your declared positive directions.

Equilibrium

Forces of 8 N and 12 N act upward. A force PPP acts downward. The particle is in equilibrium. Find PPP.

Solution
  1. Equilibrium makes the vertical total zero.
  2. 8+12−P=08+12-P=08+12−P=0
  3. P=20 NP=20\text{ N}P=20 N

Answer: P=20 NP=20\text{ N}P=20 N.

Perpendicular to a force

A resultant is perpendicular to FFF. The other forces have a combined 18 N component opposite to FFF. Find FFF.

Solution
  1. The component along FFF must be zero.
  2. 18−F=018-F=018−F=0
  3. F=18 NF=18\text{ N}F=18 N

Answer: F=18 NF=18\text{ N}F=18 N.

Exam-style practice

Three coplanar forces, all acting in the same flat plane, act at a point. A force P NP\text{ N}P N acts along line lll. A 5 N force has signed component 4.33 N along lll; a 10 N force has signed component -5.00 N along lll. The resultant is perpendicular to lll.

(a) Find PPP.

(b) The component perpendicular to lll is 6.16 N. State the magnitude of the resultant.

Solution
  1. The component along lll is zero.
  2. P+4.33−5.00=0P+4.33-5.00=0P+4.33−5.00=0
  3. P=0.670 NP=0.670\text{ N}P=0.670 N
  4. The perpendicular component is given as 6.16 N. With no component along lll, this is the full resultant magnitude.

Answers: P=0.670 NP=0.670\text{ N}P=0.670 N; resultant 6.16 N6.16\text{ N}6.16 N.

Resultant forces

Many forces.
One combined effect.

Several forces can act at one point. Add their horizontal and vertical effects first. Then one right-angled triangle gives the resultant's size and direction.

Start the lesson

Start with two directions

  • A force is a push or pull. Its direction matters as well as its size. Force is measured in newtons, written N.
  • Coplanar forces lie in the same flat plane.
  • The resultant is one force with the same combined effect as all the original forces.
  • Choose two perpendicular directions. They meet at a right angle, which is an angle of exactly 90°.
  • We use a horizontal x-axis and a vertical y-axis.
  • Right and up are positive. Left and down are negative.
  • A component is the part of a force along one chosen axis.

Component totals

X=∑horizontal componentsX=\sum \text{{horizontal components}}X=∑horizontal components
Y=∑vertical componentsY=\sum \text{{vertical components}}Y=∑vertical components

X is the signed horizontal total. Y is the signed vertical total. The symbol Σ means “add all”.

  • The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle and is the longest side. An angled force forms this hypotenuse in a component triangle.
  • If its angle is measured from the x-axis, the horizontal part uses cosine and the vertical part uses sine.
  • Give each component its sign before adding.

One angled force F

Fx=Fcos⁡θF_x=F\cos\thetaFx​=Fcosθ
Fy=Fsin⁡θF_y=F\sin\thetaFy​=Fsinθ

θ (theta) is the angle measured from the x-axis. The subscript x or y names the direction of the component.

Turn the totals into one force

  • Draw X and Y as the two perpendicular sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • The diagonal is the resultant R.
  • Magnitude means size. It is always non-negative.
  • Pythagoras gives the magnitude.
  • The square X² means X × X. The square-root symbol √ reverses squaring, so √25 = 5.
  • The tangent ratio gives an acute reference angle.
  • We call that angle α (alpha). The calculator key tan⁻¹ reverses tangent to find α from a ratio.

Magnitude and reference angle

R=X2+Y2R=\sqrt{{X^2+Y^2}}R=X2+Y2​
tan⁡α=∣Y∣∣X∣\tan\alpha=\frac{{|Y|}}{{|X|}}tanα=∣X∣∣Y∣​

The vertical bars mean absolute value: use the size without its sign. Then use the signs of X and Y to state the correct quadrant and direction.

  • The two axes divide the plane into four regions called quadrants.
  • Quadrant I is top-right, II is top-left, III is bottom-left, and IV is bottom-right.

The six-step method

  1. Choose positive x and positive y directions.
  2. Resolve every angled force into components.
  3. Add the signed horizontal components to get X.
  4. Add the signed vertical components to get Y.
  5. Use Pythagoras to find R.
  6. Use tangent to find the acute angle, then state a complete direction.

Resolve first. Add second. Combine last.

  • Keep unrounded component values in your calculator.
  • Round only the final requested answers.
  • A complete result needs a magnitude, unit, angle, reference axis and sense such as above or below.

Worked example

Positive X and positive Y

Forces of 50 N, 40 N and 30 N act at a point. After resolving and adding them, their totals are X = 30 N and Y = 10 N. Find the resultant.

Solution
  1. R=302+102R=\sqrt{{30^2+10^2}}R=302+102​
  2. R=1000=31.6 NR=\sqrt{{1000}}=31.6\text{{ N}}R=1000​=31.6 N
  3. tan⁡θ=1030\tan\theta=\frac{{10}}{{30}}tanθ=3010​
  4. θ=18.4∘\theta=18.4^\circθ=18.4∘

Resultant = 31.6 N at 18.4° above the positive x-axis.

Use given sine and cosine values

The component expressions are X = 7 - 8 cos α - 6 sin α and Y = 8 sin α - 6 cos α. Given sin α = 3/5 and cos α = 4/5, substitute those exact ratios directly.

Direction wording

  • An angle alone does not identify a unique direction.
  • Name the reference line: positive x-axis, negative x-axis, horizontal, or another stated force.
  • Name the sense: above, below, clockwise, or anticlockwise.
  • If X is negative, it is usually simplest to measure from the negative x-axis.

Key ideas

  1. Choose perpendicular axes and positive directions.
  2. Resolve each force, keeping the signs.
  3. Add components to obtain X and Y.
  4. Find R from Pythagoras.
  5. Find an acute angle from |Y|/|X|.
  6. Use the signs to state the direction fully.

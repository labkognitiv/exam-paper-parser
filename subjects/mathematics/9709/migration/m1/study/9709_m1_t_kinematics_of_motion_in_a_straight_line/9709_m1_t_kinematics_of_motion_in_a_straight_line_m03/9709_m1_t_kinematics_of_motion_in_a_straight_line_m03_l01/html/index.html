Mechanics 1 · Kinematics of motion in a straight line

Passing fixed points

Match each distance with the time interval that produced it.

Start the lesson
SetupWorked examplesKey ideas

Before you calculate

  • A fixed point is a named position such as A, B or C on the line of travel.
  • An interval is the time between two named instants. A cumulative time starts at the beginning, so the interval BC uses the cumulative time at C minus the cumulative time at B.
  • Use displacement s for a directed change in position, and distance for a positive length.
  • Use u for velocity at the start of the chosen interval, a for constant acceleration, and t for that interval's duration.
  • Units: distance is metres (m), time is seconds (s), speed is metres per second (m s⁻¹), acceleration is metres per second squared (m s⁻²).

Constant acceleration

s=ut+½at²    v=u+at    v²=u²+2as

Write one equation for the correct interval. For a distance from A to C, use the whole elapsed time. For BC, subtract the A-to-B displacement from the A-to-C displacement.

  1. Draw the points in order.
  2. Write cumulative times from the start.
  3. Translate each stated distance into an interval equation.
  4. Solve the simultaneous equations, then check the units and the order of points.

Worked examples

Worked example

One particle, two intervals

A particle passes A with speed u and constant acceleration a. It travels AB = 55 m in 5 s and BC = 65 m in the next 5 s.

  1. From A to B, the interval is 5 s: 55=5u+½a(5²)=5u+12.5a.
  2. From A to C, the cumulative time is 10 s. The distance AC is 55+65=120 m: 120=10u+½a(10²)=10u+50a.
  3. Double the first equation: 110=10u+25a.
  4. Subtract: 10=25a, so a=0.4m s⁻².
  5. Substitute: 55=5u+12.5(0.4)=5u+5, hence u=10m s⁻¹.

u = 10 m s⁻¹, a = 0.4 m s⁻².

Worked example

Equal time intervals

A cyclist passes A, B and C in order. AB and BC each take 2 s. The speed at B is 4.5m s⁻¹ and AB = 5/4 BC.

  1. Use B as the middle instant. For AB, going backwards 2 s: sAB=4.5(2)-½a(2²)=9-2a.
  2. For BC, going forwards 2 s: sBC=4.5(2)+½a(2²)=9+2a.
  3. Set the ratio: 9-2a=5/4(9+2a).
  4. Multiply by 4: 36-8a=45+10a. Then -18a=9.
  5. a=-0.5m s⁻², so the cyclist is decelerating.

Deceleration = 0.5 m s⁻².

Worked example

Show that

A car passes P, Q, R, S at equal 10 s intervals. Its speed at Q is 20 m s⁻¹ and QR is 1.5 PQ. Show that a=0.8m s⁻².

  1. Let acceleration from Q onward be a. Previous interval PQ has displacement 20(10)-½a(10²)=200-50a.
  2. Next interval QR has displacement 20(10)+½a(10²)=200+50a.
  3. Use the ratio: 200+50a=1.5(200-50a).
  4. Expand and collect terms: 200+50a=300-75a, so 125a=100.
  5. a=0.8m s⁻², as required.

a = 0.8 m s⁻².

Check 1

BC takes 5 s, while AC takes 10 s. Which time belongs in the BC equation when using cumulative equations?

Choose A or B.

Worked example

Speed after fixed points

A car passes A at 14 m s⁻¹ with acceleration 4 m s⁻². It takes 5 s from A to B and 3 s from B to C.

  1. Total elapsed time A to C is 5+3=8s.
  2. Use v=u+at: v=14+4(8)=46m s⁻¹.

Speed at C = 46 m s⁻¹.

Worked example

Distance after a later speed

A particle passes A at 19 m s⁻¹ with acceleration 3 m s⁻². AB = 100 m, BC = 148 m. Later at D its speed is 61 m s⁻¹. Find CD.

  1. Known distance AC: 100+148=248m.
  2. Use v²=u²+2as from A to D: 61²=19²+2(3)sAD.
  3. 3721=361+6sAD, so sAD=560m.
  4. Subtract AC: CD=560-248=312m.

CD = 312 m.

Worked example

Distance in the nth second

A particle starts with speed u and acceleration a. It travels 52 m during the 2nd second and 64 m during the 4th second.

  1. Distance in the 2nd second is s(2)-s(1)=2u+½a(2²)-[u+½a(1²)]=52, so u+1.5a=52.
  2. Distance in the 4th second is s(4)-s(3)=u+3.5a=64.
  3. Subtract: 2a=12, hence a=6m s⁻².
  4. u+1.5(6)=52, so u=43m s⁻¹.

u = 43 m s⁻¹, a = 6 m s⁻².

Worked example

Watch the interval start

  • If BC takes 5 s, do not write sBC=5u+12.5a unless u is the speed at B.
  • When u is speed at A, write sAB=5u+12.5a, sAC=10u+50a, then sBC=sAC-sAB.

Key ideas

  • Use cumulative times from the named start point.
  • For a later interval, subtract cumulative displacements.
  • At a middle point, use its actual speed when starting a fresh interval.
  • Check whether a stated distance is an interval distance or a total distance.

Worked example

Try it yourself, then check

A particle passes A at speed u with acceleration a. AB = 36 m in 4 s and AC = 92 m in 8 s.

  1. 36=4u+8a; 92=8u+32a.
  2. Double the first: 72=8u+16a. Subtract: 20=16a, so a=1.25.
  3. 36=4u+10, hence u=6.5m s⁻¹.

u = 6.5 m s⁻¹, a = 1.25 m s⁻².

Worked example

Exam-style consistency check

A train passes A, B and C in order. AB takes 6 s, BC takes 4 s. Speed at A is 12 m s⁻¹ and BC = 2AB.

  1. AB=12(6)+½a(6²)=72+18a.
  2. AC=12(10)+½a(10²)=120+50a, so BC=AC-AB=48+32a.
  3. Set 48+32a=2(72+18a). Then -4a=96, so a=-24.
  4. Check: v=12-24t, so velocity is zero at t=0.5s; the train reverses before B. The stated data are inconsistent with passing A, B and C in order.

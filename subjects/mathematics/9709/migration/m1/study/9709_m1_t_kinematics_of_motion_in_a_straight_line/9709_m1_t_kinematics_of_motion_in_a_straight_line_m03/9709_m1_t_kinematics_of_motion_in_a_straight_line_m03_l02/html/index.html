Mathematics 9709 · Mechanics 1

Meeting, collision and overtaking

Give every body a position on one line, use one shared clock, then solve where positions match.

Set upWorked examplesChecksRecap

A meeting, collision or overtaking occurs when two bodies occupy the same position at the same time.

One origin, one shared clock

  • Choose one origin and one positive direction. Position x is signed distance from that origin.
  • Define one clock. If A starts 3 s before B and t is measured from B, A has moved for t+3 seconds. If A starts after B by d, use t−d for A’s motion time, valid only after its start.
  • Write xA(t) and xB(t) in the same coordinate system. Set them equal for a meeting.
  • For a closing gap, distances add to the initial gap. For overtaking, the faster body gains the initial separation.
  • When solving a quadratic, reject a negative time or a root before a delayed body has started.

Useful models

x=ut+½at²

x=v(t−d)   for a constant-speed body with delay d

xA(t)=xB(t)

Complete the square by adding and subtracting the same number. This shows a minimum such as d=(t−3)²+11. Calculus gives the same result by setting the derivative of distance to zero.

Worked examples

Worked example · moving towards each other

P starts from rest with acceleration 0.5 m s−2; Q is 10 m away and moves towards P at 0.75 m s−1.

  1. xP=½(0.5)t²=0.25t², xQ=0.75t.
  2. 0.25t²+0.75t=10, so t²+3t−40=(t+8)(t−5)=0.
  3. Reject t=−8; time is positive.

Collision at t=5 s.

Worked example · delayed overtaking

Cyclist travels 36 m in 12 s, then at 6 m s−1. A car starts 24 s later from rest at 4 m s−2.

  1. xC=36+6(t−12)=6t−36; xcar=½(4)(t−24)²=2(t−24)².
  2. 2(t−24)²=6t−36 ⇒ t²−51t+594=(t−18)(t−33)=0.
  3. Reject 18 because the car has not started.

Overtaking at t=33 s.

Worked example · different starts

A moves at 10 m s−1 and passes O 2 s before B. B passes O at 16 m s−1, decelerating at 2 m s−2.

  1. xA=10(t+2)=20+10t.
  2. xB=16t−t².

Worked example · meet twice

A passes O 4 s before B at 8 m s−1. B passes O at 20 m s−1, decelerating at 2 m s−2.

  1. xA=8(t+4)=8t+32, xB=20t−t².
  2. 8t+32=20t−t² ⇒ (t−4)(t−8)=0.

They are level at t=4 s and t=8 s.

Worked example · minimum separation

Using the two positions above:

  1. d=xA−xB=20+10t−(16t−t²)=t²−6t+20.
  2. d=(t²−6t+9)+11=(t−3)²+11. Since a square is at least zero, minimum occurs at t=3.

Minimum distance = 11 m.

Worked example · collision speeds

P is 5 m behind Q. Speeds are 8 and 3 m s−1, decelerations 2 and 2.5 m s−2.

  1. 8t−t²=5+3t−1.25t² ⇒ t²+20t−20=0.
  2. Positive root t=−10+2√30≈0.954 s.
  3. vP=8−2(0.954)=6.09, vQ=3−2.5(0.954)=0.614.

Speeds: P 6.09 m s−1; Q 0.614 m s−1.

Worked example · delayed same-distance check

A starts at 5 m s−1; B passes O 3 s later at 14 m s−1, decelerating at 2 m s−2.

  1. xA=5(t+3)=5t+15, xB=14t−t².
  2. 5t+15=14t−t² ⇒ t²−9t+15=0.
  3. t=(9±√21)/2=2.21, 6.79 s.

They are level at 2.21 s and 6.79 s after B starts.

Worked example · combined particle

A is 0.9 m behind a combined particle. Speeds are 0.6 and 0.5 m s−1.

  1. 0.6t−0.5t=0.9 ⇒ t=9 s.

A catches it after 9 s.

Worked example · calculus motion

aX=12t+12, aY=24t−8, both start from rest.

  1. Integrate: vX=6t²+12t, vY=12t²−8t.
  2. Equal velocities: 6t²+12t=12t²−8t ⇒ t=10/3 s (reject t=0).
  3. Integrate again: xX=2t³+6t², xY=4t³−4t².
  4. At t=10/3, xX=3800/27, xY=2800/27, so AB=1000/27≈37.0 m.

AB = 1000/27 m, about 37.0 m.

Worked example · shared start

Car starts from rest with acceleration 3 m s−2; cyclist passes at 12 m s−1.

  1. xcar=1.5t², xcyc=12t.
  2. 1.5t²=12t ⇒ t=0 ext{ or }8. Reject the starting instant.

Overtakes after 8 s, 96 m from A.

Key ideas

  1. Choose origin, positive direction and shared clock.
  2. Put start offsets inside each body’s motion time.
  3. Set positions equal and reject roots outside the story.
  4. Use completing the square or calculus to locate minimum separation.
  5. Check units, signs, joins and whether the result is a time, position or speed.

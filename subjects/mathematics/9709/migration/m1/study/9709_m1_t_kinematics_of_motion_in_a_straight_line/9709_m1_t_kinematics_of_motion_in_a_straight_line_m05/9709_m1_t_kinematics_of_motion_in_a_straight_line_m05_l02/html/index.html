Instantaneous rest and extreme velocity

Read a velocity function, find when velocity is zero, and locate maximum or minimum velocity.

Set upWorked examplesChecksRecap

A moving particle may pause for an instant, reverse direction, or reach its fastest velocity without stopping for a whole interval.

Translate the words

  • Instantaneously at rest means velocity is zero at one instant: v=0. It may move immediately afterwards.
  • Speed is the magnitude of velocity: speed=|v|. A velocity of −4 m s−1 has speed 4 m s−1.
  • If v is given as a function of time, acceleration is its derivative: a=dv/dt. This means the gradient, or rate of change, of the velocity-time graph.
  • At a smooth maximum or minimum of v, the graph has a horizontal tangent, so a=0. Check endpoints too when a time interval is given.
  • Keep only times in the stated domain, such as 0≤t≤20.

A safe method

  1. Write the velocity expression and its time domain.
  2. For instantaneous rest, set v=0 and solve.
  3. For extreme velocity, differentiate to find a, set a=0, then substitute that time into v.
  4. Compare endpoints if the domain is closed.
  5. State velocity and speed separately when the velocity is negative.

Worked examples

Worked example · two rest times

A particle has velocity v=2t²−5t+3. Find when it is instantaneously at rest.

  1. Set velocity to zero: 2t²−5t+3=0.
  2. Factor: (2t−3)(t−1)=0.
  3. Therefore t=3/2=1.5 or t=1.

Rest times: t=1 s and t=1.5 s.

Worked example · maximum velocity

For 0≤t≤20, v=0.6t−0.03t². Find maximum velocity.

  1. Differentiate term by term: a=dv/dt=0.6−0.06t.
  2. Set acceleration to zero: 0.6−0.06t=0, so t=10 ext{ s}.
  3. Substitute: v(10)=0.6(10)−0.03(10²)=6−3=3.
  4. The downward-opening quadratic gives a maximum at this turning point; endpoints give v(0)=0 and v(20)=0.

Maximum velocity = 3 m s−1.

Worked example · minimum velocity and speed

v=4t²−20t+21. Find minimum velocity and speed at that instant.

  1. a=dv/dt=8t−20.
  2. 8t−20=0, so t=2.5.
  3. v(2.5)=4(2.5²)−20(2.5)+21=25−50+21=−4.
  4. Velocity is negative, so speed is its magnitude: |−4|=4.

Minimum velocity = −4 m s−1; speed = 4 m s−1.

Worked example · displacement at next rest

A particle has v=6t−2t² and starts at O. Find displacement when it next comes to rest.

  1. 0=6t−2t²=2t(3−t), so t=0 or t=3. The next time is 3 s.
  2. Displacement is the integral of velocity: s=∫(6t−2t²)dt=3t²−(2/3)t³.
  3. s(3)=3(3²)−(2/3)(3³)=27−18=9.

Displacement = 9 m.

Worked example · constants from conditions

v=qt+rt², with v=4 m s−1 at t=1 and t=2. Find v(t), then a at t=0.5.

  1. At t=1: q+r=4. At t=2: 2q+4r=4.
  2. Subtract twice the first equation: (2q+4r)−(2q+2r)=4−8, so 2r=−4, r=−2.
  3. Then q−2=4, so q=6. Thus v=6t−2t².
  4. Differentiate: a=6−4t. At 0.5 s, a=6−4(0.5)=4 m s⁻².

v=6t−2t²; a(0.5)=4 m s−2.

Worked example · time domain

Solve 5t²−24t−5=0 when t is time after release.

  1. (5t+1)(t−5)=0.
  2. So t=−0.2 or t=5 ext{ s}.
  3. Negative time is before release and is outside the domain t≥0.

Keep t=5 s only.

Worked example · labelled velocity sketch

For v=2t²−5t+3, rest roots are t=1 and 1.5. The acceleration is 4t−5, so the turning point is at t=1.25.

  1. v(1.25)=2(1.25²)−5(1.25)+3=−0.125.
  2. Plot the intercepts (1,0), (1.5,0), start (0,3), and turning point (1.25,−0.125).

Worked example · full exam-style method

For 0≤t≤10, v=0.4t²−4t+7.5, find rest times, minimum velocity and speed then.

  1. Set v=0 and multiply by 10: 4t²−40t+75=0.
  2. t=(40±√(1600−1200))/8=(40±20)/8, so t=2.5 or t=7.5.
  3. a=dv/dt=0.8t−4. Set a=0: t=5.
  4. v(5)=0.4(25)−20+7.5=−2.5.
  5. Speed is |−2.5|=2.5.

Rest at 2.5 s and 7.5 s; minimum velocity −2.5 m s−1; speed 2.5 m s−1.

Key ideas

  1. Rest means v=0 at an instant; it does not mean permanent stillness.
  2. Extreme velocity occurs where a=dv/dt=0, with endpoint checks when needed.
  3. Negative velocity can still have positive speed.
  4. Integrate velocity only when displacement is asked for.
  5. Reject roots outside the time domain.

From s(t) to v(t) to a(t)

Differentiate displacement to find velocity, then differentiate velocity to find acceleration.

StartWorked examplesChecksRecapMore examples

The calculus chain

  • s(t) is displacement in metres. Its sign follows the chosen positive direction.
  • v(t)=ds/dt is velocity in m/s. It tells how quickly displacement changes. Speed is the magnitude of velocity.
  • a(t)=dv/dt=d²s/dt² is acceleration in m/s². It tells how quickly velocity changes.
  • Power rule: multiply the coefficient by the exponent, then reduce the exponent by 1. For example, d(5t³)/dt=5(3)t²=15t². A constant differentiates to zero.
  • t1/2=√t; a negative power is a reciprocal, so t−n=1/tn. Expressions with √t or a denominator require t>0.
  • For a fraction, rewrite it as a negative power first. For an inequality, preserve the direction when multiplying by a positive quantity, then use the time domain given.
  • Rest means v=0. Zero acceleration means a=0, which identifies a stationary value of velocity.
Order
  1. Differentiate s to obtain v.
  2. Differentiate v to obtain a.
  3. Substitute the time only after the needed function is found.

Worked examples

From displacement

s=t³−4t²+4t. Find velocity and acceleration at t=2.

  1. v=ds/dt=3t²−8t+4.
  2. a=dv/dt=6t−8.
  3. v(2)=3(2)²−8(2)+4=0.
  4. a(2)=6(2)−8=4 m/s².

Velocity = 0 m/s; acceleration = 4 m/s².

When acceleration is positive

v=4t−t³/16. Find when a>0.

  1. a=dv/dt=4−3t²/16.
  2. 4−3t²/16>0.
  3. Multiply by 16: 64−3t²>0, so t²<64/3.
  4. For physical time t≥0, 0≤t<8/√3.

Acceleration is positive for 0≤t<8/√3.

Fractional and negative powers

v=−2+800/t²=−2+800t−2. Find a at t=20.

  1. a=dv/dt=−1600t−3.
  2. a(20)=−1600/20³=−0.2 m/s².

Acceleration = −0.2 m/s².

Maximum acceleration

a=15t−3t² for 0≤t≤5. Find its maximum.

  1. Differentiate: da/dt=15−6t.
  2. Set the gradient to zero: 15−6t=0, so t=2.5.
  3. a(2.5)=15(2.5)−3(2.5)²=37.5−18.75=18.75 m/s².

Maximum acceleration = 18.75 m/s². Since d²a/dt²=−6<0, this stationary value is a maximum; the endpoints give 0.

Minimum velocity

v=t²−8t+12 for 0≤t≤8.

  1. a=dv/dt=2t−8.
  2. a=0 gives 2t−8=0, so t=4.
  3. v(4)=4²−8(4)+12=16−32+12=−4 m/s.

Minimum velocity = −4 m/s. Acceleration changes from negative to positive at t=4, so this is a minimum; endpoints give 12. The negative sign gives direction.

Acceleration from s(t)

s=2t²−(80/3)t3/2. Find when a=0.

  1. v=ds/dt=4t−40t1/2.
  2. a=dv/dt=4−20t−1/2=4−20/√t.
  3. 4−20/√t=0, so 20/√t=4.
  4. √t=5, hence t=25 s.

Acceleration is zero at t=25 s.

Do not stop differentiating early

s=t³−6t²+4t. Find velocity when acceleration is zero.

  1. v=ds/dt=3t²−12t+4.
  2. a=dv/dt=6t−12.
  3. a=0 gives t=2.
  4. v(2)=3(2)²−12(2)+4=−8 m/s.

Velocity = −8 m/s.

Recap

  • v=ds/dt; a=dv/dt.
  • Velocity can be negative; speed is its magnitude.
  • Set v=0 for rest. Set a=0 for a stationary velocity value.
  • Respect the stated time domain and keep units with each result.

Try it yourself

v=0.6t−0.03t². Find a at t=5 and when v is greatest.

  1. a=dv/dt=0.6−0.06t.
  2. a(5)=0.6−0.06(5)=0.3 m/s².
  3. Velocity is greatest when a=0: 0.6−0.06t=0, so t=10 s.

a(5)=0.3 m/s²; greatest velocity at 10 s because acceleration changes from positive before 10 to negative after 10.

Exam-style

s=t³−5t²+3t. Find v, a at t=4, and v when a=0.

  1. v=3t²−10t+3.
  2. a=6t−10; a(4)=24−10=14 m/s².
  3. a=0 gives t=5/3.
  4. v(5/3)=3(25/9)−10(5/3)+3=−16/3 m/s.

v=3t²−10t+3; a(4)=14 m/s²; v when a=0 is −16/3 m/s.

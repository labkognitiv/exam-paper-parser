Reading motion graphs

A graph tells a story about velocity, displacement, acceleration and distance.

StartWorked examplesChecksRecapMore examples

Read the axes first

  • A velocity-time (v-t) graph has time t in seconds on the horizontal axis and velocity v in m/s on the vertical axis.
  • A displacement-time (s-t) graph has time horizontally and displacement s in metres vertically.
  • Δ means change. A gradient is rise divided by run, so on a v-t graph a=Δv/Δt (acceleration). Above the axis is positive; below is negative.
  • On a v-t graph, signed area means displacement. Add the sizes of all areas for total distance.
  • On an s-t graph, gradient means velocity. Positive slope means positive velocity; a horizontal line means rest.
Shape tools
  • Triangle: three sides; area ½×base×height.
  • Rectangle: four right angles; area base×height.
  • Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides; area ½(a+b)h, where a and b are the parallel sides and h is perpendicular height.
Exact v-t coordinates

(0,0), (2,20), (6,20), (10,30), (14,10). These points define the four intervals used below.

Worked examples

Gradient on a v-t graph

A car's velocity rises uniformly from 2 m/s to 8 m/s in 12 s.

  1. Change in velocity: Δv=8−2=6 m/s.
  2. Change in time: Δt=12 s.
  3. a=Δv/Δt=6/12=0.5 m/s².

Acceleration = 0.5 m/s².

If velocity falls from 10 to 0 in 12 s, a=(0−10)/12=−0.83 m/s²; deceleration magnitude is 0.83 m/s².

Area gives displacement

For a v-t graph: triangle 0-2 s rises to 20 m/s; rectangle 2-6 s at 20; trapezium 6-10 s rises 20 to 30; trapezium 10-14 s falls 30 to 10.

  1. A₁=½(2)(20)=20 m.
  2. A₂=4(20)=80 m.
  3. A₃=½(20+30)(4)=100 m.
  4. A₄=½(30+10)(4)=80 m.
  5. s=20+80+100+80=280 m.

Displacement = 280 m.

Reading an s-t graph

A straight rising section means constant positive velocity; a horizontal section means zero velocity; a straight falling section means constant negative velocity. For any interval, average velocity=Δs/Δt.

The exact s-t graph has points (0,0), (20,40), (40,40), (60,−20). Its gradients are 40/20=2 m/s, 0/20=0, and (−20−40)/20=−3 m/s.

Forward, then return

A tool's forward v-t area is 1.08 m. It returns to its start in 3 s, with a triangular return graph below the axis. Assume this triangular profile; under that assumption let V be the greatest return speed.

  1. Net displacement is zero, so return area size is 1.08 m.
  2. ½(3)V=1.08.
  3. 1.5V=1.08, so V=0.72 m/s.

Greatest return speed = 0.72 m/s.

Distance versus displacement

If area above the v-t axis is 64.5 m and area below is 22.0 m, signed displacement is 64.5−22.0=42.5 m. Total distance is 64.5+22.0=86.5 m.

Average speed

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. A runner covers 120 m in 15 s.

  1. average speed=120/15=8 m/s.
  2. For an outward 70 m and return 70 m journey in 28 s: (70+70)/28=5 m/s.

Exam-style graph

A particle has velocity 5 m/s at t=0, accelerates uniformly to 17 m/s in 8 s, then moves at 17 m/s for 12 s.

  1. a=(17−5)/8=1.5 m/s².
  2. Distance in 20 s is trapezium plus rectangle: ½(5+17)(8)+17(12)=88+204=292 m.

Acceleration = 1.5 m/s²; distance = 292 m.

Recap

  • v-t gradient is acceleration.
  • v-t signed area is displacement.
  • Total distance adds area sizes.
  • s-t gradient is velocity.
  • State whether an answer is speed, velocity, distance or displacement.

More examples

Try it yourself

A v-t graph has points (0,0), (6,9), (10,9), (14,0).

  1. a=(9−0)/(6−0)=1.5 m/s².
  2. distance=½(6)(9)+4(9)+½(4)(9)=27+36+18=81 m.

Distance = 81 m.

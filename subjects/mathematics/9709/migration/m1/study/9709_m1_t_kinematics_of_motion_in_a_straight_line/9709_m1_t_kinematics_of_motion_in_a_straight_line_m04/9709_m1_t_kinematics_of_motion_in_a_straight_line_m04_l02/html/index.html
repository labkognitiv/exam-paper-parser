Mechanics 1 · Kinematics

Constructing and solving graphs

Turn a motion story into a velocity-time graph, then read distance, displacement and unknown values from it.

Start the lesson
SetupWorked examplesKey ideas

Read the axes first

  • A triangle has three straight sides; its area is half its base multiplied by its perpendicular height.
  • A rectangle has four right angles; its area is width multiplied by height.
  • A trapezium has a pair of parallel sides, which remain the same distance apart. Its area is half the sum of those side lengths multiplied by the perpendicular gap between them.
  • On a velocity-time graph, multiplying time in seconds by velocity in metres per second gives displacement in metres. Read each width as an elapsed time, not automatically the time at its right edge.
  • A velocity-time graph has time t horizontally in seconds (s), and velocity v vertically in metres per second (m s⁻¹).
  • A positive velocity is above the time axis. A negative velocity is below it.
  • The gradient means change in velocity divided by change in time, so it is acceleration in m s⁻².
  • The signed area between the graph and the time axis equals displacement in metres. Areas below the axis are negative.
  • Total distance uses the magnitudes of all regions, so add positive lengths above and below the axis.
  1. Mark every change of acceleration and every stated time.
  2. Write a formula for each triangle, rectangle or trapezium before solving.
  3. Use cumulative time from the start for a whole journey.
  4. Check whether the question asks for distance, displacement, speed or velocity.

Worked examples

Worked example

Find an unknown time

A body starts at 170 m s⁻¹ and reaches 200 m s⁻¹ with constant acceleration over time T. The total distance is 2775 m.

  1. The graph is a trapezium. Its parallel sides are 170 and 200, and its width is T.
  2. 2775=½(170+200)T=185T.
  3. Divide both sides by 185: T=2775/185=15s.

T = 15 s.

Worked example

Shade only the useful region

  • For displacement at a time, shade from the start to that time only.
  • For total distance, include every region and use positive area magnitudes.
  • For a return to the start, positive and negative signed areas cancel.

Worked example

Find constant speed from total distance

A train accelerates uniformly from rest for 20 s, travels at constant speed V until 180 s, then stops at 200 s. Total distance is 2775 m.

  1. Acceleration triangle area: ½(20)(V)=10V.
  2. Constant-speed rectangle: (180-20)V=160V. Braking triangle: ½(20)(V)=10V.
  3. 2775=(10+160+10)V=180V.
  4. V=2775/180=15.416...m s⁻¹.

V = 15.4 m s⁻¹ (3 s.f.).

Worked example

Sketch from a story

From rest, a car accelerates at 0.75 m s⁻² for 4 s, travels at constant speed until t=54, then decelerates at 0.5 m s⁻² until t=60.

  1. At 4 s, v=0.75(4)=3m s⁻¹.
  2. Plot points (0,0),(4,3),(54,3),(60,0).
  3. Join with a rising line, horizontal line, then falling line. Their gradients are 0.75, 0 and −0.5 m s⁻².

The graph has the three stated phases.

Worked example

Show a given distance

A parachutist's velocity-time graph has three regions: triangle area 125 m, trapezium area 525 m, and rectangle area 800 m.

  1. Area 1: ½(5)(50)=125.
  2. Area 2: ½(7)(50+100)=525.
  3. Area 3: 8(100)=800.
  4. Add: 125+525+800=1450m.

Total distance travelled = 1450 m.

Worked example

Negative velocity

A graph has positive area 40 m + 80 m, then a negative triangle of area −8 m from t=16 to 24 s.

  1. Positive displacement before reversal: 40+80=120m.
  2. Negative triangle: ½(8)(-V)=-8, so V=2m s⁻¹.
  3. Velocity is negative: v=-2m s⁻¹.

Speed = 2 m s⁻¹; velocity = −2 m s⁻¹.

Worked example

Try it yourself

A car accelerates from rest to speed V in 10 s, travels at V for 30 s, then decelerates to rest in 20 s. Total distance is 900 m.

  1. 900=½(10)V+30V+½(20)V=5V+30V+10V=45V.
  2. V=900/45=20m s⁻¹.

V = 20 m s⁻¹.

Worked example

Sketch displacement-time behaviour

  • Positive velocity gives increasing displacement.
  • Constant velocity gives a straight line with constant gradient.
  • Increasing velocity makes the displacement curve steeper.
  • When velocity is zero, the displacement-time graph is flat for that instant.

Key ideas

  • Total distance: add every positive area magnitude.
  • Displacement: add positive areas and subtract negative areas.
  • Total time is the full horizontal span.
  • Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.
  • Average velocity = displacement ÷ total time.

Worked example

Exam-style practice

A particle moves at 6 m s⁻¹ for 20 s, accelerates uniformly to V in 5 s, then moves at V for 15 s. Total distance is 460 m.

  1. Rectangle: 20(6)=120. Trapezium: ½(5)(6+V)=15+2.5V. Rectangle: 15V.
  2. 460=120+15+2.5V+15V=135+17.5V.
  3. 325=17.5V, so V=130/7=18.6m s⁻¹.
  4. Sketch: horizontal at 6 until 20 s, rising to 18.6 at 25 s, then horizontal to 40 s.

V = 18.6 m s⁻¹.

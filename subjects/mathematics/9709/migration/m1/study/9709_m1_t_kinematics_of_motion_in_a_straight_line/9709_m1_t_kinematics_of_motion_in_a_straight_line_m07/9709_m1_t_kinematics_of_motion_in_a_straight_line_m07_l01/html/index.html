Piecewise motion at joins

Bookkeep each time phase carefully. At a join, test the stated continuity condition.

StartWorked examplesChecksRecapMore examples

Intervals and joins

  • A piecewise formula applies only on its stated interval, such as 0≤t≤5 or 5≤t≤10.
  • At a join t=T, substitute T into the formula before and after the join. A continuity statement means the velocities match.
  • Differentiate each velocity to compare accelerations. Acceleration may match or jump.
  • For distance, integrate each phase separately. If velocity changes sign, split again at v=0 and add magnitudes.
  • Constants are found by substituting the actual join or given time. Keep the endpoint values explicit.
Join checklist
  1. Write both formulas and their intervals.
  2. Substitute the join time into both.
  3. Set equal only when continuity is stated.
  4. Differentiate both and compare one-sided accelerations.
  5. Integrate each interval separately for distance.

Worked examples

Acceleration across a join

For 0≤t≤5, v₁=0.72t²−0.096t³. For 5≤t≤10, v₂=2.4t−0.24t².

  1. a₁=dv₁/dt=1.44t−0.288t²; a₁(5)=7.2−7.2=0.
  2. a₂=dv₂/dt=2.4−0.48t; a₂(5)=2.4−2.4=0.

Acceleration is 0 m/s² on both sides at t=5, so there is no instantaneous acceleration change.

Find constants from speed and distance

v=0.04t³+ct²+kt; v=10 at t=5 and distance AB=25 m with s(0)=0.

  1. 10=0.04(125)+25c+5k, so 5c+k=1.
  2. s=∫vdt=0.01t⁴+(c/3)t³+(k/2)t²+C; s(0)=0 gives C=0.
  3. 25=0.01(625)+(c/3)(125)+(k/2)(25), so 10c+3k=4.5.
  4. Subtract equations: 5c+k=1 and 10c+3k=4.5. Thus c=−0.3, k=2.5.

Distance across two phases

Use the same v₁ and v₂ above on 0≤t≤5 and 5≤t≤10.

  1. s₁=∫₀⁵(0.72t²−0.096t³)dt=[0.24t³−0.024t⁴]₀⁵=30−15=15 m.
  2. s₂=∫₅¹⁰(2.4t−0.24t²)dt=[1.2t²−0.08t³]₅¹⁰=40−20=20 m.
  3. distance=s₁+s₂=15+20=35 m.

Distance travelled = 35 m.

Matching speeds at t=60

For 0≤t≤60, v=k₁t−0.005t²; for t≥60, v=k₂/√t. First-phase distance is 540 m.

  1. 540=[0.5k₁t²−(0.005/3)t³]₀⁶⁰=1800k₁−360, so k₁=0.5.
  2. v(60)=0.5(60)−0.005(60²)=12 m/s.
  3. Continuity: k₂/√60=12, so k₂=12√60.

Extreme velocity

v=t²−8t+15 for 0≤t≤10.

  1. a=dv/dt=2t−8.
  2. a=0 gives t=4.
  3. v(4)=16−32+15=−1 m/s.
  4. Since acceleration is negative before 4 and positive after 4, this is a minimum.

Minimum velocity = −1 m/s at t=4 s.

Distance, not only displacement

v=12t−t²−20 for 0≤t≤12. Solve v=0: t=2 and t=10.

  1. Velocity is negative on 0 to 2, positive on 2 to 10, negative on 10 to 12.
  2. Integrating once over 0 to 12 gives signed displacement only.
  3. For total distance, integrate separately on [0,2], [2,10], [10,12] and add absolute values.
  4. Let F(t)=6t²−t³/3−20t. Then F(0)=0, F(2)=−56/3, F(10)=200/3, F(12)=48. Distance = |−56/3−0|+|200/3−(−56/3)|+|48−200/3| = 56/3+256/3+56/3 = 368/3 m ≈ 122.667 m.

Key ideas

  • Keep each formula with its exact interval.
  • Use the actual join time for continuity and one-sided checks.
  • Differentiate both sides to compare acceleration.
  • Split integrals at joins and at v=0 when finding distance.
  • For a maximum of velocity, acceleration changes from positive to negative; for a minimum, negative to positive.

Constants at a join

For 0≤t≤4, v₁=3t−0.25t²; for t≥4, v₂=kt+c. Velocity is continuous at 4 and v₂=8 at 6.

  1. v₁(4)=12−4=8.
  2. Continuity: 4k+c=8.
  3. At t=6: 6k+c=8.
  4. Subtract: 2k=0, so k=0, c=8.

Full model

v=kt² for 0≤t≤5; then v=25−2(t−5) for 5≤t≤10; velocity is continuous at 5.

  1. At 5, second velocity is 25: 25k=25, so k=1.
  2. s₁=∫₀⁵t²dt=[t³/3]₀⁵=125/3.
  3. Rewrite second phase: v=35−2t; s₂=∫₅¹⁰(35−2t)dt=100.
  4. total=125/3+100=425/3 m.

Exam-style

For 0≤t≤4, v=pt²; for 4≤t≤8, v=32−qt. Continuous at 4 and at rest at 8.

  1. 0=32−8q, so q=4.
  2. Continuity: 16p=32−4(4)=16, so p=1.
  3. For 0≤t≤6, v=2t; for 6≤t≤10, v=18−t: s₁=∫₀⁶2t dt=36, s₂=∫₆¹⁰(18−t)dt=40.
  4. total distance=36+40=76 m.

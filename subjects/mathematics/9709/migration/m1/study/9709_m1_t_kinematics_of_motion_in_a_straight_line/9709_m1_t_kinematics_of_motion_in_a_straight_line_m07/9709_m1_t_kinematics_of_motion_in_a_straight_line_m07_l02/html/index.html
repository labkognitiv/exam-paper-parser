Sketching multi-phase velocity-time graphs

Read each interval, join continuous values, and show shape, key times and key velocities.

Set upWorked examplesChecksRecap

A velocity-time graph is a picture of how velocity changes with time. Each phase has its own shape.

Choose the shape before drawing

  • A straight rising line means constant positive acceleration. A straight falling line means constant negative acceleration.
  • A horizontal line means constant velocity and zero acceleration.
  • A curved line means changing acceleration. A curve that gets steeper has increasing gradient.
  • At a join, use the end velocity of one phase as the start velocity of the next. If velocity is continuous, do not draw a vertical jump.
  • Velocity below the time axis is negative. The signed area under a v-t graph is displacement; positive and negative regions have their signs.

Four checks for every phase

  1. Where does the segment start, and what is its velocity?
  2. Where does it finish, and what is its velocity?
  3. Is it a line, curve or horizontal segment?
  4. What does its gradient or concavity do?

Mark joins, key times, key velocities and rest points where v=0. A sketch is a velocity-time graph, with t on the horizontal axis and v in m s−1 on the vertical axis.

Worked examples

Worked example · three straight phases

A particle starts from rest. Its velocity is 8 m s−1 at t=8, 2 m s−1 at t=20, and 6.5 m s−1 at t=26.

  1. Plot key points (0,0), (8,8), (20,2), (26,6.5).
  2. Join successive points with straight lines because each phase changes velocity uniformly.

The sketch is the labelled key points joined in order.

Worked example · curve then line

From 0 to 5 s, velocity rises quadratically from 0 to 20. From 5 to 15 s it rises linearly to 40. From 15 to 35 s it curves down to 0.

  1. Use a curved rising segment from (0,0) to (5,20).
  2. Use a straight segment from (5,20) to (15,40).
  3. Use a curved falling segment from (15,40) to (35,0), ending at rest.

Label t=5,15,35 and v=20,40,0; preserve the joins.

Worked example · negative velocity

v=12t−t²−20 for 0≤t≤12. Find rest times and describe the graph.

  1. At rest, 0=12t−t²−20, so t²−12t+20=0=(t−2)(t−10).
  2. Rest times are t=2 and t=10. The axis of symmetry is (2+10)/2=6.
  3. v(6)=72−36−20=16. Start and finish values are v(0)=−20, v(12)=−20.

Below the axis means negative velocity, not negative distance.

Worked example · displacement shading

For 20≤t≤26, shade displacement from the start up to a chosen time t.

  1. Shade the area under the graph from t=0 to the chosen t, stopping at t rather than automatically at 26.
  2. Area above the axis contributes positive displacement; area below contributes negative displacement.

Area under a velocity-time graph represents displacement.

Worked example · turning point from acceleration

Given v(0)=0 and a=0.05−0.001t, find when velocity is greatest.

  1. At a velocity maximum, graph gradient is zero, so 0.05−0.001t=0.
  2. 0.001t=0.05 ⇒ t=50.
  3. Before t=50 acceleration is positive and velocity rises; after t=50 acceleration is negative and velocity falls.

Greatest velocity occurs at t=50 s.

Worked example · two-phase pencil check

Key points are (0,0), (4,4.8), (15,4.8), (20,0). The first phase is curved, then constant, then linear.

  1. Curve from (0,0) to (4,4.8).
  2. Horizontal line from (4,4.8) to (15,4.8).
  3. Straight falling line from (15,4.8) to (20,0).

The join times 4 and 15 and velocities 4.8 are labelled.

Worked example · final exam sketch

A particle starts from rest, rises linearly to 12 m s−1 at t=6, stays at 12 until t=10, then falls linearly to rest at t=16.

  1. Join (0,0) to (6,12) with a straight line.
  2. Join (6,12) to (10,12) horizontally.
  3. Join (10,12) to (16,0) with a straight line.

Mark t=6,10,16 and v=12.

Worked example · second exam sketch

For 0≤t≤12, v=12t−t²−20. The rest times are t=2 and 10; the maximum is v=16 at t=6.

  1. Draw an inverted parabola through (0,−20), (2,0), (6,16), (10,0), (12,−20).
  2. State that the graph is below the axis at the start and end, so velocity is negative there.

The graph shape and labelled key points communicate the motion.

More labelled sketches

Keep equal time spacing on the horizontal axis. Curved sections show changing acceleration; straight sections show constant acceleration; horizontal sections show constant velocity.

Key ideas

  1. For each interval, record start and end coordinates.
  2. Choose line, curve or horizontal shape from the velocity rule.
  3. Join continuously when velocity matches; mark a corner when acceleration changes.
  4. Use graph gradient for acceleration and signed area for displacement.
  5. Label joins, rest points, extrema, signs, times and velocities.

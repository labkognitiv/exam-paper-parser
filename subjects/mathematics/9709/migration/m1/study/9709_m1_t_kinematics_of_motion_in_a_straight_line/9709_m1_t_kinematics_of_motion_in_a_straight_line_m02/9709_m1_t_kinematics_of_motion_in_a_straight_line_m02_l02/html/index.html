Two bodies in vertical motion

Use one clock for the question, write both heights from the same zero level, then compare the heights.

StartWorked examplesChecksRecapMore examples

One clock, two heights

  • Height h is vertical displacement above one chosen zero level. h0 is starting height; hP and hQ are the two particle heights.
  • u is initial velocity, v final velocity, t time, and g=10 m s−2 is the gravity approximation used here.
  • Choose upward as positive. A launch upward uses h=h0+ut−5t²; a drop from rest uses h=h0−5t².
  • Use the same clock time t for both particles. The shared clock time t measures from the first launch. If Q starts after delay d, Q has elapsed time t−d. Expanding (t−d)²=t²−2dt+d² keeps that delay visible.
  • Meeting means hP=hQ. Equal heights do not imply equal velocities.
Six steps
  1. Choose one positive direction and zero height.
  2. Choose one clock.
  3. Write P's height.
  4. Write Q's height, including its starting offset and delay.
  5. Set heights equal.
  6. Use each particle's own elapsed time for velocity.

Worked examples

Same launch time

P is projected upward from ground at 18 m/s. Q is dropped from rest from 20 m at the same instant.

  1. hP=18t−5t²; hQ=20−5t².
  2. At a meeting, 18t−5t²=20−5t².
  3. Cancel equal terms: 18t=20, so t=10/9=1.11 s.
  4. h=18(10/9)−5(10/9)²=13.8 m.

They meet after 1.11 s at height 13.8 m.

Delayed projection

P leaves O upward at 20 m/s. Q leaves O upward at 30 m/s one second later.

  1. Let t be time after P starts. P has height hP=20t−5t².
  2. Q has moved for t−1 seconds: hQ=30(t−1)−5(t−1)².
  3. Equate and expand: 20t−5t²=30t−30−5(t²−2t+1)=40t−35−5t².
  4. 20t=35, so t=1.75 s.

Meeting time = 1.75 s after P starts.

Velocities at meeting

Use the same P and Q launches. They meet 1.75 s after P starts; Q has moved 0.75 s.

  1. P: vP=20−10(1.75)=2.5 m/s upward.
  2. Q: vQ=30−10(0.75)=22.5 m/s upward.

P: 2.5 m/s upward; Q: 22.5 m/s upward.

Opposite directions

P and Q launch upward at 16 m/s and 10 m/s from the same point.

  1. P stops rising when 0=16−10t, giving t=1.6 s.
  2. Q stops rising when 0=10−10t, giving t=1.0 s.
  3. Between 1.0 and 1.6 seconds Q is descending while P is still ascending.

They move in opposite directions for 1.0<t<1.6.

Different starting heights

P launches at 15 m/s from ground. Q drops from rest at height 18 m at the same instant.

  1. hP=15t−5t²; hQ=18−5t².
  2. 15t−5t²=18−5t², hence t=1.2 s.
  3. h=15(1.2)−5(1.2)²=10.8 m.

Meeting height = 10.8 m.

After a collision

Two particles move together downward at 4 m/s immediately after colliding at height 12 m.

  1. Start a new model at collision: downward positive, u=4, a=10, s=12.
  2. v²=u²+2as=4²+2(10)(12)=256.
  3. v=16 m/s.

Speed at ground = 16 m/s.

Recap

  • One clock, one zero height, one positive direction.
  • Write each height from its own start point.
  • Use t−delay for a later launch.
  • Equate heights for meeting time.
  • Use each particle's own elapsed time for velocity and state direction.

More examples

Worked example

P launches upward at 14 m/s. Q is dropped from rest at 21 m at the same instant.

  1. hP=14t−5t²; hQ=21−5t².
  2. Equate: 14t−5t²=21−5t², so t=1.5 s.
  3. h=14(1.5)−5(1.5)²=9.75 m.

Meeting after 1.5 s at 9.75 m.

Exam example

P launches upward from O at 18 m/s. One second later Q launches upward from O at 28 m/s.

  1. Use one clock t after P starts: hP=18t−5t².
  2. Q elapsed time is t−1: hQ=28(t−1)−5(t−1)².
  3. Expand: 28(t−1)=28t−28 and (t−1)²=t²−2t+1, so hQ=23t−33−5t².
  4. Equate: 18t−5t²=38t−33−5t², hence 20t=33 and t=1.65 s.
  5. Q moved 0.65 s: vQ=28−10(0.65)=21.5 m/s upward.

Meeting time = 1.65 s; Q velocity = 21.5 m/s upward.

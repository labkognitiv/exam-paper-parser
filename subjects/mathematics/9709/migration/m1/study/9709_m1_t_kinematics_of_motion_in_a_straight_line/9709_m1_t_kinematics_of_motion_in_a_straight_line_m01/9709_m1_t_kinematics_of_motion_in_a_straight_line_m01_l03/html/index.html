Slopes, liquids and changing stages

The four constant-acceleration equations still work when a particle moves on a slope or through a liquid. The key decision is where the acceleration changes.

Start hereWorked examplesChecksRecap

Choose the stage first

  • Displacement s is signed distance along the line.
  • Velocity has speed and direction. u is initial velocity; v is final velocity.
  • Acceleration a is change in velocity per second. Time t is measured in seconds.
  • Units: m means metres, s means seconds, m s−1 means metres per second, and m s−2 means metres per second squared.
  • For constant a: v=u+at, s=ut+½at², v²=u²+2as, s=½(u+v)t.
  • A smooth plane has no friction. θ is the angle to the horizontal; g=10 m s−2 is the lesson approximation for gravity. sin θ is the sine ratio, and the gravity component along the plane is g sin θ.
  • Choose positive direction first. Opposite acceleration gets a minus sign.
Stage rule
  • List u, v, a, s, t for one uniform stage.
  • Use a new set of values when the surface or medium changes.
  • At instantaneous rest, v = 0. The clock continues, so add stage times.

Worked examples

Down a smooth slope

A particle starts from rest on a smooth 30° slope and travels 0.9 m. Find its speed.

  1. Take down the plane as positive.
  2. a = 10 sin 30° = 5 m s−2
  3. u=0, s=0.9,  v2=u2+2as
  4. v2=2(5)(0.9)=9,  v=3

Speed = 3 m s−1.

Up the slope

A particle is projected up a slope at 12 m s−1. Its acceleration is 5.68 m s−2 down the slope. Find the distance before rest.

  1. Take up the slope as positive, so a=−5.68,  u=12,  v=0.
  2. 0=122+2(−5.68)s
  3. s=144/11.36=12.7 m

Distance = 12.7 m. The negative acceleration points down the slope.

Two media, two stages

Use subscripts to keep stages separate: v₁ is the final speed of stage 1 and v₂ is the final speed of stage 2. A particle starts from rest, accelerates at 10 m s−2 through 5 m of air, then at 3.5 m s−2 through a further 4 m of liquid.

  1. Air: v12=0+2(10)(5)=100, so v1=10 m s−1.
  2. Liquid starts with that speed: v22=102+2(3.5)(4)=128.
  3. v2=√128=11.3 m s−1 (3 s.f.).

Surface speed = 10 m s−1; bottom speed = 11.3 m s−1.

Velocity-time graph

On a velocity-time graph, gradient means acceleration. In the air the line goes from (0,0) to (1,10), then in liquid to (2.36,12).

  1. Air: a=(10−0)/(1−0)=10 m s−2.
  2. Liquid: a=(12−10)/(2.36−1)=2/1.36=1.47 m s−2.

The shallower second line shows the smaller acceleration after entering liquid.

Finding an unknown initial speed

A particle moves 0.8 m in 0.6 s and then a further 0.8 m in 1.0 s, with constant acceleration. Find the acceleration and initial speed.

  1. First interval: 0.8=0.6u+½a(0.6)²=0.6u+0.18a.
  2. Total interval: 1.6=1.6u+½a(1.6)²=1.6u+1.28a.
  3. Multiply the first equation by 8: 6.4=4.8u+1.44a. Multiply the second by 3: 4.8=4.8u+3.84a.
  4. Subtract: −1.6=2.40a, so a=−2/3 m s−2.
  5. Substitute: 0.8=0.6u−0.12, so 0.6u=0.92 and u=23/15 m s−1.

Deceleration = 2/3 m s−2; initial speed = 23/15 m s−1.

Recap

  • Choose a positive direction and write signs deliberately.
  • On a smooth slope, a=g sin θ down the slope.
  • Use one suvat set per constant-acceleration stage.
  • At a boundary, the previous final velocity becomes the next initial velocity.
  • Check units, sign and whether the result matches the motion.

Additional worked examples

From rest down a slope

With a=2.5 m s−2 and s=8 m, v2=0+2(2.5)(8)=40, so v=6.32 m s−1.

Stopping up a slope

With u=5.4, v=0, a=−6, 0=5.42−12s, so s=2.43 m.

Liquid after entry

At entry u=10 m s−1; through 4 m at a=3.5, v2=100+28=128, so v=11.3 m s−1.

Exam-style sequence
  1. Smooth 30° slope, released from rest, after 0.8 s: a=5, v=0+5(0.8)=4 m s−1.
  2. Down a slope from u=3 for 8 m at a=2.5: v2=9+40=49, hence v=7 m s−1.
  3. For 0.8 m in the first 0.6 s and a total of 1.6 m in the first 1.6 s, use 0.8=0.6u+0.18a and 1.6=1.6u+1.28a. Multiply to align the u terms: a=−2/3 m s−2; substitution gives u=23/15 m s−1.

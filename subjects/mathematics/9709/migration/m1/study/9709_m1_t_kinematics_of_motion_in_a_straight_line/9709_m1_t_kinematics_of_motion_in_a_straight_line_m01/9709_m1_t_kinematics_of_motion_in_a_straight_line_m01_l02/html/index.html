Mechanics 1 · Kinematics of motion in a straight line

Vertical motion under gravity

Choose a direction, model gravity carefully, and use the same constant-acceleration equations for upward and downward motion.

Start the lesson
SetupMethodExamplesKey ideasKey ideas

Before you calculate

  • Displacement means the signed change in position from the start point. Take it as positive upwards unless the question makes another choice more useful.
  • The model ignores air resistance and uses g = 10 m/s² unless a question states otherwise. Here g is the positive magnitude of gravitational acceleration.
  • Units: m means metres, s means seconds, m/s means metres per second, and m/s² means metres per second squared. An acceleration of −10 m/s² reduces upward velocity by 10 m/s each second.
  • Gravity pulls downwards. Acceleration is the change in velocity each second, so it is a=-g=-10 m s⁻² when upwards is positive.
  • Velocity is the rate of change of displacement, so it has a direction and a sign. Speed is the positive size of velocity.
  • At the greatest height, the particle is at rest for an instant: v=0. It then starts moving down.

Constant-acceleration equations

v=u+at    s=ut+½at²    v²=u²+2as

Here u is initial velocity, v final velocity, a acceleration, t time and s displacement. Choose an equation that leaves out a quantity you do not need and contains the quantity you want to find.

The six-step setup

  1. Draw a vertical line and mark the start and target points.
  2. Choose upwards or downwards as positive.
  3. Use g=10 m s⁻² as the positive magnitude of gravity, then give acceleration a the sign set by your direction choice.
  4. List known u,v,a,s,t, including signs and units: metres (m), seconds (s), and metres per second (m s⁻¹).
  5. Choose an equation that omits a quantity you do not need and includes the quantity you want.
  6. Reject impossible time roots and state direction when velocity is signed.

The equations do not change. Your sign convention changes the values you put into them.

Read the working

  • Displacement s is measured from the launch point. Height above the ground may have a different zero.
  • Adjacent letters mean multiplication: ut means u × t. A raised 2 means square, so t² means t × t. Calculate the square before multiplying by ½a.
  • Rearranging means doing the same operation to both sides. From 0 = 400 − 20s, add 20s to both sides to get 20s = 400, then divide by 20.
  • An equation involving t² is quadratic. Its solutions are called roots. Factoring rewrites the expression as a product of brackets. A product is zero only if at least one factor is zero.
  • For example, (5t + 1)(t − 5) expands to 5t² − 25t + t − 5 = 5t² − 24t − 5. The factors give 5t + 1 = 0 or t − 5 = 0.
  • Subtract 1 and divide by 5 in the first equation to get t = −0.2. Add 5 in the second to get t = 5. Then decide which times belong to the motion.
  • A square root reverses squaring. Both +15 and −15 square to 225, but speed is the non-negative magnitude of velocity.

Worked examples

Worked example

Greatest height

A particle is projected upwards from the ground at 20 m s⁻¹. Find its greatest height.

Solution

  1. Upwards is positive, so u=20, v=0, a=-10.
  2. Use v²=u²+2as because time is not needed.
  3. 0=20²+2(-10)s=400-20s, so s=20 m.

Greatest height = 20 m.

Worked example

Speed at a height

From the ground, a particle is projected upwards at 25 m s⁻¹. Find its speed at height 20 m.

  1. u=25, a=-10, s=20.
  2. v²=25²+2(-10)(20)=225.
  3. v=15 m s⁻¹ for the upward crossing, while the downward crossing has velocity -15 m s⁻¹.

Speed = 15 m s⁻¹.

Dropped from rest

A stone is dropped from rest and falls 45 m. Find its speed.

Solution

  1. Choose downwards as positive. Then u = 0, a = +10 m/s² and s = 45 m.
  2. Time is unused. Use v² = u² + 2as = 0 + 2 × 10 × 45 = 900.
  3. Take the positive square root because the stone moves in our positive direction: v = √900 = 30 m/s.

Speed = 30 m/s downwards.

Worked example

Time to the ground

A particle is projected upwards at 24 m s⁻¹ from a point 5 m above the ground.

  1. The ground is 5 m below the launch point, so the displacement is s=-5, so -5=24t-5t².
  2. 5t²-24t-5=0=(5t+1)(t-5).
  3. The root t=-0.2 is before projection, so reject it. t=5 s.

Time to ground = 5 s.

Worked example

Time above a level

A particle is projected upwards at 20 m s⁻¹. Find the time for which it is higher than 15 m.

  1. Height is s=20t-5t². Set it equal to 15 at each crossing.
  2. 15=20t-5t²⇒ t²-4t+3=0=(t-1)(t-3).
  3. It is above the level between the crossings, so duration 3-1=2 s.

Time above 15 m = 2 s.

Worked example

First and second crossing

A particle is projected upwards at 30 m s⁻¹. Find when it first reaches 33.75 m and its speed then.

  1. 33.75=30t-5t²⇒ 4t²-24t+27=0.
  2. Roots are t=1.5 and 4.5. The first is the upward crossing.
  3. v=30-10(1.5)=15 m s⁻¹.

First time = 1.5 s; speed = 15 m s⁻¹.

Worked example

Total distance and time

A particle is projected upwards at 10 m s⁻¹ from height 15 m.

  1. Rise to the apex: 0=10²-20s⇒ s=5 m.
  2. Distance down to ground is 20 m, so total distance is 5+20=25 m.
  3. Upward time is t=1 s. Downward time from rest through 20 m: 20=5t²⇒ t=2 s.

Total distance = 25 m; total time = 3 s.

Check 2

At the highest point, which statement is correct?

Choose A or B.

Key ideas

  • Choose a positive direction before assigning signs.
  • At the apex, use v=0 for that instant.
  • For a height threshold, solve for both crossing times and subtract.
  • For total distance, add path lengths. Displacement may cancel while distance remains positive.
  • Use the sign of velocity to state rising or falling.

More worked examples

Find a greatest height

A particle is projected vertically upwards at 18 m/s. Find the greatest height above its launch point.

Solution

  1. Upwards positive gives u = 18 m/s, a = −10 m/s². At the highest point v = 0.
  2. Time is unused: 0 = 18² − 20s = 324 − 20s.
  3. Add 20s to both sides: 20s = 324. Divide by 20: s = 16.2 m.

Greatest height above launch = 16.2 m.

Speed above the launch point

A particle is projected upwards at 22 m/s. Find its speed 12 m above its launch point.

Solution

  1. Upwards positive: u = 22 m/s, a = −10 m/s², s = 12 m.
  2. Use v² = u² + 2as: v² = 484 − 240 = 244.
  3. Speed is √244 = 15.620… m/s. Keep the unrounded value until the final answer.
  4. At this height velocity is positive when rising and negative when falling; the speed has the same magnitude.

Speed ≈ 15.6 m/s to three significant figures.

Another drop

A stone is dropped from rest and falls 32 m. Find its speed.

Solution

  1. Choose downwards positive: u = 0, a = +10 m/s² and s = 32 m.
  2. v² = u² + 2as = 0 + 2 × 10 × 32 = 640.
  3. Take the non-negative square root for speed: √640 = 25.298… m/s.

Speed ≈ 25.3 m/s.

When factoring is not convenient

  • For a quadratic At² + Bt + C = 0, the quadratic formula is t = [−B ± √(B² − 4AC)] / (2A).
  • Here A, B and C are the numerical coefficients of t², t and the constant term. These capital letters do not name acceleration.
  • The ± sign asks for two calculations: use plus once and minus once. Evaluate the square root first, then divide the complete numerator by 2A.
  • A negative time is before the chosen start of the motion; it is excluded when finding a time after projection.

Time to the ground from a raised point

A particle is projected upwards at 16 m/s from 3 m above the ground. Find the time to reach the ground.

Solution

  1. Upwards positive: u = 16 m/s, a = −10 m/s² and displacement to the ground s = −3 m.
  2. Use s = ut + ½at²: −3 = 16t − 5t². Add 5t² and subtract 16t to obtain 5t² − 16t − 3 = 0.
  3. In the quadratic formula, A = 5, B = −16 and C = −3. The expression under the square root is (−16)² − 4(5)(−3) = 316.
  4. Thus t = [16 ± √316] / 10. The roots are approximately −0.178 s and 3.378 s.
  5. Reject the negative root, which lies before projection. The positive root describes the ground crossing.

Time to ground ≈ 3.38 s.

Duration above a level

A particle is projected upwards at 24 m/s from ground level. Find how long it is higher than 20 m.

Solution

  1. At each crossing, s = 20, u = 24 and a = −10, so 20 = 24t − 5t².
  2. Rearrange: 5t² − 24t + 20 = 0. The quadratic formula gives t = [24 ± √(576 − 400)] / 10 = (12 ± 2√11)/5.
  3. The crossing times are approximately 1.07335 s and 3.72665 s. The particle is above the level between them.
  4. Subtract the exact roots before rounding: duration = 4√11/5 = 2.65330… s.
  5. Using rounded times too early can change the final decimal. Keep exact values until the final step.

Duration ≈ 2.65 s to three significant figures.

Show a greatest height

A particle is projected upwards from the ground at 30 m/s. Show that its greatest height is 45 m.

Solution

  1. At the highest point, v = 0. Upwards positive gives u = 30 m/s and a = −10 m/s².
  2. Use v² = u² + 2as: 0 = 900 − 20s.
  3. Add 20s, then divide by 20: s = 900/20 = 45 m.

Greatest height = 45 m, as required.

At least a given height

A particle is projected upwards from the ground at 20 m/s. Find the total time for which it is at least 15 m above the ground.

Solution

  1. The boundary crossings satisfy 15 = 20t − 5t².
  2. Move all terms to one side and divide by 5: t² − 4t + 3 = 0.
  3. Factor as (t − 1)(t − 3) = 0, giving t = 1 s and t = 3 s.
  4. “At least” includes both boundary instants: 1 ≤ t ≤ 3. The length of that interval is 3 − 1 = 2 s.

Total time = 2 s.

Distance from an elevated launch

A particle is projected upwards at 12 m/s from 8 m above the ground. Find total distance before reaching the ground.

Solution

  1. First find the upward rise. With upwards positive, u = 12 m/s, v = 0 and a = −10 m/s².
  2. 0 = 12² − 20s gives 20s = 144, so the rise is 7.2 m.
  3. At the top it is 8 + 7.2 = 15.2 m above ground, so it then travels downwards 15.2 m.
  4. Distance adds both path lengths: 7.2 + 15.2 = 22.4 m. Overall displacement is −8 m, a different quantity.

Total distance = 22.4 m.

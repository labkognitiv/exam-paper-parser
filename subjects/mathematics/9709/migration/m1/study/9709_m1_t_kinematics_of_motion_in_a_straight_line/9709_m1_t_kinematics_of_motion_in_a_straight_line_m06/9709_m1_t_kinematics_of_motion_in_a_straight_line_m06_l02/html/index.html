Mathematics 9709 · Mechanics 1

Evaluate velocity and displacement by integration

Build velocity from acceleration, build displacement from velocity, and use limits carefully.

Set upWorked examplesChecksRecap

Acceleration tells how velocity changes. Velocity tells how position changes. Integration reverses each relationship.

The integration chain

For the power rule, n must not equal −1: integrate tn by raising the power to n+1 and dividing by n+1. The constant C records an unknown starting value; use v(0) or s(0) before substituting a time. A fractional power such as t1/2 means the square root of t, so t1/2 becomes t3/2 after integration and is divided by 3/2.

  • Integrate a(t) to obtain v(t), then substitute a time.
  • Integrate v(t) to obtain s(t), then substitute a time.
  • Each indefinite integration introduces a constant. Use an initial condition such as v(0)=0 or s(0)=0 to find it.
  • Displacement from time a to time b is s(b)−s(a); the constant cancels.
  • Velocity may be negative. Speed is its magnitude. Keep units: velocity m s−1, acceleration m s−2, displacement m.

Power rule

∫tⁿdt=tⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)+C

Increase the power by one, then divide by the new power. For example, ∫3t²dt=t³ and ∫4t1/2dt=(8/3)t3/2.

Worked examples

Worked example · build velocity

a(t)=3t²−4t. Find velocity at t=2, leaving the initial velocity unspecified.

  1. v=∫(3t²−4t)dt=t³−2t²+C₁.
  2. At t=2, v(2)=8−8+C₁=C₁ m s−1.

The initial condition is needed to give a numerical velocity.

For v(t)=t³−2t²+6, displacement is s(t)=¼t⁴−(2/3)t³+6t+C₂. At t=3 this is 81/4−18+18+C₂=81/4+C₂ m.

Worked example · evaluate velocity

A particle starts from rest with a=2t+3. Find velocity at t=4.

  1. v=∫(2t+3)dt=t²+3t+C.
  2. Starts from rest, so v(0)=0=C. Thus v=t²+3t.
  3. v(4)=4²+3(4)=28.

Velocity = 28 m s−1.

Worked example · evaluate displacement

A particle starts from O with v=0.5t²+2. Find displacement at t=6.

  1. s=∫(0.5t²+2)dt=(1/6)t³+2t+C.
  2. Starts at O: s(0)=0, so C=0.
  3. s(6)=216/6+12=36+12=48.

Displacement = 48 m.

Worked example · use limits

Given v=6t−2, find displacement from t=1 to t=4.

  1. S(t)=∫(6t−2)dt=3t²−2t.
  2. S(4)−S(1)=(48−8)−(3−2)=40−1=39.
  3. The constant is absent because it cancels in the difference.

Displacement = 39 m.

Worked example · solve time then substitute

A particle starts from rest with a=0.5t. Find when its velocity is 16 m s−1, then displacement.

  1. v=∫0.5t,dt=0.25t²+C; v(0)=0 gives C=0.
  2. 0.25t²=16→ t²=64→ t=8 s. Choose the positive time.
  3. s=∫0.25t²dt=t³/12+C; s(0)=0 gives C=0.
  4. s(8)=512/12=42.666….

Time 8 s; displacement 42.7 m to 3 s.f.

Worked example · fractional power

A particle starts from rest with a=3t1/2. Find velocity at t=9.

  1. v=∫3t1/2dt=3(2/3)t3/2+C=2t3/2+C.
  2. Rest at t=0 gives C=0.
  3. v(9)=2(93/2)=2(27)=54.

Velocity = 54 m s−1.

Worked example · join value

For 0≤t≤4, a=0.6t, starting from rest. Find speed at t=4.

  1. v=∫0.6t,dt=0.3t²+C.
  2. v(0)=0→ C=0, so v(4)=0.3(16)=4.8.

Speed at the join = 4.8 m s−1; this value starts the next phase.

Worked example · interval displacement

For 0≤t≤5, v=12−2t. Find displacement during the first 5 s.

  1. s=∫₀⁵(12−2t)dt=[12t−t²]₀⁵.
  2. s=(60−25)−0=35.

Displacement = 35 m. Since velocity stays positive, distance is also 35 m.

Worked example · Worked example

A particle starts from rest with a=5−t. Find velocity and displacement at t=4.

  1. v=∫(5−t)dt=5t−0.5t²+C₁; v(0)=0 gives C₁=0.
  2. v(4)=20−8=12 m s−1.
  3. s=∫(5t−0.5t²)dt=2.5t²−t³/6+C₂; s(0)=0 gives C₂=0.
  4. s(4)=40−64/6=29.333….

Velocity = 12 m s−1; displacement = 29.3 m.

Worked example · interval warning

Given v=0.5t²+2, find displacement from t=2 to t=6.

  1. S(t)=t³/6+2t+C.
  2. S(6)−S(2)=(36+12)−(8/6+4)=42.666….

Displacement = 42.7 m. The lower limit must be t=2, not t=0.

Worked example · fractional power exam

A particle starts from rest with a=4t1/2. Find speed and displacement at t=9.

  1. v=∫4t1/2dt=(8/3)t3/2; v(9)=(8/3)(27)=72.
  2. s=∫(8/3)t3/2dt=(16/15)t5/2.
  3. s(9)=(16/15)(243)=3888/15=259.2.

Speed = 72 m s−1; displacement = 259.2 m.

Worked example · final interval

v=t²−6t+8. Find displacement from t=1 to t=5.

  1. S(t)=t³/3−3t²+8t.
  2. S(5)=125/3−75+40=20/3; S(1)=1/3−3+8=16/3.
  3. S(5)−S(1)=4/3.

Displacement = 4/3 m. Keep the sign because displacement, not distance, was requested.

Key ideas

  1. Integrate acceleration to get velocity and use an initial velocity to find the constant.
  2. Integrate velocity to get displacement and use an initial position to find the constant.
  3. For an interval, calculate S(final)−S(initial).
  4. Apply the power rule carefully, including fractional powers.
  5. Substitute the time only after building the required expression, then attach units.

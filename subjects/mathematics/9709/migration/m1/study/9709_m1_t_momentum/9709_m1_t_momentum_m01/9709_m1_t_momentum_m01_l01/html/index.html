Momentum and direct collisions

Track mass and direction before and after an impact.

Picture two trolleys meeting on one straight track.

Momentum measures motion

  • A heavier object has more momentum at the same velocity.
  • A faster object has more momentum at the same mass.
  • Velocity includes direction, so momentum includes direction too.

Definition

Linear momentum is mass multiplied by velocity.

Formula

  • p means momentum.
  • m means mass, measured in kilograms, written kg.
  • v means velocity, measured in metres per second.
  • m s^-1 means metres per second; the power −1 means “per second”.
p = mv
  • Read this as “momentum equals mass multiplied by velocity”.
  • Momentum is measured in kg m s^-1, also written N s.

Choose a sign before calculating

  • Choose one direction as positive. Right is often convenient.
  • A velocity in the opposite direction is negative.
  • Speed is only a size. A negative result for velocity means motion opposite to the positive direction.
  • When a particle rebounds, it reverses direction, so its velocity changes sign.

Worked example

A particle has mass 0.20 kg and velocity 2.0 m s−1 to the right. Find its momentum.

  1. Use p = mv.
  2. Substitute the mass and signed velocity: p = 0.20 × 2.0.
  3. Calculate: p = 0.40 kg m s^-1.

Answer: 0.40 kg m s−1 to the right.

Conservation during a collision

  • The system is the pair of particles considered together.
  • During a brief collision, the particles push on each other.
  • An impulse changes momentum. An external impulse comes from something outside the system.
  • If no external impulse acts during the collision, total linear momentum stays constant.
  • Add every signed momentum before the collision. Set that total equal to the sum after.

Conservation of linear momentum

  • u means velocity before the collision.
  • v means velocity after the collision.
  • The small lower numbers, called subscripts, label particle 1 and particle 2.
  • A stationary particle has velocity 0, so its momentum is 0.
m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2

This applies when the external impulse on the two-particle system is zero during the collision.

Worked examples

Worked example

A 0.3 kg particle P moves right at 4 m s−1 and collides directly with a stationary 0.2 kg particle Q. Afterwards Q moves right at 3 m s−1. Find P's velocity v.

  1. Choose right as positive.
  2. Write initial momentum = final momentum.
  3. 0.3(4)+0.2(0)=0.3v+0.2(3)
  4. 1.2 = 0.3v + 0.6.
  5. Subtract 0.6 from both sides: 0.6 = 0.3v.
  6. Divide both sides by 0.3: v = 2.

Answer: P moves right at 2 m s−1.

Key ideas

  • p = mv, with unit kg m s−1 or N s.
  • Choose a positive direction before writing velocities.
  • Opposite motion gets a negative velocity.
  • With no external impulse: total momentum before = total momentum after.
  • Use the sign of a solved velocity to state its direction.

More worked examples

Worked example

A particle has momentum 4 N s and kinetic energy 16 J. Find its speed and mass.

  • Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it is moving.
  • It is written E_k and measured in joules, written J.
  • The small lower k labels kinetic energy.
  • In u², the square means u × u.
  • The relationship is E_k = 1/2 mu².
  • Here mu = 4, so m = 4/u.
  1. Substitute into kinetic energy: 16 = 1/2(4/u)u².
  2. Cancel one factor of u: 16 = 2u.
  3. Divide by 2: u = 8 m s^-1.
  4. Then m = 4/8 = 0.5 kg.

Answer: speed 8 m s−1; mass 0.5 kg.

Worked example

A 0.4 kg particle A moves right at 5 m s−1 and collides with a stationary 0.6 kg particle B. Afterwards A continues right at 2 m s−1. Find B's speed.

  1. Choose right as positive.
  2. 0.4(5)+0.6(0)=0.4(2)+0.6v.
  3. 2.0 = 0.8 + 0.6v.
  4. 1.2 = 0.6v, so v = 2.0.

Answer: B moves right at 2.0 m s−1.

Worked example

A 0.6 kg particle P moves right at 3 m s−1 and collides with a stationary 0.4 kg particle Q. Afterwards P continues right at 1.5 m s−1. Find Q's speed.

  1. 0.6(3)+0=0.6(1.5)+0.4v.
  2. 1.8 = 0.9 + 0.4v.
  3. 0.9 = 0.4v, so v = 2.25.

Answer: Q moves right at 2.25 m s−1.

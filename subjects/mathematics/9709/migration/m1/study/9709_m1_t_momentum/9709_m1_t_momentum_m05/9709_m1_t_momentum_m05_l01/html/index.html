Kinetic energy loss in collisions

Find the motion first. Then account for the energy.

Start the lesson
EnergyPlanKey ideasMore examples

Momentum first, energy second

  • Kinetic energy is the energy a moving body has. It is measured in joules, written J.
  • For mass m and speed v, E_k=\frac12mv^2. The square applies to the speed.
  • Momentum is mass multiplied by velocity. Velocity carries a direction sign.
  • An external impulse is the change in momentum caused by forces outside the colliding pair. When it is negligible during the short collision, total momentum is conserved.
  • Let m_P,m_Q be the two masses, u the initial velocity of P, and v_P,v_Q their velocities after collision.
  • Stationary means at rest, so velocity is zero.

m_Pu+m_Q(0)=m_Pv_P+m_Qv_Q

  • Total kinetic energy means the sum for every body.
  • If no energy is released or supplied during the collision, total kinetic energy after cannot exceed total kinetic energy before.
  • \text{KE lost}=\text{KE before}-\text{KE after}

Find the speeds first. Then do the energy accounting.

A reliable five-step plan

  1. Write one momentum equation.
  2. Solve for the missing speed.
  3. Find total kinetic energy before.
  4. Find total kinetic energy after.
  5. Subtract: before minus after.

For bodies 1 and 2, m_1,m_2 are their masses, u_1,u_2 are their velocities before collision, and v_1,v_2 are their velocities after collision.

m_1u_1+m_2u_2=m_1v_1+m_2v_2

\text{KE before}=\frac12m_1u_1^2+\frac12m_2u_2^2

\text{KE after}=\frac12m_1v_1^2+\frac12m_2v_2^2

  • Use the same sign for the same direction throughout.
  • Use a negative velocity for the opposite direction.
  • Square every speed in a kinetic energy calculation.

Worked example

Find one unknown speed

P has mass 3 kg and moves at 7 m s−1. It hits stationary Q of mass 2 kg. P then moves at 3 m s−1 in the same direction.

Solution

  1. Momentum: 3(7)+2(0)=3(3)+2v.
  2. 21=9+2v\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=6\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. Kinetic energy before: \frac12(3)(7^2)=73.5\text{ J}.
  4. Kinetic energy after: \frac12(3)(3^2)+\frac12(2)(6^2)=49.5\text{ J}.
  5. Loss: 73.5-49.5=24\text{ J}.

Answer: v=6\text{ m s}^{-1} and kinetic energy lost 24\text{ J}.

Worked example

Coalescence

A 0.4 kg body moving at 6 m s−1 catches a 0.2 kg body moving at 3 m s−1 in the same direction. They coalesce, so they join and move as one body.

Solution

  1. Momentum: 0.4(6)+0.2(3)=(0.4+0.2)v.
  2. 3=0.6v\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=5\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. Kinetic energy before: \frac12(0.4)(6^2)+\frac12(0.2)(3^2)=8.1\text{ J}.
  4. Kinetic energy after uses one combined mass: \frac12(0.6)(5^2)=7.5\text{ J}.
  5. Loss: 8.1-7.5=0.6\text{ J}.

Answer: common speed 5\text{ m s}^{-1}; loss 0.6\text{ J}.

Worked example

Opposite directions

A has mass 5 kg and moves right at 4 m s−1. B has mass 1 kg and moves left at 2 m s−1. After collision B moves right at 6 m s−1.

Solution

  1. Choose right as positive.
  2. Momentum: 5(4)+1(-2)=5v+1(6).
  3. 18=5v+6\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=2.4\text{ m s}^{-1} to the right.
  4. Kinetic energy before: \frac12(5)(4^2)+\frac12(1)(2^2)=42\text{ J}. The negative velocity is squared.
  5. Kinetic energy after: \frac12(5)(2.4^2)+\frac12(1)(6^2)=32.4\text{ J}.
  6. Loss: 42-32.4=9.6\text{ J}.

Answer: A moves right at 2.4\text{ m s}^{-1}; loss 9.6\text{ J}.

Key ideas

  • Use signed velocities in momentum.
  • Use squared speeds in kinetic energy.
  • Find all missing speeds before comparing energies.
  • Add every body’s kinetic energy before and after.
  • Kinetic energy lost equals total before minus total after.

More worked examples

Worked example

A speed difference after collision

A has mass 6 kg and moves right at 5 m s−1. B has mass 2 kg and moves left at 3 m s−1. After collision both move right, and B is 2 m s−1 faster than A.

Solution

  1. Let A’s speed be v, so B’s speed is v+2.
  2. Momentum: 6(5)+2(-3)=6v+2(v+2).
  3. 24=8v+4\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=2.5, so B’s speed is 4.5.
  4. Kinetic energy before: \frac12(6)(5^2)+\frac12(2)(3^2)=84\text{ J}.
  5. Kinetic energy after: \frac12(6)(2.5^2)+\frac12(2)(4.5^2)=39\text{ J}.
  6. Loss: 84-39=45\text{ J}.

Answer: speeds 2.5\text{ m s}^{-1} and 4.5\text{ m s}^{-1}; loss 45\text{ J}.

Worked example

Two possible directions

A has mass 4 kg and moves at 6 m s−1 towards stationary B of mass m kg. After collision A has speed 1.5 m s−1 and B has speed 3 m s−1. Find the kinetic energy loss for each possible direction of A.

Case 1: A continues

A → 1.5 m s−1

B → 3 m s−1

Case 2: A rebounds

A ← 1.5 m s−1

B → 3 m s−1

Solution

  1. Case 1, A continues forward. 4(6)=4(1.5)+3m, so m=6\text{ kg}.
  2. Loss: 72-[\tfrac12(4)(1.5^2)+\tfrac12(6)(3^2)]=40.5\text{ J}.
  3. Case 2, A rebounds. Its velocity is -1.5, so 4(6)=4(-1.5)+3m and m=10\text{ kg}.
  4. Loss: 72-[\tfrac12(4)(1.5^2)+\tfrac12(10)(3^2)]=22.5\text{ J}.

Answer: 40.5\text{ J} if A continues; 22.5\text{ J} if A rebounds.

Worked example

Watch the squares

Consider two energy calculations. Their masses differ, so they cannot describe the same two particles before and after one collision.

Solution

  1. Do not use \frac12(3)(2)+\frac12(2)(5). Each speed must be squared.
  2. First calculation: \frac12(5)(4^2)+\frac12(3)(2^2)=46\text{ J}.
  3. Second calculation: \frac12(3)(2^2)+\frac12(2)(5^2)=31\text{ J}.
  4. Their numerical difference is 46-31=15\text{ J}. Because the mass inventory changes, it is not a physically valid collision-energy loss.

Answer: the two evaluations are 46\text{ J} and 31\text{ J}; their numerical difference is 15\text{ J}.

Try it yourself

Try it yourself

A 2 kg body moving at 8 m s−1 hits a stationary 3 kg body. The 2 kg body continues in the same direction at 2 m s−1. Find the other speed and the kinetic energy loss.

Solution

  1. Momentum: 2(8)=2(2)+3v.
  2. 16=4+3v\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=4\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. Kinetic energy before: \frac12(2)(8^2)=64\text{ J}.
  4. Kinetic energy after: \frac12(2)(2^2)+\frac12(3)(4^2)=28\text{ J}.
  5. Loss: 64-28=36\text{ J}.

Answer: other speed 4\text{ m s}^{-1}; loss 36\text{ J}.

Exam-style example

Exam-style example

P has mass 0.5 kg and speed v. It hits stationary Q of mass 1.5 kg. After collision P has speed w and Q has speed 2w, both in the original direction. The kinetic energy loss is 24 J. Find v and w.

Solution

  1. Momentum: 0.5v=0.5w+1.5(2w), hence v=7w.
  2. Write the loss equation: \frac12(0.5)v^2-[\frac12(0.5)w^2+\frac12(1.5)(2w)^2]=24.
  3. Simplify: 0.25v^2-3.25w^2=24.
  4. Substitute v=7w: 0.25(7w)^2-3.25w^2=24.
  5. 9w^2=24\quad\Rightarrow\quad w^2=\frac83. Speeds are non-negative, so take the positive square root.
  6. w=\sqrt{\frac83},\qquad v=7\sqrt{\frac83}.

Answer: w=\sqrt{\frac83}\text{ m s}^{-1}, v=7\sqrt{\frac83}\text{ m s}^{-1}.

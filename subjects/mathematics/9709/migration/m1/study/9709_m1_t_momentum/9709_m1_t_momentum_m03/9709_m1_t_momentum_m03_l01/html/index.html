Direction, rebound and unknown mass

Use one sign convention from before impact to after impact.

Start the lesson
SignsReboundKey ideasMore examples

Choose the positive direction

  • Speed tells us how fast a body moves. It is never negative; it is zero at rest.
  • Velocity is speed with direction.
  • Momentum is mass multiplied by velocity, so momentum also has a direction.
  • Choose one direction as positive before writing any momentum.
  • A velocity in the opposite direction is negative.
  • Mass stays positive. The sign belongs to velocity.
  • During each short impact, take the external impulse as negligible: forces outside the colliding pair do not appreciably change its total momentum.
  • Use v for an unknown velocity after impact, unless another symbol is named.
  • For example, with right positive, a 0.4 kg body moving right at 3 m s−1 and a 0.2 kg body moving left at 5 m s−1 have total momentum 0.4(3)+0.2(-5)=0.2\text{ kg m s}^{-1} to the right.

Write the signs before solving.

Read the motion after impact

  • Rebounds means a body moves back the way it came.
  • Its signed velocity therefore changes sign.
  • Its speed after rebound need not equal its speed before impact. Write +u before and -w after when the two speeds are different.
  • Instantaneously at rest means its velocity is zero at that moment.
  • A negative answer gives direction: translate it into words.

Worked example

Same line, opposite directions

P has mass 0.25 kg and velocity 0.8 m s−1 right. Q has mass 0.40 kg and velocity 1.5 m s−1 left. After collision Q is at rest. Find P’s velocity after the collision.

Solution

  1. Choose right as positive. P starts at +0.8\text{ m s}^{-1} and Q starts at -1.5\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  2. Q is at rest afterwards, so its final momentum is zero.
  3. 0.25(0.8)+0.40(-1.5)=0.25v+0
  4. 0.20-0.60=0.25v
  5. v=-1.6\text{ m s}^{-1}. The negative result means left.

Answer: 1.6\text{ m s}^{-1} to the left.

Worked example

Rebound after impact

P has mass 0.12 kg and moves right at 6 m s−1. It hits stationary Q of mass 0.18 kg. P rebounds at 1.5 m s−1. Find Q’s speed.

Solution

  1. Choose right as positive.
  2. P rebounds to the left, so its final velocity is -1.5\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. 0.12(6)+0=0.12(-1.5)+0.18v
  4. 0.72=-0.18+0.18v
  5. 0.90=0.18v\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=5

Answer: 5\text{ m s}^{-1} to the right.

Energy check: kinetic energy, the energy of motion, is ½ × mass × speed². It rises from 2.16 J to 2.385 J for these stated velocities. This requires 0.225 J released from another energy store; the momentum answer alone does not establish an ordinary passive collision.

Worked example

Unknown mass from a rebound

A has mass m kg and moves right at 4 m s−1. It hits stationary B of mass 0.30 kg. A rebounds at 1 m s−1; B moves right at 2.5 m s−1.

Solution

  1. Choose right as positive. A’s signed velocities are +4 before and -1 after.
  2. m(4)+0=m(-1)+0.30(2.5)
  3. 4m=-m+0.75
  4. Add m to both sides: 5m=0.75.
  5. Divide both sides by 5: m=0.15.

Answer: m=0.15\text{ kg}.

Worked example

When both bodies stop

A has mass 3.0 kg and moves right at u m s−1. B has mass 2.0 kg and moves left at 4 m s−1. After collision, both are instantaneously at rest.

Solution

  1. Choose right as positive.
  2. Before collision, A has velocity +u and B has velocity -4.
  3. After collision, both velocities are zero. Both final momentum terms remain in the equation.
  4. 3.0u+2.0(-4)=3.0(0)+2.0(0)
  5. 3u-8=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad u=\frac83

Answer: u=\frac83\text{ m s}^{-1}.

Check 1

Upward is positive. A ball rebounds downward. What sign has its velocity after impact?

Key ideas

  • Choose one positive direction and keep it throughout the collision.
  • Use signed velocity in every momentum term.
  • A rebound reverses the sign of that body’s velocity.
  • At rest means velocity zero; keep the body in the equation.
  • Use momentum before = momentum after, then interpret the answer’s sign.

More worked examples

Worked example

Show a velocity with direction

A has mass 0.6 kg and moves upward at 12 m s−1. B has mass 0.4 kg and moves downward at 18 m s−1. After collision B is at rest. Show A’s velocity.

Solution

  1. Choose upward as positive.
  2. Initial momentum: 0.6(12)+0.4(-18)=7.2-7.2=0.
  3. Final momentum: 0.6v+0.
  4. 0.6v=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=0
  5. Zero velocity has no direction because A is instantaneously at rest.

Answer: 0\text{ m s}^{-1}.

Worked example

Linked collisions with an unknown

P has mass m kg and moves right at 6 m s−1. It hits stationary Q of mass 0.4 kg. P continues right at 2 m s−1, while Q moves at v. Q then hits stationary R of mass 0.8 kg. Q stops and R moves right at 1.2 m s−1.

Solution

  1. First collision: m(6)+0=m(2)+0.4v.
  2. Subtract 2m: 4m=0.4v, so v=10m.
  3. Use this expression in the second collision.
  4. 0.4(10m)+0=0+0.8(1.2)
  5. 4m=0.96\quad\Rightarrow\quad m=0.24

Answer: m=0.24\text{ kg}.

Worked example

Your turn

A has mass 0.20 kg and moves right at 7 m s−1. B has mass 0.30 kg and moves left at 2 m s−1. After collision A rebounds left at 1 m s−1. Find B’s velocity.

Solution

  1. Choose right as positive.
  2. 0.20(7)+0.30(-2)=0.20(-1)+0.30v
  3. 1.4-0.6=-0.2+0.30v
  4. 1.0=0.30v\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=\frac{10}{3}

Answer: \frac{10}{3}\text{ m s}^{-1} to the right.

Worked example

Exam-style example

P has mass m kg and moves right at 5 m s−1. It hits stationary Q of mass 0.25 kg. P rebounds at 0.5 m s−1, while Q moves right at 1.1 m s−1.

  • (a) Find the mass m.
  • (b) Explain why P’s velocity after impact is negative in your equation.

Solution

  1. Choose right as positive. P’s final velocity is -0.5\text{ m s}^{-1} because it rebounds.
  2. m(5)+0=m(-0.5)+0.25(1.1)
  3. 5m=-0.5m+0.275
  4. 5.5m=0.275\quad\Rightarrow\quad m=0.05
  5. P’s velocity after impact is negative because it moves opposite to the chosen positive direction.

Answer: m=0.05\text{ kg}.

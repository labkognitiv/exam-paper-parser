Momentum with kinematics and dynamics

Link the motion before impact, the collision, and the motion after impact.

Start the lesson

Three stages, three models

  • Kinematics describes how position and velocity change. Acceleration is the change in velocity per second.
  • Momentum is mass multiplied by signed velocity. An external impulse is the momentum change caused by forces outside the colliding pair.
  • A smooth surface is modelled with no friction. The resultant force is the total force after accounting for directions.
  • The letter g denotes gravitational acceleration, acting vertically downward. On a slope at angle θ to the horizontal, the component of weight down the slope is mg sin θ.
  • Before impact: use kinematics, Newton’s second law or energy to find each impact speed.
  • During impact: choose a positive direction and use signed momentum. The collision is brief, so external impulse is usually negligible.
  • After impact: reset the clock if needed, then use kinematics, energy or a meeting condition.
  • Newton’s second law, N2L, is F=ma: resultant force equals mass times acceleration.
  • SUVAT links displacement s, initial velocity u, final velocity v, acceleration a and time t for constant acceleration.
  • Useful equations include v^2=u^2+2as, v=u+at and s=ut+\frac12at^2.
  • Unless stated otherwise, use g=10\text{ m s}^{-2}.

Worked example

Three stages in one problem

A 0.4 kg particle slides from rest for 1.8 m down a smooth 30° slope. It hits a stationary 0.2 kg particle. After impact the 0.4 kg particle continues at 2 m s−1. Find the other speed.

Solution

  1. On a smooth slope, Newton’s second law gives 0.4g\sin30^\circ=0.4a, so a=5\text{ m s}^{-2}.
  2. Use v^2=u^2+2as: v^2=0+2(5)(1.8)=18, so the impact speed is \sqrt{18}\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. Take right as positive. Momentum during impact gives 0.4\sqrt{18}+0=0.4(2)+0.2v.
  4. v=\frac{0.4\sqrt{18}-0.8}{0.2}=2\sqrt{18}-4.

Answer: v=2\sqrt{18}-4\approx4.49\text{ m s}^{-1} to the right.

Build the collision chain

Worked example

First find the impact speed

P has mass 0.30 kg and slides from rest 2.5 m down a smooth 30° slope. It then hits stationary Q of mass 0.20 kg. P continues at 2.0 m s−1. Find Q’s speed.

Solution

  1. Along the slope, 0.30g\sin30^\circ=0.30a, so a=5.
  2. v^2=0^2+2(5)(2.5)=25, so P reaches Q at 5\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. Momentum: 0.30(5)+0=0.30(2.0)+0.20w.
  4. 1.50=0.60+0.20w\quad\Rightarrow\quad w=4.5.

Answer: 4.5\text{ m s}^{-1}.

Worked example

Momentum along a slope

A and B have masses 0.4 kg and 0.2 kg. Just before impact, they move down the same smooth slope at 3 m s−1 and 2 m s−1. After impact A moves down at 2.5 m s−1. Find B’s speed.

Solution

  1. Take down the slope as positive.
  2. The impact is brief, so the impulse from gravity during it is negligible. Conserve momentum along the slope.
  3. 0.4(3)+0.2(2)=0.4(2.5)+0.2v.
  4. 1.6=1.0+0.2v\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=3.

Answer: 3\text{ m s}^{-1} down the slope.

Worked example

When particles coalesce

P has mass m and velocity 5 m s−1. Q has mass 2m and velocity 4 m s−1. They coalesce, meaning they stick together and share one velocity.

Solution

  1. Same direction: m(5)+2m(4)=3mv, so v=\frac{13}{3}\approx4.33\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  2. Q in the opposite direction: write its velocity as -4.
  3. m(5)+2m(-4)=3mv\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=-1\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  4. The negative result means the combined particle moves opposite to the chosen positive direction.

Answer: \frac{13}{3}\text{ m s}^{-1} in the same-direction case; 1\text{ m s}^{-1} opposite in the second case.

Worked example

A vertical meeting

A is projected upward from O at 30 m s−1. At the same instant, B is released from rest 15 m above O. Take upward as positive. They collide after 0.5 s.

Solution

  1. Both particles have acceleration -10\text{ m s}^{-2}, so their relative acceleration is zero.
  2. Their initial closing speed is 30\text{ m s}^{-1}, so they close 15 m in 15/30=0.5\text{ s}.
  3. A’s velocity at impact: v_A=30-10(0.5)=25\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  4. B’s velocity at impact: v_B=0-10(0.5)=-5\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  5. A’s displacement is s=30(0.5)-5(0.5)^2=13.75\text{ m}. B has fallen 1.25 m, so it is also at 13.75 m above O.

Answer: impact velocities +25\text{ m s}^{-1} and -5\text{ m s}^{-1} at height 13.75\text{ m}.

Check 1

A particle rebounds up a slope when down the slope is positive. Which post-impact velocity is possible?

Key ideas

  • Choose the positive direction before writing momentum.
  • Find every speed immediately before impact.
  • Use signed velocities during the collision.
  • For coalescence, use total mass times one common velocity.
  • Reset t=0 at impact when time is measured from the collision.
  • Check whether the answer asks for speed, velocity, time, distance or angle.

More worked examples

Worked example

Continue the motion after impact

Two equal masses move upward at 45 m s−1 and 75 m s−1, then coalesce. The collision is 218.75 m above O and occurs 3.5 s after the original start. Find the common speed and total time until the combined particle reaches O.

Solution

  1. Momentum: m(45)+m(75)=2mv, so v=60\text{ m s}^{-1} upward.
  2. Reset the post-collision clock. Now u=60, a=-10 and s=-218.75.
  3. -218.75=60t-5t^2, so 5t^2-60t-218.75=0.
  4. t=\frac{60\pm\sqrt{7975}}{10}. The roots are approximately 14.9303 and -2.9303.
  5. Reject the negative root because it lies before the post-collision clock starts.
  6. Add the separate 3.5 s before collision: 3.5+14.9303=18.4303\text{ s}.

Answer: common speed 60\text{ m s}^{-1} upward; total time 18.4\text{ s} to 3 s.f.

Worked example

Meeting again after collision

After collision, A moves from the same point at constant speed 0.60 m s−1. B starts at 0.12 m s−1 with acceleration 0.16 m s−2 in the same direction. Find when B catches A.

Solution

  1. Reset t=0 at the collision.
  2. A’s displacement: s_A=0.60t.
  3. B’s displacement: s_B=0.12t+\frac12(0.16)t^2=0.12t+0.08t^2.
  4. At a meeting, positions are equal: 0.60t=0.12t+0.08t^2.
  5. t(0.48-0.08t)=0, giving t=0 or t=6. The zero root is their starting meeting.
  6. At 6 s, s=0.60(6)=3.6\text{ m}.

Answer: B catches A after 6\text{ s}, 3.6\text{ m} from the collision point.

Worked example

Barrier and rebound

B moves down a smooth 30° slope at 3 m s−1, then travels 1.6 m to a barrier with acceleration 5 m s−2. After rebound its speed is reduced to 10% of its impact speed.

Solution

  1. Take down the slope as positive.
  2. v^2=3^2+2(5)(1.6)=25, so the speed at the barrier is 5\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. Ten percent means 0.10\times5=0.5\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  4. Rebound reverses direction, so the signed velocity is negative.

Answer: rebound velocity -0.5\text{ m s}^{-1}.

Worked example

Work and energy after impact

Q has mass 0.10 kg and speed 10 m s−1. It collides and coalesces with stationary R of mass 0.40 kg. The combined particle travels 0.40 m up a smooth slope and stops. Find the slope angle θ.

Solution

  1. Momentum: 0.10(10)=(0.10+0.40)v, so v=2\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  2. Kinetic energy is energy of motion: E_k=\frac12mv^2. Just after collision it is \frac12(0.50)(2^2)=1\text{ J}.
  3. Raising a mass through vertical height h stores gravitational potential energy. The energy gained is mgh. Here h=0.40\sin\theta, so the gain is (0.50)(10)(0.40)\sin\theta=2\sin\theta J.
  4. The slope is smooth, so kinetic energy becomes potential energy: 1=2\sin\theta.
  5. \sin\theta=0.5\quad\Rightarrow\quad\theta=30^\circ.

Answer: \theta=30^\circ.

Try it yourself

Try it yourself

P has mass 0.50 kg and slides from rest 1.6 m down a smooth 30° slope. It collides with stationary Q of mass 0.30 kg. P continues at 1.0 m s−1. Find Q’s speed.

Solution

  1. Along the slope, a=g\sin30^\circ=5\text{ m s}^{-2}.
  2. v^2=0^2+2(5)(1.6)=16, so P’s impact speed is 4\text{ m s}^{-1}.
  3. Momentum: 0.50(4)+0=0.50(1.0)+0.30w.
  4. 2.0=0.5+0.30w\quad\Rightarrow\quad w=5.0.

Answer: 5.0\text{ m s}^{-1}.

Exam-style example

Exam-style example

A and B have masses 0.20 kg and 0.40 kg. Immediately before they collide and coalesce 12 m above the ground, A moves upward at 18 m s−1 and B downward at 6 m s−1. Find their speed just after collision and at the ground.

Solution

  1. Take upward as positive.
  2. 0.20(18)+0.40(-6)=0.60v.
  3. 3.6-2.4=0.60v\quad\Rightarrow\quad v=2\text{ m s}^{-1} upward.
  4. For the motion to the ground, u=2, a=-10 and s=-12.
  5. v^2=2^2+2(-10)(-12)=244.
  6. Speed is the non-negative magnitude: v=\sqrt{244}\approx15.6\text{ m s}^{-1}. The velocity at the ground is downward.

Answer: 2\text{ m s}^{-1} upward after collision; ground speed 15.6\text{ m s}^{-1}.

Check 2

After impact, two particles start from the same point. At their next meeting, which condition must hold?

Final checklist

  1. Choose the positive direction.
  2. Find the pre-impact speeds with SUVAT, N2L or energy.
  3. Use signed momentum at impact.
  4. If particles coalesce, combine their masses.
  5. Reset the clock for post-impact motion.
  6. Give the requested type of answer with units and direction where needed.

A clean chain might read: pre-impact speed 5 → momentum gives 2 → after impact use v^2=2^2+2(10)(3).

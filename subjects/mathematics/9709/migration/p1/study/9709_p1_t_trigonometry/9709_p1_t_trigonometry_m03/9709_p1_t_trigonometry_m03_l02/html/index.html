SHOW AS TARGET FORM

Move the equation into shape.

In a “show that” question, transform the given expression until it matches the requested target form. Stop there.

1. What “show that” asks

  • A target form tells you which trig function should remain: sine, cosine or tangent.
  • Collect everything on one side so the other side is zero before reading coefficients.
  • Stop at the requested target form. If the prompt asks for a ratio in terms of k, continue the algebra to that ratio.
  • Useful identities are tan θ = sin θ / cos θ and sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

Target shapes

  • a sin²θ + b sinθ + c = 0
  • a cos²θ + b cosθ + c = 0
  • a tan²θ + b tanθ + c = 0
  • a sin²θ + b, or a cos²θ + b
  • aX²+bX+c=0 where X is cos²θ

2. The standard move set

  1. Clear fractions by multiplying every term by a common denominator factor.
  2. Replace tan with sin/cos when sine and cosine are the requested language.
  3. Use sin²θ=1−cos²θ, or cos²θ=1−sin²θ, to leave one trig function.
  4. Collect every term on one side and make the other side zero.
  5. Read a, b and c only after the target shape is reached.

Your turn

The target uses cosine only. How should sin²θ be replaced?

Core recap before the examples

  • First identify the requested target function.
  • Clear denominators by multiplying every term.
  • Use an identity to leave one trig function.
  • Collect to one side equal to zero, then read coefficients.
  • Keep restrictions from cancelled denominators. If the prompt asks for a ratio in terms of k, continue solving for that ratio.

3. Target quadratic in cosine

Worked example 1

Show that cos(2x)(tan²(2x)+3)+3=0 becomes 2cos²(2x)+3cos(2x)+1=0.

  1. Replace tan²(2x) with sin²(2x)/cos²(2x).
  2. Distribute cos(2x): sin²(2x)/cos(2x)+3cos(2x)+3=0.
  3. Multiply every term by cos(2x): sin²(2x)+3cos²(2x)+3cos(2x)=0.
  4. Replace sin²(2x) with 1−cos²(2x).
  5. Collect: 2cos²(2x)+3cos(2x)+1=0.

Worked example 2

Show that (sin θ+2cos θ)(1+sin θ−cos θ)=sin θ(1+cos θ) becomes 3cos²θ−2cosθ−1=0.

  1. Expand: sinθ+sin²θ−sinθcosθ+2cosθ+2sinθcosθ−2cos²θ.
  2. Subtract the right side sinθ+sinθcosθ and cancel the matching sinθ terms.
  3. Combine the remaining terms: sin²θ+2cosθ−2cos²θ=0.
  4. Replace sin²θ by 1−cos²θ.
  5. Rearrange and change the overall sign: 3cos²θ−2cosθ−1=0.

4. Fractions and common denominators

Worked example 3

Show that (cosθ−4)/sinθ − 4sinθ/(5cosθ−2)=0 becomes 9cos²θ−22cosθ+4=0.

  1. Use common denominator sinθ(5cosθ−2).
  2. Numerator: (cosθ−4)(5cosθ−2)−4sin²θ.
  3. Expand: 5cos²θ−22cosθ+8−4sin²θ.
  4. Replace sin²θ by 1−cos²θ.
  5. Collect: 9cos²θ−22cosθ+4=0.

Worked example 4

Show that 4sin x + 5/tan x + 2/sin x = 0 becomes 4cos²x−5cosx−6=0.

  1. Replace 1/tan x by cosx/sinx.
  2. Multiply every term by sinx: 4sin²x+5cosx+2=0.
  3. Replace sin²x by 1−cos²x.
  4. Rearrange: 4cos²x−5cosx−6=0.

5. Quadratic in sine

Worked example 5

Show that 3cosθ=8tanθ becomes 3sin²θ+8sinθ−3=0.

  1. Replace tanθ with sinθ/cosθ.
  2. Multiply by cosθ: 3cos²θ=8sinθ.
  3. Replace cos²θ by 1−sin²θ.
  4. Collect: 3sin²θ+8sinθ−3=0.

Worked example 6

Show that 1/(sinθ+cosθ)+1/(sinθ−cosθ)=1 becomes 2sin²θ−2sinθ−1=0.

  1. Common denominator is sin²θ−cos²θ.
  2. The numerator becomes 2sinθ.
  3. Multiply through, then replace cos²θ by 1−sin²θ.
  4. Collect: 2sin²θ−2sinθ−1=0.

6. Multi-angle, parameter and biquadratic forms

Worked example 7

Show that (cosθ+4)/(sinθ+1)+5sinθ−5=0 becomes 5cos²θ−cosθ−4=0.

  1. Put 5sinθ−5 over denominator sinθ+1: ((cosθ+4)+(5sinθ−5)(sinθ+1))/(sinθ+1)=0.
  2. Multiply through by sinθ+1, preserving the denominator restriction sinθ≠−1.
  3. Expand the numerator, then replace sin²θ with 1−cos²θ.
  4. Collect: 5cos²θ−cosθ−4=0.

Worked example 8

Show that 3cos⁴θ+4sin²θ−3=0 becomes 3X²−4X+1=0, X=cos²θ.

  1. Replace sin²θ with 1−cos²θ.
  2. Obtain 3cos⁴θ−4cos²θ+1=0.
  3. Let X=cos²θ, so cos⁴θ=X².
  4. 3X²−4X+1=0.

Worked example 9

Show that (tan x+cos x)/(tan x−cos x)=k becomes (k+1)sin²x+(k−1)sinx−(k+1)=0.

  1. Replace tanx by sinx/cosx.
  2. Multiply numerator and denominator by cosx.
  3. Cross-multiply and replace cos²x with 1−sin²x.
  4. Collect: (k+1)sin²x+(k−1)sinx−(k+1)=0.

7. Reduce to tangent form

Worked example 10

Show that sin2x+3cos2x=3(sin2x−cos2x) becomes tan2x=3.

  1. Expand the right side and collect on the left.
  2. Obtain −2sin2x+6cos2x=0.
  3. Divide every term by cos2x, after checking it is non-zero.
  4. Rearrange: tan2x=3.

Worked example 11

Show that (2sinθ+cosθ)/(sinθ+cosθ)=2tanθ becomes tan²θ=1/2.

  1. Cross-multiply: 2sinθ+cosθ=2tanθ(sinθ+cosθ).
  2. Replace tanθ by sinθ/cosθ and multiply by cosθ.
  3. Expand: 2sinθcosθ+cos²θ=2sin²θ+2sinθcosθ.
  4. Cancel the common 2sinθcosθ term, giving cos²θ=2sin²θ.
  5. Divide by cos²θ, with cosθ≠0: tan²θ=1/2.

8. Convert to a sin²θ+b

Worked example 12

Show that (tan²θ−1)/(tan²θ+1)=2sin²θ−1.

  1. Replace tan²θ with sin²θ/cos²θ.
  2. Multiply numerator and denominator by cos²θ.
  3. Use sin²θ+cos²θ=1 in the denominator.
  4. Replace cos²θ with 1−sin²θ: 2sin²θ−1.

9. Show, then express a trig ratio

Worked example 13

Given (tanx+sinx)/(tanx−sinx)=k with sinx≠0, show it becomes (1+cosx)/(1−cosx)=k, then express cosx in terms of k.

  1. Replace tanx by sinx/cosx.
  2. Factor sinx from numerator and denominator, retaining the original non-zero restrictions.
  3. Multiply the remaining numerator and denominator by cosx: (1+cosx)/(1−cosx)=k.
  4. Cross-multiply: 1+cosx=k−kcosx.
  5. Collect and divide by k+1: cosx=(k−1)/(k+1), k≠−1.

10. Watch the common slips

  • When clearing fractions, multiply every term.
  • 1−sin²θ=+cos²θ, not −cos²θ.
  • Do not read c until the equation has been collected to one side equal to zero.
  • Keep denominator restrictions when cancelling.

10. Try it yourself: page 10

Question 1

Show that 2 sin θ + 1/cos θ = 3 tan θ may be expressed as 2 sin θ cos θ = 3 sin θ − 1.

  1. Replace tan θ with sin θ/cos θ: 2 sin θ + 1/cos θ = 3 sin θ/cos θ.
  2. Multiply every term by cos θ: 2 sin θ cos θ + 1 = 3 sin θ.
  3. Rearrange: 2 sin θ cos θ = 3 sin θ − 1.

Question 2

Show that cos θ = 2 − sin²θ/cos θ may be expressed as cos θ = 1/2.

  1. Multiply every term by cos θ: cos²θ = 2 cos θ − sin²θ.
  2. Use sin²θ = 1 − cos²θ: cos²θ = 2 cos θ − (1 − cos²θ).
  3. Cancel cos²θ from both sides: 0 = 2 cos θ − 1.
  4. Therefore cos θ = 1/2.

11. Try it yourself: page 11

Question 3: identity check

Show that 3 sin x + 4 cos x = 3 sin x − 4 cos x + 8 cos x may be expressed as cos x = 0 or a trivial identity.

  1. Move everything to the left: 3 sin x + 4 cos x − 3 sin x + 4 cos x − 8 cos x = 0.
  2. Combine like terms: 0 = 0.
  3. The equation is an identity and holds for every x. The printed “cos x = 0 or” is a source inconsistency.

Question 4

Show that 2 cos²θ − sin θ = 1 may be expressed as 2 sin²θ + sin θ − 1 = 0.

  1. Replace cos²θ with 1 − sin²θ: 2(1 − sin²θ) − sin θ = 1.
  2. Expand: 2 − 2 sin²θ − sin θ = 1.
  3. Collect to one side and multiply by −1: 2 sin²θ + sin θ − 1 = 0.

Exam-style 1

Show that 5 cos θ + 2/cos θ = 7 may be expressed as 5 cos²θ − 7 cos θ + 2 = 0.

  1. Multiply every term by cos θ: 5 cos²θ + 2 = 7 cos θ.
  2. Collect: 5 cos²θ − 7 cos θ + 2 = 0.

Exam-style 2

Show that 3 tan θ + 2 sin θ = 0 may be expressed as sin θ(3 + 2 cos θ) = 0.

  1. Replace tan θ: 3 sin θ/cos θ + 2 sin θ = 0.
  2. Multiply every term by cos θ: 3 sin θ + 2 sin θ cos θ = 0.
  3. Factor sin θ: sin θ(3 + 2 cos θ) = 0.

Key ideas

  • Choose the target function first.
  • Clear denominators, replace identities, collect to zero, then read coefficients.
  • Stop once the requested form is reached.

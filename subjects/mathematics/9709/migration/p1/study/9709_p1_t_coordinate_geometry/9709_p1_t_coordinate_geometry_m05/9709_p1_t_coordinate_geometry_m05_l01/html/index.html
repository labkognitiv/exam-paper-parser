Tangent at a known point

Use the radius to find the tangent.

At its contact point, a circle’s tangent is perpendicular to the radius. That right angle determines the tangent gradient.

Start the lesson

The radius and tangent rule

  • The centre C is the fixed middle point of a circle.
  • A radius CP joins the centre C to a point P on the circle.
  • A tangent is a line that touches the circle at P.
  • The tangent and radius are perpendicular: they meet at a right angle.
  • A line’s gradient measures its change in y divided by its change in x.
m tangent × m radius = −1
m tangent = −1/(m radius)

For finite non-zero gradients: flip the fraction, then change its sign.

  • If the radius is horizontal, its gradient is 0 and the tangent is vertical.
  • If the radius is vertical, its gradient is undefined and the tangent is horizontal.
  • So the negative-reciprocal formula is used only when the radius gradient is finite and non-zero.
Mini exampleRadius gradientTangent gradient
C(0,0), P(3,4)(4−0)/(3−0)=4/3−3/4
C(2,5), P(6,8)(8−5)/(6−2)=3/4−4/3
C(1,−2), P(4,2)(2−(−2))/(4−1)=4/3−3/4

A four-step method

  • First check that the given point P lies on the circle.
  • In standard form (x−a)²+(y−b)²=r², the centre is C(a,b).
  • If the circle is in general form, complete the square first to find its centre.
  • Point-gradient form writes a line through (xP,yP) with gradient m.
  • We use mr for the radius gradient and mt for the tangent gradient.
  1. Find the centre C(a,b).
  2. Find the radius gradient mr=(yP−b)/(xP−a).
  3. Find the perpendicular tangent gradient, including horizontal or vertical exceptions.
  4. Write the tangent through P: y−yP=mt(x−xP).
Asked for y=mx+cExpand and make y the subject.
Asked for px+qy=kClear fractions and collect x and y.
Asked to “show that”Work to the stated form exactly.

Known-point tangents in standard form

Worked example

Give the answer with integer coefficients

The circle is (x−3)²+(y−1)²=25. B(7,4) lies on it. Find the tangent at B in the form px+qy=k.

Solution

  1. The centre is C(3,1). Check B: (7−3)²+(4−1)²=16+9=25.

  2. mCB=(4−1)/(7−3)=3/4
  3. mt=−1/(3/4)=−4/3
  4. y−4=−4/3(x−7)
  5. 3y−12=−4x+28
  6. 4x+3y=40

Worked example

Give the answer as y=mx+c

A circle has centre C(4,5), and B(7,9) lies on it. Find the tangent at B.

Solution

  1. mCB=(9−5)/(7−4)=4/3
  2. mt=−1/(4/3)=−3/4
  3. y−9=−3/4(x−7)
  4. y−9=−3x/4+21/4
  5. y=−3x/4+21/4+36/4
  6. y=−3x/4+57/4

Find the centre first

Worked example

Complete the square, then show the result

The circle is x²+y²−8x+4y−5=0. P(1,2) lies on it. Show that its tangent at P is 4y=3x+5.

Solution

  1. x²−8x=(x−4)²−16, y²+4y=(y+2)²−4
  2. (x−4)²−16+(y+2)²−4−5=0
  3. (x−4)²+(y+2)²=25, so C(4,−2)

  4. Check P: (1−4)²+(2+2)²=9+16=25.

  5. mCP=(2−(−2))/(1−4)=4/(−3)=−4/3
  6. mt=−1/(−4/3)=3/4
  7. y−2=3/4(x−1)
  8. 4y−8=3x−3
  9. 4y=3x+5

Worked example

Reuse a centre from an earlier part

The centre is C(−2,−3), and A(1,1) lies on the circle. Find the tangent at A in the form px+qy=k.

Solution

  1. mCA=(1−(−3))/(1−(−2))=4/3
  2. mt=−1/(4/3)=−3/4
  3. y−1=−3/4(x−1)
  4. 4y−4=−3x+3
  5. 3x+4y=7

Where two tangents meet

  • Write the tangent equation at each contact point P and Q.
  • Solve the two line equations simultaneously to find their meeting point T.
  • Symmetric contact points often give gradients equal in size and opposite in sign.
  • Diametrically opposite points are the two ends of one straight line through the centre.
  • Their radii point along the same line, so their tangents are parallel and have no finite meeting point.

Worked example

Symmetric contact points

A circle has centre C(4,−2), radius 5, and points P(1,2), Q(7,2). The tangent at P is 4y=3x+5. Find T, where the two tangents meet.

Solution

  1. Both points lie on the circle: CP²=(−3)²+4²=25 and CQ²=3²+4²=25.

  2. mCQ=4/3, so tangent gradient at Q=−3/4
  3. y−2=−3/4(x−7)
  4. 4y+3x=29

  5. Use 4y−3x=5 and 4y+3x=29. Add them:

    8y=34 ⇒ y=17/4

  6. Subtract them:

    −6x=−24 ⇒ x=4
  7. T=(4,17/4)

Worked example

Find both tangents from a general circle

The circle is x²+y²−4x+6y−77=0. A(−7,0) and B(11,0) lie on it. Find where the tangents at A and B meet.

Solution

  1. (x−2)²−4+(y+3)²−9−77=0
  2. (x−2)²+(y+3)²=90, so C(2,−3)

  3. Check both points:

    A check: (−7−2)²+(0+3)²=81+9=90
    B check: (11−2)²+(0+3)²=81+9=90

  4. At A:

    mCA=3/(−9)=−1/3, so mt,A=3
    y=3(x+7)=3x+21

  5. At B:

    mCB=3/9=1/3, so mt,B=−3
    y=−3(x−11)=−3x+33
  6. 3x+21=−3x+33 ⇒ 6x=12 ⇒ x=2
  7. y=3(2)+21=27
  8. T=(2,27)

Find a companion point on a horizontal or vertical chord

  • A chord joins two points on a circle.
  • A horizontal chord has the same y-coordinate at both ends.
  • The symbol ∥ means “is parallel to”.
  • Its midpoint lies on the vertical line through the centre.
  • So the two x-coordinates are equal distances from the centre’s x-coordinate.
PQ ∥ x-axis: yQ=yP, xQ=2xC−xP
PQ ∥ y-axis: xQ=xP, yQ=2yC−yP

Worked example

Reflect across the centre line

The centre is C(4,−2). P(1,2) and Q lie on the circle, with PQ parallel to the x-axis. Find Q.

Solution

  1. The chord is horizontal, so yQ=yP=2.

  2. P is 3 units left of x=4 because 4−1=3.

  3. Q is 3 units right of x=4:

    xQ=4+3=7
  4. Q=(7,2)

Check 2

A vertical chord has one endpoint R(6,−1) and centre C(2,3). What is the other endpoint’s y-coordinate?

Use the tangent’s intercepts

  • The x-intercept is where a line meets the x-axis, so y=0.
  • The y-intercept is where it meets the y-axis, so x=0.
  • The axes are perpendicular. The intercepts and origin form a right-angled triangle.
  • Use the absolute values of the intercepts as positive base and height lengths.
Area=1/2 × |x-intercept| × |y-intercept|

Worked example

Find the tangent, then the triangle area

The circle is x²+y²−6x+4y−27=0. P(5,4) lies on it. Its tangent meets the axes at A and B. Find the area of triangle OAB.

Solution

  1. (x−3)²−9+(y+2)²−4−27=0
  2. (x−3)²+(y+2)²=40, so C(3,−2)

  3. Check P: (5−3)²+(4+2)²=4+36=40.

  4. mCP=6/2=3, so mt=−1/3
  5. y−4=−1/3(x−5)
  6. 3y−12=−x+5 ⇒ x+3y=17

  7. Set y=0: x=17, so A(17,0).

  8. Set x=0: 3y=17 ⇒ y=17/3, so B(0,17/3).

  9. Area=1/2(17)(17/3)=289/6
  10. Area of OAB=289/6

Key ideas

  • Check the contact point lies on the circle.
  • Find the centre before finding a radius gradient.
  • A radius is perpendicular to its tangent.
  • Use negative reciprocals only for finite, non-zero gradients.
  • Use horizontal and vertical exceptions directly.
  • Solve two tangent equations to find their intersection.
  • Opposite diameter-end tangents are parallel.
  • Reflect a chord endpoint across the correct centre line.
  • Set y=0 or x=0 to find intercepts.

Your turn

Meet two symmetric tangents

A circle has centre C(3,1), radius 5, and contact points P(0,5), Q(6,5). Find the meeting point T.

Solution

  1. CP²=(−3)²+4²=25=CQ².

  2. At P:

    mCP=(5−1)/(0−3)=4/(−3)=−4/3, so mt,P=3/4
    y−5=3x/4 ⇒ 4y−3x=20

  3. At Q:

    mCQ=(5−1)/(6−3)=4/3, so mt,Q=−3/4
    y−5=−3/4(x−6) ⇒ 4y+3x=38

  4. Add: 8y=58 ⇒ y=29/4.

  5. Subtract: −6x=−18 ⇒ x=3.

  6. T=(3,29/4)

Your turn

Find an x-intercept

The circle is (x−2)²+(y−1)²=10. P(5,2) lies on it. Its tangent meets the x-axis at A. Find A’s x-coordinate.

Solution

  1. C(2,1), and P check: 9+1=10.

  2. mCP=1/3, so mt=−3
  3. y−2=−3(x−5)
  4. y=−3x+17

  5. At A, y=0:

    0=−3x+17 ⇒ x=17/3
  6. xA=17/3

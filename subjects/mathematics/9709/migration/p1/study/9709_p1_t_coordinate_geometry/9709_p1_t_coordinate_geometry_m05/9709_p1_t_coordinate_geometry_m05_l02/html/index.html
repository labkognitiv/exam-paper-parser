Tangents · repeated roots · contact chords

Two lines that
just touch

From a point outside a circle, two tangents can reach the circle. A repeated-root condition finds their gradients before you know where they touch.

Start the lesson

The setup

A tangent touches a circle at one contact point. A radius to that point is perpendicular to the tangent. From an external point TTT outside the circle, exactly two tangents exist.

The subscripts in (xT,yT)(x_T,y_T)(xT​,yT​) label the coordinates of point TTT. A non-vertical line through T(xT,yT)T(x_T,y_T)T(xT​,yT​) with unknown gradient mmm is

y−yT=m(x−xT)y-y_T=m(x-x_T)y−yT​=m(x−xT​)

Substitute this line into the circle. A tangent meets the circle once, so the resulting quadratic in xxx has a repeated root. Its discriminant is zero.

Δ=B2−4AC=0\Delta=B^2-4AC=0Δ=B2−4AC=0

Here A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C are the three coefficients in a quadratic Ax2+Bx+C=0Ax^2+Bx+C=0Ax2+Bx+C=0. They are not point labels in this calculation.

A slope equation cannot represent a vertical line. Also test x=xTx=x_Tx=xT​ separately: substitute it into the circle and see whether there is exactly one point of intersection.

The five-step method

  1. Write the non-vertical line through TTT with unknown gradient mmm.
  2. Substitute it into the circle.
  3. Expand and collect a quadratic in xxx.
  4. Set its discriminant to zero.
  5. Solve for mmm. Then separately check the vertical line through TTT.

First check: is the point outside?

For a circle with centre CCC and radius rrr, compare squared distance TC2TC^2TC2 with r2r^2r2.

  • TC2>r2TC^2>r^2TC2>r2: TTT is outside and two tangents exist.

Chord of contact

If tangents from TTT touch at AAA and BBB, the segment ABABAB is the chord of contact. It is perpendicular to CTCTCT.

Let MMM be where ABABAB meets CTCTCT. Symmetry makes MMM the midpoint of ABABAB. It is not usually the midpoint of CTCTCT.

CM⋅CT=r2soCM=r2CTCM\cdot CT=r^2\qquad\text{so}\qquad CM=\frac{r^2}{CT}CM⋅CT=r2soCM=CTr2​

  • Similar triangles have the same angles, so matching sides are proportional.
  • Triangles CMACMACMA and CATCATCAT are similar: their angles at MMM and AAA are right angles, and they share the angle at CCC.
  • Hence CMCA=CACT\frac{CM}{CA}=\frac{CA}{CT}CACM​=CTCA​, so CM⋅CT=CA2=r2CM\cdot CT=CA^2=r^2CM⋅CT=CA2=r2.
  • Since MMM bisects CTCTCT exactly when CM=CT2CM=\frac{CT}{2}CM=2CT​, the product gives CT2=2r2CT^2=2r^2CT2=2r2.

The two tangent lengths are equal. From right triangle TACTACTAC:

TA=TB=TC2−r2TA=TB=\sqrt{TC^2-r^2}TA=TB=TC2−r2​

Key ideas

  • Substitute a variable-gradient line and use Δ=0\Delta=0Δ=0 for tangency.
  • Test the vertical line separately because slope form omits it.
  • Compare TC2TC^2TC2 with r2r^2r2 before claiming two tangents.
  • The chord of contact is perpendicular to CTCTCT, and CM⋅CT=r2CM\cdot CT=r^2CM⋅CT=r2.
  • MMM always bisects chord ABABAB; it bisects CTCTCT only when CT2=2r2CT^2=2r^2CT2=2r2.

Parallel tangents

Parallel tangents have the same gradient. The diameter through their two contact points is perpendicular to both and passes through the circle's centre.

Worked example: parallel tangents of gradient 1

The circle is (x−4)2+(y+1)2=40(x-4)^2+(y+1)^2=40(x−4)2+(y+1)2=40. Two parallel tangents of gradient 1 touch it at A,BA,BA,B.

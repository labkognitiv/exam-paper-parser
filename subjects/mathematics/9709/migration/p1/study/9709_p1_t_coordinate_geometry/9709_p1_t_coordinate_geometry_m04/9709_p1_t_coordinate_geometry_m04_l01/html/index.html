Circles · centre · radius

Read a circle
from its equation

A circle is every point the same distance from one fixed point. Its standard equation stores that centre and distance.

Start the lesson

The standard form

The fixed point is the centre. The fixed distance from the centre to the circle is the radius.

In the formula below, (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) is any point on the circle, (a,b)(a,b)(a,b) is the centre, and rrr is the radius.

(x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2(x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2

  • (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) is any point on the circle.
  • (a,b)(a,b)(a,b) is the centre and rrr is the radius.
  • Squaring means multiplying a value by itself. The right side is r2r^2r2, not rrr.
  • The bracket signs look opposite to the centre coordinates: x+2=x−(−2)x+2=x-(-2)x+2=x−(−2).

Worked example: read an equation

(x−3)2+(y−5)2=16(x-3)^2+(y-5)^2=16(x−3)2+(y−5)2=16

  1. Match the brackets with (x−a)2+(y−b)2(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2(x−a)2+(y−b)2.
  2. The centre is (3,5)(3,5)(3,5).
  3. r2=16r^2=16r2=16, so the positive radius is r=4r=4r=4.

Worked example: write an equation

A circle has centre (3,−2)(3,-2)(3,−2) and radius 555.

  1. a=3, b=−2, r=5a=3,\ b=-2,\ r=5a=3, b=−2, r=5.
  2. (x−3)2+(y−(−2))2=52(x-3)^2+(y-(-2))^2=5^2(x−3)2+(y−(−2))2=52.
  3. Answer: (x−3)2+(y+2)2=25(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25(x−3)2+(y+2)2=25.

Centre and one point

If the centre and a point on the circle are known, their squared distance is r2r^2r2. You can avoid finding a square root.

The known point is (x1,y1)(x_1,y_1)(x1​,y1​). The small 1 labels that point.

r2=(x1−a)2+(y1−b)2r^2=(x_1-a)^2+(y_1-b)^2r2=(x1​−a)2+(y1​−b)2

Worked example: centre (5,1)(5,1)(5,1), through (−1,−2)(-1,-2)(−1,−2)

  1. r2=(−1−5)2+(−2−1)2r^2=(-1-5)^2+(-2-1)^2r2=(−1−5)2+(−2−1)2
  2. r2=(−6)2+(−3)2=36+9=45r^2=(-6)^2+(-3)^2=36+9=45r2=(−6)2+(−3)2=36+9=45
  3. Answer: (x−5)2+(y−1)2=45(x-5)^2+(y-1)^2=45(x−5)2+(y−1)2=45.

Endpoints of a diameter

A diameter passes through the centre and joins two points on the circle. The midpoint is halfway between its endpoints: average the two x-coordinates and average the two y-coordinates.

For endpoints (x1,y1)(x_1,y_1)(x1​,y1​) and (x2,y2)(x_2,y_2)(x2​,y2​):

(a,b)=(x1+x22,y1+y22)(a,b)=\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)(a,b)=(2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​)

  1. Find the midpoint.
  2. Find r2r^2r2 from the centre to either endpoint.
  3. Write the standard equation.

Worked example: diameter endpoints (−1,−2)(-1,-2)(−1,−2) and (7,4)(7,4)(7,4)

  1. (a,b)=(−1+72,−2+42)=(3,1)(a,b)=\left(\frac{-1+7}{2},\frac{-2+4}{2}\right)=(3,1)(a,b)=(2−1+7​,2−2+4​)=(3,1).
  2. r2=(−1−3)2+(−2−1)2=(−4)2+(−3)2=25r^2=(-1-3)^2+(-2-1)^2=(-4)^2+(-3)^2=25r2=(−1−3)2+(−2−1)2=(−4)2+(−3)2=25.
  3. Answer: (x−3)2+(y−1)2=25(x-3)^2+(y-1)^2=25(x−3)2+(y−1)2=25.

Three points: find the right angle

A semicircle is half a circle. An angle standing on its diameter is 90°. The converse lets you work backwards: if two sides at a point are perpendicular, the third side, across from that right-angle vertex, is the diameter. This is the opposite side.

Check easy cases first. A vertical side has undefined gradient. A horizontal side has gradient 0. If they share a vertex, they are perpendicular. For two finite gradients, use m1m2=−1m_1m_2=-1m1​m2​=−1.

  1. Compute the three side gradients, checking vertical and horizontal sides directly.
  2. Find the perpendicular pair. Their shared vertex is the right angle; the opposite side is the diameter.
  3. Find the midpoint of the diameter.
  4. Find r2r^2r2 from the centre to any point and write the equation.

Worked example: vertical and horizontal sides

A(7,1), B(7,9), C(1,9)A(7,1),\ B(7,9),\ C(1,9)A(7,1), B(7,9), C(1,9).

  1. ABABAB is vertical and BCBCBC is horizontal, so the angle at BBB is 90°.
  2. ACACAC is the diameter, so (a,b)=(7+12,1+92)=(4,5)(a,b)=\left(\frac{7+1}{2},\frac{1+9}{2}\right)=(4,5)(a,b)=(27+1​,21+9​)=(4,5).
  3. r2=(7−4)2+(1−5)2=9+16=25r^2=(7-4)^2+(1-5)^2=9+16=25r2=(7−4)2+(1−5)2=9+16=25.
  4. Answer: (x−4)2+(y−5)2=25(x-4)^2+(y-5)^2=25(x−4)2+(y−5)2=25.

Key ideas

  • Standard form is (x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2(x−a)2+(y−b)2=r2.
  • Read the centre by reversing the bracket signs. Take the positive square root to find the radius.
  • With a centre and point, squared distance gives r2r^2r2.
  • With a diameter, midpoint gives the centre.
  • With three points, prove a right angle before treating the opposite side as a diameter.

Three slips to avoid

Wrong radius side

(x−3)2+(y−4)2=5(x-3)^2+(y-4)^2=5(x−3)2+(y−4)2=5

The radius is 5, so the right side must be 52=255^2=2552=25.

Correct equation

(x−3)2+(y−4)2=25(x-3)^2+(y-4)^2=25(x−3)2+(y−4)2=25

Wrong centre sign

From (x+2)2+(y−3)2=16(x+2)^2+(y-3)^2=16(x+2)2+(y−3)2=16, the centre is not (2,3)(2,3)(2,3).

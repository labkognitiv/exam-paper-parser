Triangles in coordinate geometry

Turn a shape into equations.

A triangle name such as isosceles or right-angled tells you which coordinate calculation to make. Read the property, choose the equation, then solve.

Start the lesson

First, read the triangle

  • A triangle is a shape made from three straight sides.
  • Its three corners are called vertices. One corner is a vertex.
  • A label such as A(5,-2) gives the horizontal coordinate first, then the vertical coordinate.
  • A side name uses its endpoints: AB means the side joining A to B.
  • A small matching tick on two sides means those sides have equal length.
  • A small square at a vertex marks a right angle of 90°.
  • A gradient measures vertical change divided by horizontal change; the letter m names a gradient.
Property Meaning Equation to use
Isosceles Two sides have equal length. AB² = AC², or the named equal pair
Equilateral All three sides have equal length. AB² = BC² = AC²
Right-angled Two sides meeting at one vertex are perpendicular. m₁m₂ = −1 when both gradients exist
Area Half of a chosen base multiplied by its perpendicular height. Area = ½ × base × height

Read the property → choose the equation → calculate → check.

Squared distance

For points (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂):

  • The small numbers below x and y are subscripts. Subscript 1 identifies the first point; subscript 2 identifies the second.

d is the distance between the two points.

d² = (x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²
  • Squaring removes the square root from the usual distance formula.
  • Equal positive lengths have equal squares, so compare squared distances directly.

Use equal distances for an isosceles triangle

  • A constant has one fixed value.
  • A letter such as p can stand for that unknown fixed value.
  • If the question says AC = BC, calculate AC² and BC².
  • Set them equal, expand the brackets, collect like terms, and solve.

Worked example

Find a parameter from equal sides

A(5,-2), B(10,3), and C(2p,p) form a triangle. Given AC = BC, find p.

Solution

  1. Use A and C:

    AC² = (2p−5)² + (p+2)²
    = (4p²−20p+25)+(p²+4p+4) = 5p²−16p+29

  2. Use B and C:

    BC² = (2p−10)² + (p−3)²
    = (4p²−40p+100)+(p²−6p+9) = 5p²−46p+109

  3. Equal sides give equal squared distances:

    5p²−16p+29 = 5p²−46p+109

  4. Subtract the same 5p² from both sides:

    −16p+29 = −46p+109

  5. Add 46p to both sides, then subtract 29:

    30p = 80
    p = 80/30 = 8/3

Check 1

AB and BC are the equal sides. Which equation starts the proof?

Use perpendicular gradients for a right angle

Gradient

m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)
  • Gradient measures vertical change divided by horizontal change.
  • Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular when their gradients multiply to −1.
  • A vertical line has no defined gradient. It is perpendicular to a horizontal line.
  • Check that denominators are not zero before using the product rule.

Worked example

Find an integer parameter from a right angle

A(5,-2), B(10,3), and C(2p,p) form a triangle. AC is perpendicular to BC, and p is an integer. Find p.

Solution

  1. The denominators are 2p−5 and 2p−10. Check p = 5/2 and p = 5 separately.

    • p = 5/2 is not an integer.
    • At p = 5, BC is vertical but AC is not horizontal, so the lines are not perpendicular.

  2. For the remaining values, calculate both gradients:

    mAC = (p+2)/(2p−5), mBC = (p−3)/(2p−10)

  3. Apply the perpendicular condition and clear the denominators:

    [(p+2)/(2p−5)] × [(p−3)/(2p−10)] = −1
    (p+2)(p−3) = −(2p−5)(2p−10)

  4. Expand each side:

    p²−p−6 = −4p²+30p−50

  5. Bring every term to the left, then factor:

    5p²−31p+44 = 0 ⇒ (p−4)(5p−11) = 0

  6. The roots are 4 and 11/5. Only 4 is an integer.

    p = 4

Check 2

One side is vertical. What must be true of a side perpendicular to it?

Choose an area method that fits the shape

  • A base is the side you choose to measure across.
  • The perpendicular height is the shortest distance from the opposite vertex to the line containing that base.
  • Perpendicular means the height meets the base at 90°.
  • An exact answer keeps fractions and square roots rather than rounding them.
  • A letter below another letter is a subscript: xA means the x-coordinate of A.
  • Vertical bars around a number mean its absolute value, its non-negative distance from zero.
Method Use it when Example
Half base × height A base is horizontal or vertical, so its perpendicular height is easy to read. A(0,0), B(6,0), C(2,3): area = ½ × 6 × 3 = 9.
Coordinate formula Any three vertices are known. A(1,1), B(4,5), C(6,2): ½|1(5−2)+4(2−1)+6(1−5)| = ½|3+4−24| = 17/2.
Two perpendicular sides The triangle is right-angled; the two legs are base and height. Legs 5 and 12: area = ½ × 5 × 12 = 30.

Coordinate area formula

Area = ½|xA(yB−yC)+xB(yC−yA)+xC(yA−yB)|
  • The subscripts tell you which vertex each coordinate belongs to.
  • Vertical bars mean take the absolute value, so a negative calculation becomes a positive area.
  • This method always works. The other two are often shorter.

Find an altitude and its foot

  • An altitude is a perpendicular segment from a vertex to the opposite side or its extension.
  • The point where it meets that line is the foot of the perpendicular.
  • In an isosceles triangle, the altitude from the vertex between the equal sides also meets the midpoint of the base.
  • A midpoint is halfway between two endpoints; average their x-coordinates and their y-coordinates.
  • For the next example, A is (-2,-1), B is (4,6), and C is (6,-3).

Worked example

Show isosceles, then find the exact area

Triangle ABC has vertices A(-2,-1), B(4,6), and C(6,-3). Show it is isosceles and find its exact area.

Solution

  1. AB² = (4−(−2))²+(6−(−1))² = 36+49 = 85
  2. BC² = (6−4)²+(−3−6)² = 4+81 = 85

    Therefore AB = BC, so the triangle is isosceles and AC is its base.

  3. AC² = (6−(−2))²+(−3−(−1))² = 64+4 = 68
  4. M = ((−2+6)/2,(−1−3)/2) = (2,−2)
  5. BM² = (4−2)²+(6−(−2))² = 4+64 = 68
  6. Area = ½√68 × √68 = 34

Worked example

Find the foot from C onto AB

Let D lie on AB with CD perpendicular to AB. Find the x-coordinate of D.

Solution

  1. mAB = (6−(−1))/(4−(−2)) = 7/6
  2. AB: y = 7x/6 + 4/3

  3. The perpendicular gradient is the negative reciprocal: flip 7/6 to 6/7 and change its sign.

    CD: y+3 = −6(x−6)/7

  4. At D, both line equations give the same y-value:

    7x/6 + 4/3 = −6(x−6)/7 − 3

  5. Multiply every term by 42, the lowest common multiple of 6 and 7:

    49x+56 = −36(x−6)−126
    49x+56 = −36x+216−126
    49x+56 = −36x+90

  6. Add 36x to both sides, then subtract 56:

    85x = 34
  7. xD = 34/85 = 2/5

Key ideas

  • Isosceles: compare the named pair of squared side lengths.
  • Equilateral: all three squared side lengths are equal.
  • Right angle: use m₁m₂ = −1 only when both gradients exist.
  • Vertical and horizontal lines are perpendicular.
  • Area is half the base times the perpendicular height.
  • The coordinate area formula works for any three vertices.
  • The foot is where an altitude meets the opposite side line.
  • Check restrictions before clearing denominators.

Your turn

Right angle and area

P(0,0), Q(6,2), R(4,8). Show the triangle is right-angled at Q and find its area.

Solution

  1. mPQ = (2−0)/(6−0) = 2/6 = 1/3
    mQR = (8−2)/(4−6) = 6/(−2) = −3
    mPQ × mQR = (1/3)(−3) = −1

  2. So PQ and QR are perpendicular.

  3. PQ² = (6−0)²+(2−0)² = 36+4 = 40
    QR² = (4−6)²+(8−2)² = 4+36 = 40
  4. Area = ½ × √40 × √40 = 20

Your turn

Equal distances with a parameter

A(1,2), B(7,4), C(p,6), and AC = BC. Find p.

Solution

  1. AC² = (p−1)²+(6−2)² = (p−1)²+16
    BC² = (p−7)²+(6−4)² = (p−7)²+4
  2. (p−1)²+16 = (p−7)²+4
    p²−2p+17 = p²−14p+53
  3. 12p = 36
    p = 3

Your turn

A horizontal base gives a vertical altitude

A(0,0), B(4,0), C(2,3). D lies on AB and CD is perpendicular to AB. Find D.

Solution

  1. Both endpoints of AB have y = 0, so AB is horizontal.

  2. The perpendicular through C is vertical, so its equation is x = 2.

  3. It meets AB, where y = 0, at D = (2,0).

Exam-style practice

Equal sides

A(3,-1), B(8,4), C(2q,q), and AC = BC. Find q. [5]

Solution

  1. AC² = (2q−3)²+(q+1)²
  2. BC² = (2q−8)²+(q−4)²

  3. Set the squared distances equal and expand each side:

    4q²−12q+9+q²+2q+1 = 5q²−10q+10
    4q²−32q+64+q²−8q+16 = 5q²−40q+80
    5q²−10q+10 = 5q²−40q+80

  4. Subtract 5q², then collect q-terms:

    −10q+10 = −40q+80
    30q = 70
    q = 70/30 = 7/3

Exam-style practice

Right angle and exact area

A(4,1), B(p,5), C(12,13), where p<8. AB is perpendicular to BC. Find p and the exact area. [7]

Solution

  1. The gradient denominators require p ≠ 4 and p ≠ 12. At either value one side is vertical, while the other is not horizontal, so neither gives a right angle.

  2. mAB = 4/(p−4), mBC = 8/(12−p)
  3. [4/(p−4)] × [8/(12−p)] = −1
    32 = −(p−4)(12−p)
    32 = p²−16p+48 ⇒ p²−16p+16 = 0

  4. Use the quadratic formula with a = 1, b = −16, c = 16:

    p = [16 ± √(256−64)]/2 = [16 ± √192]/2
    √192 = 8√3 ⇒ p = 8 ± 4√3

    Since p < 8, keep p = 8−4√3.

  5. AB² = (p−4)²+(5−1)² = (4−4√3)²+16 = 80−32√3
    BC² = (12−p)²+(13−5)² = (4+4√3)²+64 = 128+32√3
  6. AB²BC² = (80−32√3)(128+32√3)
    = 10240+2560√3−4096√3−3072 = 7168−1536√3
    7168−1536√3 = (48√3−16)²
    Area = ½√(7168−1536√3) = 24√3−8

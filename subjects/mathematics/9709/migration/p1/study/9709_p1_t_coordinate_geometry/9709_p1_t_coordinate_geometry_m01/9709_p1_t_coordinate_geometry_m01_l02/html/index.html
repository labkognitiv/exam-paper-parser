Parallel lines · perpendicular lines · bisectors

Same direction,
or a right-angle turn?

A line's gradient describes its direction. Compare two gradients and you can decide whether the lines are parallel or perpendicular, then build a new line through the point you need.

Start the lesson

Two gradient rules

  • A line's gradient measures its slope: the change in yyy divided by the change in xxx.
  • Parallel lines point in the same direction and never meet.
  • Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle, which is 90°.
  • m1m_1m1​ and m2m_2m2​ mean the gradients of the first and second lines. The small numbers identify the lines; they are not powers.

Parallel lines

m1=m2m_1=m_2m1​=m2​

This rule applies when the gradients are defined. Two vertical lines are also parallel, although their gradients are undefined.

Perpendicular lines

m1m2=−1orm⊥=−1mm_1m_2=-1 \qquad\text{or}\qquad m_{\perp}=-\frac1mm1​m2​=−1orm⊥​=−m1​

  • m⊥m_{\perp}m⊥​ means the gradient perpendicular to mmm.
  • For a fraction, swap numerator and denominator, then change the sign.
  • A vertical line is perpendicular to a horizontal line; this is the exception to the finite-gradient formula.

For perpendicular gradients: flip the fraction, then flip the sign.

Three quick spot-checks

  • m1=3, m2=3m_1=3,\ m_2=3m1​=3, m2​=3: equal, so parallel.
  • m1=2, m2=−12m_1=2,\ m_2=-\frac12m1​=2, m2​=−21​: product -1, so perpendicular.
  • m1=−34, m2=43m_1=-\frac34,\ m_2=\frac43m1​=−43​, m2​=34​: product -1, so perpendicular.

What a perpendicular bisector does

Perpendicular bisector

A perpendicular bisector is a line that cuts a line segment exactly in half and meets it at a right angle.

  • A line segment is the part of a line between two endpoints, such as AAA and BBB.
  • The midpoint is the point halfway between those endpoints.
  • The bisector passes through the midpoint and has the perpendicular gradient.
  • The point-gradient form y−y1=m(x−x1)y-y_1=m(x-x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​) describes a line of gradient mmm through the known point (x1,y1)(x_1,y_1)(x1​,y1​).
  • Each bracket measures a coordinate change from that point.
  • In (x1,y1)(x_1,y_1)(x1​,y1​) and (x2,y2)(x_2,y_2)(x2​,y2​), subscript 1 labels the first endpoint and subscript 2 labels the second. Keep each endpoint's coordinates together.

  1. Find the midpoint MMM: M=(x1+x22,y1+y22)M=\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)M=(2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​)
  2. Find the gradient of ABABAB, then its perpendicular gradient: mAB=y2−y1x2−x1,m⊥=−1mABm_{AB}=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1},\qquad m_{\perp}=-\frac1{m_{AB}}mAB​=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​,m⊥​=−mAB​1​
  3. Use the midpoint in point-gradient form: y−yM=m⊥(x−xM)y-y_M=m_{\perp}(x-x_M)y−yM​=m⊥​(x−xM​)

Equidistant points

  • Equidistant means the same distance away.
  • If AC=BCAC=BCAC=BC, point CCC is equidistant from AAA and BBB.
  • Every such point lies on the perpendicular bisector of ABABAB. The set of all possible positions is called a locus.
  • Squaring both distances removes the square roots. The x2x^2x2 and y2y^2y2 terms then cancel, leaving a straight-line equation.

Key ideas

  • Parallel finite-gradient lines: equal gradients.
  • Perpendicular finite-gradient lines: their gradients multiply to -1.
  • A perpendicular bisector needs the midpoint and the perpendicular gradient.
  • Points equidistant from two fixed points lie on their perpendicular bisector.
  • When a line equation is not in y=mx+cy=mx+cy=mx+c form, rearrange it first so the gradient is visible.

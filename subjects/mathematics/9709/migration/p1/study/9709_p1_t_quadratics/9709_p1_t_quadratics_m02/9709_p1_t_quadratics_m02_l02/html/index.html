Special square forms

Same quadratic.
Different surface.

A question may hide completed-square form inside a bracket, an outside factor, or a negative leading term. Expand the target first. Its coefficients tell you what every unknown must be.

Start the lesson

Recognise the form before you calculate

  • A quadratic has a highest power of 2, such as x2+6x+5x^2+6x+5x2+6x+5.
  • A coefficient is a number multiplying a variable. In 6x6x6x, the coefficient of xxx is 6.
  • A constant has no variable. In x2+6x+5x^2+6x+5x2+6x+5, the constant is 5.
  • Completed-square form rewrites the quadratic using one squared bracket.
  • Different-looking forms can represent exactly the same expression.

Four forms you may see

  • Standard completed square: x2+6x+5=(x+3)2−4x^2+6x+5=(x+3)^2-4x2+6x+5=(x+3)2−4.
  • Coefficient inside the square: (kx+a)2+b(kx+a)^2+b(kx+a)2+b, for example 4x2−12x+13=(2x−3)2+44x^2-12x+13=(2x-3)^2+44x2−12x+13=(2x−3)2+4.
  • Factor outside the whole bracket: a[(x+c)2+d]a[(x+c)^2+d]a[(x+c)2+d], for example 2x2+4x+12=2[(x+1)2+5]2x^2+4x+12=2[(x+1)^2+5]2x2+4x+12=2[(x+1)2+5].
  • Negative-leading maximum form: A−(x−b)2A-(x-b)^2A−(x−b)2, for example 6x−x2−5=4−(x−3)26x-x^2-5=4-(x-3)^26x−x2−5=4−(x−3)2.

Surface changes. The check stays the same: expand and compare every term.

Compare-coefficients method

  1. Expand the target form.
  2. Match the x2x^2x2, xxx, and constant terms.
  3. Solve for the unknown letters.
  4. Substitute them back and expand once more to check.

Worked example

Express 4x2−12x+134x^2-12x+134x2−12x+13 in the form (2x+a)2+b(2x+a)^2+b(2x+a)2+b.

  1. Expand the target: (2x+a)2+b=4x2+4ax+a2+b.(2x+a)^2+b=4x^2+4ax+a^2+b.(2x+a)2+b=4x2+4ax+a2+b.
  2. Match the coefficient of xxx: 4a=−12,a=−3.4a=-12,\quad a=-3.4a=−12,a=−3.
  3. Match constants: a2+b=13,a^2+b=13,a2+b=13, 9+b=13,b=4.9+b=13,\quad b=4.9+b=13,b=4.
  4. Check: (2x−3)2+4=4x2−12x+9+4=4x2−12x+13.(2x-3)^2+4=4x^2-12x+9+4=4x^2-12x+13.(2x−3)2+4=4x2−12x+9+4=4x2−12x+13.

Answer: 4x2−12x+13=(2x−3)2+44x^2-12x+13=(2x-3)^2+44x2−12x+13=(2x−3)2+4.

When the whole bracket keeps a factor

  • The required form a[(x+c)2+d]a[(x+c)^2+d]a[(x+c)2+d] keeps aaa outside the entire bracket.
  • First factor aaa out of every term.
  • Then complete the square inside the bracket.
  • Keep the outside factor in place when you simplify.

Worked example

Write 2x2+4x+122x^2+4x+122x2+4x+12 in the form 2[(x+c)2+d]2[(x+c)^2+d]2[(x+c)2+d].

  1. Factor 2 from the whole quadratic: 2x2+4x+12=2(x2+2x+6).2x^2+4x+12=2(x^2+2x+6).2x2+4x+12=2(x2+2x+6).
  2. Inside the bracket, half the coefficient 2 to get 1.
  3. Add and subtract 121^212: x2+2x+6=x2+2x+1−1+6.x^2+2x+6=x^2+2x+1-1+6.x2+2x+6=x2+2x+1−1+6.
  4. The first three terms form a square: x2+2x+1=(x+1)2.x^2+2x+1=(x+1)^2.x2+2x+1=(x+1)2.
  5. So 2x2+4x+12=2[(x+1)2+5].2x^2+4x+12=2[(x+1)^2+5].2x2+4x+12=2[(x+1)2+5].
  6. Check: 2[(x+1)2+5]=2(x2+2x+1+5)=2x2+4x+12.2[(x+1)^2+5]=2(x^2+2x+1+5)=2x^2+4x+12.2[(x+1)2+5]=2(x2+2x+1+5)=2x2+4x+12.

Answer: 2[(x+1)2+5]2[(x+1)^2+5]2[(x+1)2+5].

The outside sign tells you minimum or maximum

  • A square such as (x+2)2(x+2)^2(x+2)2 is always zero or positive.
  • If its coefficient is positive, the graph opens upward and its turning point is a minimum.
  • If its coefficient is negative, the graph opens downward and its turning point is a maximum.
  • The turning point, also called the vertex, is where the curve changes direction.

Worked example · positive coefficient

Express 3x2+12x+53x^2+12x+53x2+12x+5 in the form a(x+h)2+ka(x+h)^2+ka(x+h)2+k.

  1. a=3a=3a=3, so factor 3 from the xxx terms: 3(x2+4x)+5.3(x^2+4x)+5.3(x2+4x)+5.
  2. Half of 4 is 2; its square is 4: 3(x2+4x+4−4)+5.3(x^2+4x+4-4)+5.3(x2+4x+4−4)+5.
  3. Form the square and distribute 3: 3((x+2)2−4)+5=3(x+2)2−12+5.3((x+2)^2-4)+5=3(x+2)^2-12+5.3((x+2)2−4)+5=3(x+2)2−12+5.
  4. Therefore 3x2+12x+5=3(x+2)2−7.3x^2+12x+5=3(x+2)^2-7.3x2+12x+5=3(x+2)2−7.

The square is zero when x=−2x=-2x=−2. The turning point is (−2,−7)(-2,-7)(−2,−7) and the minimum value is −7-7−7.

Worked example · negative coefficient

Express −x2+6x−5-x^2+6x-5−x2+6x−5 in the form a(x+h)2+ka(x+h)^2+ka(x+h)2+k.

  1. Factor out −1-1−1. Every sign inside changes: −(x2−6x)−5.-(x^2-6x)-5.−(x2−6x)−5.
  2. Half of −6-6−6 is −3-3−3; its square is 9: −(x2−6x+9−9)−5.-(x^2-6x+9-9)-5.−(x2−6x+9−9)−5.
  3. Form the square and distribute the outside minus: −((x−3)2−9)−5=−(x−3)2+9−5.-((x-3)^2-9)-5=-(x-3)^2+9-5.−((x−3)2−9)−5=−(x−3)2+9−5.
  4. Therefore −x2+6x−5=−(x−3)2+4.-x^2+6x-5=-(x-3)^2+4.−x2+6x−5=−(x−3)2+4.

The square is zero when x=3x=3x=3. The turning point is (3,4)(3,4)(3,4) and the maximum value is 444.

Key ideas

  • Read the required form before choosing a method.
  • For (kx+a)2+b(kx+a)^2+b(kx+a)2+b, expand and compare coefficients.
  • For a[(x+c)2+d]a[(x+c)^2+d]a[(x+c)2+d], factor aaa from the entire quadratic, then complete the square inside.
  • For a negative leading coefficient, factor out the negative sign carefully; every enclosed sign changes.
  • At the turning point, the squared bracket equals zero.
  • Positive outside coefficient means minimum. Negative outside coefficient means maximum.
  • Finish by expanding your result and comparing every term.

Exact quadratic solutions

Keep the roots exact.

When a quadratic does not factor neatly, completing the square can isolate one square. Taking its square root then gives every real solution.

Start the lesson · Go to practice

Square roots give two possibilities

  • A solution, or root, is a value of the variable that makes an equation true.
  • Squaring a positive number or its negative gives the same result: 52=(−5)2=255^2=(-5)^2=2552=(−5)2=25.
  • The symbol 25\sqrt{25}25​ means the principal, nonnegative square root, which is 5.
  • When solving x2=25x^2=25x2=25, include both signs: x=5x=5x=5 or x=−5x=-5x=−5.
  • Write both at once as x=±5x=\pm5x=±5. Read ±\pm± as “plus or minus”.

Taking the square root

(x+1)2=7⟹x+1=±7(x+1)^2=7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x+1=\pm\sqrt7(x+1)2=7⟹x+1=±7​

Both signs are required because both values square to 7.

Exact surd form

  • A rational number can be written as a fraction of two integers, with a nonzero denominator.
  • A surd is a root left in exact form when it is not a rational number.
  • 8\sqrt88​ is exact; 2.828…2.828\ldots2.828… is a decimal approximation.
  • “Give an exact answer” means keep the root symbol. Do not replace it with a rounded decimal.
  • To simplify a surd, find the largest perfect-square factor and take its square root outside.
8=4×2=22\sqrt8=\sqrt{4\times2}=2\sqrt28​=4×2​=22​
18=9×2=32\sqrt{18}=\sqrt{9\times2}=3\sqrt218​=9×2​=32​
20=4×5=25\sqrt{20}=\sqrt{4\times5}=2\sqrt520​=4×5​=25​

Isolate the square first. Then take both square roots.

From square form to solutions

  • Write the quadratic equation in completed-square form.
  • The number aaa multiplies the whole squared bracket and must be nonzero.
  • The letters hhh and kkk are constants.

General square form

a(x+h)2+k=0a(x+h)^2+k=0a(x+h)2+k=0
  1. Move kkk to the other side: a(x+h)2=−ka(x+h)^2=-ka(x+h)2=−k.
  2. Divide both sides by aaa: (x+h)2=−ka(x+h)^2=-\frac{k}{a}(x+h)2=−ak​.
  3. Call the right-hand side r=−kar=-\frac{k}{a}r=−ak​ and check its sign.
  4. If r>0r>0r>0, then x=−h±rx=-h\pm\sqrt rx=−h±r​: two distinct real roots.
  5. If r=0r=0r=0, then x=−hx=-hx=−h: one repeated root.
  6. If r<0r<0r<0, there are no real roots because the square of a real number cannot be negative.

For a monic quadratic, a=1a=1a=1, so r=−kr=-kr=−k.

Worked example 1

Solve x2+6x−1=0x^2+6x-1=0x2+6x−1=0 in exact form.

Solution

  1. Half of 6 is 3; its square is 9.
  2. (x+3)2−9−1=0(x+3)^2-9-1=0(x+3)2−9−1=0
  3. (x+3)2=10(x+3)^2=10(x+3)2=10
  4. x+3=±10x+3=\pm\sqrt{10}x+3=±10​
  5. x=−3±10x=-3\pm\sqrt{10}x=−3±10​

Solutions: x=−3+10x=-3+\sqrt{10}x=−3+10​ or x=−3−10x=-3-\sqrt{10}x=−3−10​.

The decimals are approximately 0.16 and -6.16, but those are not exact answers.

What one repeated root means

  • The graph of a quadratic is a parabola.
  • The x-axis is the horizontal line where y = 0.
  • A real root is an x-coordinate where the parabola meets the x-axis.
  • When the isolated square equals zero, both plus-or-minus cases give the same x-value.
  • On the graph, the turning point lies on the x-axis and the curve touches it once.
  • That single x-value is called a repeated root.

Key ideas

  • Complete the square.
  • Isolate the squared bracket.
  • Check whether its value is positive, zero or negative.
  • For a positive value, take both square roots and keep the surd exact.
  • For zero, the plus and minus cases coincide, giving one repeated root.
  • For a negative value, state that there are no real roots.

Exactly one root

  • The x-axis is the horizontal line where y=0y=0y=0.
  • A real root is the x-coordinate where the parabola meets this line.
  • Exactly one root occurs when the turning point sits on the x-axis, so the curve just touches it.

Worked example 4

Find ccc so that x2+6x+c=0x^2+6x+c=0x2+6x+c=0 has exactly one root. State the root.

Solution

  1. Half of 6 is 3; its square is 9.
  2. x2+6x+c=(x+3)2−9+cx^2+6x+c=(x+3)^2-9+cx2+6x+c=(x+3)2−9+c
  3. The constant after completing the square is k=c−9k=c-9k=c−9.
  4. For exactly one root, set k=0k=0k=0: c−9=0c-9=0c−9=0, so c=9c=9c=9.
  5. (x+3)2=0(x+3)^2=0(x+3)2=0, so the repeated root is x=−3x=-3x=−3.

c=9c=9c=9; repeated root x=−3x=-3x=−3.

Watch out

Keep the plus-or-minus

Wrong: (x+3)2=10⇒x=−3+10(x+3)^2=10\Rightarrow x=-3+\sqrt{10}(x+3)2=10⇒x=−3+10​.

Correct: x=−3±10x=-3\pm\sqrt{10}x=−3±10​. The negative of the principal square root gives a second solution.

Move the constant with its sign changed

Wrong: (x+3)2−10=0⇒(x+3)2=−10(x+3)^2-10=0\Rightarrow (x+3)^2=-10(x+3)2−10=0⇒(x+3)2=−10.

Correct: add 10 to both sides, giving (x+3)2=10(x+3)^2=10(x+3)2=10.

Use the one-root condition

After completing the square, exactly one root means the remaining constant is zero. Then the squared bracket itself must equal zero.

Quadratic inequalities

Find the boundaries.
Choose the region.

A quadratic can be positive in one part of the number line and negative in another. Its roots mark possible boundaries between sign regions.

Start the lesson

Read the signs first

  • An inequality compares values. <<< means less than and >>> means greater than.
  • ≤\le≤ means less than or equal to. ≥\ge≥ means greater than or equal to.
  • A strict sign, <<< or >>>, excludes a boundary. An open circle shows this on a number line.
  • A non-strict sign, ≤\le≤ or ≥\ge≥, includes a boundary. A filled circle shows this.
  • An interval is a connected set of values. −2<x<3-2<x<3−2<x<3 means every value between −2-2−2 and 333.
  • In interval notation, square brackets include endpoints: [−2,3][-2,3][−2,3]. Round brackets exclude them: (−2,3)(-2,3)(−2,3).
  • x<−2 or x>3x<-2\text{ or }x>3x<−2 or x>3 means the two outside regions.

Four building blocks

  1. Set the quadratic equal to zero. For x2−x−6=0x^2-x-6=0x2−x−6=0, factorise to (x−3)(x+2)=0(x-3)(x+2)=0(x−3)(x+2)=0.
  2. The roots, x=−2x=-2x=−2 and x=3x=3x=3, are boundary values. They split the number line into regions.
  3. The coefficient of x2x^2x2 gives direction: positive opens upward; negative opens downward.
  4. Below the xxx-axis means the quadratic is negative. Above it means positive.

A reliable method

  1. Move everything to one side so the other side is zero.
  2. Solve the boundary equation ax2+bx+c=0ax^2+bx+c=0ax2+bx+c=0.
  3. Mark the critical values on a small sketch or number line.
  4. Choose where the curve is above or below the xxx-axis.
  5. Copy the strict or non-strict endpoint signs carefully.

Key ideas

  • Move everything to one side.
  • Solve the matching equation to find boundary values.
  • Use direction and the inequality sign to choose inside or outside.
  • Use open endpoints for <<< and >>>.
  • Use filled endpoints for ≤\le≤ and ≥\ge≥.
  • For a squared expression, keep both square-root branches.

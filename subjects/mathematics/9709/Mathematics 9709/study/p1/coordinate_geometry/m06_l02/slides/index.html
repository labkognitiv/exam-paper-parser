Worked example · our two circles

How do C₁ and C₂ sit?

C₁: (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25 and C₂: (x − 8)² + (y − 4)² = 10. Do they meet? If so, how?

  1. Read off the centres and radii: (2, 1), r₁ = 5 and (8, 4), r₂ = √10 ≈ 3.16.
  2. The centres are 6 along, 3 up:
    d = √(6² + 3²) = √45 != 3√5 ≈ 6.71
  3. The sum and the difference of the radii:
    r₁ + r₂ = 5 + √10 ≈ 8.16 r₁ − r₂ = 5 − √10 ≈ 1.84
  4. 1.84 < 6.71 < 8.16. So d is in between: the circles cross at two points.

Worked example · where they touch

The touching point is on the line of centres

Circle S has centre (1, 1) and radius √5. Circle T has centre (7, 4) and radius 2√5. Show that they touch, and find the point R where they touch.

  1. The centres are 6 along, 3 up:
    d = √(6² + 3²) = √45 = 3√5 r₁ + r₂ = √5 + 2√5 = 3√5
    They are equal, so the circles touch on the outside.
  2. R is √5 from S's centre, out of 3√5. That is √5 ÷ 3√5 = 1/3 of the way to T's centre.
  3. A third of the jump (6, 3) is (2, 1):
    !R = (1 + 2, 1 + 1) = (3, 2)

Part 2

The common chord

When two circles cross, subtract one equation from the other. What is left is a straight line.

What a common chord is · Subtract to get the line · The two points · The line of centres · The kite

A new word

Common chord

IN PLAIN WORDS
When two circles cross at P and Q, the chord PQ belongs to both circles. "Common" means shared.
EXAMPLE
C₁ and C₂ cross at P(5, 5) and Q(7, 1). PQ is their common chord. It lies on the line 2x + y = 15.
WATCH OUT
To find its line, write both circles in expanded form, x² + y² + … = 0, with just 1 in front of x² and y².

Worked example · subtract to get the line

The equation of the common chord

C₁: x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0 and C₂: (x − 8)² + (y − 4)² = 10. Find the equation of their common chord.

  1. Expand C₂ so both look the same:
    x² − 16x + 64 + y² − 8y + 16 = 10 x² + y² − 16x − 8y + 70 = 0
  2. P and Q fit both equations. So subtract one from the other. The x² and the y² cancel:
    −4x − (−16x) = 12x −2y − (−8y) = 6y −20 − 70 = −90
  3. What is left is a straight line. Divide by 6:
    12x + 6y − 90 = 0 !2x + y = 15

Worked example · your go first

The two points, and the chord's length

Use 2x + y = 15 to find P and Q, where C₁ and C₂ cross. Then find the exact length PQ.

This is lesson 10 again: a line and a circle. Try it first.

  1. Get y on its own, y = 15 − 2x. Put it into C₁. Then y − 1 becomes 14 − 2x:
    (x − 2)² + (14 − 2x)² = 25 5x² − 60x + 175 = 0 x² − 12x + 35 = 0
  2. (x − 5)(x − 7) = 0. Put x = 5 and x = 7 into y = 15 − 2x:
    !P = (5, 5), Q = (7, 1)
  3. P to Q is 2 along, 4 down:
    PQ = √(2² + 4²) = √20 != 2√5

The kite

The centres and the crossing points make a kite

Join C₁, P, C₂ and Q. C₁P = C₁Q = 5 and C₂P = C₂Q = √10. Two pairs of equal sides: a kite (lesson 4).

Its diagonals are C₁C₂ = 3√5 and PQ = 2√5. They cross at a right angle.

Area = ½ × 3√5 × 2√5 = ½ × 6 × 5 = 15.
Kite area: half of one diagonal times the other.

Sliding a circle

A translation slides the whole circle

A translation is a slide. No turning, no stretching. The vector 3−2 means 3 right and 2 down.

Every point of C₁ slides the same way. The centre goes from (2, 1) to (2 + 3, 1 − 2) = (5, −1). The radius stays 5.

New circle: (x − 5)² + (y + 1)² = 25.
Move the centre. Keep the radius.

The sign trap

Translate C₁ by 3−2

C₁ is (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25.

✗ Adding inside the brackets

x − 2 + 3 = x + 1 y − 1 − 2 = y − 3 (x + 1)² + (y − 3)² = 25

That circle has centre (−1, 3). It moved left and up: the wrong way both times.

✓ Moving the centre

Centre = (2 + 3, 1 − 2) = (5, −1) !(x − 5)² + (y + 1)² = 25

The number in a bracket is the opposite of the centre's number. Move the centre first, then write the brackets.

Reflecting in a line

Reflect C₂ in the line y = 2

A reflection flips the circle over a mirror line. Only the centre needs working out.

Find the centre

C₂ has centre (8, 4) and radius √10.

How far from the mirror?

From y = 4 down to y = 2 is 2.

The same on the other side

2 below y = 2 is y = 0. New centre (8, 0).

Same radius

(x − 8)² + y² = 10.

x stays 8: the mirror is flat, so only y changes.

Worked example · your go first

Reflect C₁ in its tangent at T(6, 4)

The line 4x + 3y = 36 is the tangent to C₁ at T(6, 4). C₁ is reflected in this line. Find the equation of the new circle.

Think about lesson 8: how does a tangent meet the radius? Try it first.

  1. The radius C₁T meets the tangent at a right angle. So the mirror is at a right angle to C₁T, at T.
  2. The new centre is as far past T as C₁ is before it. So T is the midpoint. Make the jump from C₁ to T again:
    C₁ to T = 4 along, 3 up New centre = (6 + 4, 4 + 3) = (10, 7)
  3. Same radius, 5:
    !(x − 10)² + (y − 7)² = 25
    The two circles touch at T.

Exam-style question · 11 marks

Two circles, and a slide

Circle C₁ has equation x² + y² − 2x + 4y = 0. Circle C₂ has centre (13, 4) and radius 2√5.

(a) Find the centre and the radius of C₁.[3]

(b) Show that C₁ and C₂ do not meet, and find the shortest distance between them.[3]

(c) C₁ is translated by 63 to give C₃. Find the equation of C₃, and show that C₃ and C₂ touch.[3]

(d) Find the coordinates of the point where C₃ and C₂ touch.[2]

Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

Part (a) · 3 marks

The centre and radius of C₁

Complete the square, as in lesson 7. Try it first.

  1. Group the x terms and the y terms:
    x² − 2x + y² + 4y = 0
  2. Complete the square on each:
    (x − 1)² − 1 + (y + 2)² − 4 = 0 (x − 1)² + (y + 2)² = 5
  3. Read them off:
    !Centre (1, −2), radius √5

Part (b) · 3 marks

They don't meet: find the gap

Compare d with r₁ + r₂. The gap is what is left over. Try it first.

  1. From (1, −2) to (13, 4) is 12 along, 6 up:
    d = √(12² + 6²) = √180 != 6√5
  2. Add the radii:
    r₁ + r₂ = √5 + 2√5 = 3√5
    6√5 is more than 3√5, so the circles do not meet.
  3. Along the line of centres, take off both radii:
    Gap = 6√5 − 3√5 != 3√5

Part (c) · 3 marks

Slide C₁ to get C₃

Move the centre, keep the radius. Then test d again. Try it first.

  1. Move the centre 6 right, 3 up:
    Centre = (1 + 6, −2 + 3) = (7, 1) !(x − 7)² + (y − 1)² = 5
  2. From (7, 1) to (13, 4) is 6 along, 3 up:
    d = √(6² + 3²) = √45 = 3√5
  3. d = 3√5 = √5 + 2√5 = r₁ + r₂. So C₃ and C₂ touch on the outside. The slide was √(6² + 3²) = 3√5: exactly the gap.

Part (d) · 2 marks

Where C₃ and C₂ touch

The touching point is on the line of centres. How far along? Try it first.

  1. R is √5 from (7, 1), out of 3√5. That is 1/3 of the way to (13, 4).
  2. A third of the jump (6, 3) is (2, 1):
    !R = (7 + 2, 1 + 1) = (9, 2)
  3. Check R is on C₂:
    (9 − 13)² + (2 − 4)² = 16 + 4 = 20 ✓
    And 20 = (2√5)².

Common mistakes · Summary

Common mistakes

Six slips that cost marks

Checking only the sum

✗ d is less than r₁ + r₂, so they cross

✓ Also check d is more than r₁ − r₂. If not, one is inside the other

Touching at the midpoint

✗ R is halfway between the centres

✓ R is r₁ from C₁: go r₁ ÷ d of the way

Subtracting before expanding

✗ (x − 2)² − (x − 8)², bracket by bracket

✓ Expand both to x² + y² + … = 0 first. Then subtract

The sign trap

✗ Translate by 3−2: (x − 2 + 3)²

✓ Move the centre to (5, −1): (x − 5)² + (y + 1)²

Changing the radius

✗ The new circle has a new radius

✓ Slides and reflections never change the radius

Reflecting by changing a sign

✗ (8, 4) reflected in y = 2 is (8, −4)

✓ 2 above the mirror becomes 2 below it: (8, 0)

Two circles: compare d with the radii

Apartd > r₁ + r₂
Touch outsided = r₁ + r₂. R is r₁ ÷ d of the way from C₁ to C₂
Crossr₁ − r₂ < d < r₁ + r₂
Touch insided = r₁ − r₂
One insided < r₁ − r₂

Methods

  1. Common chord: expand both, subtract, then put the line back into one circle. The line of centres cuts it in half at 90°.
  2. Translate: add the vector to the centre. Same radius.
  3. Reflect: reflect the centre only. Same radius. In a tangent at T, T is the midpoint of the old and new centres.

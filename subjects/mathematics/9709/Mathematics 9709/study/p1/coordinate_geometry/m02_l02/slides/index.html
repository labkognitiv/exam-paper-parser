A new word

Quadrilateral

IN PLAIN WORDS
A flat shape with four straight sides. "Quad" means four. The corners are called vertices.
EXAMPLE
ABCD with A(0, 2), B(5, 1), C(8, 6), D(3, 7). The letters go round the edge in order: A to B to C to D, and back to A.
WATCH OUT
A and C are opposite, not next to each other. AC and BD are the diagonals: they go across the inside, not round the edge.

Part 2

The parallelogram

Three corners are given. Where is the fourth?

Opposite sides are the same jump · Finding D · Two ways · Drag D · Why the order matters

Opposite sides

Opposite sides are the same jump

Walk from A to B: 5 along, 1 down. Now walk from D to C. It is the same walk: 5 along, 1 down.

The same is true of the other pair. B to C and A to D are both 3 along, 5 up.

Same walk means same gradient and same length.
In a parallelogram, opposite sides are the same jump.

Worked example · the fourth corner

A(0, 2), B(5, 1) and C(8, 6)

These are three corners of parallelogram ABCD, in that order. Find D.

  1. In order means AD is the side opposite BC. So find the jump from B to C:
    Along = 8 − 5 = 3 Up = 6 − 1 = 5
  2. Make the same jump from A:
    !D = (0 + 3, 2 + 5) = (3, 7)
  3. Check the other pair. A to B is 5 along, 1 down. D to C is 8 − 3 = 5 along and 6 − 7 = −1 up ✓

Another way to get the same corner

Two ways to find D

A(0, 2), B(5, 1), C(8, 6) are three corners of parallelogram ABCD. Find D.

Way 1: the same jump

B to C = 3 along, 5 up D = (0 + 3, 2 + 5) !D = (3, 7)

Quick, and easy to check on the grid.

Way 2: the diagonals share a midpoint

Midpoint of AC = (4, 4) (5 + x) ÷ 2 = 4 5 + x = 8 x = 3 (1 + y) ÷ 2 = 4 !y = 7

The midpoint of BD must also be (4, 4). Use this way when a question says "hence" after a midpoint.

Both ways give D(3, 7). A parallelogram's diagonals always cut each other in half.

Opposite sides

Same gradients? Then it is a parallelogram.

One corner

Gradients multiply to −1? Then it is a rectangle.

Two sides next to each other

Same length? Then it is a rhombus, or a square if check 3 passed too.

Worked example · a rectangle

E(0, 3), F(6, 0), G(8, 4), H(2, 7)

Show that EFGH is a rectangle, and find its area.

  1. Gradients of the four sides, up ÷ along:
    EF = −3 ÷ 6 = −1/2 HG = −3 ÷ 6 = −1/2 FG = 4 ÷ 2 = 2 EH = 4 ÷ 2 = 2
    Opposite sides match, so EFGH is a parallelogram.
  2. The corner at F: −1/2 × 2 = −1. That is a right angle, so EFGH is a rectangle.
  3. The two side lengths:
    EF = √(6² + 3²) = √45 = 3√5 FG = √(2² + 4²) = √20 = 2√5
  4. Area of a rectangle is length × width:
    Area = 3√5 × 2√5 = 6 × 5 != 30

Area from the diagonals

A rhombus is half of its box

Here is a rhombus with corners (1, 4), (4, 6), (7, 4) and (4, 2). Its diagonals are 6 long and 4 long.

Draw a box around it. The box is 6 by 4.

The diagonals cut the box into 4 small rectangles. The rhombus takes exactly half of each one. Count the squares if you like.

Box = 6 × 4 = 24 !Rhombus = 24 ÷ 2 = 12

Area = half of one diagonal times the other.

The formula

Area from the two diagonals

Area = ½ × d₁ × d₂
½The shape is half of the box around its diagonals.
d₁The length of one diagonal. Use the distance formula.
d₂The length of the other diagonal.
Only if they cross at 90°Rhombus, square and kite: yes. A rectangle or a plain parallelogram: no, use its sides.

In a square both diagonals are the same length d, so the area is ½ × d × d = ½d².

Worked example · your go first

R(1, 1), S(5, 3), T(7, 7), U(3, 5)

Show that RSTU is a rhombus, and find its area.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. Compare the squares of the sides:
    RS² = 4² + 2² = 20 ST² = 2² + 4² = 20
    TU and UR are the same jumps backwards, so they are 20 too. Four equal sides: a rhombus.
  2. Diagonal RT has gradient 6 ÷ 6 = 1. SU has gradient 2 ÷ (−2) = −1. They multiply to −1 ✓
  3. The diagonal lengths:
    RT = √(6² + 6²) = √72 = 6√2 SU = √(2² + 2²) = √8 = 2√2
  4. Half of one times the other:
    Area = ½ × 6√2 × 2√2 = ½ × 12 × 2 != 12

Part 4

Kites and squares

A mirror line, or a centre, finds the missing corner.

What a kite is · The mirror finds the corner · A square from its diagonal · Quick check

A new word

Kite

IN PLAIN WORDS
Two pairs of equal sides, with each pair next to each other. One diagonal is a mirror line: fold along it and the two halves match.
EXAMPLE
W(0, 1), X(5, 2), Y(5, 6), Z(1, 6). WX = WZ = √26 and YX = YZ = 4. The mirror line is WY.
WATCH OUT
The mirror diagonal WY cuts XZ in half, at a right angle. XZ does not cut WY in half.

The mirror finds the corner

W(0, 1) and Y(5, 6) are on the mirror line. X is (5, 2). Find Z.

1. Two lines

Mirror WY: gradient 5 ÷ 5 = 1, so y = x + 1. XZ crosses it at 90°, so gradient −1 through X: y = −x + 7.

2. Where they meet

x + 1 = −x + 7, so 2x = 6 and x = 3. Then y = 4. They meet at M(3, 4), the midpoint of XZ.

3. The same jump again

X to M is 2 left, 2 up. Do it again from M: Z = (1, 6).

Worked example · your go first

P(2, 0) and R(4, 8) are opposite corners of square PQRS

Find the centre, the equation of QS, the corners Q and S, and the area.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. The centre is the midpoint of PR: M = (3, 4).
  2. PR has gradient 8 ÷ 2 = 4. QS crosses it at 90°, through M:
    Gradient of QS = −1/4 !y − 4 = −1/4 (x − 3)
  3. M to R is 1 along, 4 up. Turn it a quarter turn: 4 along, 1 down. Go that way and the opposite way from M:
    Q = (3 + 4, 4 − 1) = (7, 3) !S = (3 − 4, 4 + 1) = (−1, 5)
  4. Both diagonals are √68, because PR² = 2² + 8² = 68:
    Area = ½ × √68 × √68 = ½ × 68 != 34

Part 5

Trapeziums and letters

One pair of parallel sides, and unknown numbers in the corners.

What a trapezium is · Parallel sides with k · One clue, one job

A new word

Trapezium

IN PLAIN WORDS
A quadrilateral with one pair of parallel sides. Its area is half the two parallel sides added, times the gap between them.
EXAMPLE
Parallel sides 7 and 4, gap 3. Area = ½ × (7 + 4) × 3 = 16.5.
WATCH OUT
The gap is measured at a right angle to the parallel sides, not along a slanted side. If a side meets both parallel sides at 90°, that side is the gap.

Parallel sides with k

P(−1, 0), Q(5, 3), R(6, 7), S(k, 5)

In quadrilateral PQRS, PQ is parallel to SR. Find k.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps.

  1. Gradient of PQ: 3 ÷ 6 = 1/2.
  2. Parallel, so SR has the same gradient. Up is 7 − 5 = 2, along is 6 − k:
    2 ÷ (6 − k) = 1/2 6 − k = 4 !k = 2
Your turn: A(0, 2), B(4, 4), C(5, 8), D(k, 7). AB is parallel to DC. Find k.

Part 6

A full exam question

Four parts, one part per slide. Try each on paper before you reveal it.

(a) Find h · (b) Find D · (c) Three exact lengths · (d) The area

Exam-style question · 10 marks

The trapezium ABCD

A is (−2, 3), B is (2, 1) and C is (h, h + 1). Angle ABC = 90°. CD is parallel to the x-axis, and AD is parallel to BC.

(a) Find the gradient of AB, and show that h = 4.[3]

(b) Find the coordinates of D.[3]

(c) Find the exact lengths of AB, BC and AD.[2]

(d) Hence find the area of the trapezium ABCD.[2]

Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

Part (a) · 3 marks

The gradient of AB, and h

Try it first. Then show the steps.

  1. Up −2, along 4:
    Gradient of AB = −2 ÷ 4 != −1/2
  2. Angle ABC = 90°, so BC is perpendicular to AB. Flip and change the sign:
    !Gradient of BC = 2
  3. Write the gradient of BC with h in it. Up is (h + 1) − 1 = h, along is h − 2:
    h ÷ (h − 2) = 2 h = 2h − 4 !h = 4

So C is (4, 5).

Part (b) · 3 marks

Find D

Give each clue one job. Try it first.

  1. CD is parallel to the x-axis, so it is flat. D is at the same height as C: D = (x, 5).
  2. AD is parallel to BC, so AD has gradient 2. From A(−2, 3) to D(x, 5), up is 5 − 3 = 2:
    2 ÷ (x − (−2)) = 2 x + 2 = 1 x = −1 !D = (−1, 5)

Part (c) · 2 marks

Three exact lengths

"Exact" means leave the square roots in. Try it first.

  1. A to B is 4 along, 2 down:
    AB = √(4² + 2²) = √20 != 2√5
  2. B to C is 2 along, 4 up:
    BC = √(2² + 4²) = √20 != 2√5
  3. A to D is 1 along, 2 up:
    !AD = √(1² + 2²) = √5

Part (d) · 2 marks

The area of the trapezium

"Hence" means use part (c). Which length is the gap?

  1. AD and BC are the parallel sides. AB meets BC at 90°, and AD is parallel to BC, so AB meets AD at 90° too. AB is the gap.
  2. Half the parallel sides added, times the gap:
    Area = ½ × (√5 + 2√5) × 2√5 = ½ × 3√5 × 2√5 = ½ × 6 × 5 != 15

√5 + 2√5 is 3√5, just like 1 apple + 2 apples = 3 apples.

Common mistakes · Summary

Common mistakes

Six slips that cost marks

Mixing up sides and diagonals

✗ In ABCD, AC is a side

✓ A and C are opposite. AC is a diagonal

Jumping from the wrong corner

✗ D = B + (the jump from B to C)

✓ AD matches BC: jump from A. D = (3, 7)

Stopping after the parallel sides

✗ Opposite sides parallel, so it's a rectangle

✓ That shows a parallelogram. You also need a right angle

Forgetting the half

✗ Rhombus area = 6√2 × 2√2 = 24

✓ ½ × 6√2 × 2√2 = 12

Halving the wrong diagonal of a kite

✗ M is the midpoint of the mirror line WY

✓ The mirror line cuts the other diagonal, XZ, in half

A slanted side as the gap

✗ Trapezium gap = any side that joins the parallel sides

✓ The gap meets the parallel sides at 90°

Summary

Everything on one page

What to show

ParallelogramBoth pairs of opposite sides have the same gradient
RectangleA parallelogram, and one corner's gradients multiply to −1
RhombusAll four sides the same length
SquareEqual sides and a right angle
AreaRectangle: length × width. Rhombus, square, kite: ½ × d₁ × d₂. Trapezium: ½ × (a + b) × gap

Finding a missing corner or letter

  1. Parallelogram: make the same jump as the opposite side, or use the shared midpoint.
  2. Kite: find M where the diagonals cross, then make the same jump again past M. Square: from the centre M, turn the half-diagonal a quarter turn.
  3. A clue with a letter: one clue, one equation. Parallel means the same gradient. A right angle means flip and change the sign. Flat means the same y.

A new word

Chord

IN PLAIN WORDS
A straight line from one point on a circle to another. A line crosses the circle at two points. The chord is only the piece between them.
EXAMPLE
The circle (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25 has centre C(2, 1) and radius 5. The line y = 2x − 8 crosses it at A(2, −4) and B(6, 4). AB is a chord.
WATCH OUT
A chord through the centre is a diameter, the longest chord. A tangent only touches once, so it makes no chord.

Worked example · the two ends

Where does y = 2x − 8 cross the circle?

The line y = 2x − 8 meets the circle (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25 at A and B. Find A and B.

  1. Put 2x − 8 in place of y. Then y − 1 becomes 2x − 9:
    (x − 2)² + (2x − 9)² = 25
  2. Multiply out both brackets. Collect everything on one side:
    x² − 4x + 4 + 4x² − 36x + 81 = 25 5x² − 40x + 60 = 0 x² − 8x + 12 = 0
  3. Factorise: (x − 2)(x − 6) = 0. So x = 2 or x = 6.
  4. Put each x back into the line to get its y:
    y = 2(2) − 8 = −4 y = 2(6) − 8 = 4 !A = (2, −4), B = (6, 4)

How long is the chord?

A question gives a line and a circle, and asks for the exact length of the chord. Build the method.

    For our chord: AB = √(4² + 8²) = √80 = 4√5. Leave the square root in. That is the exact length.

    Worked example · a line with k in it

    For which k does the line cut the circle?

    The line y = x + k meets the circle x² + y² = 18 at two different points. Find the set of values of k.

    You met b² − 4ac in lesson 5. Try it first, then show the steps.

    1. Put x + k in place of y, and tidy:
      x² + (x + k)² = 18 2x² + 2kx + k² − 18 = 0
    2. Two different points means b² − 4ac > 0. Here a = 2, b = 2k, c = k² − 18:
      (2k)² − 4 × 2 × (k² − 18) > 0 4k² − 8k² + 144 > 0 k² < 36
    3. k can be anything between −6 and 6:
      !−6 < k < 6
      At k = 6 and k = −6 the line only touches.

    The formula

    Half a chord, from the radius and the distance

    Triangle CMA has a right angle at M. So Pythagoras works: d² + (half-chord)² = r². Get the half-chord on its own:

    half-chord = √( r² − d² )
    r, the radiusFrom the centre C to an end, A. It is the longest side of the triangle.
    d, the distanceFrom C to the chord, at a right angle. It lands on the midpoint M.
    Subtract, then rootr is the longest side, so take d² away from r².
    Then double itThe whole chord is AB = 2√(r² − d²).

    Worked example · length and area

    Chord AB, from the centre

    M(4, 0) is the midpoint of chord AB. The circle has centre C(2, 1) and radius 5. Find the exact length AB, and the area of triangle CAB.

    1. d is the distance CM. C to M is 2 along, 1 down:
      d = √(2² + 1²) = √5
    2. The half-chord, from the formula:
      AM = √(25 − 5) = √20 = 2√5
    3. Double it. It matches the distance formula from before ✓
      !AB = 2 × 2√5 = 4√5
    4. Triangle CAB has base AB. Its height is d, the right-angle line CM:
      Area = ½ × 4√5 × √5 = ½ × 4 × 5 != 10

    Part 3

    How far is a point from a line?

    A formula that measures straight to a line, at a right angle.

    The distance formula · Using it · Two ways to get d · Cut, touch or miss, by distance

    The formula

    The distance from a point to a line

    The shortest way from a point to a line meets the line at a right angle. This formula gives that distance straight away.

    d = | ax₀ + by₀ + c |√(a² + b²)
    a, b and cWrite the line as ax + by + c = 0 first: everything on one side, 0 on the other.
    x₀ and y₀The point, often the centre. Put its numbers in place of x and y.
    The bars | |They mean "drop any minus sign". A distance is never negative.
    UnderneathOnly the two numbers in front of x and y. Square them, add, then root.

    Using the formula

    How far is C(2, 1) from y = 2x − 8?

    Try it first. Then show the steps.

    1. Everything on one side: 2x − y − 8 = 0. So a = 2, b = −1, c = −8.
    2. Put in x₀ = 2 and y₀ = 1:
      d = |4 − 1 − 8| ÷ √(4 + 1) = |−5| ÷ √5 = 5 ÷ √5 != √5
      The same √5 as CM. And 5 ÷ √5 = √5, because √5 × √5 = 5.
    Your turn: how far is (−1, 3) from the line 5x + 12y − 5 = 0?

    Two ways to get d

    How far is the centre C(2, 1) from chord AB?

    A(2, −4) and B(6, 4) are on the line y = 2x − 8.

    Way 1: through the midpoint

    M = ((2 + 6) ÷ 2, (−4 + 4) ÷ 2) = (4, 0) CM = √(2² + 1²) !d = √5

    Use this when you already know both ends of the chord.

    Way 2: the formula

    Line: 2x − y − 8 = 0 d = |4 − 1 − 8| ÷ √5 !d = √5

    Use this when you only have the line's equation.

    Both give √5. Pick the way that needs less work.

    Cuts: d less than r

    Part 4

    Finding the centre

    The perpendicular bisector of every chord goes through the centre.

    Two chords point to the centre · The method · A worked example

    Every chord points to the centre

    Two perpendicular bisectors meet at the centre

    The line from C at a right angle to a chord hits its midpoint. So it is the chord's perpendicular bisector (lesson 2).

    Chord AB: through M(4, 0), gradient −1/2. Chord PQ, from (−1, 5) to (5, 5): the line x = 2. Both go through C.

    Where two of them cross, you have found the centre.
    The perpendicular bisector of a chord goes through the centre.

    The method

    Find the centre from one chord and one line

    A question gives you a chord PQ, and a line that the centre is on.

    Midpoint of the chord

    Add and halve the ends of PQ.

    Flip the gradient

    Gradient of PQ, then flip it and change the sign.

    The bisector

    The line through the midpoint with that new gradient.

    Meet the other line

    Solve the two lines together. That point is the centre.

    Then the radius is the distance from the centre to P, or to Q.

    Worked example · your go first

    P(−3, 5) and Q(3, 7) are the ends of a chord

    The centre of the circle lies on the line y = 2x + 1. Find the centre, and the equation of the circle.

    Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

    1. Midpoint of PQ is (0, 6). Gradient of PQ is 2 ÷ 6 = 1/3.
    2. Flip and change the sign: −3. The bisector goes through (0, 6):
      y − 6 = −3(x − 0) y = −3x + 6
    3. The centre is on both lines:
      −3x + 6 = 2x + 1 5 = 5x x = 1, y = 3
    4. r² is the distance from (1, 3) to P, squared:
      r² = 4² + 2² = 20 !(x − 1)² + (y − 3)² = 20
      Check with Q: 2² + 4² = 20 ✓

    A full exam question

    Four parts, one part per slide. Try each on paper before you reveal it.

    (a) Centre and radius · (b) A and B · (c) A right angle · (d) Length and area

    Exam-style question · 12 marks

    A chord of a circle

    The circle x² + y² − 6x + 4y + 3 = 0 has centre C. The line x + 2y = 4 meets the circle at A and B.

    (a) Find the coordinates of C and the radius of the circle.[3]

    (b) Find the coordinates of A and B.[4]

    (c) M is the midpoint of AB. Show that CM is perpendicular to AB.[2]

    (d) Find the exact length of AB, and the area of triangle CAB.[3]

    Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

    Part (a) · 3 marks

    The centre and the radius

    Complete the square, as in lesson 7. Try it first.

    1. Group the x terms and the y terms:
      x² − 6x + y² + 4y + 3 = 0
    2. Complete the square on each:
      (x − 3)² − 9 + (y + 2)² − 4 + 3 = 0 (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 10
    3. Read off the centre and radius:
      !C = (3, −2), r = √10

    Part (b) · 4 marks

    Find A and B

    Tip: getting x on its own, x = 4 − 2y, avoids fractions. Try it first.

    1. Put 4 − 2y in place of x. Then x − 3 becomes 1 − 2y:
      (1 − 2y)² + (y + 2)² = 10
    2. Multiply out and tidy:
      1 − 4y + 4y² + y² + 4y + 4 = 10 5y² = 5 y = 1 or y = −1
    3. Put each y into x = 4 − 2y:
      x = 4 − 2(1) = 2 x = 4 − 2(−1) = 6 !A = (2, 1), B = (6, −1)

    Part (c) · 2 marks

    Show that CM is perpendicular to AB

    "Show that" means: work out both gradients and multiply. Try it first.

    1. Midpoint of A(2, 1) and B(6, −1): M = (4, 0).
    2. Up ÷ along for each line:
      Gradient of AB = −2 ÷ 4 = −1/2 Gradient of CM = 2 ÷ 1 = 2
    3. Multiply them:
      !−1/2 × 2 = −1
      So CM is perpendicular to AB.

    Part (d) · 3 marks

    The length of AB, and the area

    "Exact" means keep the square roots. Which length is the height? Try it first.

    1. A to B is 4 along, 2 down:
      AB = √(4² + 2²) = √20 != 2√5
    2. The height is CM, from C(3, −2) to M(4, 0):
      d = √(1² + 2²) = √5
    3. Half of base times height:
      Area = ½ × 2√5 × √5 = ½ × 2 × 5 != 5
      Check: √(r² − d²) = √(10 − 5) = √5, half of 2√5 ✓

    Common mistakes · Summary

    Common mistakes

    Six slips that cost marks

    The whole chord in Pythagoras

    ✗ d² + AB² = r²

    ✓ The right angle is at the midpoint: d² + (½AB)² = r²

    Mixing up the ends

    ✗ x = 2 goes with y = 4, so (2, 4)

    ✓ Put each x into the line: (2, −4) and (6, 4)

    Only one square root

    ✗ (x − 2)² = 16, so x = 6

    ✓ x − 2 = 4 or −4, so x = 6 or x = −2

    The wrong sign for c

    ✗ 2x − y = 8, so c = 8

    ✓ Make it 2x − y − 8 = 0 first: c = −8

    The radius as the height

    ✗ Area of CAB = ½ × AB × r

    ✓ The height meets AB at 90°: ½ × AB × d

    ≥ for two points

    ✗ Two points: b² − 4ac ≥ 0

    ✓ Two different points: b² − 4ac > 0. Equal to 0 is a tangent

Parallel lines

Lines that go the same way and never meet.

Parallel lines

Same steepness, never meet

Look at y = ½x + 3 and y = ½x − 1. Both have gradient ½. Go 2 along and both go 1 up.

They climb at the same rate, so the gap between them never changes. They never meet. Lines like this are parallel.

Only the number on the end is different. It moves the line up or down.
Parallel lines have the same gradient.

Finding the gradient

When the line isn't y = mx + c

Exam questions often give a line like 2x + 3y = 6. What is its gradient?

It is not 2. You have to get y on its own first:

2x + 3y = 6 3y = −2x + 6 !y = −2/3 x + 2

Now the number in front of x is the gradient: −2/3. The grid agrees: 3 along, 2 down.

Your turn: the gradient of 4x + 2y = 7 is…

Worked example · parallel line through a point

Parallel to y = 3x − 2, through (1, 5)

Find the equation of the line, in the form y = mx + c.

  1. Parallel means the same gradient:
    m = 3
  2. Put m = 3 and (1, 5) into y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). Multiply out, then add 5:
    y − 5 = 3(x − 1) y − 5 = 3x − 3 !y = 3x + 2
  3. Check with (1, 5): 3 × 1 + 2 = 5 ✓
Your turn: parallel to y = −2x + 1, through (3, 4)

Part 2

Perpendicular lines

Lines that meet at a right angle.

Turning a line 90° · Flip and change the sign · Two worked examples

Turning a line

Turn a line 90° and watch its gradient

Take a line with gradient 2. Its step is 1 along, 2 up.

Turn the step a quarter turn. The 2 up becomes 2 to the left. The 1 along becomes 1 up.

Going left is negative, so the new gradient is 1 ÷ (−2) = −1/2.
The 2 flipped over and changed sign.

The rule

Flip it and change the sign

A perpendicular line meets another line at a right angle. To get its gradient, flip the fraction and change the sign. The two gradients always multiply to −1.

Quick check

Parallel, perpendicular or neither?

1. A line has gradient −5. A line perpendicular to it has gradient…
2. The lines y = 3x + 1 and y = −⅓x + 4 are…
3. The lines 2x + y = 3 and y = 2x − 1 are…

Worked example · perpendicular line through a point

Perpendicular to y = 3x + 1, through (6, 1)

Find the equation of the line, in the form y = mx + c.

  1. The line y = 3x + 1 has gradient 3. Flip it and change the sign:
    m = −1/3
  2. Put m = −1/3 and (6, 1) into the formula. Multiply out, then add 1:
    y − 1 = −1/3 (x − 6) y − 1 = −1/3 x + 2 !y = −1/3 x + 3
  3. Check with (6, 1): −2 + 3 = 1 ✓

−1/3 × 6 = −2, because a third of 6 is 2.

Worked example · the line is given as ax + by = c

Perpendicular to x + 2y = 4, through (3, 1)

Find the equation of the line, in the form y = mx + c.

  1. Get y on its own to find the gradient:
    2y = −x + 4 y = −1/2 x + 2
  2. The gradient is −1/2. Flip it and change the sign:
    !m = 2
  3. Put m = 2 and (3, 1) into the formula:
    y − 1 = 2(x − 3) y − 1 = 2x − 6 !y = 2x − 5
Your turn: the gradient of a line perpendicular to 5x + 2y = 1

The perpendicular bisector

The line that cuts AB in half, at a right angle.

What it is · The four steps · A worked example · Equal distances give the same line

What it is

The perpendicular bisector of AB

Cut AB in half, at a right angle. The line that does it has three facts.

To find it: midpoint → gradient of AB → flip and change the sign → the line through M.

Worked example · the perpendicular bisector of AB

A(1, 2) and B(7, 10)

Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB.

  1. Find the midpoint:
    M = (4, 6)
  2. Find the gradient of AB. Then flip it and change the sign:
    Gradient of AB = 8 ÷ 6 = 4/3 !Bisector gradient = −3/4
  3. Write the line through M(4, 6):
    y − 6 = −3/4 (x − 4) y − 6 = −3/4 x + 3 !y = −3/4 x + 9
  4. With whole numbers only: times by 4, then move the x across:
    4y = −3x + 36 !3x + 4y = 36

Worked example · your go first

P(−3, 1) and Q(5, −3)

Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of PQ, in the form y = mx + c.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. Midpoint. Add and halve:
    x = (−3 + 5) ÷ 2 = 1 y = (1 + (−3)) ÷ 2 = −1
  2. Gradient of PQ. Up −4, along 8. Then flip and change the sign:
    Gradient of PQ = −4 ÷ 8 = −1/2 !Bisector gradient = 2
  3. Line through M(1, −1). Careful: y − (−1) is y + 1:
    y + 1 = 2(x − 1) y + 1 = 2x − 2 !y = 2x − 3

Another way to get the same line

Equal distances give the same line

C(x, y) is the same distance from A(1, 2) as from B(7, 10). Find the line C lies on.

Way 1: the four steps

M = (4, 6) Gradient of AB = 4/3 Bisector gradient = −3/4 !Line: 3x + 4y = 36

This is the worked example you just saw.

Way 2: equal distances

Try the first line yourself, then show the steps.

  1. The distances are equal, so their squares are too. Squares have no square roots:
    (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = (x − 7)² + (y − 10)²
  2. Multiply out. x² and y² are on both sides, so they cancel:
    −2x + 1 − 4y + 4 = −14x + 49 − 20y + 100
  3. Collect x and y on one side. Divide by 4:
    12x + 16y = 144 !3x + 4y = 36

Both ways give 3x + 4y = 36. Points equally far from A and B are always on the perpendicular bisector.

Parallel with k

The line through P(1, k) and Q(4, 13) is parallel to y = 3x + 7

Find k.

  1. Parallel means the same gradient. So the gradient of PQ is 3:
    (13 − k) ÷ (4 − 1) = 3 (13 − k) ÷ 3 = 3
  2. Times both sides by 3, then solve:
    13 − k = 9 !k = 4
Your turn: the line through (2, k) and (5, 11) is parallel to y = 2x + 1. Find k.

Perpendicular with k

The line through A(2, 1) and B(6, k) is perpendicular to 2x + y = 5

Find k.

  1. Gradient of 2x + y = 5. Get y on its own:
    y = −2x + 5
  2. AB is perpendicular, so flip −2 and change the sign:
    !Gradient of AB = 1/2
  3. Write the gradient of AB with k in it, and solve:
    (k − 1) ÷ (6 − 2) = 1/2 k − 1 = 2 !k = 3

(k − 1) ÷ 4 = 1/2, so k − 1 is half of 4.

Where a line crosses the axes

3x + 4y = 36 crosses the x-axis at R and the y-axis at S

Find R and S, and the distance RS.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. On the x-axis, y is 0. Put y = 0 in:
    3x = 36 x = 12 !R = (12, 0)
  2. On the y-axis, x is 0. Put x = 0 in:
    4y = 36 y = 9 !S = (0, 9)
  3. R, S and the origin make a right-angled triangle. Use Pythagoras:
    RS = √(12² + 9²) = √225 != 15

Where two lines meet

y = 2x − 3 meets x + y = 9

Find the point where the lines meet.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. Where they meet, the point is on both lines. So swap the y in the second line for 2x − 3:
    x + (2x − 3) = 9 3x − 3 = 9 3x = 12 !x = 4
  2. Put x = 4 back into either line:
    y = 2 × 4 − 3 != 5
  3. They meet at (4, 5). Check in the other line: 4 + 5 = 9 ✓

Finding B from the bisector

A is (1, 3). The perpendicular bisector of AB is y = −x + 8

Find B.

Hint: AB is perpendicular to the bisector, and M is where they cross.

  1. The bisector has gradient −1. AB is perpendicular, so its gradient is 1. Line AB through A(1, 3):
    y − 3 = 1(x − 1) !y = x + 2
  2. M is where AB meets the bisector:
    x + 2 = −x + 8 2x = 6 x = 3, y = 5 !M = (3, 5)
  3. M is halfway, so B is the same jump again. A to M is 2 along, 2 up:
    !B = (3 + 2, 5 + 2) = (5, 7)

A bisector with k

A is (k, 1) and B is (5, 7). The perpendicular bisector of AB has gradient −2/3

Find k.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. Work backwards. The bisector is −2/3, so AB is perpendicular to it. Flip and change the sign:
    !Gradient of AB = 3/2
  2. Write the gradient of AB with k in it. Up is 7 − 1 = 6, along is 5 − k:
    6 ÷ (5 − k) = 3/2 5 − k = 4 !k = 1
  3. So A is (1, 1) and M is (3, 4).

If 6 ÷ (something) = 1.5, then the something is 6 ÷ 1.5 = 4. So 5 − k = 4.

Part 5

A full exam question

Four parts, one part per slide. Try each on paper before you reveal it.

(a) Midpoint and gradient · (b) Show the bisector · (c) Where it crosses the axes · (d) Where it meets another line

Exam-style question · 10 marks

The points P(−2, 7) and Q(4, −1)

(a) Find the midpoint M of PQ and the gradient of PQ.[2]

(b) Show that the perpendicular bisector of PQ is 3x − 4y + 9 = 0.[3]

(c) The bisector crosses the x-axis at R and the y-axis at S. Find the exact length RS.[3]

(d) The bisector meets the line x + y = 11 at T. Find T.[2]

Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

Part (a) · 2 marks

Midpoint and gradient

Try it first. Then show the steps.

  1. Add and halve:
    !M = ((−2 + 4) ÷ 2, (7 + (−1)) ÷ 2) = (1, 3)
  2. Up −8, along 6:
    Gradient of PQ = −8 ÷ 6 != −4/3

Part (b) · 3 marks

Show the bisector is 3x − 4y + 9 = 0

"Show that" means every step must be written down.

  1. Flip −4/3 and change the sign:
    !Bisector gradient = 3/4
  2. Line through M(1, 3):
    y − 3 = 3/4 (x − 1)
  3. Times both sides by 4. Then move everything to one side:
    4y − 12 = 3x − 3 0 = 3x − 4y + 9 !3x − 4y + 9 = 0

Part (c) · 3 marks

R, S and the exact length RS

Try it first. Then show the steps.

  1. R is on the x-axis, so y = 0:
    3x + 9 = 0 !R = (−3, 0)
  2. S is on the y-axis, so x = 0:
    −4y + 9 = 0 !S = (0, 9/4)
  3. Pythagoras. 3² = 9, and (9/4)² = 81/16:
    RS = √(9 + 81/16) = √(225/16) != 15/4

9 is 144/16, so 9 + 81/16 = 225/16. Then √225 = 15 and √16 = 4.

Part (d) · 2 marks

Where the bisector meets x + y = 11

Try it first. Then show the steps.

  1. From x + y = 11, y = 11 − x. Swap that into the bisector:
    3x − 4(11 − x) + 9 = 0 3x − 44 + 4x + 9 = 0 7x = 35 !x = 5
  2. Then y = 11 − 5 = 6. So T = (5, 6). Check: 15 − 24 + 9 = 0 ✓

Common mistakes

Six slips that cost marks

Flipping but not changing the sign

✗ Perpendicular to 2 is 1/2

✓ It's −1/2. Check: 2 × (−1/2) = −1

✗ 2x + 3y = 6 has gradient 2

✓ Get y on its own: y = −2/3 x + 2, so −2/3

Bisector through the wrong point

✗ A line through A or B

✓ The bisector goes through the midpoint M

Bisector with AB's gradient

✗ Bisector of AB has gradient 4/3

✓ It's perpendicular to AB: −3/4

A parallel line with the same c

✗ Parallel to y = 3x − 2 is y = 3x − 2

✓ Same m, new c from the point: y = 3x + 2

✓ y − (−1) is y + 1

A new word

Tangent

IN PLAIN WORDS
A straight line that touches a circle at one point and does not go inside it. The point where it touches is the point of contact. We call it P.
EXAMPLE
The line 3x + 4y = 39 touches our circle at P(5, 6), and nowhere else.
WATCH OUT
The dashed line goes in at one point and out at another. It cuts the circle, so it is not a tangent.

Watch it happen

Why the tangent meets the radius at 90°

A line through P usually cuts the circle a second time. Watch that second point slide round towards P.

The line turns as the point moves. When the two points meet, the line only touches. That is the tangent.

The circle looks the same on both sides of the radius CP, so the tangent can't lean either way.
The tangent is at 90° to the radius.

The rule

From the radius gradient to the tangent gradient

tangent gradient = −1 ÷ radius gradient
Radius gradientUp ÷ along, from C to P. For P(5, 6) it is 4 ÷ 3 = 4/3.
1 ÷ it: flip it4/3 turned upside down is 3/4.
The minus: change the sign3/4 becomes −3/4. This is "flip and change the sign" from lesson 2.
CheckThe two gradients multiply to −1: 4/3 × (−3/4) = −1.

It needs a gradient to flip. If the radius is flat or straight up, Part 3 shows what to do.

Worked example · the running circle

(x − 2)² + (y − 2)² = 25, and P(5, 6)

Find the equation of the tangent at P, in the form ax + by = c.

  1. The centre is C(2, 2). From C to P is 3 along and 4 up. P is on the circle, because
    3² + 4² = 25 ✓
  2. Radius gradient, then flip and change the sign:
    Radius gradient = 4/3 !Tangent gradient = −3/4
  3. The line through P(5, 6). Multiply by 4 to clear the fraction:
    y − 6 = −3/4 (x − 5) 4y − 24 = −3x + 15 !3x + 4y = 39
  4. Check with P: 3 × 5 + 4 × 6 = 15 + 24 = 39 ✓

The slip that costs the most marks

Which point goes into the line?

Same circle, same P(5, 6). The tangent gradient is −3/4.

✗ Through the centre C(2, 2)

y − 2 = −3/4 (x − 2) 4y − 8 = −3x + 6 3x + 4y = 14

Check P: 15 + 24 = 39, not 14. This line misses P. It runs straight through the middle of the circle.

✓ Through the point of contact P(5, 6)

y − 6 = −3/4 (x − 5) 4y − 24 = −3x + 15 !3x + 4y = 39

The tangent touches the circle at P, so P must be on it.

Use C to find the gradient. Use P to write the line.

A new word

Normal

IN PLAIN WORDS
The line through P at 90° to the tangent. It runs along the radius, so it always goes through the centre.
EXAMPLE
At P(5, 6) the normal has the radius gradient, 4/3: y − 6 = 4/3 (x − 5), which tidies to 4x − 3y = 2.
WATCH OUT
Don't flip for the normal. It keeps the radius gradient. Check: C(2, 2) fits, because 8 − 6 = 2.

No working. Tap its point of contact. Your tap snaps to the nearest grid point.

Worked example · your go first

x² + y² + 4x − 2y − 20 = 0, and P(1, 5)

Find the tangent to the circle at P, in the form ax + by = c.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. The centre is hidden. Complete the square, as in lesson 7:
    x² + 4x = (x + 2)² − 4 y² − 2y = (y − 1)² − 1 !(x + 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25
    So C is (−2, 1) and the radius is 5.
  2. From C(−2, 1) to P(1, 5) is 3 along, 4 up. 3² + 4² = 25 ✓, so P is on the circle. Radius gradient 4/3, so the tangent gradient is −3/4.
  3. The line through P:
    y − 5 = −3/4 (x − 1) 4y − 20 = −3x + 3 !3x + 4y = 23

Where two tangents meet

The tangents at P(5, 6) and R(6, −1) cross at T. Find T.

1. The tangent at P

We found it already: 3x + 4y = 39.

2. The tangent at R

C to R is 4 along, 3 down: gradient −3/4. Flip and change the sign: 4/3. So y + 1 = 4/3 (x − 6), which is 4x − 3y = 27.

3. Solve them together

Times 3 and times 4 make the y's match: 9x + 12y = 117 and 16x − 12y = 108. Add: 25x = 225, so x = 9. Then 4y = 39 − 27, so y = 3. T is (9, 3).

Predict · then watch

From T(9, 3), two tangents

One touches the circle at P(5, 6). The other touches at R(6, −1). Each tangent is at 90° to its radius.

Which is longer, TP or TR?

The tangent length

You don't need P to find TP

Often a question gives you T but not the point of contact. Use the right angle at P instead.

Triangle CPT has a right angle at P. So CT is its longest side, and Pythagoras gives CT² = TP² + r².

CT² = 7² + 1² = 50 TP² = CT² − r² = 50 − 25 = 25 !TP = 5

Tangent length² = CT² − r². Take away, because CT is the longest side.

Part 5

A full exam question

Four parts, one part per slide. Try each on paper before you reveal it.

(a) Centre and radius · (b) The tangent · (c) A triangle's area · (d) A tangent length

Exam-style question · 10 marks

The circle x² + y² − 8x − 2y − 3 = 0

The point P(6, 5) lies on the circle.

(a) Find the centre of the circle, and its exact radius.[3]

(b) Find the equation of the tangent at P, in the form ax + by = c.[3]

(c) The tangent meets the x-axis at A and the y-axis at B. Find the area of triangle OAB, where O is the origin.[2]

(d) Find the exact length of AP.[2]

Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

Part (a) · 3 marks

Centre and exact radius

Try it first. Then show the steps.

  1. Complete the square on the x's and on the y's:
    x² − 8x = (x − 4)² − 16 y² − 2y = (y − 1)² − 1
  2. Put them back, and move the numbers across:
    (x − 4)² + (y − 1)² = 3 + 16 + 1 !(x − 4)² + (y − 1)² = 20
  3. Centre (4, 1). Radius √20 = 2√5. "Exact" means leave the root in.

Part (b) · 3 marks

The tangent at P(6, 5)

Centre from (a), then the four steps. Try it first.

  1. From C(4, 1) to P(6, 5) is 2 along, 4 up. 2² + 4² = 20 ✓
  2. Radius gradient 4 ÷ 2 = 2. Flip and change the sign: the tangent gradient is −1/2.
  3. The line through P. Multiply by 2:
    y − 5 = −1/2 (x − 6) 2y − 10 = −x + 6 !x + 2y = 16

Part (c) · 2 marks

The area of triangle OAB

Where does a line cross each axis? Try it first.

  1. On the x-axis, y = 0: x = 16, so A is (16, 0). On the y-axis, x = 0: 2y = 16, so B is (0, 8).
  2. OAB has a right angle at O. Half the base times the height:
    Area = ½ × 16 × 8 != 64

Part (d) · 2 marks

The exact length of AP

A is on the tangent at P. So AP is a tangent length. Try it first.

  1. From C(4, 1) to A(16, 0) is 12 along, 1 down:
    CA² = 12² + 1² = 145
  2. The right angle is at P, so take away r² = 20:
    AP² = 145 − 20 = 125 !AP = √125 = 5√5
  3. Check the long way. A to P is 10 left, 5 up: 10² + 5² = 125 ✓

Common mistakes

Six slips that cost marks

Using the radius gradient for the tangent

✗ Radius 4/3, so the tangent is 4/3

✓ Flip and change the sign: −3/4

Writing the line through the centre

✗ y − 2 = −3/4 (x − 2)

✓ Through P: y − 6 = −3/4 (x − 5)

Reading the centre with the wrong sign

✗ x² + 4x … so the centre's x is 4

✓ (x + 2)², so the centre's x is −2

Flipping a flat radius

✗ Radius gradient 0, so the tangent is −1 ÷ 0

✓ Flat radius, so the tangent is straight up: x = 7

Flipping for the normal

✗ The normal at P has gradient −3/4

✓ The normal runs along the radius: 4/3

Adding in the tangent length

✗ TP² = CT² + r² = 75

✓ CT is the longest side: TP² = 50 − 25 = 25

The facts

Tangent gradientFlip the radius gradient and change its sign. They multiply to −1
Flat or straight upFlat radius: tangent x = P's x. Straight-up radius: tangent y = P's y
NormalThrough P with the radius gradient. It goes through C too
Tangent lengthTP² = CT² − r²

The tangent at P

  1. Centre C: read it off, or complete the square first. Check P is on the circle.
  2. Radius gradient: up ÷ along from C to P.
  3. Tangent gradient: flip and change the sign.
  4. Line through P (not C): y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), then the form asked for. Check P fits.

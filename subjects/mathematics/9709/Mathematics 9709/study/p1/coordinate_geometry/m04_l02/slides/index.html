Multiplying out our circle

Same circle, two equations

Multiply out each bracket:
(x − 2)² = x² − 4x + 4
(y − 1)² = y² − 2y + 1

So x² − 4x + 4 + y² − 2y + 1 = 25. Collect the plain numbers: 4 + 1 − 25 = −20.

x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0
Every point on the circle fits both equations. Only the look has changed.

A new word

General form

IN PLAIN WORDS
A circle's equation with the brackets multiplied out and everything on one side: x² + y² + Ax + By + C = 0. A, B and C are just numbers.
EXAMPLE
x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0. Here A = −4, B = −2, C = −20.
WATCH OUT
A, B and C are not the centre or the radius. They hide them. Some questions call these numbers a, b and c. Read carefully: in lesson 6, (a, b) was the centre.

Worked example · standard form to general form

(x + 1)² + (y − 4)² = 10

Write the equation in the form x² + y² + Ax + By + C = 0.

  1. Multiply out each bracket:
    (x + 1)² = x² + 2x + 1 (y − 4)² = y² − 8y + 16
  2. Put them back and collect the plain numbers:
    x² + 2x + 1 + y² − 8y + 16 = 10 !x² + y² + 2x − 8y + 7 = 0
    1 + 16 − 10 = 7.
  3. The centre (−1, 4) is now hidden in the +2x and the −8y. Notice: each is −2 times the centre's number.

Completing the square, from P1 Quadratics

Halve, square, take away

x² − 4x = (x − 2)² − 4
Halve the x numberHalf of −4 is −2. It goes in the bracket.
Take its square away(x − 2)² = x² − 4x + 4 has an extra 4. So take 4 away.
The same for yy² − 2y = (y − 1)² − 1. Half of −2 is −1, and (−1)² = 1.
Why bother?The bracket shows the centre straight away. x − 2 means the centre's x is 2.

Worked example · general form to standard form

x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0

Find the centre and radius of the circle.

  1. Group the x terms and the y terms. Move the plain number across:
    (x² − 4x) + (y² − 2y) = 20
  2. Complete the square on each:
    (x − 2)² − 4 + (y − 1)² − 1 = 20
  3. Move the −4 and −1 across. They become +4 and +1:
    !(x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25
    Centre (2, 1), radius √25 = 5. Our circle again.

Worked example · your go first

x² + y² + 8x + 2y − 3 = 0

Find the centre and the exact radius.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. Group, and move the 3 across:
    (x² + 8x) + (y² + 2y) = 3
  2. Half of 8 is 4. Half of 2 is 1:
    (x + 4)² − 16 + (y + 1)² − 1 = 3
  3. Move the 16 and 1 across:
    (x + 4)² + (y + 1)² = 20
    Centre (−4, −1), radius √20 = 2√5.

A shortcut, once you trust the method

Halve and change the sign. Then r² = (half)² + (half)² − C

This is completing the square in one line. Use it to check your answer. Say each row first.

A range for k

x² + y² + 2x − 6y + k = 0 is a circle. What can k be?

Complete the square, but keep k as a letter. Try it first.

  1. Group, and move k across:
    (x² + 2x) + (y² − 6y) = −k (x + 1)² − 1 + (y − 3)² − 9 = −k
  2. Move the 1 and 9 across:
    (x + 1)² + (y − 3)² = 10 − k
  3. For a real circle, r² must be more than 0:
    10 − k > 0 !k < 10

The board shows k = 1, 6 and 9. As k grows towards 10, the circle shrinks onto its centre (−1, 3).

Touching an axis

x² + y² + 6x − 2y + k = 0 touches the x-axis. Find k.

Completing the square gives:

(x + 3)² + (y − 1)² = 10 − k

The centre is (−3, 1). "Touches the x-axis" hands you the radius (lesson 6).

Highest, lowest, left and right

x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0: the four extreme points

1. Centre and radius first

Complete the square: centre (2, 1), radius 5. You can't find the top of a circle without these.

2. Up and down by r

Keep x the same. Highest: (2, 1 + 5) = (2, 6). Lowest: (2, 1 − 5) = (2, −4).

3. Left and right by r

Keep y the same. Leftmost: (2 − 5, 1) = (−3, 1). Rightmost: (2 + 5, 1) = (7, 1).

Tap it on the grid

Your tap snaps to the nearest grid point. When you get it, the circle appears.

Worked example · crossing the axes

x² + y² − 2x + 4y − 8 = 0

Find where the circle crosses the x-axis and the y-axis.

On the x-axis every point has y = 0. Try it first.

  1. x-axis: put y = 0. You get a quadratic in x:
    x² − 2x − 8 = 0 (x − 4)(x + 2) = 0
    So (4, 0) and (−2, 0).
  2. y-axis: put x = 0. This one doesn't factorise, so complete the square:
    y² + 4y − 8 = 0 (y + 2)² = 12 !y = −2 ± 2√3
    So (0, −2 + 2√3) and (0, −2 − 2√3).

The chord triangle

Half a chord, the gap, and the radius

The same circle has centre (1, −2) and r² = 1 + 4 + 8 = 13. It cuts a chord from (−2, 0) to (4, 0) on the x-axis. Its length is 6.

Drop a line from the centre straight to the chord. It cuts the chord in half, at 90°. The gap is 2, the centre's distance below the x-axis.

Half the chord, the gap and the radius make a right-angled triangle:

3² + 2² = 13 = r² ✓

Know any two of the three, and Pythagoras gives the third.

Worked example · any line

Where y = −x + 8 meets our circle

The line y = −x + 8 meets x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0 at P and Q. Find P and Q, the length PQ, and the midpoint of PQ.

Same as lesson 5: substitute, then solve. Try it first.

  1. Put −x + 8 in place of y, and multiply out:
    x² + (8 − x)² − 4x − 2(8 − x) − 20 = 0 2x² − 18x + 28 = 0 x² − 9x + 14 = 0
  2. Factorise: (x − 2)(x − 7) = 0, so x = 2 or 7. Then y = −x + 8:
    !P(2, 6) and Q(7, 1)
  3. Length and midpoint, as in lesson 1:
    PQ = √(5² + 5²) = 5√2 !Midpoint = (9/2, 7/2)

Part 5

Building a circle from clues

A point on the circle must fit its equation. Each point gives you one equation.

One unknown · Two unknowns · Centre on a line

Worked example · one unknown

x² + y² + kx − 4y − 5 = 0 passes through (1, −2)

Find k. Then find the centre and radius.

The point is on the circle, so it fits the equation. Try it first.

  1. Put x = 1 and y = −2 into the equation:
    1² + (−2)² + k(1) − 4(−2) − 5 = 0 1 + 4 + k + 8 − 5 = 0 k + 8 = 0 !k = −8
  2. So the circle is x² + y² − 8x − 4y − 5 = 0. The shortcut gives centre (4, 2) and r² = 16 + 4 + 5 = 25, radius 5.

Two unknowns

x² + y² + Ax + By − 15 = 0 passes through P(2, 7) and Q(3, 0)

Two unknowns need two equations: one from each point. Try it first.

  1. Q(3, 0): 9 + 0 + 3A + 0 − 15 = 0
    3A = 6 !A = 2
  2. P(2, 7): 4 + 49 + 2A + 7B − 15 = 0
    2A + 7B = −38 4 + 7B = −38 !B = −6
  3. x² + y² + 2x − 6y − 15 = 0: centre (−1, 3), r² = 1 + 9 + 15 = 25, radius 5.

A point on an axis is kind: a 0 wipes out one letter.

    A full exam question

    Four parts, one part per slide. Try each on paper before you reveal it.

    (a) Centre and radius · (b) The y-axis · (c) The chord · (d) A line

    Exam-style question · 11 marks

    The circle x² + y² − 6x + 2y − 15 = 0

    The circle C has equation x² + y² − 6x + 2y − 15 = 0.

    (a) Find the coordinates of the centre of C and the radius of C.[3]

    (b) Find the coordinates of the points where C meets the y-axis.[2]

    (c) Find the length of the chord in part (b), and the distance from the centre of C to this chord.[2]

    (d) The line x + y = 7 meets C at P and Q. Find the coordinates of P, Q and the midpoint of PQ.[4]

    Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

    Part (a) · 3 marks

    Centre and radius

    Complete the square on x, then on y. Try it first.

    1. Group, and move the 15 across:
      (x² − 6x) + (y² + 2y) = 15
    2. Half of −6 is −3. Half of 2 is 1:
      (x − 3)² − 9 + (y + 1)² − 1 = 15 (x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 25
    3. Centre (3, −1), radius √25 = 5.

    Part (b) · 2 marks

    Where C meets the y-axis

    Which letter is 0 on the y-axis? Try it first.

    1. Put x = 0 into the general form:
      y² + 2y − 15 = 0 (y + 5)(y − 3) = 0
    2. y = 3 or y = −5:
      !(0, 3) and (0, −5)

    Part (c) · 2 marks

    The chord and the gap

    Draw the chord triangle. Try it first.

    1. The chord runs from (0, 3) to (0, −5):
      !Length = 3 − (−5) = 8
    2. The chord is on the y-axis. The centre (3, −1) is 3 across from it:
      !Distance = 3
      Check with the chord triangle: 4² + 3² = 25 = r² ✓

    Part (d) · 4 marks

    The line x + y = 7

    Get y on its own, then substitute. Try it first.

    1. y = 7 − x. Put it into the equation of C:
      x² + (7 − x)² − 6x + 2(7 − x) − 15 = 0 2x² − 22x + 48 = 0 x² − 11x + 24 = 0
    2. (x − 3)(x − 8) = 0, so x = 3 or 8. Then y = 7 − x:
      !P(3, 4) and Q(8, −1)
    3. Add and halve:
      !Midpoint = (11/2, 3/2)

    Common mistakes · Summary

    Common mistakes

    Six slips that cost marks

    Reading the x number as the centre

    ✗ x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0 has centre (−4, −2)

    ✓ Halve and change the sign: (2, 1)

    Taking C as r²

    ✗ r² = 20

    ✓ Complete the square: r² = 4 + 1 + 20 = 25

    Forgetting to take the square away

    ✗ x² − 4x = (x − 2)²

    ✓ x² − 4x = (x − 2)² − 4

    A sign slip moving numbers across

    ✗ r² = 4 + 1 − 20 = −15

    ✓ −20 moves across as + 20: r² = 25

    Not dividing first

    ✗ 2x² + 2y² − 8x + 4 = 0 has centre (4, 0)

    ✓ Divide by 2 first: x² + y² − 4x + 2 = 0, centre (2, 0)

    The inequality the wrong way round

    ✗ 10 − k > 0, so k > 10

    ✓ 10 − k > 0 means 10 > k: k < 10

    Summary

    Everything on one page

    The two forms

    Standard form(x − a)² + (y − b)² = r². Centre and radius on show
    General formx² + y² + Ax + By + C = 0. Multiply out the brackets to get it
    Back againComplete the square on x and y. Centre: halve A and B, change the signs
    Is it a circle?Same number in front of x² and y², no xy, and r² > 0

    Using it

    1. Extreme points: from the centre, go r up, down, left or right.
    2. Axis crossings: x-axis, put y = 0. y-axis, put x = 0. Solve the quadratic.
    3. Any line: substitute for y, solve, then use distance and midpoint.
    4. Chord triangle: (half chord)² + gap² = r².
    5. Unknown numbers: each point on the circle gives one equation.

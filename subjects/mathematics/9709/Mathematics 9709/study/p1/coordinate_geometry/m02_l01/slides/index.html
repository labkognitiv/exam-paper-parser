Part 1

Reading a triangle

Before any working: which corner is the question about, and what does the triangle's name promise?

Angle ABC · Three kinds of triangle

A new word

Angle ABC

IN PLAIN WORDS
The corners of a triangle are called vertices (one is a vertex). Angle ABC is the corner at the middle letter, B. It sits between the two sides that meet there: BA and BC.
EXAMPLE
A(0, 1), B(4, 3), C(3, 5). "Angle ABC = 90°" means side AB and side BC are at a right angle.
WATCH OUT
Angle ACB is the corner at C, not at B. Always read the middle letter.

Part 2

Right angles

Two sides at 90° have gradients that multiply to −1. You met this in lesson 2.

Show a right angle · Drag C · Which corner?

Worked example · a right angle

A(0, 1), B(4, 3) and C(3, 5)

Show that triangle ABC is right-angled at B.

  1. The right angle is at B. The two sides that meet at B are AB and BC. So we need those two gradients.
  2. A to B is 4 along, 2 up:
    Gradient AB = 2 ÷ 4 = 1/2
  3. B to C is 1 left, 2 up:
    Gradient BC = 2 ÷ (−1) = −2
  4. Multiply the two gradients:
    !1/2 × (−2) = −1
    So AB is perpendicular to BC. Angle ABC = 90° ✓

Part 3

Area

Every triangle is half of a box. The trick is finding the height.

Half the box · ½ × base × height · Your turn · √a × √b · Quick check

Half of the box

A triangle is half of the box around it

On the left, K(1, 1), L(7, 1), N(7, 5). KL is flat: 6 along. LN goes straight up: 4 up. A flat side and a straight-up side always meet at a right angle.

The triangle is exactly half of the 6 by 4 box: ½ × 6 × 4 = 12. You don't need gradients here. A straight-up side has no gradient, because along is 0.

On the right, the top corner is not above an end. The dashed height cuts it into two pieces, and each piece is half of its own box. So it is still half.
Area = ½ × base × height.

The formula

Area of a triangle

Area = ½ × base × height
½The triangle is half of the box around it.
BaseAny side you choose. Pick a flat or straight-up side if there is one.
HeightFrom the opposite corner to the base, meeting it at 90°. Not a slanted side.
Reading the heightFlat base: take away the y's. Straight-up base: take away the x's.

In a right-angled triangle, the two sides at the right angle are a base and its height.

Slanted sides

√a × √b = √(ab)

Back to ABC, right-angled at B. The two sides at B are slanted, so their lengths are square roots. Multiply square roots by multiplying the numbers inside:

√20 × √5 = √100 = 10 √8 × √2 = √16 = 4 √7 × √7 = 7

Now the area. AB and BC are a base and its height:

AB = √(4² + 2²) = √20 BC = √(1² + 2²) = √5 Area = ½ × √20 × √5 = ½ × 10 != 5

Part 4

Equal sides

Compare squared lengths. Then use the apex to find the height.

Show it is isosceles · The altitude · Area of an isosceles triangle · Equilateral

Worked example · equal sides

G(−2, 1), H(4, −1) and J(2, 5)

Show that triangle GHJ is isosceles.

  1. You don't know which two sides are equal. So find all three. Squares are enough: no square roots needed.
    GH² = 6² + 2² = 40 HJ² = 2² + 6² = 40 GJ² = 4² + 4² = 32
  2. GH² = HJ², so GH = HJ. Equal squares mean equal lengths. Two sides are equal: GHJ is isosceles ✓
  3. The equal sides meet at H, so H is the apex. GJ is the base.

A new word

Altitude

IN PLAIN WORDS
The line from a corner that meets the opposite side at a right angle. The point where it lands is the foot. Its length is the height.
EXAMPLE
Isosceles triangle DEF has apex F(3, 4) and base D(1, 0) to E(7, 2). The altitude from F lands at M(4, 1), the midpoint of DE.
WATCH OUT
It lands on the midpoint only when it comes from the apex of an isosceles triangle. In any other triangle you have to find the foot.

Area of an isosceles triangle

Triangle DEF: the height lands in the middle

1. The foot is the midpoint

F is the apex, so the altitude lands halfway along DE: M = ((1 + 7) ÷ 2, (0 + 2) ÷ 2) = (4, 1).

2. The height FM

F(3, 4) to M(4, 1) is 1 along, 3 down. So FM = √(1² + 3²) = √10.

3. Half of base times height

DE = √(6² + 2²) = √40. Area = ½ × √40 × √10 = ½ × √400 = ½ × 20 = 10.

Worked example · your go first

K(−1, 1) and L(3, 1)

KLN is equilateral, with N above KL. Find N exactly, and the area.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. KL = 3 − (−1) = 4, so every side is 4. N is the apex, so it sits above the midpoint M(1, 1).
  2. Half the base is 2. Use Pythagoras in the half triangle KMN:
    h² + 2² = 4² h² = 12 h = √12 = 2√3
  3. N is 2√3 above M:
    !N = (1, 1 + 2√3)
  4. Half of base times height:
    Area = ½ × 4 × 2√3 != 4√3

Part 5

Letters in the corners

One fact about the triangle gives one equation for the letter.

A right angle with k · Equal sides with p · Two answers · Your turn

A right angle with a letter

P(1, 1), Q(3, 5) and R(k, 4)

Angle PQR = 90°. Find k.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps.

  1. Angle PQR is at Q, so PQ is perpendicular to QR. P to Q is 2 along, 4 up: gradient PQ = 2.
  2. Flip and change the sign: gradient QR = −1/2.
  3. Write the gradient of QR with k in it. Up is 4 − 5 = −1. Along is k − 3:
    −1 ÷ (k − 3) = −1/2 k − 3 = 2 !k = 5
  4. Check: R(5, 4). Q to R is 2 along, 1 down, so the gradient is −1/2 ✓

Equal sides with a letter

A(−3, 0), B(6, 1), C(p, 5). AC = BC. Find p.

Equal lengths, so equal squares. Write AC² and BC² with p in them. The trap is the bracket.

✗ Squaring a bracket the quick way

(p + 3)² + 5² = (p − 6)² + 4² p² + 9 + 25 = p² + 36 + 16 34 = 52

The p's vanish and you get nonsense. (p + 3)² is not p² + 9. You lost the middle term.

✓ Multiply the bracket out in full

(p + 3)² + 25 = (p − 6)² + 16 p² + 6p + 34 = p² − 12p + 52 18p = 18 !p = 1

(p + 3)² = p² + 6p + 9. The p² on each side cancel, and a simple equation is left.

Check: C(1, 5). AC² = 4² + 5² = 41 and BC² = 5² + 4² = 41 ✓

Two answers

A(−1, 2), B(4, 7) and C(k, k − 1)

C is somewhere on the dashed line y = x − 1. As k changes, C slides along it. We want angle ACB = 90°.

How many places on the line give a right angle at C?

The algebra · your go first

A(−1, 2), B(4, 7), C(k, k − 1). Angle ACB = 90°.

Try it first. The right angle is at C, the middle letter.

  1. Gradients of the two sides that meet at C. Up ÷ along, from A and from B:
    Gradient AC = (k − 3) ÷ (k + 1) Gradient BC = (k − 8) ÷ (k − 4)
  2. They multiply to −1. Clear the fractions: top × top = −(bottom × bottom).
    (k − 3)(k − 8) = −(k + 1)(k − 4) k² − 11k + 24 = −k² + 3k + 4 2k² − 14k + 20 = 0 k² − 7k + 10 = 0
  3. Factorise: (k − 2)(k − 5) = 0, so k = 2 or k = 5.
  4. Put each k into C(k, k − 1):
    !C = (2, 1) or (5, 4)

Part 6

The foot of the altitude

When the base is slanted, the height is hiding. Two lines find it.

The method · A worked example · Which way to the height?

The method

Find the foot in four steps

The base line

Find the equation of the line through the base.

The altitude

Through the third corner. Flip and change the sign of the base gradient.

Where they meet

Solve the two equations together. That point is the foot.

Height and area

Corner to foot is the height. Then ½ × base × height.

Worked example · your go first

A(−2, 3), B(7, 0) and C(2, 5)

Find the foot D of the altitude from C to AB. Then find the area of triangle ABC.

Try it first. Follow the four steps.

  1. Line AB. Gradient = −3 ÷ 9 = −1/3, through B(7, 0). Multiply y = −1/3 (x − 7) by 3:
    3y = −x + 7 !x + 3y = 7
  2. The altitude through C(2, 5) has gradient 3:
    y − 5 = 3(x − 2) !y = 3x − 1
  3. Put y = 3x − 1 into x + 3y = 7: x + 9x − 3 = 7, so x = 1 and y = 2. The foot is D(1, 2).
  4. The height, the base, then the area:
    CD = √(1² + 3²) = √10 AB = √(9² + 3²) = 3√10 !Area = ½ × 3√10 × √10 = 15

Part 7

A full exam question

Four parts, one part per slide. Try each on paper before you reveal it.

(a) Show k = −5 · (b) The area · (c) The line AC · (d) The foot D and BD

Exam-style question · 12 marks

The triangle ABC

A is (5, 2), B is (1, 5) and C is (k, −3). Angle ABC = 90°.

(a) Find the gradient of AB, and show that k = −5.[3]

(b) Find the area of triangle ABC.[3]

(c) Find the equation of AC, in the form ax + by = c.[2]

(d) D is the foot of the perpendicular from B to AC. Find D, and the exact length of BD.[4]

Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

Part (a) · 3 marks

The gradient of AB, and k

Try it first. Then show the steps.

  1. A to B is 4 left, 3 up:
    Gradient AB = 3 ÷ (−4) != −3/4
  2. Angle ABC = 90°, so BC is perpendicular to AB. Flip and change the sign: gradient BC = 4/3.
  3. From B(1, 5) to C(k, −3), up is −3 − 5 = −8 and along is k − 1:
    −8 ÷ (k − 1) = 4/3 −24 = 4(k − 1) k − 1 = −6 !k = −5

Part (b) · 3 marks

The area

The right angle is at B. Which two sides are the base and the height?

  1. AB and BC meet at 90°, so they are a base and its height. A to B is 4 along, 3 up:
    AB = √(4² + 3²) = √25 = 5
  2. B to C is 6 left, 8 down:
    BC = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10
  3. Half of one times the other:
    Area = ½ × 5 × 10 != 25

Part (c) · 2 marks

The equation of AC

Try it first. You need a gradient and a point.

  1. C(−5, −3) to A(5, 2) is 10 along, 5 up:
    Gradient AC = 5 ÷ 10 = 1/2
  2. Through A(5, 2). Double everything to clear the half:
    y − 2 = 1/2 (x − 5) 2y − 4 = x − 5 !x − 2y = 1

Part (d) · 4 marks

The foot D, and BD

Try it first. Base line, altitude, meet.

  1. BD is perpendicular to AC, so its gradient is −2. Through B(1, 5):
    y − 5 = −2(x − 1) !y = −2x + 7
  2. Put it into x − 2y = 1:
    x − 2(−2x + 7) = 1 5x − 14 = 1 x = 3, so y = 1 !D = (3, 1)
  3. B to D is 2 along, 4 down:
    !BD = √(2² + 4²) = √20 = 2√5

Check with (b): AC = √125 = 5√5, and ½ × 5√5 × 2√5 = 25 ✓

Common mistakes · Summary

Common mistakes

Six slips that cost marks

Testing the wrong corner

✗ Right angle at B: check AB and AC

✓ The middle letter: the sides that meet at B, AB and BC

Flipping without the sign

✗ AB has gradient 2, so BC has gradient 1/2

✓ Flip and change the sign: −1/2

Forgetting the half

✗ Area = √20 × √5 = 10

✓ Area = ½ × √20 × √5 = 5

Adding inside the square roots

✗ √20 × √5 = √25

✓ √20 × √5 = √(20 × 5) = √100 = 10

A slanted side as the height

✗ Height = the side from the top corner

✓ The height meets the base at 90°. Find the foot

Squaring a bracket the quick way

✗ (p + 3)² = p² + 9

✓ (p + 3)² = p² + 6p + 9

Summary

Everything on one page

What to show

Right angle at BGradients of AB and BC multiply to −1. Flat meets straight-up: always 90°
IsoscelesTwo squared lengths are equal. Find all three if no pair is named
EquilateralThe apex is above the midpoint of the base. Pythagoras gives the height
Area½ × base × height. Right-angled: ½ × the two sides at 90°. √a × √b = √(ab)

Finding the height, or a letter

  1. Flat or straight-up base: read the height off the grid. Isosceles apex: the foot is the midpoint of the base.
  2. Slanted base: base line, altitude through the corner, solve together. That point is the foot.
  3. A letter: a right angle gives gradients that multiply to −1. Equal sides give equal squares. A quadratic gives two answers.

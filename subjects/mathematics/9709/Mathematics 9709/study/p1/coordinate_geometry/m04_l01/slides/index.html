Part 1

Where the equation comes from

A circle is every point at the same distance from one centre.

Centre and radius · The same distance every time · Distance becomes an equation · The formula · Drag a point

Two new words

Centre and radius

IN PLAIN WORDS
Pin one end of a string to the grid and pull it tight. The pin is the centre. The string's length is the radius, written r. Swing the other end all the way round: that path is the circle.
EXAMPLE
Our circle for this lesson: centre (2, 1), radius 5. Every point on it is exactly 5 from (2, 1).
WATCH OUT
A diameter goes right across, through the centre. It is two radii long: here, 10.

The same distance every time

Every point on the circle is 5 from the centre

Walk from the centre (2, 1) to the point (5, 5): 3 along, 4 up.

Distance is Pythagoras on the two: 3² + 4² = 25, so the distance is √25 = 5.

Try any other point on the circle. The sum always comes to 25.
On the circle means: distance from the centre = 5.

Distance becomes an equation

Any point (x, y) on the circle

Now let the point be any point on the circle. We don't know its numbers, so call it (x, y).

From the centre (2, 1): along = x − 2 and up = y − 1.

Pythagoras says along² + up² = distance². The distance is 5, so:

!(x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25

This is the equation of the circle. A point is on the circle exactly when its numbers make this true.

Check (5, 5): 3² + 4² = 25 ✓

The formula

The standard form of a circle

(x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²
aThe centre's x. In our circle, 2.
bThe centre's y. In our circle, 1.
r²The radius squared. Ours is 25, so the radius is √25 = 5.
The minus signsThey are part of the formula. They come from along = x − a and up = y − b.

"Standard form" just means this tidy shape: two brackets squared, equal to one number.

Part 2

Reading and writing the equation

The centre and radius are sitting in the equation. You just have to read them the right way round.

Read the centre and radius · Tap the centre · Write the equation · Quick check

Read the centre and radius

The centre is the number that makes each bracket zero

(x + 3) is the same as (x − (−3)). So the centre's x is −3. The sign flips. Say each row first.

EquationCentrer²Radius
(x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25(2, 1)255
(x + 3)² + (y − 4)² = 16(−3, 4)164
x² + (y + 2)² = 9(0, −2)93
(x − 5)² + (y + 1)² = 20(5, −1)20√20 = 2√5
(x + 1)² + y² = 18(−1, 0)18√18 = 3√2

x² on its own is (x − 0)², so the centre's x is 0. If r² is not a square number, leave the radius as a surd.

Tap it on the grid

(x + 1)² + (y − 3)² = 9

No circle is drawn yet. Read the equation, then tap where the centre goes. Your tap snaps to the nearest grid point.

Ask: what number for x makes (x + 1) zero? What number for y makes (y − 3) zero?

When you get it, the circle appears. Its radius is √9 = 3.

Worked example · write the equation

Centre (−3, 2), radius 3√2

Write down the equation of the circle in standard form.

  1. Put the centre in the brackets. Take away each number:
    (x − (−3))² + (y − 2)² = r² (x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = r²
  2. The right side is r², so square the radius. Square the 3 and the √2:
    r² = (3√2)² = 3² × 2 = 18
  3. Put it together:
    !(x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 18
    Check with (0, 5), which is 3 along and 3 up: 3² + 3² = 18 ✓

Three points, one test

Is the point inside, on or outside?

Put each point into (x − 2)² + (y − 1)². That gives the distance² from the centre. Compare it with r² = 25.

(5, 4): 3² + 3² = 18 (6, −2): 4² + (−3)² = 25 (−2, 5): (−4)² + 4² = 32

18 < 25: inside. Closer to the centre than the circle is.

25 = 25: on the circle.

32 > 25: outside. Further away than the circle.

Less than r²: inside. Equal: on. More: outside.

Part 4

Finding the circle from clues

Every question comes down to two jobs: find the centre, then find r².

The recipe · Centre and a point · A diameter · Diameter² is not r² · Touching the axes · Touching both

The recipe

Any circle question in four stages

Find the centre

Given, or a midpoint, or where two lines meet.

Find r²

Distance² from the centre to any point on the circle.

Write it down

(x − a)² + (y − b)² = r². No square root needed.

Check a point

Put a known point in. It should give r² exactly.

Worked example · centre and one point

Centre (1, −2), through (4, 3)

Find the equation of the circle.

  1. The point is on the circle, so the radius is the distance from the centre to it:
    Along = 4 − 1 = 3 Up = 3 − (−2) = 5
  2. We need r², not r. So skip the square root:
    r² = 3² + 5² = 9 + 25 != 34
  3. Write it down:
    !(x − 1)² + (y + 2)² = 34
    The radius is √34, but the equation only needs 34.

A diameter

A(−3, 0) and B(5, 4) are the ends of a diameter

A diameter goes through the centre. Try it first, then show the steps.

  1. The centre is halfway along AB: the midpoint.
    !Centre = ((−3 + 5) ÷ 2, (0 + 4) ÷ 2) = (1, 2)
  2. r² is from the centre to one end, B:
    r² = 4² + 2² = 20
    !(x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 20
Your turn: a circle has centre (2, −1). One end of a diameter is (5, 3). Find the other end.

The usual slip with a diameter

Diameter² is not r²

A(−3, 0) and B(5, 4) are the ends of a diameter. Find r².

✗ Using the whole diameter

AB² = 8² + 4² = 80 r² = 80

80 is the diameter squared. That circle would be twice as wide, and would not pass through A or B.

✓ From the centre

Centre = (1, 2) r² = 4² + 2² = 20

Or halve first: r = √80 ÷ 2 = 2√5, so r² = 20. Halving the length divides the square by 4.

The radius runs from the centre. Find the centre first, then measure from it.

Touching the axes

"Touches the axis" hands you the radius

Touches means it meets the axis at one point, without crossing. The radius is the gap from the centre to that axis.

Touching both axes

It touches both axes and passes through (2, 1)

The centre is (r, r). So the equation is (x − r)² + (y − r)² = r². Put in (2, 1):

(2 − r)² + (1 − r)² = r² r² − 4r + 4 + r² − 2r + 1 = r² r² − 6r + 5 = 0

Part 5

Hidden centres

Sometimes the centre is not given. A right angle, or a chord, gives it away.

The angle in a semicircle · Using it backwards · The perpendicular bisector of a chord · Centre on a line

The angle in a semicircle

Join a point on the circle to both ends of a diameter

The diameter runs from (−3, 1) to (7, 1). A semicircle is half a circle: the part on one side of a diameter.

Pick a point P on the circle. Draw lines from P to both ends of the diameter.

The angle at P will be…

A right angle at P means the side opposite P is a diameter.

Using it backwards

A(−2, 4), B(−2, −2) and C(6, −2) lie on a circle. Find it.

1. Find the right angle

AB is straight up (same x). BC is flat (same y). Straight up meets flat at 90°, so the right angle is at B. With slanted sides, check that the gradients multiply to −1.

2. The side opposite is a diameter

The side that does not touch B is AC. So AC is a diameter.

3. Midpoint, then r²

Centre = ((−2 + 6) ÷ 2, (4 − 2) ÷ 2) = (2, 1). From the centre to A: 4² + 3² = 25. Our circle again: (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 25.

The perpendicular bisector of a chord

Every chord's perpendicular bisector goes through the centre

A chord is a straight line joining two points on the circle. The centre is the same distance, 5, from both ends.

Points the same distance from both ends lie on the perpendicular bisector (lesson 2). So the centre is on it.

Two chords give two bisectors.
The centre is where the bisectors meet.

Worked example · your go first

A(−1, 1) and B(5, −1) lie on a circle. Its centre lies on y = x.

Find the equation of the circle.

Try it on paper first. Then show the steps one at a time.

  1. The perpendicular bisector of AB. Midpoint (2, 0). Gradient of AB is −2 ÷ 6 = −1/3, so flip and change the sign: 3.
    y − 0 = 3(x − 2) y = 3x − 6
  2. The centre is on both lines. Set them equal:
    3x − 6 = x 2x = 6 !x = 3, y = 3
  3. r² from the centre (3, 3) to A(−1, 1):
    r² = 4² + 2² = 20
    !(x − 3)² + (y − 3)² = 20
    Check B: 2² + 4² = 20 ✓

Part 6

A full exam question

Four parts, one part per slide. Try each on paper before you reveal it.

(a) The right angle · (b) The circle · (c) Inside or outside · (d) The other end

Exam-style question · 9 marks

Three points on a circle

The points A(−1, 4), B(1, 0) and C(5, 2) lie on the circle C.

(a) Show that angle ABC = 90°.[2]

(b) Hence find the equation of C.[3]

(c) Determine whether the origin lies inside, on or outside C.[2]

(d) BE is a diameter of C. Find the coordinates of E.[2]

Get a pen. Do all four parts on paper, then go to the next slide.

Part (a) · 2 marks

Show that angle ABC = 90°

Which two sides meet at B? Try it first.

  1. The two sides that meet at B are AB and BC:
    Gradient of AB = −4 ÷ 2 = −2 Gradient of BC = 2 ÷ 4 = 1/2
  2. Multiply them:
    −2 × 1/2 = −1
    So AB is perpendicular to BC, and angle ABC = 90°.

Part (b) · 3 marks

Hence find the equation of C

"Hence" means use part (a). What does the right angle tell you?

  1. The right angle is at B, so the side opposite B, AC, is a diameter.
  2. The centre is the midpoint of AC:
    !Centre = ((−1 + 5) ÷ 2, (4 + 2) ÷ 2) = (2, 3)
  3. r² from the centre to A(−1, 4):
    r² = (−1 − 2)² + (4 − 3)² = 9 + 1 = 10
    !(x − 2)² + (y − 3)² = 10

Part (c) · 2 marks

Inside, on or outside?

"With working" means show the sum. Try it first.

  1. Put the origin (0, 0) into the left side:
    (0 − 2)² + (0 − 3)² = 4 + 9 = 13
  2. Compare with r² = 10: 13 > 10, so the origin lies outside C.

Part (d) · 2 marks

The other end of the diameter from B

The centre is the middle of BE. Try it first.

  1. From B(1, 0) to the centre (2, 3) is 1 along, 3 up.
  2. Make the same jump again from the centre:
    !E = (2 + 1, 3 + 3) = (3, 6)
    Check: (3 − 2)² + (6 − 3)² = 1 + 9 = 10 ✓

Common mistakes

Six slips that cost marks

Keeping the bracket's sign

✗ (x + 3)² + (y − 2)² = 18 has centre (3, −2)

✓ The signs flip: centre (−3, 2)

Forgetting the square root

✗ (x + 1)² + y² = 18 has radius 18

✓ 18 is r². The radius is √18 = 3√2

Squaring a surd radius wrongly

✗ r = 2√3, so r² = 2 × 3 = 6

✓ Square both parts: 2² × 3 = 12

Using the diameter as the radius

✗ r² = AB² = 80

✓ Measure from the centre: r² = 20

Comparing with r instead of r²

✗ 18 > 5, so (5, 4) is outside

✓ Compare with r² = 25: 18 < 25, inside

The wrong gap for "touches the x-axis"

✗ Centre (3, 2) touches the x-axis, so r = 3

✓ The gap down to the x-axis is the y number: r = 2

Finding the centre and r²

  1. Centre and a point: r² = along² + up². No square root.
  2. Diameter: centre = midpoint. r² from the centre to one end.
  3. Touches an axis: r is the gap to that axis. Both axes: centre (r, r).
  4. Right angle at B: the side opposite B is a diameter.
  5. Two points and a line: the centre is where the perpendicular bisector meets the line.

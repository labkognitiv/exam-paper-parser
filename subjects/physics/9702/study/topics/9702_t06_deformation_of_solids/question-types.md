### Archetype: Quantitative Calculation & Multi-Step Derivation

**Example 1 (9702_m16_12_q19)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 19 The Young modulus of steel is twice that of copper. A 50 cm length of copper wire of diameter 2.0 mm is joined to a 50 cm length of steel wire of diameter 1.0 mm, making a combination wire of length 1.0 m, as shown. f...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: With the same force and length, $e = FL/(AE)$, so the smaller area of steel increases its extension by 4 times but its larger $E$ halves it, giving a ratio of 2.
- **Target Answer**: Option B

**Example 2 (9702_s16_11_q03)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 3 A lift is supported by two steel cables, each of length 10 m and diameter 0.5 cm. steel cables 10 m NOT TO SCALE lift The cables extend by 1 mm when a man of mass 80 kg steps into the lift. What is the best estimate of...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Gives $E \approx 2 \times 10^{11} \text{N m}^{-2}$ from $E = FL/(Ax)$ with half the weight on each cable.
- **Target Answer**: Option C

- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Each side is two springs in series with $k/2$, and the two sides in parallel double it back to $k$.

**Example 2 (9702_s16_13_q22)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 22 The diagram shows a beam supported on two pivots. X Y Which statement describes the state of the top surface X and of the bottom surface Y? A Both X and Y are in compression. B Both X and Y are in tension. C X is in c...

### Archetype: Definition, Law & Conservation Principle

**Example 1 (9702_s16_22_q03_a)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: Define the Young modulus.
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Young modulus = stress / strain
- **Target Answer**: Young modulus = stress / strain, where the material obeys Hooke's law.

**Example 2 (9702_s18_23_q04_a)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: Define the Young modulus of a material.
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: force $= 0.55 / 0.085$ ($= 6.47 \text{N}$) ceiling $= 6.47 - (0.056 \times 9.81)$ $= 5.9 \text{N}$
- **Target Answer**: Young modulus $E =$ stress / strain, where stress is force per unit area and strain is extension per unit original length, for Hooke's law behaviour.

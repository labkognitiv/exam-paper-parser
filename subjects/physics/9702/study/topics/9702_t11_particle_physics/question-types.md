## Authentic Cambridge Past Paper Examples

### Archetype: Quantitative Calculation & Multi-Step Derivation

**Example 1 (9702_s16_13_q39)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 39 Radon Rn 222 86 is the start of a decay chain that forms bismuth Bi 214 83 by α and β– emission. For the decay of each nucleus of radon, how many α particles and β– particles are emitted? α particles β– particles A 1 ...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Two $\alpha$ particles give $\Delta A = -8$ and $\Delta Z = -4$, then one $\beta^-$ gives $\Delta Z = +1$ for a net $222$ to $214$ and $86$ to $83$.
- **Target Answer**: Option B

**Example 2 (9702_s16_13_q40)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 40 The magnitude of the charge on the proton may be regarded as +1 unit. On this basis, the charges on the up (u) quark, down (d) quark and their antiquarks (u and d) are not whole units. Which row in the table shows the...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: u is $+\frac{2}{3}$ and d is $-\frac{1}{3}$, and each antiquark has the opposite sign, giving $\bar{u} = -\frac{2}{3}$ and $\bar{d} = +\frac{1}{3}$.
- **Target Answer**: Option C

### Archetype: Graphical Sketching & Trend Interpretation

**Example 1 (9702_s17_11_q39)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 39 A radioactive nucleus is formed by β– decay. This nucleus then decays by α-emission. Which graph of nucleon number N plotted against proton number Z shows the β– decay followed by the α-emission? A B 236 N 236 N 234 2...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: The first step keeps $N=234$ and increases $Z$ by $1$ for $\beta^-$ decay, then $Z$ falls by $2$ and $N$ falls by $4$ from $92,234$ to $90,230$ for $\alpha$-emission.
- **Target Answer**: Option B

**Example 2 (9702_m18_12_q39)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 39 A nucleus of neptunium-236 contains 93 protons and 143 neutrons. This nucleus decays with the emission of an α-particle. The nucleus formed then emits a β– particle. Which diagram shows the changes in the number P of ...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: The track goes $(93,143)$ to $(91,141)$ for $\alpha$-decay, then to $(92,140)$ for $\beta^-$-decay, matching the expected $P$ and $N$ changes.
- **Target Answer**: Option D

### Archetype: Physical Mechanism & Field Explanation

**Example 1 (9702_m16_12_q38)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 38 A sample of an isotope emits β– particles. The emitted β– particles have a range of energies. What must also be emitted? A antineutrinos B neutrinos C antineutrons D neutrons
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: In $\beta^-$ decay a neutron changes to a proton with emission of an electron and an electron-antineutrino to conserve energy and lepton number.
- **Target Answer**: Option A

**Example 2 (9702_m16_12_q39)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 39 A nucleus of magnesium decays into a nucleus of sodium by emitting a β+ particle. The decay is represented by the equation shown. Mg 23 12 →  Na P Q + β + 0 1 What are the values of P and Q? P Q A 22 11 B 22 13 C 23 1...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: P = 23 conserves nucleon number and Q = 11 conserves proton number, since $23 = P + 0$ and $12 = Q + 1$.
- **Target Answer**: Option C

### Archetype: Definition, Law & Conservation Principle

**Example 1 (9702_w22_21_q06_a)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: A lepton is an example of a fundamental particle.  State what is meant by fundamental particle.
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: particle with no internal structure / particle which cannot be broken down into anything smaller
- **Target Answer**: A fundamental particle has no internal structure and cannot be broken down into smaller particles.

**Example 2 (9702_s25_22_q07_a)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 7 (a) State what is meant by a fundamental particle. [1] (b) A nucleus X has 14 nucleons and p protons. The ratio of charge to mass for nucleus X is 4.1 × 107 C kg-1. (i) Determine p. p =  [3] (ii) Nucleus X undergoes β-...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: (a particle that) cannot be divided/subdivided (into smaller particles)
- **Target Answer**: A fundamental particle is a particle that cannot be divided or subdivided into smaller particles.

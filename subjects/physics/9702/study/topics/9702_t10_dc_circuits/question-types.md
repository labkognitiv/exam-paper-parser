### Archetype: Quantitative Calculation & Multi-Step Derivation

**Example 1 (9702_m16_12_q05)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 5 PQR and XYZ are wires in a circuit. A galvanometer connects Q and Y as a null indicator. P Q R X Y Z When the galvanometer reads zero, which statement is correct? A The potential difference between Q and Y is infinite....
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Zero galvanometer current means no charge is driven between Q and Y, so $V = IR = 0$ across QY.
- **Target Answer**: Option B

**Example 2 (9702_m16_12_q34)** - Marks: 1

- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Going round with current $I$, $V_1$ is traversed from $-$ to $+$ while $V_2$ and $V_3$ are traversed from $+$ to $-$, giving $V_1 - V_2 - V_3 = I(R_1+R_2+R_3)$.
- **Target Answer**: Option D

### Archetype: Physical Mechanism & Field Explanation

**Example 1 (9702_m16_12_q35)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 35 Three resistors, each of resistance R, are connected in a network, as shown. R X Y R R The total resistance between points X and Y is 8.0 Ω. What is the value of R ? A 2.7 Ω B 4.0 Ω C 5.3 Ω D 12 Ω
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: The parallel combination gives $2R/3 = 8.0\,\Omega$, so $R = 12\,\Omega$.
- **Target Answer**: Option D

**Example 2 (9702_s16_11_q36)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 36 Two resistors of resistances R1 and R2 are connected in parallel. R1 X Y R2 What is the combined resistance between X and Y? 2 1 + R R D R R C R A R1  +  R2 B 1 R R R 2 2 1 2 1 2 1 + R R
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Inverting $\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}$ gives $R = \frac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$.
- **Target Answer**: Option B

### Archetype: Definition, Law & Conservation Principle

**Example 1 (9702_s18_11_q33)** - Marks: 1

- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: The junction rule for currents is Kirchhoff's first law and charge cannot be created or lost at a point, so inflow equals outflow.
- **Target Answer**: Option A

**Example 2 (9702_s22_11_q36)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 36 The sum of the currents entering a junction in an electrical circuit is always equal to the sum of the currents leaving the junction. Why is this? A It is a consequence of the conservation of charge. B It is a consequ...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Charge cannot be created or destroyed at a junction, so total charge arriving per second must equal total charge leaving per second.
- **Target Answer**: Option A

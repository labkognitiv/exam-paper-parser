# Area and constructing motion graphs

## A journey can have opposite directions

Imagine a delivery robot. It travels east, waits, then travels west back towards its starting point.

A velocity-time graph gives more than a list of velocities. The **area between the graph and the time axis** tells us the robot's **displacement**. This is useful because the graph includes direction as well as speed.

Earlier lessons established that the vertical axis on a velocity-time graph is velocity and the horizontal axis is time. They also established that its gradient is acceleration. This lesson uses a different feature: its area. Do not use the gradient when the question asks for displacement.

<a id="definition-9702_def_displacement"></a>

> **Definition to learn: displacement.** distance in a specified direction from a point

Displacement is the directed change from a reference point. It is not the total length of the route. A positive displacement means the object finishes in the chosen positive direction. A negative displacement means it finishes in the chosen negative direction.

Choose a positive direction before you calculate. In this lesson, take east as positive. Velocities above the time axis are then eastward and positive. Velocities below the axis are westward and negative.

## Why graph area gives displacement

For a short time interval, a constant velocity means:

\[
\text{displacement}=\text{velocity}\times\text{time}
\]

The units confirm this:

\[
\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times\mathrm{s}
\]

\[
=\mathrm{m}
\]

On a velocity-time graph, velocity is the height and time is the width. Height times width is an area. So the area has units of metres and represents displacement.

<a id="formula-9702_formula_uniform_motion_displacement"></a>

> **Formula to learn: Displacement in uniform motion.** \(s = vt\)

Here \(s\) is displacement in metres, \(v\) is velocity in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), and \(t\) is time in seconds. This controlled formula applies only when velocity is uniform. On a graph, that is a horizontal velocity-time section, so its area is a rectangle.

> **Syllabus relationship to use.** The signed area under a velocity-time graph equals displacement.

The mapped formula ID 9702_formula_displacement_velocity_time_graph has no standalone controlled formula record. Its registry alias points to \(s=vt\), which applies only to uniform velocity. The relationship above is taught because the verified syllabus outcome explicitly requires it. It is not presented as a new verified controlled formula.

Ask yourself two questions before adding any areas:

1. Is the region above or below the time axis?
2. Is the region a rectangle, triangle or trapezium?

The sign comes first. A geometric area is always positive, but a graph region below the axis contributes a **negative displacement**.

## Worked example: a single positive region

A cyclist's velocity-time graph is a horizontal line at \(5.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) from \(t=0\) to \(t=8.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Find the displacement.

The line is above the axis, so the displacement is positive. The region is a rectangle.

\[
\text{area}=\text{height}\times\text{width}
\]

\[
\text{displacement}=5.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times8.0\,\mathrm{s}
\]

\[
\text{displacement}=+40\,\mathrm{m}
\]

**Answer: \(40\,\mathrm{m}\) east.**

Check the unit. \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times\mathrm{s}\) gives \(\mathrm{m}\), which is a displacement unit. The sign is positive because the graph stayed above the axis.

What would a horizontal line on the time axis mean? Its height is zero, so its area and displacement for that interval are zero. The object is stationary.

## Triangles and trapezia

Velocity-time lines do not always stay horizontal. A straight sloping line makes a triangle or trapezium.

For a triangle:

\[
\text{area}=\frac{1}{2}\times\text{base}\times\text{perpendicular height}
\]

For a trapezium:

\[
\text{area}=\frac{1}{2}\times(\text{sum of parallel sides})\times\text{perpendicular distance between them}
\]

On a velocity-time graph, the parallel sides are velocities and the perpendicular distance between them is a time interval. Use the velocity values from the graph, including their signs.

## Worked example: a changing positive velocity

An object's velocity increases in a straight line from \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) to \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) during \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Find its displacement in that interval.

The velocities are positive, so the graph is above the axis. The shape is a trapezium, not a rectangle, because its height changes.

\[
\text{displacement}=\frac{1}{2}\times(2.0+8.0)\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times3.0\,\mathrm{s}
\]

\[
\text{displacement}=\frac{1}{2}\times10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times3.0\,\mathrm{s}
\]

\[
\text{displacement}=+15\,\mathrm{m}
\]

**Answer: \(15\,\mathrm{m}\) east.**

Check the size. The velocity is between \(2.0\) and \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), so over three seconds the displacement must be between \(6.0\) and \(24\,\mathrm{m}\). \(15\,\mathrm{m}\) is sensible.

A student uses \(8.0\times3.0\) and obtains \(24\,\mathrm{m}\). Which choice identifies the mistake?

- **A:** The student used the final velocity for the whole interval, even though the velocity changed.
- **B:** The student should make every area negative because velocity changed.

**Feedback for A:** Correct. \(8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) is reached only at the end. The sloping line has a smaller average height, so a trapezium area is needed.

**Feedback for B:** Not correct. The sign depends on whether the graph is above or below the time axis, not on whether the line slopes. This graph stays above the axis, so its displacement is positive.

## A return journey: displacement and distance differ

The signed-area rule gives displacement. To find total **distance**, add the magnitudes of every separate area. Do not cancel a westward section when finding distance.

## Worked example: combining positive and negative areas

A robot travels with velocity \(+4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\). It then stays still for \(2.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Finally, it travels with velocity \(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for \(4.0\,\mathrm{s}\).

First calculate each signed area.

\[
\text{first displacement}=+4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times3.0\,\mathrm{s}=+12\,\mathrm{m}
\]

\[
\text{stationary displacement}=0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times2.0\,\mathrm{s}=0\,\mathrm{m}
\]

\[
\text{final displacement}=-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times4.0\,\mathrm{s}=-8.0\,\mathrm{m}
\]

Now add the signed displacements:

\[
\text{total displacement}=+12+0-8=+4\,\mathrm{m}
\]

**Displacement answer: \(4\,\mathrm{m}\) east.**

For total distance, use the size of each travelled section:

\[
\text{total distance}=12+0+8=20\,\mathrm{m}
\]

**Distance answer: \(20\,\mathrm{m}\).**

Check the story. The robot went \(12\,\mathrm{m}\) east, then \(8\,\mathrm{m}\) west. It is \(4\,\mathrm{m}\) east of the start, but it travelled \(20\,\mathrm{m}\) altogether.

The final graph region is below the time axis. Does that make its geometric area negative before you calculate it? No. Find its size normally, then attach a negative sign because the velocity is negative.

## Constructing a velocity-time graph from words

When a question gives a written motion description, translate one stage at a time. Do not draw a familiar-looking curve before deciding what each sentence means.

For the robot in the worked example:

| Time interval | Velocity | What to draw |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(0\) to \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\) | \(+4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) | A horizontal line above the axis. |
| \(3.0\) to \(5.0\,\mathrm{s}\) | \(0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) | A horizontal line on the axis. |
| \(5.0\) to \(9.0\,\mathrm{s}\) | \(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) | A horizontal line below the axis. |

Use this method:

1. Label the horizontal axis time, in seconds.
2. Label the vertical axis velocity, in metres per second.
3. Choose a scale that fits every stated time and velocity.
4. Mark the start and end time of each stage.
5. Draw the stated velocity level for each stage.
6. Check whether each stage is above, on or below the axis.

If a description says velocity changes uniformly from one value to another, connect the two values with a straight sloping line. If it says velocity stays constant, draw a horizontal line. A graph sketch must represent the motion, not decorate the page.

A runner moves west at a constant \(3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for \(5.0\,\mathrm{s}\), with east positive. Where should that section appear?

- **A:** A horizontal line at \(-3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), below the time axis.
- **B:** A horizontal line at \(+3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), above the time axis.

**Feedback for A:** Correct. West is the chosen negative direction. Constant velocity gives a horizontal line, and the negative velocity places it below the time axis.

**Feedback for B:** Not correct. The speed is \(3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), but velocity also needs direction. A positive value would describe eastward motion in this sign convention.

## Common mistakes to repair

**Mistake: using the area under a displacement-time graph to find displacement.**

The graph type matters. For this lesson, area under a **velocity-time graph** gives displacement. The previous lesson used gradients to obtain other quantities.

**Mistake: adding every region as positive.**

Use signed area for displacement. A region below the time axis represents motion in the negative direction and must be subtracted.

**Mistake: giving total signed area as distance.**

Signed areas may cancel for displacement. Distance uses the size of each section, so it does not cancel a return journey.

**Mistake: drawing a rising line for a constant velocity.**

Constant velocity means the vertical value does not change. Draw a horizontal line. A rising or falling line means velocity changes.

## Core recap

- **Displacement** is **distance in a specified direction from a point**.
- The **signed area under a velocity-time graph** gives displacement.
- Area above the time axis is positive. Area below it is negative.
- Use rectangle, triangle or trapezium area according to the shape.
- For total distance, add the magnitudes of the separate areas.
- Construct each graph stage from its stated time interval, velocity value and direction.
- Check graph type before choosing area or gradient.

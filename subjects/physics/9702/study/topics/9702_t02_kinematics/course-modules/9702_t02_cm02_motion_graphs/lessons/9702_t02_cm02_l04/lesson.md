# Gradient: velocity and acceleration

## What does a line's steepness tell you?

A motion graph does more than show a journey. Its **gradient**, also called its slope, can give a physical quantity.

Imagine two walkers shown on a displacement-time graph. One line rises by \(30\,\mathrm{m}\) in \(10\,\mathrm{s}\). The other rises by \(60\,\mathrm{m}\) in the same time. The second line is steeper. It shows the greater velocity.

The first decision is always: *Which quantity is on the vertical axis?* A gradient on a displacement-time graph means velocity. A gradient on a velocity-time graph means acceleration. The same calculation shape has a different physical meaning because the vertical axis changes.

## Gradient means change divided by time

For any graph, calculate gradient using a change in the vertical quantity divided by a change in the horizontal quantity:

\[
\text{gradient}=\frac{\text{change in vertical-axis quantity}}{\text{change in horizontal-axis quantity}}
\]

On both graph types in this lesson, time is on the horizontal axis. Time is measured in seconds, \(\mathrm{s}\). Read two clear points from the line, subtract in the same order, and keep the units visible.

**Teacher question:** A horizontal line has no rise as time passes. What is its gradient?

Its gradient is zero. The physical meaning depends on the graph type. A horizontal displacement-time line means zero velocity, while a horizontal velocity-time line means zero acceleration.

## Gradient of a displacement-time graph

On a **displacement-time graph**, the vertical axis is displacement in metres, \(\mathrm{m}\). Its gradient has units:

\[
\frac{\mathrm{m}}{\mathrm{s}}=\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

Metres per second is the unit of velocity. This is why the gradient gives velocity.

> **Definition to learn: velocity.** rate of change of displacement

This definition is exactly what the gradient calculation does. It divides a change in displacement by a change in time. A positive gradient means positive velocity in the chosen direction. A negative gradient means velocity in the opposite direction. A steeper line has a larger magnitude of velocity.

For a **straight line**, the gradient is the same anywhere on the line. The velocity is constant. Choose points far apart on the straight line, because a small reading error then changes the gradient less.

### Worked example 1: Constant velocity from a straight line

A displacement-time graph is a straight line. It passes through \((2.0\,\mathrm{s}, 8.0\,\mathrm{m})\) and \((8.0\,\mathrm{s}, 32.0\,\mathrm{m})\). Find the velocity.

First identify the graph type. The vertical values are displacements, so its gradient is velocity.

**Step 1: Find the changes.**

\[
\Delta s=32.0\,\mathrm{m}-8.0\,\mathrm{m}=24.0\,\mathrm{m}
\]

\[
\Delta t=8.0\,\mathrm{s}-2.0\,\mathrm{s}=6.0\,\mathrm{s}
\]

**Step 2: Divide displacement change by time change.**

\[
\text{velocity}=\frac{24.0\,\mathrm{m}}{6.0\,\mathrm{s}}
\]

\[
\text{velocity}=4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

**Answer:** the velocity is **\(4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)** in the graph's positive direction.

**Check:** the line rises, so the velocity must be positive. The units simplify to metres per second, which is a velocity unit.

A displacement-time graph falls in a straight line as time increases. Which statement is correct?

- **A.** The object has negative velocity in the chosen direction.
- **B.** The object has negative acceleration.

**Feedback for A:** Correct. On a displacement-time graph, a falling line has negative gradient, so the velocity is in the chosen negative direction.

**Feedback for B:** The graph does not give acceleration from its gradient. A straight falling line has constant negative velocity. Its acceleration is zero.

### Curves need a tangent

A curved displacement-time graph has a changing gradient. The object is not moving at constant velocity.

To find velocity at one particular time, draw a **tangent** at that point. A tangent is a straight line that touches the curve at the chosen point and has the same direction there. Calculate the tangent's gradient, not the gradient of a line joining two distant points on the curve.

Why? A distant line gives an average velocity over an interval. A tangent gives the velocity at one instant.

**Teacher question:** If a displacement-time curve becomes steeper as time passes, is the object's velocity getting larger or smaller?

The velocity magnitude is getting larger, because each successive tangent is steeper. The next lesson uses graph areas, so do not use an area here to find displacement.

## Gradient of a velocity-time graph

Now change only the vertical axis. On a **velocity-time graph**, the vertical axis is velocity in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Its gradient has units:

\[
\frac{\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}{\mathrm{s}}
\]

\[
=\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

Metres per second per second is the unit of acceleration.

> **Definition to learn: acceleration.** rate of change of velocity

> **Formula to learn: Acceleration.**
>
> \[
> a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
> \]

Here \(a\) is acceleration in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), \(\Delta v\) is the change in velocity in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), and \(\Delta t\) is the time interval in \(\mathrm{s}\). Use this relation when comparing velocities over a time interval. On a velocity-time graph, it is the gradient.

A positive gradient means positive acceleration. A negative gradient means negative acceleration. Negative acceleration does not automatically mean slowing down. It tells you the acceleration direction relative to the sign convention. An object slows down only when its velocity and acceleration have opposite signs.

### Worked example 2: Acceleration from a velocity-time line

A velocity-time graph is a straight line. At \(t=3.0\,\mathrm{s}\), the velocity is \(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). At \(t=8.0\,\mathrm{s}\), the velocity is \(+8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Find the acceleration.

First identify the graph type. The vertical values are velocities, so its gradient is acceleration.

**Step 1: Calculate the change in velocity.**

\[
\Delta v=(+8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})-(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})
\]

\[
\Delta v=+10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

The plus sign shows that velocity increases in the chosen positive direction.

**Step 2: Calculate the time interval.**

\[
\Delta t=8.0\,\mathrm{s}-3.0\,\mathrm{s}=5.0\,\mathrm{s}
\]

**Step 3: Use the acceleration formula.**

\[
a=\frac{+10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}{5.0\,\mathrm{s}}
\]

\[
a=+2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

**Answer:** the acceleration is **\(+2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)**.

**Check:** the line rises, so its gradient and acceleration should be positive. The velocity crosses zero during this interval, but the gradient remains positive on both sides of the crossing.

### Instantaneous acceleration from a curve

On a curved velocity-time graph, acceleration changes with time. To find acceleration at one time, draw a tangent at that time. Then choose two well-separated points on the tangent and calculate its gradient.

Suppose the tangent at \(t=6.0\,\mathrm{s}\) passes through \((2.0\,\mathrm{s}, 3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})\) and \((10.0\,\mathrm{s}, 11.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})\).

\[
\Delta v=11.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}-3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}=8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

\[
\Delta t=10.0\,\mathrm{s}-2.0\,\mathrm{s}=8.0\,\mathrm{s}
\]

\[
a=\frac{8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}{8.0\,\mathrm{s}}=1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

The instantaneous acceleration at \(6.0\,\mathrm{s}\) is **\(1.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)**. The two points came from the tangent, not necessarily from the curve.

On a velocity-time graph, a straight line slopes downward while velocity is still positive. Which statement is correct?

- **A.** The acceleration is negative and the object is slowing down.
- **B.** The acceleration is positive because the velocity is above zero.

**Feedback for A:** Correct. The negative slope gives negative acceleration. Since the velocity is positive, the acceleration is in the opposite direction to the motion, so the speed decreases.

**Feedback for B:** Being above zero says the object moves in the chosen positive direction. Acceleration comes from the slope, which is negative here.

## Mistakes worth repairing

**Mistake: Using the graph title but ignoring the axes.** A graph called "motion graph" is not enough information. Read both axis labels and units before deciding what the gradient means.

**Mistake: Calculating rise divided by run from a curved graph with a long line.** That gives an average gradient over the interval. Use a tangent for an instantaneous velocity or acceleration.

**Mistake: Dropping a negative sign in \(\Delta v\).** Subtract the full signed value. In Worked example 2, subtracting \(-2.0\) correctly creates an increase of \(+10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).

**Mistake: Calling a negative gradient "deceleration" without context.** State the sign first. Then compare signs of velocity and acceleration to decide whether speed increases or decreases.

## Core recap

- Gradient means vertical change divided by time change.
- On a **displacement-time graph**, gradient is **velocity**. Its unit is \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
- On a **velocity-time graph**, gradient is **acceleration**. Its unit is \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).
- **Velocity** is rate of change of displacement.
- **Acceleration** is rate of change of velocity: \(a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}\).
- Use a straight-line gradient for constant values. Use a tangent gradient for the value at one instant on a curve.

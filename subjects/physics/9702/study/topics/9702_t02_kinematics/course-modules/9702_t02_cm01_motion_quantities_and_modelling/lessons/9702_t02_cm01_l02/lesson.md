# Acceleration and describing changing motion

## A bus leaving a stop

A bus is moving east. At first it travels at **4.0 m s⁻¹ east**. A few seconds later it travels at **10.0 m s⁻¹ east**.

The bus has not only moved. Its **velocity has changed**. Physics gives a name to how quickly that change happens: **acceleration**.

Before using that idea, recall one result from Lesson 1. Velocity needs a direction. In this lesson, choose **east as positive** unless a question gives a different direction. Then a westward velocity is negative.

<a id="definition-9702_def_velocity"></a>

> **Definition to learn: velocity.** rate of change of displacement

**Displacement** is the directed change in position. So velocity tells us how quickly position changes and in which direction. For example, \(+4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) means \(4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) east in this sign convention. It is not the same as a speed of \(4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), which gives no direction.

What must change before an object can accelerate: its position, its velocity, or both? Keep that question in mind. An object can be accelerating even while its velocity is zero for one instant, because its velocity can be changing through that instant.

## What acceleration measures

Acceleration is not another word for speed. It measures the **change in velocity for each second**.

<a id="definition-9702_def_acceleration"></a>

> **Definition to learn: acceleration.** rate of change of velocity

Read this carefully. The word **velocity** includes direction. Therefore acceleration can happen in three different everyday-looking situations:

- an object gets faster in its direction of travel;
- an object gets slower in its direction of travel; or
- an object turns, so its direction of velocity changes.

This lesson uses straight-line motion. Turning motion needs further ideas later in the course.

The exact definition is the sentence to learn for an exam. In plainer language, compare the velocity at two times, find how much it changed, then share that change over the time interval.

<a id="formula-9702_formula_acceleration"></a>

> **Formula to learn: Acceleration.** \(a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}\)

Here:

- **\(a\)** is acceleration, measured in metres per second per second, \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\);
- **\(\Delta v\)** means change in velocity, measured in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\);
- **\(\Delta t\)** means the time interval, measured in seconds, \(\mathrm{s}\);
- the Greek letter **\(\Delta\)** means "change in". It is not a multiplication sign.

For a straight journey, calculate the change in velocity as:

\[
\Delta v=v-u
\]

where **\(u\)** is the initial velocity and **\(v\)** is the final velocity. The signs are part of the values. Do not remove them.

Why is the unit \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)?

\[
\frac{\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}{\mathrm{s}}
\]

\[
=\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\times\mathrm{s^{-1}}
\]

\[
=\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

Read \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) as "metres per second per second". An acceleration of \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) means the velocity changes by \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) every second.

## Worked example: getting faster eastward

A cyclist changes velocity from \(+4.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) to \(+10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) in \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Find the acceleration.

First decide what has changed. Both velocities are eastward, but the second one is larger. The cyclist is getting faster eastward, so the acceleration should be positive.

\[
\Delta v=v-u
\]

\[
\Delta v=(+10.0)-(+4.0)=+6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

Now divide the velocity change by the time interval:

\[
a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
\]

\[
a=\frac{+6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}{3.0\,\mathrm{s}}
\]

\[
a=+2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

**Answer: the cyclist's acceleration is \(+2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), or \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) east.**

Check the meaning. In three seconds, a change of \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) each second gives a total change of \(6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). That matches the change from \(4.0\) to \(10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).

Suppose the same velocity change took \(6.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Would the acceleration be larger or smaller? The velocity change is shared over more seconds, so it would be smaller.

## A sign tells a direction, not whether something slows down

With east chosen positive, a **positive acceleration** points east and a **negative acceleration** points west. The sign tells the direction of the acceleration. It does **not** by itself say whether the object is speeding up or slowing down.

Compare the direction of velocity with the direction of acceleration:

| Velocity and acceleration | What happens to speed? |
| --- | --- |
| Same direction | Speed increases. |
| Opposite directions | Speed decreases. |

For example, an object moving west has negative velocity. If it slows while moving west, its acceleration points east, so its acceleration is positive. This can feel surprising only if a negative sign is treated as the word "slowing". It is not. It means the chosen negative direction.

An object slowing down has **acceleration opposite to its velocity**. The informal word **deceleration** means this slowing-down situation. In calculation, keep using acceleration and its sign.

An object moving east has velocity \(-8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Is that statement possible when east is positive? No. The words and sign disagree. A negative velocity means westward motion in this convention.

Consider a car travelling west at \(-12\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Its velocity becomes \(-6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) after \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Which acceleration is correct?

- **A:** \(+2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)
- **B:** \(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)

**Feedback for A:** Correct. The change is \((-6)-(-12)=+6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Dividing by \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\) gives \(+2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). The car is moving west but slowing, so its acceleration points east.

**Feedback for B:** Not correct. The car's final velocity is less negative than its initial velocity. Its velocity therefore changes in the positive, eastward direction. A negative acceleration would make the westward velocity more negative and the car faster westward.

## Worked example: slowing while moving west

A trolley moves west. Take east as positive. Its velocity changes from \(-9.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) to \(-3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) in \(2.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Find its acceleration and describe the motion.

The trolley remains westward because both velocities are negative. Its speed falls from \(9.0\) to \(3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), so it slows down. Expect the acceleration to point east, which is positive.

\[
\Delta v=v-u
\]

\[
\Delta v=(-3.0)-(-9.0)=+6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

\[
a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
\]

\[
a=\frac{+6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}{2.0\,\mathrm{s}}
\]

\[
a=+3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

**Answer: \(+3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), which is \(3.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) east. The trolley is slowing while travelling west.**

Check the sign. A positive acceleration acts against a negative velocity. That is the opposite-direction pattern for slowing down.

## Worked example: speeding up westward

A skateboarder moves west. Take east as positive. The velocity changes from \(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) to \(-8.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) in \(3.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Find the acceleration.

This time the final velocity is more negative. The skateboarder becomes faster westward, so acceleration should also point west and be negative.

\[
\Delta v=v-u
\]

\[
\Delta v=(-8.0)-(-2.0)=-6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

\[
a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
\]

\[
a=\frac{-6.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}{3.0\,\mathrm{s}}
\]

\[
a=-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

**Answer: \(-2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), or \(2.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) west.**

The negative answer does not mean the skateboarder slows. Velocity and acceleration are both westward, so the speed increases.

## A zero acceleration result

If an object's velocity stays exactly the same, its change in velocity is zero.

\[
\Delta v=0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
\]

\[
a=\frac{0}{\Delta t}=0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}
\]

This is **zero acceleration**. It can describe an object at rest, but it can also describe an object moving at constant velocity. For example, a train travelling steadily at \(20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) east has zero acceleration if that velocity does not change.

A train has a constant velocity of \(20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) east for \(5.0\,\mathrm{s}\). Which statement is correct?

- **A:** Its acceleration is \(0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).
- **B:** Its acceleration is \(20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

**Feedback for A:** Correct. The train's velocity has no change, so \(\Delta v=0\). Therefore \(a=0/5.0=0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Moving does not automatically mean accelerating.

**Feedback for B:** Not correct. \(20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) is the velocity, not a change in velocity each second. Acceleration needs a velocity change and a time interval.

## Common mistakes to repair

**Mistake: "Negative acceleration means slowing down."**

This is only true when velocity is positive. First identify the velocity direction. Then compare it with acceleration. Same direction means speed increases. Opposite directions means speed decreases.

**Mistake: subtracting magnitudes and losing directions.**

For westward motion, write negative velocities before subtracting. For example, \((-3)-(-9)\), not \(3-9\) without an explained sign convention.

**Mistake: writing \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) for acceleration.**

That is the unit of velocity. Acceleration is a change in velocity per second, so its unit is **\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)**.

**Mistake: calling any moving object accelerated.**

Ask one question: is its velocity changing? If not, acceleration is zero.

## Core recap

- **Velocity** is the **rate of change of displacement**. It includes direction.
- **Acceleration** is the **rate of change of velocity**.
- Use **\(a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}\)**. Keep signs and units in every line.
- **\(\Delta v=v-u\)**. The initial velocity is \(u\); the final velocity is \(v\).
- An acceleration of **\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)** means a velocity change each second.
- Compare directions. Velocity and acceleration in the same direction mean speed increases. Opposite directions mean speed decreases.
- Zero acceleration means constant velocity, which may be zero or non-zero.

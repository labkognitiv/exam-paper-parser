# Adding and subtracting coplanar vectors

Suppose you walk **6.0 m east** and then **8.0 m north**. Those are two vector movements. A single vector can describe the same overall change in position. It is called the **resultant**.

This lesson adds and subtracts **coplanar vectors**. Coplanar means that the vectors lie in the same flat plane, such as a map or a sheet of paper.

## Vector quantity

From the previous lesson, a vector needs a size and a direction.

> **Definition to learn: vector quantity.** a physical quantity that has magnitude and direction

**Magnitude** means numerical size. A complete numerical vector report includes magnitude, a unit and direction. For example, **7.0 m east** is complete. **7.0 m** alone is not a complete displacement.

An arrow represents a vector. Its length represents magnitude when a scale is stated. Its arrowhead shows direction. The tail is the start and the head is the finish.

## Adding vectors: follow the route

To add \(\vec{A}\) and \(\vec{B}\), draw \(\vec{A}\) first. Move \(\vec{B}\), without changing its length or direction, so that its tail touches the head of \(\vec{A}\). This is a **head-to-tail construction**.

Draw the resultant \(\vec{R}\) from the original start to the final finish:

\[
\vec{R}=\vec{A}+\vec{B}.
\]

The equation means that \(\vec{R}\) has the same overall effect as taking both steps in order. It does not mean you can always add the magnitudes as ordinary numbers.

Which construction represents \(\vec{A}+\vec{B}\)?

- **A:** Put the tail of \(\vec{B}\) at the head of \(\vec{A}\), then draw \(\vec{R}\) from the tail of \(\vec{A}\) to the head of \(\vec{B}\).
- **B:** Put both tails together, then draw \(\vec{R}\) from the head of \(\vec{A}\) to the head of \(\vec{B}\).

**Feedback for A:** Correct. It follows the first vector and then the second. The resultant joins the original start to the final finish.

**Feedback for B:** Not correct for addition. An arrow between two heads does not show the whole route. It can represent a vector difference in another construction, but not head-to-tail addition.

## Vectors on one line

If two vectors point along the same line in the same direction, add their magnitudes. If they point in opposite directions, subtract the smaller magnitude from the larger. The resultant points in the direction of the larger vector.

### Worked example: opposite collinear forces

Two forces act on a box:

\[
F_1=15\,\mathrm{N\ east}
\]

\[
F_2=9.0\,\mathrm{N\ west}.
\]

They point in opposite directions, so they partly cancel.

\[
R=15\,\mathrm{N}-9.0\,\mathrm{N}=6.0\,\mathrm{N}.
\]

The larger force points east. Therefore,

\[
\boxed{\vec{R}=6.0\,\mathrm{N\ east}}
\]

**Check:** the answer is smaller than \(15\,\mathrm{N}\), not \(24\,\mathrm{N}\), because the forces are opposed.

## Perpendicular vectors make a right-angled triangle

Now return to the **6.0 m east** and **8.0 m north** route. The vectors are perpendicular. A head-to-tail diagram makes a right-angled triangle. The resultant is the longest side.

### Worked example: a two-step displacement

**Step 1: draw the route.** Draw \(6.0\,\mathrm{m}\) east. From its head, draw \(8.0\,\mathrm{m}\) north. Draw \(\vec{R}\) from the original tail to the final head.

**Step 2: find the magnitude.**

\[
R=\sqrt{(6.0\,\mathrm{m})^2+(8.0\,\mathrm{m})^2}
\]

\[
R=\sqrt{36\,\mathrm{m^2}+64\,\mathrm{m^2}}
=\sqrt{100\,\mathrm{m^2}}=10\,\mathrm{m}.
\]

**Step 3: find the direction.** Let \(\theta\) be measured north of east. The north side is opposite \(\theta\), and the east side is next to it.

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{8.0}{6.0}
\]

\[
\theta=53.1^\circ.
\]

\[
\boxed{\vec{R}=10\,\mathrm{m\ at\ }53.1^\circ\text{ north of east}}
\]

**Check:** the arrow points north-east. Its magnitude is larger than either single step but smaller than \(6.0+8.0=14.0\,\mathrm{m}\), which fits two non-collinear vectors.

Read direction words carefully. **\(53.1^\circ\) north of east** means start facing east, then turn towards north. It is not the same as \(53.1^\circ\) east of north.

## Subtracting vectors means reversing one vector

For subtraction, rewrite the operation:

\[
\vec{A}-\vec{B}=\vec{A}+(-\vec{B}).
\]

The vector \(-\vec{B}\) has the same magnitude as \(\vec{B}\), but the opposite direction. Reverse the vector after the minus sign, then use the normal head-to-tail addition method. Do not reverse \(\vec{A}\).

### Worked example: subtracting perpendicular vectors

Let

\[
\vec{A}=9.0\,\mathrm{m\ east}
\]

and

\[
\vec{B}=12\,\mathrm{m\ north}.
\]

Find \(\vec{A}-\vec{B}\).

**Step 1: reverse the subtracted vector.**

\[
-\vec{B}=12\,\mathrm{m\ south}.
\]

**Step 2: draw and calculate.** Draw \(9.0\,\mathrm{m}\) east, then \(12\,\mathrm{m}\) south from its head. The resultant points south-east.

\[
R=\sqrt{(9.0\,\mathrm{m})^2+(12\,\mathrm{m})^2}
=\sqrt{225\,\mathrm{m^2}}=15\,\mathrm{m}.
\]

**Step 3: give the direction.**

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{12}{9.0}
\]

\[
\theta=53.1^\circ.
\]

\[
\boxed{\vec{A}-\vec{B}=15\,\mathrm{m\ at\ }53.1^\circ\text{ south of east}}
\]

**Check:** \(\vec{B}\) pointed north. After subtraction, the result must include a southward part. A north-east answer would show addition instead.

For \(\vec{P}-\vec{Q}\), which vector must reverse before you draw the route?

- **A:** \(\vec{P}\)
- **B:** \(\vec{Q}\)

**Feedback for A:** Not correct. Reversing \(\vec{P}\) changes the vector that appears first and no longer represents the stated operation.

**Feedback for B:** Correct. \(\vec{P}-\vec{Q}=\vec{P}+(-\vec{Q})\). Reverse only \(\vec{Q}\), while keeping its magnitude unchanged.

## More than two vectors

Keep extending the head-to-tail route. The resultant still joins the original start to the final finish.

A survey robot moves **10 m west**, then **3.0 m east**, then **7.0 m south**. First combine the opposite horizontal vectors. They give **7.0 m west**. The final route is therefore \(7.0\,\mathrm{m}\) west and \(7.0\,\mathrm{m}\) south.

\[
R=\sqrt{(7.0\,\mathrm{m})^2+(7.0\,\mathrm{m})^2}=9.9\,\mathrm{m}
\]

\[
\tan\theta=\frac{7.0}{7.0}=1,
\qquad \theta=45^\circ.
\]

The resultant is **\(9.9\,\mathrm{m}\) at \(45^\circ\) south of west**.

Do not use perpendicular-component resolution in this lesson. The next lesson introduces that different method.

## A common wrong turn

Some students draw the resultant from the final point back to the start. That arrow has the right length but the wrong direction. A resultant must point **from start to finish**.

Another common mistake is to draw the original \(\vec{B}\) after \(\vec{A}\) for \(\vec{A}-\vec{B}\). That is addition. Reverse \(\vec{B}\) first.

## Core recap

- A **vector quantity** has magnitude and direction. Report its value, unit and direction.
- To add vectors, preserve each vector and place them **head-to-tail**.
- The **resultant** points from original start to final finish.
- Opposite collinear vectors partially cancel. Keep the direction of the larger vector.
- For a perpendicular route, use the completed right-angled triangle for magnitude and direction.
- To subtract \(\vec{B}\), reverse it: \(\vec{A}-\vec{B}=\vec{A}+(-\vec{B})\).

## Authentic Cambridge Past Paper Examples

### Archetype: Quantitative Calculation & Multi-Step Derivation

**Example 1 (9702_m16_12_q03)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: K-1. 3 The SI unit of specific heat capacity is J kg-1 What is the unit of specific heat capacity expressed in SI base units? A m s-2 K-1 B kg m s-1 K-1 C m2 s-2 K-1 D kg m2 s-1 K-1
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Since $\text{J} = \text{kg m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}$, dividing by $\text{kg}$ and $\text{K}$ leaves $\text{m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}\text{K}^{-1}$.
- **Target Answer**: Option C

**Example 2 (9702_s16_11_q02)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 2 Which pair of quantities do not have the same SI base units? A electromotive force and electric potential difference B pressure and stress C spring constant and moment of a force D torque and work
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Spring constant is force per extension in $\text{N m}^{-1}$ while moment of a force is force times distance in $\text{N m}$, so base units differ.
- **Target Answer**: Option C

### Archetype: Graphical Sketching & Trend Interpretation

**Example 1 (9702_s16_11_q05)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 5 A calibration graph is produced for a faulty ammeter. 1.0 ammeter reading / A 1.0 0 0 true current / A Which ammeter reading will be nearest to the true current? A 0.2 A B 0.4 A C 0.6 A D 0.8 A
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: At an ammeter reading of $0.8\text{ A}$ the curve is closest to the line where reading equals true current, with true current about $0.75\text{ A}$.
- **Target Answer**: Option D

**Example 2 (9702_m19_12_q04)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 4 A digital balance is used to weigh ingredients in a laboratory. When a weight is applied to the digital balance, an electronic circuit generates a current which is then converted into a digital readout on the display. ...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Passes near $0.5\text{ mA}$ at $5\text{ N}$ and $2.0\text{ mA}$ at $30\text{ N}$ with a decreasing gradient, matching the higher low-weight sensitivity.
- **Target Answer**: Option A

- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: It correctly gives nano as $10^{-9}$, micro as $10^{-6}$ and pico as $10^{-12}$.

**Example 2 (9702_s16_13_q01)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 1 Which list contains only SI base units? A ampere, kelvin, joule, gram B kilogram, newton, metre, ampere C metre, coulomb, second, kelvin D second, kelvin, ampere, kilogram
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Second, kelvin, ampere and kilogram are all SI base units for time, temperature, current and mass.

### Archetype: Definition, Law & Conservation Principle

**Example 1 (9702_w18_12_q33)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 33 Which two units are used to define the coulomb? A ampere and second B ampere and volt C volt and ohm D volt and second
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Charge is current multiplied by time, so $1 \text{ C} = 1 \text{ A} \times 1 \text{ s}$.
- **Target Answer**: Option A

**Example 2 (9702_s19_13_q32)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 32 Which two units are used to define the volt? A ampere and ohm B coulomb and joule C coulomb and ohm D coulomb and second
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Potential difference is energy per unit charge, so $1 \text{ V} = 1 \text{ J C}^{-1}$ using joule and coulomb.
- **Target Answer**: Option B

### Archetype: Diagrammatic Vector & Circuit Construction

**Example 1 (9702_s16_11_q01)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 1 Three wires each exert a horizontal force on a vertical pole, as shown. Which vector diagram shows the resultant force R acting on the pole? A B C D R R R R
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: This places the east, south and north-west forces head-to-tail in turn, so the dashed $R$ from start to finish is the correct vector sum.
- **Target Answer**: Option B

**Example 2 (9702_s16_12_q03)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 3 The diagram shows two vectors X and Y, drawn to scale. X Y If X = Y - Z, which diagram best represents the vector Z? C D A B
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Points down and to the right with length matching $Y-X$, which is $Y$ plus the long down-right reversed $X$.
- **Target Answer**: Option A

### Archetype: Experimental Uncertainty & Measurement Analysis

**Example 1 (9702_m16_12_q04)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 4 Quantity X has a fractional uncertainty of x. Quantity Y has a fractional uncertainty of y. X ? What is the fractional uncertainty in 2 Y A x + y B x - y C x + 2y D x - 2y
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: Division adds the fractional uncertainties and $Y^2$ contributes $2y$, giving $x + 2y$.
- **Target Answer**: Option C

**Example 2 (9702_s16_11_q04)** - Marks: 1
- **Question Prompt**: 4 When performing an experiment, a student should minimise the uncertainty of any measurement. In which case is the student reducing the systematic error in a measurement? A adjusting a voltmeter needle pointer to the ze...
- **Mark Scheme Criteria**: A non-zero needle gives the same zero offset to every potential difference reading, which is a systematic error, so resetting it to zero removes it.
- **Target Answer**: Option A
